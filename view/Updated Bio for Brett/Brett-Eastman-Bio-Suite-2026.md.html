C · Long bio (≈650 words)

Book jackets, keynote introductions, board packets, major partnership documents.

Brett Eastman came to faith in college because someone took a chance and invited him to a small group Bible study. He heard the message of the gospel in a room safe enough to argue with it, and was encouraged to gather friends of his own and start another group. Seven of the young men in that group came to Christ, and three of them went into full-time ministry. Nearly everything he has built since has been an attempt to reproduce that room at scale.

His first career was in technology. After a marketing degree from San Diego State University he worked in Silicon Valley during the early personal-computing era, in training and marketing roles alongside Apple, Xerox, and Novell — an education in how a product becomes a category and how a category becomes a movement. When he sensed a call into ministry he earned an M.Div. from Talbot School of Theology at Biola University and later completed a management program at Northwestern's Kellogg School of Management.

He joined the senior staff at Willow Creek Community Church under Bill Hybels, spending seven years in Chicago before Rick Warren and the Saddleback team invited him to California to champion small group ministry, leadership development, and the membership process. Saddleback had more than ten thousand people and seventy-three off-campus small groups when he arrived. The math was the whole problem. With only two or three pastoral staff assigned to groups, the ministry had to shift from a staff-driven model to a volunteer-driven one, which connected roughly half the congregation, and then to a resource-driven model built on video curriculum. The first series they produced, on the book of James, mobilized a hundred people to open their homes. The production was crude; the lesson was not.

That lesson became the 40 Days of Purpose campaign, which Brett helped design and which he took to market one church at a time — coaching the first twenty-nine congregations to run it before the model spread to tens of thousands of churches worldwide. He wrote and produced the first Purpose Driven small group series for Rick Warren, Doing Life Together, published by Zondervan, which has sold more than four million copies and shaped the design of dozens of small group series and church campaigns that followed. The "smart curriculum" model he engineered for that series — content built so a church could run it, adapt it, and teach from it without a consultant in the room — is still the architecture underneath most of what he builds.

In 2006 he founded LifeTogether Ministries. Over the two decades since, he and his team have produced hundreds of custom video and print campaigns for many of the hundred largest and fastest-growing churches in America, along with denominations, faith-based nonprofits, and most of the major Christian publishing houses, working with authors including Max Lucado, John Maxwell, John Ortberg, and Christine Caine.

His recent work has moved the same campaign engine toward families and the professionals who serve them. With Tom Conway he co-founded Family Legacy by Design, which coaches families of wealth through the five areas of legacy and certifies advisors to lead those conversations rather than outsource them. He serves as chief marketing officer at Equity Partners and is writing Built to Grow, a book for Christian financial advisors on scaling with purpose and exiting with peace. He continues to collaborate with Ron Blue and the Ron Blue Institute on biblical financial wisdom resources for local churches, and he has spent the past two years building AI-assisted publishing systems that let a single message be produced as a sermon, a small group series, a family journey, and a churchwide campaign at once.

Brett married his high school sweetheart, Dee, right out of college. Dee is the founding director of The Daniel Plan and a contributing author to the number one New York Times bestseller of the same name. They have five children — Joshua, Breanna, and identical triplets Melody, Meagan, and Michelle — and live in Southern California.

"In the dream-it-up phase of a project, where you imagine the possibilities and produce a scenario for communicating your message in a compelling way, I never met anyone who can match Brett's genius." — Rick Warren, Saddleback Church

If it's accurate and you can source it, it's a strong line and I'll write it back in. If it's "we did early design work that influenced it," that needs different wording.

  • Kellogg. Your files say a management certificate / one-year business management program, not a degree. I wrote it that way. My prior note had it as "graduate of Kellogg" — the files are more precise, so the files win.
  • Willow Creek before Saddleback. Two files list Saddleback first, but the narrative passages clearly go Willow → Saddleback. I sequenced it that way throughout. Confirm.
