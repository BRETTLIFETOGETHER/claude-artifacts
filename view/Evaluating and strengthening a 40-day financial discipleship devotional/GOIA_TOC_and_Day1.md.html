DAY 1

The Foundation

The Question Underneath Every Financial Question

God's ownership is the place your heart can finally rest.

TODAY'S VERSES

"The earth is the Lord's, and everything in it, the world, and all who live in it." — Psalm 24:1 (NIV)

"For from him and through him and for him are all things. To him be the glory forever! Amen." — Romans 11:36 (NIV)

I was on a plane to Grand Rapids, on my way to meet my editor, when the young woman sitting next to me caught my attention. She was maybe twenty, well-dressed, working her way through one of those in-flight shopping catalogs — the ones that sell designer luggage and things nobody actually needs at thirty-five thousand feet. What I remember isn't the catalog. It's her face. She looked miserable. Not bored. Genuinely, deeply unhappy — the way a person looks when they've been reaching for something for a long time and keep coming up just short.

I thought about her for days afterward. And what I eventually concluded was this: whatever she was looking for in that catalog, she already had the answer to her real question. She just didn't know yet that it wasn't in there.

Most of us, at some point, have been that person. Reaching. Calculating. Wondering if the next raise, the next paid-off card, the next milestone will finally produce the feeling we're after. We don't usually say it out loud, but the question is always running somewhere in the background: Will I be okay? Will I ever have enough? And if I do have enough — will it stay?

Those are real questions. They deserve a real answer. And what forty years in the financial world taught me is that you've been asking the right question — just in the wrong direction.

The Three Wrong Questions

I spent twenty-five years as a financial advisor. I sat with thousands of families — struggling ones, comfortable ones, wealthy ones — and underneath every financial conversation, regardless of income level, the same three questions kept surfacing:

  • Will I ever have enough?
  • Will it continue to be enough?
  • How much is enough, anyway?

These aren't bad things to wonder. But I came to believe they were the wrong questions to lead with. Not because they're unimportant — but because they put you at the center of an equation you were never designed to solve. The moment you lead with "will I have enough," you've already assumed that you are the one responsible for making sure the answer is yes. And that assumption is where the weight comes from.

Underneath those three questions, I almost always found one of three deeper ones: What will it take to feel successful? What will it take to feel significant? What will it take to feel secure? And the culture's answer is always the same: more. More income, more options, more cushion, more status.

Here is what forty years taught me: more doesn't answer those questions. It moves them.

The Three Right Questions

Scripture offers three different questions — ones that actually lead somewhere:

  • Who owns it?
  • How much is enough? (As a stewardship question, not a scarcity question.)
  • Is the next steward chosen and prepared?

That first question — who owns it? — changes everything downstream. Once you answer it honestly, the weight shifts. Not because your circumstances change, but because your role changes. You stop being the owner responsible for guaranteeing outcomes, and you become a steward responsible for faithfulness. Those are very different jobs. And only one of them was designed for you.

Psalm 24:1 doesn't negotiate: "The earth is the Lord's, and everything in it." Romans 11:36 takes it further — everything is from him, through him, and for him. That's not just a theological sentence. It's a description of reality. Your income, your savings, your home, your plans, your family — none of it is self-originated or self-sustained. It was entrusted to you. You didn't create it. You're managing it for Someone who was here before it existed.

If God owns it, then you are not alone in carrying it. And if you are not alone in carrying it, the question changes — from "Will I be okay?" to "What does faithful look like today?"

What This Does to the Story

Early in my career, I was asked to testify before a congressional subcommittee. The senator asked what I would tell the American family about their finances. I gave him four principles. He picked up his pencil, wrote them down, and said, "It seems to me those would work at any income level." I said, "You're right, senator — including the United States government." We had quite a conversation after that.

But those principles only work if they're built on the right foundation. And the foundation isn't discipline, or willpower, or a better budget app. The foundation is a settled answer to the ownership question. Because the way you handle money always follows what you believe about money. Behavior follows belief — not as a motivational phrase, but as how formation actually works.

If you believe your money is yours — earned by your effort, secured by your planning — then every financial pressure becomes a personal verdict. Every unexpected expense feels like a threat. Every decision carries the weight of your future. The result is exactly what I watched thousands of people carry into my office: not financial ignorance, but financial fear dressed up as responsibility.

If you believe your money is God's — entrusted to you, accountable to him, managed on his behalf — the weight redistributes. You still work hard. You still plan thoughtfully. You still take your responsibilities seriously. But you stop acting like everything is riding on you. Because it isn't. The Owner is steady. And a steady Owner means you can be faithful without being frantic.

That's the re-ordering that this first week is after. Not a financial plan. A different starting place.

THINK ABOUT IT

When something financial goes wrong — an unexpected bill, an income drop, an uncertain season — what is your first internal move? What do you reach for?

The culture's three questions (Will I have enough? Will it last? How much is enough?) assume you are responsible for the answers. What would change in your daily life if you genuinely believed you weren't?

I've seen that when people let God's ownership become their real foundation — not just a theological idea, but a lived one — it changes their contentment, their confidence, and the way they communicate with their spouse about money. Which of those three do you most need to change?

REFLECT AND RESPOND

The Three Questions Inventory — (10 minutes) Write short, honest answers.

  1. The financial question I carry most often is:

  2. Underneath it, what I'm really asking is: Will I be successful? / Will I be significant? / Will I be secure?

  3. The way I currently try to answer that question is:

  4. If God is the Owner, what changes about who has to answer it?

The "From Him" Reset — (2 minutes) Read Romans 11:36 slowly: "For from him and through him and for him are all things."

  • Where am I acting like the "from" — like I'm the source?
  • Where am I living as if it's "for me" rather than "for him"?
  • What is one area I can return to God today?

TODAY'S DECLARATION

"God, you are the Owner. Everything I have and everything I am belongs to you. I am not the source. Teach me to steward what you've entrusted to me with open hands and a steady heart."

ONE STEP TODAY

Choose one — and do it today, not tomorrow.

The Quiet Transfer — Choose one area you've been carrying like an owner. Pray one sentence: "Father, this belongs to you. Teach me to steward it." Don't perform it. Mean it.

The Decision Pause — Before your next money decision today, pause for ten seconds and say: "God, you're the Owner. Lead me." Then proceed with steadiness, not urgency.

The Stewardship Conversation — Tell one trusted person: "I'm thinking through what it would actually mean to live like God owns what I have. Will you ask me next week what changed?"

CLOSING PRAYER

Father, you are the source of every good gift, and you do not change. Forgive me for the ways I've lived like everything depends on me. Today I return to the foundation: everything is from you, through you, and for you. Teach me to live with open hands — not passive, but trusting. You are the Owner, and I am yours. Amen.
