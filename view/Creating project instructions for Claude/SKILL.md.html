Warm Pastoral Voice

Josh's voice for churchwide curriculum, books, and website copy. Warm, pastoral, invitational. Never preachy, never academic.

Core feel

Every line should sound like an invitation, not an assignment. Short. Plain words. Forward-moving. If a sentence needs a semicolon or a subordinate clause to land, it's not this voice yet — break it apart.

The repeating-word device

This is the signature move. When listing parallel items (weekly themes, a triad of taglines, a list of "best for" audiences), lock every item into the same grammatical scaffold so the list reads like one sentence in six beats.

Pattern A — past participle + infinitive:

Created to Know God / Designed to Belong / Formed to Become Like Jesus / Gifted to Serve / Sent to Make a Difference / Called to Multiply

Pattern B — imperative verb + "Your" + noun:

Remember Your Story / Recognize Your Design / Renew Your Relationship / Refocus Your Season / Respond to Your Assignment / Reproduce Your Wisdom

Pattern C — anaphora triad (for taglines, 3 lines max):

Helping Churches Go Deeper. / Helping Disciples Grow Stronger. / Helping Every Person Discover a Life That Matters. (Same opening word, widening scope each line: institution → group → individual.)

Rule: pick ONE scaffold per list and hold it for every item. Don't let item 4 break the grammar of items 1–3. If you can't make all six items fit one scaffold without forcing it, it's a sign the theme needs more development — flag that instead of forcing a bad fit.

When NOT to force it (per Josh's own correction): spinoff/niche adaptations (e.g., a "Men's Purpose Journey" spinoff) don't need the same rigid scaffold as a flagship campaign — those can stay looser and more conversational.

Title + subtitle formula

  • Title: 2–4 words, evocative, plain. Not a full sentence. ("Rooted," "Made for More," "Your Next Faithful Chapter")
  • Subtitle: one line that names the promise or the shift, often "[Number] Days/Weeks to [verb list]" or "[Verb phrase] Every [noun] with [what matters]." ("40 Days to Know God, Belong, Grow, Serve, Live Sent, and Multiply Your Life")

Tension → resolve fast

Name the friction in a few words, then move immediately to the aspirational resolution in the same sentence. Don't dwell.

"Helping people move beyond success toward significance."

Not: several sentences building up the problem before getting to the hope. One clause of tension, then forward motion.

Word bank

Reach for: discover, belong, grow, serve, sent, multiply, flourish, significance, whole-life, legacy, calling, mission, rooted, alive, together

Avoid: jargon, therapeutic/clinical language, guilt or shame framing, anything that reads like a syllabus or a corporate mission statement

Self-check before delivering copy

  1. Could this be read aloud in a warm invitation, not a lecture? If not, cut clauses.
  2. If it's a list of parallel items, do they all share one grammatical scaffold?
  3. Is there a tension named AND resolved in the same breath, not two paragraphs apart?
  4. Any word from the "avoid" list snuck in?
