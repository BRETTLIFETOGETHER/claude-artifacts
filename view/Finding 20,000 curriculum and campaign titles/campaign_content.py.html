"q3":[

],
"qnote":"Before, during, after. That is the whole structure of every testimony ever told, and the person answering does not need to know that is what they are doing.",
"steps":[
("One &mdash; Ask badly, on purpose","Do not send a form. Walk up to somebody after a service and say the four lines on the card. Ask eight people in one morning and six will say yes. A written invitation to share a testimony gets a twelve percent response rate; asking in person gets seventy."),
("Two &mdash; Put them in their own seat","Film in the worship center, in the row they actually sit in, in whatever they wore that day. Familiar space produces an unguarded face. A studio produces a performance, and a performance is the one thing that will not work."),
("Three &mdash; Two people, one phone","One person films, one person asks. Never the same person. The one asking sits just beside the lens so the eyeline is right and the person is talking to a human rather than to glass. Phone horizontal, on anything stable, framed from the chest up."),
("Four &mdash; One light, nothing else","A single lamp or a window to one side. Do not light the stage. Do not use the house system. If you can see their eyes clearly, you are finished with lighting."),
("Five &mdash; Ask the three questions and then be quiet","Ask, then wait. The usable answer almost always arrives after the pause the interviewer wanted to fill. Say nothing for four full seconds after they stop talking. That silence is where the real sentence lives."),
("Six &mdash; Ask the follow-up once","One only: <em>can you tell me about a specific day?</em> Generalities are unusable; a Tuesday is usable. That single question converts more mediocre testimonies than any amount of editing."),
("Seven &mdash; Say thank you and stop","Do not ask them to redo it. Do not ask them to be more concise. You will fix it in the cut, and asking a person to perform their own life a second time is where sincerity goes to die."),
],
"pairs":"Film couples together. A husband and wife on the same bench tell a better story than either alone, because they correct each other and the correcting is the best footage you will get. Film single people alone and never apologize for it &mdash; a single person telling their own story straight to camera is one of the most underused images in a church service.",
