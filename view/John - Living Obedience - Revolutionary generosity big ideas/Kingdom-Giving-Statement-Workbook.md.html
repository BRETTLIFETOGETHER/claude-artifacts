YOUR KINGDOM GIVING STATEMENT

A workbook for settling five decisions

Companion to Visionary Generosity and the Two Engines workshop The Signatry with LifeTogether

Before you begin

This workbook does not ask you for anything. No one will follow up, no one will call, and nothing you write here is submitted anywhere. It exists to help you write one page — a statement of what you believe about what you own and what you intend to do about it — and then to hand that page to two people who will hold you to it.

It should take about two hours, and it works far better with your spouse than alone.

Five decisions sit underneath everything else. Most people never make any of them explicitly, which is why their giving drifts. Make them once, in writing, and the rest becomes arithmetic.

This workbook is not legal, tax, or financial advice. Every decision here should go through your own attorney and advisor before it becomes a transaction.

PART ONE — OWNERSHIP

Almost every Christian affirms that God owns it all, and almost every Christian manages money as though the affirmation were poetry. The gap between the two is where most of the difficulty lives. What closes it is not a stronger feeling. It is a sentence you are willing to sign.

Read: Psalm 24:1. 1 Chronicles 29:11–14. Deuteronomy 8:17–18.

1. In your own words, and without using a phrase you have heard in a sermon: what do you actually believe about who owns what is in your accounts?

2. Where does your behavior currently contradict that belief? Be specific. Name a decision, not a category.

3. If God owns it, what is your actual job description? Write it in one sentence, the way you would write a role for someone you were hiring.

PART TWO — ENOUGH

A tithe answers the question of the floor. Nobody ever gave you a ceiling. Naming the number at which your household has enough is the single most predictive decision in this entire field, and it is the one most people cannot make on the first attempt.

If you cannot write a number today, write down what makes it hard. That answer is worth more than a number you don't believe.

Read: Proverbs 30:8–9. 1 Timothy 6:6–10. Exodus 16:16–20.

PART THREE — SIGHT

You will fund what you can see, and you will fund generically if you cannot see anything specific. That is not a character flaw; it is how attention works. The people whose giving changed something almost always went somewhere first.

Read: Jeremiah 1:11–13. Amos 7:7–8. Nehemiah 2:11–18.

9. Three things you have seen with your own eyes in the last five years that you could not stop thinking about. Not causes. Not categories. Things you saw.

10. What has God already given you access to, expertise in, or grief over? These three channels are where vision usually arrives.

Access: ___________

Expertise: ___________

Grief: ___________

11. Ask someone who knows you well: what do you think I actually care about? Write their answer here, and note where it differs from yours.

PART FOUR — SOIL

13. What finished looks like there. Be concrete enough that someone else could tell whether it happened.

16. What would you fund if you did not need to be proven right?

PART FIVE — THE YES

Obedience here does not mean compliance with a rule. It means being close enough to be told something specific, and having settled the answer before the question arrives. A family that deliberates every opportunity on its merits gives far less than a family that decided once.

Read: 1 Samuel 15:22. 2 Corinthians 8:1–5. Proverbs 3:27–28.

17. How long did your last significant gift take, from the moment you were convinced to the moment the transfer settled? ______

18. What slowed it down?

19. What has to be built or agreed so that a yes takes days rather than months?

20. Who is permitted to tell you no about money? Name a person, not a principle.

21. What is the largest thing you own that you have never once considered giving — and what is the real reason it never crossed your mind?

What to do with this page

Give it to two people. Not your advisor — a friend, and someone in the next generation. A statement nobody has seen is a private intention, and private intentions decay.

Read it once a year, on the same date. Change what has genuinely changed. Notice what you keep softening; that is where the real work is.

Take it to your advisor as a brief, not a request. This page tells the person managing your money what you believe about it. Most advisors have never been told, and most would serve you better if they were.
