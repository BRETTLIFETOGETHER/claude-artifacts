Building the vision and obedience prayer prompts — 21 days plus situational prayers

VISION & OBEDIENCE

Twenty-one days of prayer prompts

Companion to Visionary Generosity and the Two Engines workshop The Signatry with LifeTogether

How to use these

These are not readings. Each day gives you a passage, a few sentences of framing, and a prayer written to be prayed out loud rather than admired. Say it if it fits. Change the words if it doesn't — the prayer is a starting place, not a script, and a borrowed prayer you don't mean is worth less than a clumsy one you do.

Three weeks, three movements. The first week asks God to show you something. The second asks whether you are willing. The third asks what the assignment is. That order is deliberate; willingness before sight produces anxiety, and assignment before willingness produces a project.

Ten minutes a day. Scripture is NIV.

WEEK ONE — SEEING

Day 1 · Psalm 24:1

Begin where the argument begins. Not with what you should give, but with a question of fact about who owns what is in your accounts.

Father, the earth is yours and everything in it, which includes the things I have spent my life calling mine. I have believed that in principle and managed it as though it were not true. Start there. Correct the sentence I have been living by.

Day 2 · Jeremiah 1:11–13

God's question to the prophet is not what will you do. It is what do you see. Sight comes before assignment, every time.

Lord, I have been asking how much. You have been asking what I see. I do not think I see very much. Open my eyes to something specific — not a cause, not a category, something real enough to have a name.

Day 3 · Nehemiah 2:11–18

Before he said a word to anyone, Nehemiah rode out at night and looked at the walls himself. Proximity preceded the plan.

God, I have made decisions from a distance about people I have never met. Give me the humility to go and look before I decide, and the honesty to admit how much I have funded on the strength of a presentation.

Day 4 · Luke 10:30–37

The priest and the Levite were not cruel. They were on their way somewhere. What made the Samaritan different was that he was willing to be interrupted.

Lord, I am efficient and I am busy, and both have protected me from things I did not want to see. Make me interruptible. Let something get through this week that I would ordinarily route around.

Day 5 · Amos 7:7–8

God shows Amos a plumb line before he says anything about it. The image comes first; the meaning follows.

Father, I want to understand before I obey, and you have often reversed that order. Show me what you want me to see, even if I do not yet know what it means or what it will cost.

Day 6 · Ephesians 3:20

Whatever you are currently imagining is smaller than what he is able to do. Sit with the discomfort of that rather than resolving it.

God, my plans are modest and my faith is calibrated to what I can manage. Enlarge what I am willing to picture. I am not asking to be impressed. I am asking to stop settling.

Day 7 · Habakkuk 2:2–3

Write it down and make it plain. Vision that stays in your head is indistinguishable from a mood.

Lord, help me put it in writing this week — what I have seen, and what I think you are asking. Give me the nerve to be specific enough to be wrong.

WEEK TWO — WILLINGNESS

Day 8 · 1 Samuel 15:22

Saul brought an offering God had not asked for. To obey is better than sacrifice — God prefers the gift he requested to the gift you selected.

Father, I have given you things you did not ask for and felt good about it. Teach me the difference between generosity I chose and obedience I received. I would rather be told than admired.

Day 9 · 2 Corinthians 8:1–5

The Macedonians gave beyond their ability, out of severe poverty, and Paul says the order was this: they gave themselves first to the Lord. The money followed.

Lord, I have been negotiating over a percentage while withholding the thing underneath it. Take me first. Then the rest of this conversation gets simpler.

Day 10 · Proverbs 30:8–9

The prayer for neither poverty nor riches is the most countercultural request in the book of Proverbs, and almost nobody prays it sincerely.

God, give me neither poverty nor riches. I have asked for the second one my whole adult life and called it prudence. Show me the number that is enough, and give me the courage to write it down.

Day 11 · Matthew 19:16–26

The man in this passage was not wicked. He was decent, observant, and unable to let go — and Jesus loved him and let him leave.

Lord, I recognize him. Show me the one thing I would not release if you asked. I would rather find out now than at the end.

Day 12 · 1 Timothy 6:17–19

Not to be arrogant, not to put hope in wealth, to be rich in good deeds, generous and willing to share. That is the whole instruction to people like me.

Father, my hope has quietly relocated. I trust the balance more than I trust you and I can prove it by how I react when it drops. Move my hope back.

Day 13 · Hebrews 13:5

Contentment is not a personality trait. It rests on a promise about presence, not provision.

Lord, the fear underneath my grip is not greed, it is that there will not be enough and I will be alone with the consequences. You said you would never leave. I am asking to believe that at the level where I make decisions.

Day 14 · Proverbs 3:27–28

Do not tell your neighbor to come back tomorrow when you have it with you now. Delay is its own kind of answer.

God, I have a habit of deciding to decide later. Show me what I have been deferring, and let me settle it this week rather than adding it to the list of things I intend to do.

WEEK THREE — ASSIGNMENT

Day 15 · Matthew 13:1–9

The seed is the same in every case. What varies is the ground. The multiple depends on where it lands.

Lord, I have been asking how much to sow and paying no attention to the soil. Show me ground worth planting in. Give me patience with the years before anything comes up.

Day 16 · Esther 4:14

For such a time as this. The position you already hold may be the assignment rather than the obstacle to it.

Father, I have been looking for a calling somewhere other than where you have already put me. Show me what my position, my history, and my grief have prepared me to fund.

Day 17 · Luke 8:1–3

Several women supported the ministry of Jesus out of their own means. Three verses, and then history moves on without them.

God, teach me to want the work more than the credit. Let there be something this year that only you and I know about.

Day 18 · Galatians 6:9

Do not become weary in doing good. The harvest comes at the proper time, which is rarely the time we would have chosen.

Lord, I am impatient with things that do not show results, and I have withdrawn from good work at exactly the point it needed me. Give me the patience to fund year seven.

Day 19 · Ecclesiastes 11:1–6

Cast your bread on the waters. Sow in the morning and in the evening, because you do not know which will succeed.

Father, I want certainty before I commit and you have not offered it. Give me the freedom to fund something unproven, and the maturity to accept it if it fails.

Day 20 · Deuteronomy 6:4–9

Talk about these things when you sit at home and when you walk along the road. Transmission happens in ordinary conversation or it does not happen.

Lord, my children will inherit what we decided without ever knowing why. Give me the words this month, and a room to say them in, and the humility to let them disagree.

Day 21 · Matthew 24:14 · Revelation 7:9

There is an ending. A great multitude from every nation, tribe, people and language. The work is finishable, and someone will write the last check.

God, let my family aim at something with an end. Show us what would be true when the work is done, and let us spend what we have been given getting there. Make our hands quick and our grip loose.

FOR PARTICULAR MOMENTS

Before a decision

Father, I am about to decide something I cannot undo. Quiet the part of me that wants to look generous and the part of me that is afraid. If this is from you, make me willing. If it is my own appetite wearing your name, make that plain before I sign. Give me people around me who will say the hard thing, and ears to hear it.

Before a family conversation

Lord, we are about to talk about money as a family and we are not good at it. Guard us from old comparisons and from the things we have never said. Give the quiet ones something to say and the loud ones the discipline to wait. Let this be the first of many rather than the only one.

After a gift that did not work

Father, it did not turn out as we hoped. Some of that was the world and some of it was my judgment, and I would rather learn than pretend. Thank you that the return was never mine to guarantee. Keep me from becoming careful in the wrong direction — I would rather be wrong occasionally than closed permanently.

After a sale, when the work is finished

Lord, the thing I built is no longer mine, and I do not entirely know who I am this morning. Thank you for the years and the people and the work. Show me what the next season is for. Do not let me spend it guarding a pile.

For someone who has almost nothing to give

Father, I have been told this conversation is for other people. But the widow gave two coins and you called it more than all the rest. Let me give what I actually have, gladly, without measuring it against anyone. Make me generous now, so I do not have to become generous later.
