LIVING OBEDIENCE — BOOK COVER PROMPTS

Prompts that render the whole cover, type included

Why the earlier ones gave you a picture

They said no text, no letters, no typography anywhere — deliberately, because image models used to mangle type and the plan was to composite it afterward. Nano Banana is now good enough at short display text that you can ask for the finished cover instead. So these prompts do the opposite: they name it a book cover and hand the model the exact words.

The four rules that decide whether it works

Say "book cover design, vertical 2:3 portrait" first. Without that phrase you get a photograph.

Put every word in quotation marks and add "spell all text exactly as written." This is what most reduces garbling.

Give it two text blocks, not four. Title and author. Adding the subtitle, the imprint line and the co-author is where models start inventing letters. Everything else gets composited later or lives on the back cover.

Tell it where the type sits and what's underneath. "Over the deep shadow in the lower third" is instruction. "Nice typography" is not.

When it comes back wrong

Do not try to fix a misspelling — regenerate. Add the failed constraint stated positively:

  • Letters wrong → "the text must read exactly LIVING OBEDIENCE, spelled correctly, no extra letters"
  • Type unreadable → "the lower third is plain deep shadow, the title sits clearly over it"
  • Too busy → "one subject only, empty background, generous negative space"
  • Drifted devotional → "serious business book design, no religious symbols, no sunbeams, no landscape"

01 · THE IMPRINT

Type-led. No photograph. The safest and the most likely to come back usable.

Book cover design, vertical 2:3 portrait. A premium hardcover business book. Deep navy blue book cloth with visible woven texture, no imagery. The title "LIVING OBEDIENCE" is stamped in warm gold foil, centered, very large, in a strong classic serif with wide letterspacing, on two lines: "LIVING" on the first line, "OBEDIENCE" on the second. Below it a short thin gold horizontal rule. At the bottom, smaller, in cream capitals: "STEVE FRENCH". Elegant, restrained, expensive. Spell all text exactly as written.

02 · THE KEYS

Photographic, and the strongest concept in the set.

Book cover design, vertical 2:3 portrait. Fine-art still-life photograph: a single worn brass key ring holding three keys resting on a scratched dark walnut desk, single raking window light from the left, deep shadow, rich texture, muted palette of blue-black and warm brass. The bottom third of the image falls into near-black empty shadow. Over the shadow, the title "LIVING OBEDIENCE" in large cream serif capitals on two lines, and beneath it smaller: "STEVE FRENCH". No other text. Spell all text exactly as written. Serious, understated, like a business book — not devotional, no sunbeams, no landscape.

Book cover design, vertical 2:3 portrait. The lower third is deep empty shadow. Over it, the title "LIVING OBEDIENCE" in large cream serif capitals on two lines, and below it smaller: "STEVE FRENCH". No other text anywhere. Spell all text exactly as written. Restrained and cinematic, no religious symbols, no sunbeams.

Deep black surrounding shadow. The lower third is empty darkness. Over it, the title "LIVING OBEDIENCE" in large cream serif capitals on two lines, and smaller beneath: "STEVE FRENCH". No other text. Spell all text exactly as written. Documentary and severe, not sentimental, no sunbeams, no landscape.

What to do with the winner

Send it back and I will rebuild it properly — correct the type to the family system, set the subtitle and the co-author line, add the Signatry imprint, apply the family grain and vignette, and output at print resolution. A generated cover gets you the concept; the composite gets you something you can hand a publisher.
