LIVING OBEDIENCE — COVER ART PROMPTS

For Nano Banana / Gemini · generate at 2:3 (1024 × 1536)

Read this first

Your references — Six40, Walking in Your Purpose, Born For This, and your own Surrender cover — all share four things: a human being or a real place, natural light doing the work, texture you can feel, and type that looks handled rather than typeset. None of them is an abstraction.

So these prompts build photographs, not gradients. Generate, send them back, and I'll composite the family type over the returned art in code — that step is not optional. A raw generation is not a cover.

The workflow. Generate three or four variations of each. Choose on the lower third first, not the top — if the bottom of the frame is busy, the title will never sit properly no matter how good the rest is.

PART 1 · FORMAT LINE — paste at the top of every prompt

Vertical 2:3 portrait book cover background artwork.

PART 2 · THE HARD RULE — paste immediately after, and again at the very end

Absolutely no text, no letters, no numbers, no symbols, no logos, no typography anywhere in the image.

PART 3 · SERIES STYLE BLOCK — identical, word for word, in every prompt

Fine-art cinematic photograph, medium-format film look, shot on 85mm at golden hour, deep saturated color with a cool shadow and a warm highlight, volumetric natural light, gentle film grain, vast quiet negative space, reverent and unhurried mood, nothing staged or stock-like. One luminous focal subject in the upper two-thirds of the frame; the lower third dissolves into darker, simpler, uncluttered space where warm light softly pools.

PART 5 · CRAFT TAIL — paste at the end of every prompt, before repeating the hard rule

Masterful, museum-quality, the work of a top editorial photographer; rich detail rewarding a close look; no digital sheen, no HDR, no over-processing.

PART 4 · THE SUBJECT BLOCKS

Change only this section between concepts. Everything else is the family.

01 · THE THRESHOLD

A yes is a door you walk through, not a feeling you have.

An open doorway in a weathered stone wall, seen from inside a dark quiet room. Warm late-afternoon light floods through the opening and spills across a worn wooden floor toward the viewer, widening as it comes. A figure stands just beyond the threshold, small, backlit, unrecognizable, caught mid-step. Dust drifts slowly through the shaft of light. The room around the doorway is deep shadow, and the pooled light on the floor in the lower third of the frame is soft, simple and empty.

Why it works: the closest photographic cousin of your Surrender cover — a single figure, a moment of decision, enormous negative space. It is also the only concept here that shows the decision rather than the destination.

02 · FIRST LIGHT ON THE ROAD

Living is present tense. New every morning.

A narrow road curving through open high country at first light, seen from a slight rise. Low golden sun breaks over a distant ridge and lays long warm light across the road surface, which winds away and disappears around the shoulder of a hill. Cool blue mist still holds in the valley folds below. No vehicles, no people, no signage. The foreground in the lower third of the frame is soft shadowed grass and simple open ground, quiet and uncluttered.

Why it works: this is the Six40 language exactly — the road, the ridge, the sunrise — done at a higher grade. The most immediately likeable of the four, and the safest with a general reader.

03 · THE KNEELING FIGURE

Obedience, shown rather than described.

A lone figure kneeling on a wide expanse of wet sand at low tide, seen from behind and some distance away, small in the frame. Early morning light comes low across the water and makes a long bright reflection on the wet sand around them. Head bowed, hands resting on the knees, entirely still. A vast pale sky above with soft banded cloud. The wet sand in the lower third of the frame is smooth and nearly empty, holding a soft mirrored glow.

Why it works: the most emotionally direct, and it is your own definition rendered literally — a knee bent. The risk is that it can tip toward devotional-stock if the figure is too central or too close; keep them small and off-centre.

04 · HANDS AND THE PAPER

A decision that gets written down.

A close overhead view of a pair of weathered hands resting on a plain wooden table beside a single sheet of heavy cream paper and an old fountain pen. Warm window light from the left rakes across the grain of the wood and the tooth of the paper. The paper is completely blank. The hands are still, not writing yet. Deep soft shadow fills the lower third of the frame with only the edge of the table catching a low warm glow.

Why it works: this is the cover for The Living Pledge, not for the flagship — but generate it now, because if it lands it settles the whole series' visual identity. The blank page is the entire idea.

CHOOSING WELL

Judge the lower third before anything else. A magnificent top with a busy bottom is a failed cover, and no amount of compositing rescues it.

Reject anything that looks lit. These should read as found light — one window, one sun, one door. The moment it looks like a studio, it looks like stock.

Reject faces. Every concept here keeps the figure turned, distant, or cropped. A recognizable face dates a cover, localizes it, and forces a permissions conversation you do not need.

Generate at least four of each. The difference between the second-best and the best generation is usually the whole cover.

Send them back and I will composite — eyebrow, title, gold rule, subtitle, author block — with the family grain and vignette so the finished covers sit properly beside anything else in the line.
