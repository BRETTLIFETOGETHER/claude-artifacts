40 DAYS OF GENEROSITY

The Six-Session Small Group Edition

Companion to the churchwide campaign · LifeTogether

Naming note. This document uses 40 Days of Generosity throughout. If a license for Journey of Generosity is secured from Generous Giving, the title changes and nothing else does — the name appears only in headers and the front matter.

Content note. Session teaching cores draw on the campaign book. Once the manuscript or final table of contents is supplied, the daily reading map below gets chapter numbers; everything else is complete as written.

HOW THE CAMPAIGN WORKS

Six weeks. Three tracks. One book.

The weekend carries the six-week message arc. The group meets once a week for these six sessions. The daily reader runs all forty days and connects the two, so a person who only reads still moves with the church, and a person in a group arrives having already thought about it.

A church can run one track, two, or all three. Most will start with one. The campaign is designed so that the weekend alone still works, which is what makes a pastor willing to try it.

THE ARC

Six sessions, and the order matters more than any single one.

Week Session The question underneath
1 Who Owns It? Whose is this, actually?
2 Enough How much do we keep?
3 Open Hands What am I afraid of?
4 What Do You See? What has God put in front of me?
5 Together Who decides, and who comes after?
6 The Return What is any of this for?

Ownership, contentment, fear, sight, community, joy. A person at any income level can travel that arc, which is deliberate — a generosity campaign that only works for the wealthy has failed before it starts.

THE GROUP COVENANT

Read aloud in Session One and posted every week after.

Nobody is asked for anything. No pledge, no commitment card, no follow-up. Not by the leader, not by the church, not in week six. If a group member feels solicited, the campaign has failed at the thing it exists to teach.

No numbers. Nobody discloses income, net worth, or what they gave. Ever. People share decisions and reasoning, never amounts.

No comparison. The widow's two coins outweighed everything else in the treasury. This room operates on that math.

What is said here stays here. Including from spouses not present.

You may pass. Every question, every week, without explanation.

SESSION ONE — WHO OWNS IT?

Participant Guide

Open: What is the most generous thing you have ever received? Not given — received.

The question underneath everything. Almost every Christian affirms that God owns it all, and almost every Christian manages money as though the affirmation were poetry. The gap between what we say and how we behave is where this whole six weeks lives. It is not closed by feeling more strongly. It is closed by a decision.

Scripture: Psalm 24:1 · 1 Chronicles 29:11–14 · Deuteronomy 8:17–18

David's prayer in 1 Chronicles is the strongest ownership text in Scripture and one of the least preached. He has just gathered an enormous offering for the temple, and rather than congratulating the givers he says the whole thing came from God's hand in the first place. Everything we gave you was already yours.

The turn. If God owns it, the question stops being how much should I part with and becomes what does the owner want done with this? One is subtraction. The other is allocation. Nobody ever gave joyfully while doing subtraction.

[LOCAL TESTIMONY — 3 minutes] A person from this congregation who can describe a moment when the ownership question became real for them. Not a wealthy person. Someone whose story the room will recognize.

Discuss 1. Where does your behavior currently contradict what you say you believe about ownership? 2. What did you learn about money in the house you grew up in that nobody ever said out loud? 3. If God owns it, what is your actual job description? Say it in one sentence. 4. What would change this week if you managed one account as a steward rather than an owner?

The practice. Write one sentence: God owns __, and my job is ____. Sign it. Date it. Bring it back in six weeks.

Pray. Thank God for something you did not earn.

Session One — Leader Script

[0:00–0:05] Covenant. Read it aloud, all five lines, without commentary. Then say plainly: Nobody in this group will be asked for anything, including in week six. Say it, don't joke about it, move on.

[0:05–0:15] Opening question. Most generous thing received. Pairs, then a few report out.

Note: Starting with receiving rather than giving does two things — it lowers the defenses of anyone who arrived expecting an ask, and it puts the room in the posture the whole campaign depends on.

[0:15–0:30] Scripture and teaching. Read 1 Chronicles 29:11–14 aloud, slowly, and let someone else read it a second time. Teach the turn: subtraction versus allocation. Ten minutes maximum.

[0:30–0:35] Testimony. Brief the person beforehand: three minutes, one moment, no numbers.

[0:35–1:00] Discussion. You will not get through all four questions. Question one is the one that matters; if the room stalls there, stay.

Note: Expect defensiveness in week one. Do not correct it. People who arrive guarded usually arrive that way because someone has used this subject on them before.

[1:00–1:10] Practice and prayer. Hand out the sentence card. Close briefly.

SESSION TWO — ENOUGH

Participant Guide

Open: When you were twenty, what number did you think would be enough? Did you reach it?

The ceiling nobody gave you. The tithe is a floor, and it has done enormous good. But a floor answers only the smallest question. Almost nobody has ever been handed a ceiling — a number at which they would say this is enough, and everything above it has somewhere else to go.

Without a ceiling, every increase in income quietly becomes an increase in lifestyle, and the giving stays flat while the capacity triples. That is not greed. It is the absence of a decision.

Scripture: Proverbs 30:8–9 · 1 Timothy 6:6–10 · Exodus 16:16–20

Agur's prayer for neither poverty nor riches is the most countercultural request in Proverbs, and he gives a reason for both halves: too little and I may steal, too much and I may forget you. He is not being humble. He is being careful about his own soul.

The manna could not be stored. Anyone who tried woke up to maggots. God built a lesson about enough into the food itself.

The practice this week is hard. Name the annual number at which your household has enough. If you cannot write it, write instead what makes it difficult. That answer is worth more than a number you do not believe.

[LOCAL TESTIMONY — 3 minutes] Someone who set a limit on their own lifestyle and can say what it cost and what it freed.

Discuss 1. What is the honest reason a number is hard to write? 2. Where did you learn what "enough" looks like — and was that source trustworthy? 3. What has increased in your life in the last ten years, and did your giving increase with it? 4. Who would you tell your number to?

The practice. Write the number, or write the obstacle. Then tell one person.

Pray. Ask for contentment before you ask for anything else.

Session Two — Leader Script

[0:00–0:10] Check in on the practice. Who wrote the sentence? Do not shame anyone who didn't.

[0:10–0:20] Opening question. The number at twenty. This one produces laughter and then something quieter. Let the quiet happen.

[0:20–0:35] Scripture and teaching. Floor versus ceiling. Ten minutes.

Note: Someone will object that setting a number is legalistic. It is a fair objection and it deserves a real answer: a floor was the rule, a ceiling is the freedom. The rule tells you the minimum. The ceiling ends the argument.

[0:35–0:40] Testimony.

[0:40–1:00] Discussion. Question one carries the session.

[1:00–1:10] Practice and prayer.

Note: This is the session where someone may become visibly uncomfortable. Fear surfaces here more than anywhere else in the six weeks. Do not pursue it in the room. Note it, and make yourself available afterward.

SESSION THREE — OPEN HANDS

Participant Guide

Open: What is the thing you would find hardest to lose? Be honest — it is often not money.

The real opponent. Almost nobody withholds because they love money. People withhold because they are afraid — of running out, of a downturn, of a child's future, of looking foolish, of needing help and having none.

Naming fear as the actual opponent changes the whole conversation. Greed you repent of. Fear you get walked out of, slowly, by someone trustworthy.

Scripture: Matthew 6:25–34 · Hebrews 13:5 · Philippians 4:11–13 · 1 Kings 17:7–16

The widow at Zarephath is asked for her last meal by a prophet she has no reason to trust, during a famine, with her son beside her. It is the most unreasonable request in Scripture and the flour did not run out. Read it as a fear story rather than a giving story and it changes shape.

What contentment actually rests on. Hebrews grounds it in a promise about presence, not provision — never will I leave you. Not you will always have enough. The comfort is a person.

[LOCAL TESTIMONY — 3 minutes] Someone who gave when it was genuinely frightening. The point is the fear, not the outcome.

Discuss 1. Name your fear specifically. Not "the future" — the actual scenario you rehearse. 2. When has God been faithful to you in a way you did not expect and have half forgotten? 3. What would you do differently if you were certain you would be cared for? 4. Where is your grip tightest, and what is it protecting?

The practice. Give something away this week that you will notice. Not a check — a thing, or an afternoon, or an introduction that costs you something.

Pray. Name the fear out loud to God. Use the actual words.

Session Three — Leader Script

[0:00–0:10] Practice check-in. Numbers or obstacles. Nobody shares the figure.

[0:10–0:20] Opening question. Hardest to lose. Expect answers about people, health, and reputation before anyone says money — that is the session's point, discovered rather than taught.

[0:20–0:35] Scripture and teaching. Read 1 Kings 17 aloud in full; it is short and it lands better read than summarized.

[0:35–0:40] Testimony.

[0:40–1:00] Discussion. Question one is the whole session. Give it twelve minutes if it needs them.

Note: This is the most pastoral week of the six. Some of what surfaces will not be about money at all. Your job is to receive it, not to solve it, and to follow up privately during the week.

[1:00–1:10] Practice and prayer.

SESSION FOUR — WHAT DO YOU SEE?

Participant Guide

Open: What have you seen in the last five years that you could not stop thinking about?

Sight comes before assignment. When God commissions the prophets, the question is not what will you do — it is what do you see. Jeremiah is shown a branch. Amos is shown a plumb line. Zechariah is shown a lampstand. The assignment follows the sight, every time.

That has an ordinary application. A person who cannot name anything specific will give generically, to whoever asks. Not because they are shallow, but because attention works that way. You fund what you can picture.

Scripture: Jeremiah 1:11–13 · Amos 7:7–8 · Luke 10:30–37 · Nehemiah 2:11–18

The priest and the Levite were not cruel. They were on their way somewhere. What made the Samaritan different is that he was willing to be interrupted — and before Nehemiah said a word to anybody, he rode out at night and looked at the walls himself.

Vision is usually given, not manufactured. It arrives through what you already have access to, what you already know how to do, or what you have already grieved. That is a relief. You are not required to invent a calling.

[LOCAL TESTIMONY — 3 minutes] Someone whose giving changed because of something they saw firsthand.

Discuss 1. What has God already put you close to — a place, a skill, a loss? 2. Where has your giving gone to something you have never actually seen? 3. Who around you is doing work you believe in, and when did you last ask them what they need? 4. What would you have to be willing to be interrupted by?

The practice. Go and look at something. A ministry, a neighborhood, a person's work. One visit, this week or scheduled before the group ends.

Pray. Ask to be shown something specific.

Session Four — Leader Script

[0:00–0:10] Practice check-in. What did people give away, and what did they notice?

[0:10–0:20] Opening question. The thing they cannot stop thinking about. Give people permission to say something small.

[0:20–0:35] Scripture and teaching. Three prophets, one question. Then access, expertise, grief.

Note: Someone will say they are not a visionary person. The answer is that Monmouth was a cloth merchant and Huntingdon could not preach — neither invented a calling, they used what was already in their hands.

[0:35–0:40] Testimony.

[0:40–1:00] Discussion.

[1:00–1:10] Practice and prayer. Get the visit on a calendar before people leave the room, or it will not happen.

---

SESSION FIVE — TOGETHER

Participant Guide

Open: Who taught you to give, and how did they teach you?

Almost nobody was argued into this. Trace any generous person back far enough and you find a room, a trip, or a person — not an argument. Generosity is caught, and it is caught mostly at home.

Children inherit what they helped decide. A family that has never researched a cause together, argued about it, or moved real money together will pass down assets without the convictions that made them mean anything. Instruction transmits almost nothing. Participation transmits nearly everything.

Scripture: Deuteronomy 6:4–9 · Psalm 78:4–7 · Joshua 4:20–24 · Acts 4:32–35

After Israel crossed the Jordan, Joshua had them stack twelve stones where everyone could see, and the stated reason is startlingly practical: so that when children ask what these mean, someone will have to tell the story. Most families have no stones. Nothing to ask about.

[LOCAL TESTIMONY — 3 minutes] A family — ideally with two generations present — who give together and can describe how.

Discuss 1. What are your family's stones? What object or practice makes a child ask? 2. What do the people closest to you know about why you give? 3. Who is missing from your generosity — a person who should be deciding with you and isn't? 4. What would it take to give one gift together this year, decided together?

The practice. Hold one conversation — with a spouse, a child, a friend, a roommate — about a gift you will make together. Let them have a real vote.

Pray. For the people who come after you.

Session Five — Leader Script

[0:00–0:10] Practice check-in. Who went and looked? What did they see?

[0:10–0:20] Opening question. Who taught you. This produces the warmest twenty minutes of the six weeks; let it run.

[0:20–0:35] Scripture and teaching. Joshua 4 is the anchor. Teach participation over instruction.

[0:35–0:40] Testimony. Two generations if you can get them.

[0:40–1:00] Discussion.

Note: Group members without children or family nearby can feel excluded this week. Widen it explicitly at the top — a household is whoever you share life with, and everybody has someone who is watching how they handle money.

[1:00–1:10] Practice and prayer.

SESSION SIX — THE RETURN

Participant Guide

Open: What has this changed for you? Anything, however small.

The giver is the primary beneficiary. That is not a marketing line, it is the claim of the text. Jesus says it is more blessed to give than to receive, and he says our hearts follow our treasure rather than the reverse — which means giving is not the report card of a changed heart, it is the mechanism of one.

And a caution, because this is where people go wrong. God is generous, and the return is real, and none of that is a promise that giving makes you wealthy. Paul praises the Macedonians for giving beyond their ability out of severe poverty and never reports that they got rich. He reports that they gave themselves to the Lord first, and the money followed. Hold the joy and the cost together or you will preach something cruel.

Aim at an end. Almost every organization will offer you perpetual need. Almost nobody will offer you a finish. A family that asks what would be true when this is done gives differently than a family asking what to do about this year's appeals.

Scripture: Acts 20:35 · 2 Corinthians 8:1–5 · 2 Corinthians 9:6–8 · Matthew 6:19–21

[LOCAL TESTIMONY — 3 minutes] Someone who has been at this for decades and can speak to the joy without exaggerating the return.

Discuss 1. What has actually changed in you over six weeks? 2. Where have you seen joy in a generous person, and what did it look like? 3. What would be true if your part of this work were finished? 4. What is the one thing you are going to do, and when?

The practice. Bring back the sentence you wrote in week one. Read it. Change what needs changing. Then write one more: When our work is done, ______ will be true.

Pray. For the courage to keep going after the group ends.

Session Six — Leader Script

[0:00–0:10] Practice check-in. The family conversation. Who had it, and how did it go?

[0:10–0:20] Opening question. What has changed. Let everyone answer who wants to.

[0:20–0:35] Scripture and teaching. Joy first, then the guardrail. Do not skip the guardrail — a final session that promises return without cost undoes five good weeks.

[0:35–0:40] Testimony. The long-haul giver.

[0:40–0:55] Discussion.

[0:55–1:05] The two sentences. Week one's, re-read. Then the end-state sentence. If the room is willing, invite three or four to read only the second one aloud — no discussion, no response, no applause. Say that before you start.

[1:05–1:15] Close. Pray. Do not summarize, do not recap, and above all do not ask for anything. Somebody in that room is waiting to see whether the covenant held for all six weeks. It should.

THE FORTY-DAY READING MAP

Six sessions, forty daily readings, and four rest days. Chapter references go in once the campaign book is final.

Days Theme Pairs with
1–7 Ownership — whose is it Session One
8–14 Enough — the ceiling Session Two
15–21 Open hands — fear and trust Session Three
22–28 Sight — what God puts in front of you Session Four
29–35 Together — family and community Session Five
36–40 The return — joy and the finish Session Six

Each daily reading follows the house format: a story or testimony, a short teaching, an exercise or action step, a next-step challenge, and three reflection questions.

LEADER PREPARATION

Recruit testimonies four weeks out. Six people, three minutes each, no numbers. Brief every one of them personally — an unbriefed testimony runs eleven minutes and mentions a dollar figure.

Vary who tells them. If all six testimonies come from the wealthiest households in the church, the campaign teaches that generosity belongs to them. Deliberately include someone who gives out of very little.

Hold the covenant. The single thing that will undo this campaign is an ask appearing in week five or six, however gentle. If the church is running a budget appeal in the same season, say so openly at the start and keep it entirely outside the group.

Expect week three to be the hard one. Fear surfaces there. Have a plan for following up privately.

Do not chase completion. A group that gets through two questions well has done better than a group that got through four.
