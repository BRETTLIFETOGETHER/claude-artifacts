Family Legacy Intelligence™

Full Platform Architecture

Central Promise: Tell us your family's story, values, relationships, responsibilities, concerns, and hopes. Family Legacy Intelligence helps the family understand where it is, identify what matters most, surface the conversations that need to happen, preserve its most important stories and wisdom, prepare future generations, coordinate its advisors, build a personalized legacy pathway, and measure meaningful progress over time.

Not estate planning. Not wealth transfer. Not family history alone. A comprehensive system for helping families transfer what matters most — faith, values, wisdom, relationships, stories, traditions, responsibility, leadership, generosity, stewardship, business ownership, family wealth, social influence, community impact, and shared purpose — while preparing the next generation to carry it wisely.

Preserving the Story. Preparing the Family. Transferring the Responsibility. Multiplying the Impact.

A note on reconciliation: the existing, already-piloted Ten-Minute Family Legacy Assessment (6 dimensions × 4 items = 24 items, /120 total: Faith & Spiritual Heritage; Family Story, Identity & Values; Relationships & Family Unity; Wisdom & Next-Generation Preparation; Stewardship, Wealth & Generosity; Succession, Communication & Enduring Legacy) is a different instrument from the 12-dimension Family Legacy Readiness Assessment built out below. Both are real, both are useful, and they're not the same tool — worth deciding whether the six-dimension version stays as the quick starting assessment and this one becomes the deeper follow-on, or whether they should merge.

1 · The Family Legacy Intelligence Profile™

The family completes guided questionnaires and optionally uploads documents, stories, photographs, videos, plans, and advisor materials. The platform builds a living family profile across four areas:

Family Identity — name, members, generations, locations, cultural background, faith tradition, history, turning points, defining experiences, shared beliefs, traditions, symbols, meaningful places and dates, distinctive family language, reputation, hopes for future generations.

Family Structure — immediate and extended family, blended family relationships, marriages, divorces, remarriages, children (biological, adopted, step), grandchildren, dependents, caregivers, family branches, family businesses, trusts or foundations, key non-family relationships.

Family Leadership — formal and informal leaders, decision makers, financial leaders, relational connectors, storytellers, spiritual leaders, business leaders, philanthropic leaders, next-generation leaders, members carrying unusual responsibility, members who feel overlooked or excluded.

Family Values — faith, integrity, work, generosity, education, family loyalty, entrepreneurship, service, hospitality, responsibility, humility, excellence, resilience, freedom, achievement, compassion, stewardship, community impact. The system identifies both stated values and values demonstrated by actual decisions and behavior — and treats the gap between the two as information, not a failure.

Family Assets, defined broadly: - Tangible: financial wealth, businesses, real estate, investments, personal property, collections, intellectual property, trusts, foundations, charitable entities. - Intangible: faith, wisdom, relationships, reputation, family stories, traditions, experience, leadership ability, social capital, professional networks, family knowledge, lessons learned, community influence, entrepreneurial experience, generosity practices.

2 · Family Self-Discovery Questionnaire

Designed so a family can begin without hiring a consultant — one member, a couple, or multiple generations can complete it.

Core Questions 1. What are you most grateful for about your family? 2. What are you most concerned about? 3. What has helped your family succeed? 4. What has caused pain or division? 5. What topics are difficult to discuss? 6. What do younger generations understand well? What do they not yet understand? 7. What responsibilities may eventually transfer? 8. What does the family want to preserve? What needs to change? 9. What does the family fear losing? What does it hope to accomplish together? 10. What would make the next transition successful — and what could cause it to fail? 11. What conversations need to happen within the next year?

Open Narrative Prompts (written or recorded) - "Tell the story of how the family became successful." - "Describe the most difficult season the family has experienced." - "What sacrifice made the family's present opportunities possible?" - "What do you wish your children understood?" - "What concerns you most about inheritance?" - "What responsibilities are the next generation not yet ready to carry?" - "What would you regret failing to communicate?" - "What does your family believe God has entrusted to it?"

The system mines these answers for recurring themes, shared values, tensions, gaps, unresolved questions, differing expectations, stories worth preserving, recommended conversations, and readiness for deeper planning.

3 · Family Legacy Assessments

3.1 — The Family Legacy Readiness Assessment (Flagship, Built Out)

Twelve dimensions. Each rated 1–5 across four statements, scored /20 per dimension, /240 total.

Communication 1. Our family discusses difficult topics directly instead of avoiding them. 2. Family members feel safe expressing disagreement. 3. We follow through on commitments made in family conversations. 4. Important information reaches everyone who needs it, not just some of us.

Trust 1. Family members are consistently honest with each other, even when it's uncomfortable. 2. Decisions affecting the whole family are made transparently. 3. Confidentiality is respected when something is shared in confidence. 4. I have confidence in the people who will make decisions after the current generation.

Shared Purpose 1. Our family could articulate, in one sentence, what we're trying to build together. 2. Younger and older generations would describe our family's purpose similarly. 3. Our major decisions are visibly guided by a shared sense of purpose, not just circumstance. 4. We revisit and discuss our shared purpose regularly, not just once.

Financial Transparency 1. Family members understand the basic shape of the family's financial picture appropriate to their age and role. 2. There are no major financial surprises waiting to happen at a future transition. 3. Financial decisions that affect multiple family members are discussed, not just announced. 4. We have a shared philosophy about money, not just shared money.

Next-Generation Preparation 1. The next generation understands the responsibilities that may eventually be theirs. 2. Younger family members are given real, age-appropriate responsibility today, not just told about the future. 3. We have a plan for developing the next generation's readiness, not just hoping they'll be ready. 4. The next generation has access to the wisdom of the generation(s) before them.

Advisor Coordination 1. Our professional advisors (legal, financial, tax, philanthropic) communicate with each other, not just with us individually. 2. Our advisors understand our family's values, not just our balance sheet. 3. We have clarity about which advisor is responsible for which decisions. 4. Advisor recommendations are evaluated against family values, not accepted automatically.

Governance 1. Our family has agreed-upon ways of making decisions together. 2. Family meetings happen regularly and are genuinely useful. 3. There's a clear process for resolving disagreement when it arises. 4. Participation in family decisions is appropriately extended across generations, not concentrated in one person.

Succession 1. There is a clear, documented plan for leadership succession (family, business, or both, as applicable). 2. The people expected to take on future responsibility know that they are expected to. 3. We have a realistic timeline for transition, not just an eventual intention. 4. There's a contingency plan if succession has to happen sooner than expected.

Generosity 1. Our family has a shared philosophy about giving, not just individual giving habits. 2. The next generation is actively involved in the family's giving, not just aware of it. 3. We evaluate the impact of our giving, not just the amount. 4. Generosity is a stated family value that's visible in actual practice.

Estate Readiness 1. Our estate plan reflects our current wishes and family reality, not an outdated version. 2. Beneficiaries understand, in general terms, what to expect. 3. We've discussed unequal distributions (if any) openly rather than leaving them to be discovered. 4. End-of-life wishes have been discussed, not just documented.

Story Preservation 1. Our family's origin and defining stories are recorded somewhere, not just remembered. 2. Younger generations have heard these stories directly, not secondhand. 3. We've preserved the "why" behind major family decisions, not just the decisions themselves. 4. There's a plan for continuing to capture stories, not just what's already been captured.

Relational Health 1. Sibling and cross-generational relationships in our family are genuinely healthy, not just civil. 2. Conflict, when it happens, gets resolved rather than buried. 3. In-laws and non-blood family members feel genuinely included. 4. I would describe our family as close, not just connected by circumstance.

Score bands (proposed, consistent with the existing assessment's banding logic): 192–240 Intentional Legacy Formation · 144–191 Emerging Legacy Alignment · 96–143 Legacy Conversations Needed · 48–95 Begin with Trust and Discovery.

3.2 — The Other Ten Assessments (Dimensions Specified, Ready for Item-Level Build-Out)

  • Family Communication Assessment — openness, listening, emotional safety, difficult conversations, conflict patterns, clarity, assumptions, avoidance, confidentiality, follow-through.
  • Family Trust Assessment — reliability, honesty, fairness, transparency, inclusion, consistency, respect, confidentiality, confidence in leadership, confidence in future decision-makers.
  • Family Values Alignment Assessment — compares founders, parents, adult children, spouses, grandchildren, family business leaders, trustees, and advisors; surfaces shared values, differing interpretations, generational differences, hidden assumptions, and areas of natural alignment. Not designed to force uniformity.
  • Next-Generation Readiness Assessment — character, work ethic, financial wisdom, emotional maturity, relational health, decision-making, stewardship, leadership, business understanding, philanthropy, family history, responsibility, collaboration, conflict handling.
  • Wealth Transfer Readiness Assessment — clarity of plans, family understanding, communication, beneficiary preparedness, trustee readiness, ownership succession, tax/legal coordination, advisor alignment, financial literacy, timing, confidentiality, risk of surprise.
  • Family Business Succession Assessment — leadership readiness, ownership readiness, role clarity, family employment expectations, compensation, decision rights, board structure, conflict, transition timing, successor development, non-family leadership, contingency planning.
  • Family Governance Assessment — decision-making, family meetings, voting, representation, committees, councils, policies, conflict resolution, communication, family constitution, accountability, participation by generation.
  • Generosity and Philanthropy Assessment — shared generosity values, giving history, family participation, charitable interests, decision-making, next-generation involvement, foundation/DAF use, impact measurement, faith motivations, community relationships.
  • Family Story Preservation Assessment — origin, migration, faith, business, sacrifice, failure, recovery, and generosity stories; photographs, letters, recordings, documents, significant objects, lessons learned — present or absent.
  • Estate Conversation Readiness Assessment — whether family members are prepared to discuss inheritance, control, responsibility, unequal distributions, trusts, caregiving, property, business ownership, charitable giving, end-of-life wishes, and family expectations.

4 · Family Dynamics Intelligence™

Maps relationships, alliances, estrangements, influence, dependency, rivalry, unresolved conflict, communication flow, decision-making power, family branches, generational differences, spouse integration, trust, caregiving responsibilities, perceived fairness, and expectations.

Critical labeling requirement: every profile is tagged by its source and confidence level, never presented as settled fact: - Individual perspective - Couple perspective - Generation perspective - Family-wide assessment - Advisor observation - Verified fact - AI interpretation

Additional family members are invited to complete confidential questionnaires for deeper accuracy. The system then surfaces areas of agreement, differences in perception, hidden assumptions, conflicting expectations, and conversations that need facilitation — it never resolves these on its own.

Framework: - What We Have Received — faith, opportunity, wisdom, relationships, assets, responsibility. - Who We Are Becoming — the character, relationships, and culture the family wants to develop. - What We Will Preserve — beliefs, stories, traditions, practices that should continue. - What We Will Change — patterns, assumptions, or behaviors the family doesn't want to repeat. - How We Will Serve — the people, communities, ministries, and causes the family hopes to strengthen. - What We Will Transfer — the wisdom, responsibility, ownership, generosity, and leadership future generations must carry.

17 · Family Legacy Outcomes Intelligence™

Measures more than whether documents got completed.

  • Relational — more meaningful conversations, greater trust, increased understanding, reduced avoidance, improved conflict resolution, better cross-generational inclusion.
  • Preparation — more family members understanding the estate plan, more beneficiaries and successors and trustees prepared, clearer ownership expectations, stronger advisor coordination.
  • Identity — stories preserved, values articulated, purpose clarified, traditions documented, wisdom transferred, history understood.
  • Stewardship — greater financial wisdom, increased responsibility, healthier attitudes toward wealth, greater generosity, more next-generation participation, clearer impact goals.
  • Governance — regular family meetings, documented decision processes, clearer roles, improved participation, stronger succession planning, fewer unresolved assumptions.

18 · Guardrails

Privacy and permission, per item: private to individual · shared with spouse · shared with selected family members · shared with a family branch · shared with advisors · shared with facilitators · available to future generations · released only after death · permanently restricted.

Professional boundaries — the platform distinguishes family education, guided reflection, coaching, and facilitation from financial planning, tax advice, legal advice, estate planning, mental-health counseling, and conflict mediation. Professional legal, tax, financial, therapeutic, and fiduciary matters are referred out, never handled in-platform.

AI interpretation labeling — every piece of content is tagged as one of: participant statement · family member perspective · uploaded fact · advisor observation · assessment result · AI-generated interpretation · professionally verified conclusion. The platform never labels a family member, diagnoses motives, or recommends major legal or financial action based on one person's narrative alone.
