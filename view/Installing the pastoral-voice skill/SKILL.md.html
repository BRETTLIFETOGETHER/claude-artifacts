name: pastoral-voice description: Applies LifeTogether's warm, pastoral, invitational voice to any campaign title, subtitle, tagline, weekly theme, brochure copy, devotional line, or sermon-series description. Use this whenever writing or revising copy for the Biblical Purpose Library, the Seven Day Experience, or any related LifeTogether material — even if the user just says "write a campaign" or "give me themes" without mentioning tone. Always run copy through these voice rules before presenting it; never let output drift academic, corporate, or preachy on this project.

Pastoral Voice

A voice skill, not a content-structure skill. It governs how things are said — word choice, sentence rhythm, and emotional register — for LifeTogether copy. Pair it with the lifetogether-campaign-builder skill's title → subtitle → 6 themes → confirm → expand workflow; this skill shapes what fills each of those slots.

The register: warm, pastoral, invitational

Three failure modes to actively avoid:

  • Preachy: telling the reader what they must do or feel. ("You must surrender every area." / "Repent and change today.") Fix by inviting instead of commanding: swap the imperative-at-the-reader for an imperative-with-the-reader, or a question.
  • Academic: doctrinal terminology, footnote energy, explaining a concept instead of extending an invitation. ("This series explores the soteriological implications of...") Fix by naming the human experience first, theology second and lightly.
  • Corporate: hype and buzzwords borrowed from marketing decks. ("A revolutionary, game-changing framework to optimize your spiritual growth.") Fix by trusting plain words — the brand's whole appeal is that it doesn't sound like a pitch.

The tell that copy has drifted into one of these: read it aloud. If it sounds like a memo, a lecture, or an ad, it's off. It should sound like a trusted pastor talking to one person over coffee.

Sentence rhythm

  • Taglines / section headers: 3–6 words, gerund-led or a short declarative fragment ending in a period, not an exclamation point.
  • "Helping Churches Go Deeper."
  • "Helping Every Person Discover a Life That Matters."
  • Subtitles: one clean promise, roughly 6–10 words, led by an action verb — not a compound sentence, not a subordinate clause.
  • "Discover the Life God Designed You to Live"
  • "Align Every Part of Your Life with What Matters Most"
  • Weekly theme lines: 2–4 words each. See the anchor-word device below — this is the single most identifying structural fingerprint of the voice.

One idea per line, always. If a line needs a comma to hold two thoughts, it's usually two lines.

The anchor-word device

Every flagship 6-week campaign repeats one root word or grammatical pattern across all six week titles, varying only what completes it. This is the device to reach for by default on flagship campaigns — and the one thing to not force on spinoffs, specialized editions, or requests that don't naturally fit it (per standing guidance: apply it automatically for a flagship campaign, but let it go when it would feel gimmicky, e.g. a Men's or Women's Purpose Journey spinoff).

Families already established in this project — reuse or extend, don't necessarily invent a new one every time:

Device Pattern Example (6 weeks)
Repeated root "Rooted in ___" Christ / Scripture / Community / Character / Mission / the Future
Repeated prefix "Re-___" Remember / Recognize / Renew / Refocus / Respond / Reproduce
Repeated possessive "Your ___" Center / People / Formation / Contribution / Mission / Legacy
Identity + calling "[Past-participle] to [verb]" Created to Know God / Designed to Belong / Formed to Become Like Jesus / Gifted to Serve / Sent to Make a Difference / Called to Multiply
Repeated state word "Alive " / "Flourishing " Alive in Christ / Alive Together / Alive in Truth / Alive through Service / Alive for Others / Alive with Eternal Purpose

When drafting new themes, pick (or invent) one device, then hold it constant across all six — the variation should live entirely in the second half of the line.

Word bank

Reach for: journey, belong, become, discover, matters, together, story, sent, multiply, flourish, whole, rooted, alive, legacy, calling, formed.

Avoid: doctrinal/technical jargon (soteriology, hermeneutics, exegesis) in headline copy, corporate buzzwords (leverage, synergy, optimize, game-changing, revolutionary), guilt-forward language (shame, failure, must repent) as an opener, and hype-superlatives.

Tension, then quick release

Name a real felt tension in one short phrase — then resolve it into hope or invitation within the same line or the next. Don't linger in the tension; that's what makes it pastoral rather than heavy.

  • "Helping people move beyond success toward significance."
  • "Especially designed for second-half adults, retirees, pastors, and legacy-minded leaders."

Both name a specific life stage or ache, then immediately point somewhere better. Avoid dwelling for more than one clause before pivoting to hope.

Legacy positioning (always in force on this project)

Never imply official or continuing affiliation with the original Purpose Driven brand. Always frame as building on, extending, or standing in the tradition of — never as "the next" or "the new" version of it.

  • Right: "LifeTogether builds on those timeless biblical foundations with a new generation of churchwide campaigns..."
  • Wrong: "The next Purpose Driven campaign for your church."

Worked example

Prompt: "Give me a flagship campaign on forgiveness."

Generic/academic draft (avoid):

Forgiveness: A Theological Study — Exploring the doctrine of forgiveness through six expository sessions on reconciliation, restitution, and reconciliation theology.

Pastoral-voice draft (aim for this):

Let It Go, Let It Heal — Six Weeks to Release What's Holding You Back Week 1: Naming the Wound · Week 2: Releasing the Debt · Week 3: Releasing the Anger · Week 4: Releasing the Past · Week 5: Releasing the Fear · Week 6: Releasing Into Freedom (anchor word: "Releasing")

Notice: plain words, one idea per line, an anchor word repeated across all six weeks, and the tension (what's holding you back) resolved by week 6 without dwelling.
