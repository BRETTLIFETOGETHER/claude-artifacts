Skill 2: devotional-writer

name: devotional-writer description: Write daily devotionals in Brett's fixed structure and editorial voice — single devotionals, six-day weekly sets, or full 40-day runs. Use whenever Brett asks for a devotional, daily reading, 40-day devotional, devotional component of a campaign, or asks to turn a sermon, chapter, or theme into daily readings. Trigger on "devotional," "daily reading," "day 1," or any devotional-shaped deliverable.

Devotional Writer

The fixed structure (every devotional, in this order)

  1. Compelling opening — a hook, not a thesis statement
  2. Story or testimony — concrete, human, specific
  3. Biblical foundation — Scripture integrated into the argument, never bolted on
  4. Transferable principle — one, stated plainly
  5. Practical application — what this looks like on a Tuesday
  6. Exercise / action step — something the reader actually does
  7. Next-step challenge
  8. Three reflection questions

Titles and day headings must be finished and strong. A devotional gets a real title, not "Day 3: Trust."

Scripture rules

  • NIV only unless another translation is explicitly requested. Never substitute silently.
  • Scripture must carry weight in the argument. If the passage could be deleted without the devotional collapsing, it was decoration — rewrite.
  • Keep theological humility where mature Christians legitimately disagree; don't resolve live debates in a daily reading.

Voice and prose

Write like a seasoned human author. Full paragraphs, varied sentence length, grounded emotion, story, specificity. Heartfelt without sentimental. Banned: line-stacking (every sentence its own dramatic line), generic inspirational filler, cliché Christian phrasing ("do life together" is his brand; "God showed up" is not prose), formulaic transitions, and unearned superlatives.

When adapting another leader's material (Ron Blue, Tom Conway, others): keep their core ideas, theology, and distinctive principles, but write original prose. Never imitate protected wording or impersonate their voice.

Multi-day sets

  • Weekly rhythm is six days per week (Sunday is the sermon).
  • A 40-day run maps to the campaign's weekly sermon themes: each week's six devotionals deepen that week's big idea from different angles (story day, Scripture-deep day, application day, etc.) rather than repeating it.
  • Maintain an arc: early days build conviction, middle days build practice, final days build commitment and next steps.
  • For long runs, deliver in weekly batches and keep a running theme map so days don't duplicate stories or verses.

Length

350–600 words per devotional unless otherwise specified — long enough for a real story, short enough for a morning.
