Skill 4: partnership-proposal

name: partnership-proposal description: Build partnership proposals, pitches, and engagement letters in Brett's discipline — partner-first framing, honest relationship labeling, clear economics. Use whenever Brett asks to draft a proposal, pitch, partnership deck, engagement offer, licensing conversation, or outreach to an organization, ministry, publisher, network, or founder (The Signatry, OCEANS, denominations, advisor networks, etc.). Trigger on "proposal," "pitch," "partnership," "engagement," "offer to [org]," or outreach to a named organization.

Partnership Proposal

The lead rule (most important)

A partnership pitch leads with the partner's mission, audience, problem, and opportunity — never with Brett's vision and why they should help. The first third of any proposal should read as if it were written by someone who cares about their organization. LifeTogether's role enters as the answer to their problem.

The division-of-strengths frame

Preferred structure: the partner brings credibility, audience, distribution, trusted relationships, content, brand; Brett brings campaign architecture, curriculum, packaging, production, marketing, customization, church adoption strategy, and AI-enabled content expansion. Ask who already owns the audience and the trust before proposing to build a channel from scratch. Distinguish a relationship from a channel.

Relationship honesty (hard rules)

  • Label every relationship precisely: historical relationship / current working relationship / active collaboration / strategic conversation / prospective opportunity. Never imply endorsement, funding, or formal partnership without evidence.
  • Label every project status: concept / exploratory / proposed / developing / pilot / active / approved / launched / paused / archived. Never assume a pitch became a partnership.
  • Provenance rule for outreach from old contact files: "your church ran [X] with us" only for verified purchase/registration records; otherwise "your name has been in my file since [era]." False-familiarity openers ("I've been following your ministry") are banned.
  • Field evidence must be verified. Invented testimonials or composite quotes are never used, even as placeholders.

Economics

Include clear economics or clearly say they're to be discussed — never vague. Where numbers are reasoned rather than market-validated, say so in the document. For church-facing partnerships, economic design must visibly protect ministry trust; churches are a transformation environment, not a sales channel. Prefer structures where philanthropic capital launches what revenue later sustains.

Document structure

  1. Their mission, audience, and the moment they're in
  2. The problem/opportunity in their world
  3. What becomes possible together (vision — full size, not shrunk)
  4. The model — who brings what, how it works
  5. Phase 1 and the first concrete step (the wedge inside the vision, not instead of it)
  6. Economics
  7. The ask and the vision last — never end on the invoice.

Voice by counterpart

Ministry founders and pastors: vision, Kingdom impact, trust, low friction. Publishers: distinctiveness, backlist revitalization, platform, derivative rights. Networks/denominations: scale, standards, member value. Donors get the donor-brief skill, not this one.
