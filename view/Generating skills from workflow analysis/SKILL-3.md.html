Skill 3: chapter-writer

name: chapter-writer description: Write long-form book chapters in Brett's authored, coaching voice — for Built to Grow, Living Obedience, Revolutionary Generosity, and any future book project. Use whenever Brett asks to write, draft, expand, or revise a chapter, book section, introduction, or manuscript, or supplies an outline/table of contents and says "write chapter X." Trigger on "chapter," "manuscript," "book," or any request for 2,000+ words of narrative prose.

Chapter Writer

Non-negotiables

  • 2,000 words minimum, often 3,000–4,000. "Expand" means deepen — new stories, sharper argument, more texture — never pad.
  • Narrative paragraphs, minimal bullets. A book chapter is prose. If a list appears, it's woven into sentences.
  • Coaching voice: direct address, first person where the author's story serves, concrete examples over abstractions, a reader being walked somewhere — not lectured.
  • Close with a reflection and/or prayer unless the book's established pattern says otherwise.
  • Deliver in 1,000–1,500-word segments when that improves quality; label each segment's place in the chapter.

Source discipline

When Brett supplies a source — outline, table of contents, transcript, prior chapter, co-author's book or notes — follow the source, not approximate memory. If the source conflicts with what you remember of the project, prefer the source and say so explicitly. Never assume an outline became final or a working title became the title; label status.

When the book carries a co-author or originating leader (Steve French, Dale Armstrong, Ron Blue, Tom Conway): preserve their core ideas, theology, and distinctive principles; write original prose; never imitate their protected wording or impersonate their voice.

Chapter anatomy that works

Open inside a story or a tension, not with the thesis. Let the argument emerge through narrative, Scripture, and example, then name the transferable principle explicitly around the two-thirds mark. Land on application and a forward pull toward the next chapter. Vary the rhythm — a chapter that alternates story / teaching / story / teaching reads as a machine.

Scripture

NIV only unless another translation is explicitly requested; never substituted silently; integrated so the passage does argumentative work. Theological humility where mature Christians legitimately disagree.

Prose bans

No line-stacking. No formulaic AI transitions ("In today's fast-paced world…"). No generic inspirational filler. No unearned "revolutionary/transformative/game-changing." No heading every 200 words — a chapter can run 1,000 words between headings, or use none.

Continuity

Before writing chapter N, check the book's established terminology, recurring frameworks, named stories, and promises made in earlier chapters. Reuse the book's language; don't invent a synonym for a framework the book already named.
