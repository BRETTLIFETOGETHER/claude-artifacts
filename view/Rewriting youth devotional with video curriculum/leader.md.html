Leading After Session Six

Before You Lead Anything

You don't have to be good with money to lead this.

That isn't a nice thing to say so you'll feel better. It's the actual best-case setup. A leader who admits they're still figuring money out builds more trust with students than one who has it handled, because this whole series is about honesty rather than competence. If a student asks you something financial you can't answer, "I don't know, I'm still working that out myself" is a better answer than a guess.

What you do need to do is read the session before you walk in. Not skim it. Read it, do the exercise yourself, and notice where it gets uncomfortable for you, because that is exactly where it will get uncomfortable for them.

Six things that will make you better at this than any amount of preparation.

Let silence work. After you ask a question, count to ten in your head. Ten is much longer than it feels. Students are used to adults rescuing silence, so they have learned to wait it out. Stop rescuing and they start talking. The best follow-up you have is "tell me more about that."

Answer first when it's hard, not when it's easy. On the light questions, let students go first. On the real ones, model it. Go first, be honest, keep it short. Your honesty sets the ceiling for the room, and they will never go deeper than you do.

Don't fix anybody. When a student says something real, the instinct is to solve it, or find a verse, or tie a bow. Resist all three. Receive it. "That's a lot to carry" is enough. This series touches fear, family money stress, and shame, and a student who gets fixed once will not say the real thing again.

Never ask for numbers, and don't let the group ask either. The no-numbers promise is signed in the front matter of every student's guide, and holding it is on you. If a student volunteers a number, don't repeat it or build on it. Move on gently.

Watch for the student carrying it at home. In most groups there is at least one student whose family is genuinely stressed about money right now. They will not tell you. They may go quiet in sessions two and four especially. Follow up privately, don't put them on the spot, and don't build a group moment around them.

Rotate and share the load. Healthy groups rotate hosting and leading. Nobody should carry six weeks alone, and rotating develops the students who will lead next.

How Each Session Runs

Every session follows the same shape, which means after week one you are running a rhythm instead of a script.

Start here is the week's opening read. You don't have to read it aloud. Most groups do better if students read it during the week and you spend your time on discussion.

Come together is prayer and the group agreement, and it takes longer in session one than anywhere else. Don't rush it. Session one's agreement is what makes sessions four and five possible.

Watch the video with fill-in blanks. The answers are what students hear on screen, so they are writing down what was just said rather than guessing. Keys are in this guide. Don't read the answers aloud during the video, and don't stop the video to catch anybody up.

Pause and reflect is sixty seconds of silence before discussion. Time it. Do not talk during it. This is the easiest thing in the session to skip and the most valuable thing in it, because the first thought out loud is rarely the one worth hearing.

Here's what that actually means is teaching content, three points, each carried by a verse. Reading it aloud is optional.

Picture this is the week's image. Worth reading aloud even if you skip the teaching points, because it's what students will remember on Thursday.

Let's talk about it moves in four tiers, easy to real. You will not get through all four in a short session, and that's fine. Pick the tier your group can actually handle and go deep on one rather than shallow on four.

The exercise is done in silence, alone. Nobody reads it out loud, with one exception in session six. Say that clearly before students start, every single week, or they will write the presentable version instead of the true one.

One thing to try, Write it down, Ask someone are take-home. Read the options aloud, let them pick, and follow up next week by asking who actually did one. Then actually ask.

This week's devotional lists the seven days that match the session. Students who read them come back different.

Before you go is a written prayer. Read it, or pray your own. If a student wants to pray, let them.

What to Do When It Gets Heavy

It will, probably in session four.

When a student shares something hard, the group's job is to receive it, not solve it. Don't rush to advice or a verse that closes it down. Sit with it. "Thanks for saying that out loud" is a complete response.

If a student discloses something beyond what a small group can hold, family crisis, real fear about safety, anything about harming themselves, that is not a group moment. Follow up privately, that day, and get your youth pastor involved. You are not the last line of help and you should not try to be.

If the group itself feels off in session two, relax. Groups usually don't gel until three or four. Finish the six weeks before deciding anything.

SESSION ONE · THE OWNERSHIP QUESTION

Who Owns Your Life?

LEADER SETUP

This session sets the tone for all six. Budget more time for Come Together than you think you need, because the group agreement is the foundation for everything after it. Read it aloud rather than summarizing, then have students sign it. It feels formal. Do it anyway. Signing something makes it real in a way that agreeing to it doesn't.

Bring pens. Every week. Students will not have them.

The one thing to get right this week: the no-numbers promise. Say it plainly and say it early. Half the room walked in braced for a money study that is going to expose them.

ANSWER KEY

  1. life · 2. image · 3. created / His · 4. good / very · 5. holding · 6. borrowed / yours

COME TOGETHER

Leader note: Pray first, then read the no-numbers paragraph word for word rather than paraphrasing. Then the agreement, then signatures. The opening question about being protective is meant to be light. Let it be light. Don't push anybody deeper here.

PAUSE AND REFLECT

Leader note: Sixty seconds, timed, silent. Say "we're going to sit here for a minute, that's on purpose" so nobody thinks something went wrong.

LET'S TALK ABOUT IT

Leader note, question one: The board question is your easiest entry. Most students can name something they're carrying that belongs to somebody else. If the room is slow, go first with a small real one of your own.

Leader note, question three: This is the honest one. Somebody on the show said that if they stopped holding it all together, they don't know what would be left of them. If a student agrees with that out loud, don't move on quickly. Sit in it. That sentence is the emotional center of the episode and it's worth more than the remaining question.

Leader note, question four: If you're short on time, this is the one to keep.

OPEN HANDS

Leader note: Say clearly that nobody reads this out loud. Then be quiet and let them write for four to five minutes. Watch for the student who fills it in fast and shallow, and don't call it out.

BEFORE YOU GO

Leader note: Read the prayer or pray your own. Then point them to the devotional, days one through seven, and tell them you'll ask next week who read any of it. Then actually ask.

LEADER'S PRAYER

God, thank you for these students and for six weeks together. Nobody here has this figured out, including me. Quiet the part of us that wants to look fine. Show us where we have been carrying weight you never asked us to carry, and teach us what it looks like to hold things the way a manager does. Amen.

SESSION TWO · THE CONTENTMENT QUESTION

Why Does More Never Feel Like Enough?

LEADER SETUP

This is the session where the quiet student gets quiet. Wanting things is embarrassing to admit out loud at this age, and half your room is carrying a comparison problem they have never named. Your job is to make wanting things normal instead of shameful.

Go first on the opening question and pick something a little embarrassing.

The one thing to get right this week: nobody compares anything. If two students start measuring what they have against each other, stop it kindly and immediately. This is the session where that does the most damage.

ANSWER KEY

  1. have · 2. finish line · 3. what is it · 4. day · 5. rotten · 6. store · 7. normal · 8. industries / business model · 9. hitting / trusting

COME TOGETHER

Leader note: The opening question asks how long the buzz lasted on something they saved for. Most students will underestimate it and then correct themselves. Let them correct themselves. Don't do it for them.

WATCH THE VIDEO

Leader note: Watch for the moment near the end of the game when sleep comes out last and Caroline has already committed her top spots. Students laugh, then go quiet. If your group reacts to that, it's your best entry into discussion, better than any question on the page.

PAUSE AND REFLECT

Leader note: The question asks what they would put in their top tier. Some students will name something small and real, like sleep. Take that as seriously as the big answers.

LET'S TALK ABOUT IT

Leader note, question two: Asking what their feed tells them they are missing works better than asking about money directly, and it gets to the same place. Most students have never said this out loud.

Leader note, question four: This is the peak. It asks whether any amount would actually let them exhale. Let the silence sit. If the honest answer in the room is no, that is exactly right, and it is the whole point of the session.

DRAW YOUR FINISH LINE

Leader note: Say plainly there are no amounts anywhere on this page. Students who hear "finish line" think you want a salary number. The last prompt, what they would do with what's left over, is the one that matters. Give them time for it.

BEFORE YOU GO

Leader note: Days eight through fourteen. Day fourteen is the hardest and best one in the week. Mention it.

LEADER'S PRAYER

God, we are all chasing something and most of us could not name it if you asked. Thank you that you sent enough every morning to people who could not store it, and that you have not changed. Teach these students what enough looks like while they are young enough for it to shape everything after. Amen.

SESSION THREE · THE STEWARDSHIP QUESTION

What Do You Do With What Isn't Yours?

LEADER SETUP

This one is funnier than the others for about ten minutes and then it turns, and the turn catches people off guard. The game is a confession game, and Jerry's ratings on screen give your students permission to be honest about their own borrowed-stuff record.

The real content is not the hoodie. It's the buried thing. Watch for the student who goes still during the pause.

The one thing to get right this week: do not let the buried-thing conversation become about talent or performance. It is about fear. When a student names something they have not done, ask what they were afraid of, not what is stopping them.

ANSWER KEY

  1. not looking · 2. holding · 3. could handle · 4. afraid · 5. identical / Faithfulness · 6. fear · 7. fear · 8. safe move / failure

COME TOGETHER

Leader note: The opening question about something they are holding that belongs to somebody else is a laugh line. Use it. The lighter this opening is, the further the group will go later.

PAUSE AND REFLECT

Leader note: The pause asks "what's your buried thing." Say out loud that they do not have to share it. Some will anyway, and that's the session working.

LET'S TALK ABOUT IT

Leader note, question two: Widening from a hoodie to time, talent, and trust is the hinge of the session. If the group stays on objects, name a bigger one yourself and let them follow.

Leader note, question three: Somebody on the show said burying it feels safer than failing at it. This is the honest one. If a student says yes, ask what the smallest version of digging it up would be, and then stop talking.

LIVE, GIVE, OWE, GROW

Leader note: Percentages only, never amounts. The point of the exercise is the two questions after the four buckets, not the buckets themselves. Most students will discover Live got everything and Give has never existed. Let that land without commentary.

BEFORE YOU GO

Leader note: Days fifteen through twenty-one. Day twenty is the four questions before you borrow, which is the single most useful page in the book for the next ten years of their lives.

LEADER'S PRAYER

God, we are holding a lot of things we did not make and calling them ours. And most of us have buried something because trying felt more dangerous than hiding. You never asked us to be impressive. Help these students dig up the thing they have been sitting on. Amen.

SESSION FOUR · THE CONFIDENCE QUESTION

What Happens When the Storm Hits?

LEADER SETUP

This is the heavy one. Read the "What to Do When It Gets Heavy" page before you walk in, even if you read it in week one.

The game is funny, six fears sorted into three piles, and then two cards land that are not funny at all: being ordinary, and being found out. Both of those live at 2:00 a.m. in most of your students. Expect the room to change temperature.

The one thing to get right this week: do not fix anybody. This session asks students to name their 2:00 a.m. fear, and the instinct to reassure is strong. Reassurance ends the conversation. Presence continues it.

If a student discloses something serious, follow up privately that same day and involve your youth pastor.

ANSWER KEY

  1. out there / in here · 2. 2:00 a.m. · 3. real · 4. care · 5. Quiet / still · 6. I am with you · 7. weather / boat · 8. outcome / Person · 9. agreement

COME TOGETHER

Leader note: Start with the silly fear. Genuinely start there. The order matters, and a room that has laughed together will go further than a room that hasn't.

PAUSE AND REFLECT

Leader note: The pause asks for their 2:00 a.m. question, "not the presentable version." Say that phrase out loud. It gives students permission they will not take otherwise.

LET'S TALK ABOUT IT

Leader note, question two: Asking whether they have stopped believing God is paying attention is the most spiritually honest question in the series. Answer it first yourself if the room stalls, and answer it truthfully.

Leader note, question three: Somebody on the show describes a prayer that felt like it hit the ceiling. Some student in your room has been there this month. The point to land is that the people literally in the boat with Jesus also thought He did not care, so that feeling is not evidence of failure.

Leader note, question four: The "even if" is where the session lands. Ask students to finish the sentence out loud if they are willing. Do not force it, and do not correct anybody's answer.

THE FIVE HABITS

Leader note: This is a relief valve after a heavy conversation, and it is meant to be. Move to something concrete on purpose. The last prompt, the smallest possible version of their weakest habit, is the whole exercise.

BEFORE YOU GO

Leader note: Days twenty-two through twenty-eight. Day twenty-six is the one to mention, since it hands them something to do when they are panicking.

LEADER'S PRAYER

God, some of us in this room are genuinely afraid, and most of us have not said so. You never told us not to be afraid because things would go our way. You said it because you are here. Be here. Amen.

SESSION FIVE · THE GENEROSITY QUESTION

What Happens When You Give?

LEADER SETUP

Students expect this week to be the ask. They are braced for a guilt trip about giving, and the session is not that. Say so at the top, out loud, in the first two minutes.

The game builds to a point most groups miss until it is named: the two hardest things to share are not things at all. Credit and feelings. Those are self.

The one thing to get right this week: nobody says what they give. Not a number, not a percentage, not a story that implies one. Generosity stops being generosity the moment it becomes a scoreboard, and in a student group it becomes one fast.

ANSWER KEY

  1. you / you · 2. have · 3. reservoir · 4. stagnant / rot · 5. sack lunch · 6. All of it · 7. holding / released · 8. poorer · 9. amount / heart

COME TOGETHER

Leader note: Start with what is easy to share. Every student has an easy one, and starting there makes the hard one sayable twenty minutes later.

PAUSE AND REFLECT

Leader note: The pause asks what they are white-knuckling. Expect answers that are not money. That is the session working, not drifting.

LET'S TALK ABOUT IT

Leader note, question two: Somebody on the show says that if he gave the credit away too, he is scared there would be nothing left that proves he mattered. That is the realest line in the episode. If a student agrees, stay there.

Leader note, question four: Asking for the actual cost, "not the noble version," is deliberate. Students will reach for the impressive answer. Push once, gently, for the true one.

THE QUIET GIFT

Leader note: This exercise only works if nobody finds out, which means you do not ask about it next week in front of the group. Say that when you assign it. The last prompt asks whether they are less interested in doing it if nobody will know. Do not soften that question.

BEFORE YOU GO

Leader note: Days twenty-nine through thirty-five. Day thirty-four is for the student who thinks they have nothing to give.

LEADER'S PRAYER

God, we have been holding on tightly and calling it being careful, and it has been making us empty. A boy handed you a sack lunch and you fed a field with it. Help these students open their hands around something small this week. Amen.

SESSION SIX · THE LEGACY QUESTION

What Mark Are You Already Making?

LEADER SETUP

Last one. Leave room, because last sessions are where people finally say the thing.

Two things are different this week. The exercise gets shared, which breaks the pattern of the other five, so tell students that before they start writing rather than after. And there is a closing beat after the prayer about what happens next Tuesday, when the videos stop.

The one thing to get right this week: get every student to name one thing they are keeping and one person who is allowed to ask them about it. That single move is what makes six weeks stick instead of fade.

ANSWER KEY

  1. faithful / trend · 2. small stuff · 3. famous / you · 4. are · 5. bowl · 6. identity · 7. wasted · 8. you / up · 9. window / view

COME TOGETHER

Leader note: The yearbook quote question, "not the one you'd actually submit, the real one," is a good laugh and a quiet setup for everything after it.

PAUSE AND REFLECT

Leader note: Ten years of the week they just had. Some students will find this genuinely unsettling. Do not rescue it.

LET'S TALK ABOUT IT

Leader note, question one: Naming an un-famous person who changed them is the warmest moment in the whole series. Give it room, and go first.

Leader note, question three: Somebody on the show says they have been seen by a lot of people and known by almost none. Some student in your room lives there. Do not rush past it.

YOUR LEGACY LETTER

Leader note: Three lines, not a book. Say clearly that they will be asked to read these to one real person this week, and that person can be someone in this room. Line two, why it holds when things get hard, takes the longest. Give it real time.

If your group is willing, have students read line three aloud to each other. That is the closing moment of the series if you want one.

BEFORE YOU GO

Leader note: Days thirty-six through forty-two. Day forty-two is the last one and it asks what they hope God does with their life. Tell them to date it and come back in a year.

LEADER'S PRAYER

God, six weeks ago this started with a sentence about everything belonging to you, including us. Thank you for what you have done in this room. You called these students light before they did anything to earn it. Let it be on, and pointed at you. Amen.

Leading After Session Six

The six weeks were the easy part. There was a rhythm, a video, a page, and a room. All of that stops now, and what is left is whatever actually got built.

Three things worth doing in the two weeks after.

Follow up individually, once. One message to each student, naming the one thing you noticed in them. Not a group text. This is the highest-return thing you will do in the whole series and it takes about twenty minutes.

Ask what they kept. Not what they learned. What they kept. If the answer is nothing, that's useful information and it isn't a failure, since most people keep nothing from most things.

Do something together that isn't a study. A meal, a service thing, anything. Groups that meet once more without curriculum usually stay groups.

And keep the student who went quiet in session four on your radar. That one matters more than the rest of this page.
