The Pastor's Library Project

Marketing Plan & Execution Outline

Companion strategy document to LifeTogether Sermon Intelligence™

Why This Document Exists

The white paper sells a category ("Sermon Intelligence"). It doesn't yet sell the thing a pastor actually buys — a managed engagement where LifeTogether comes alongside them, in person and digitally, builds their private ministry library, and pipes it straight into templates, production, and print.

That's the gap. This document closes it in two parts:

  1. The Marketing Plan — how we position, package, and sell this
  2. The Build Outline — what has to exist internally before we can deliver it

PART ONE: THE MARKETING PLAN

1. Reframing What We're Actually Selling

Every AI competitor sells a tool. We need to sell a relationship with a deliverable at the end of it.

Generic AI sermon tools The Pastor's Library Project
Pastor prompts a chatbot LifeTogether team does discovery with the pastor
Output is a document on a screen Output is tagged, searchable intelligence and printed, bound, distributable curriculum
No relationship to existing curriculum Flows directly into the Master Title Directory, campaign formats, and small group system already in market
No production pipeline 25 years of publishing, design, and print production behind it
Zero church network validation Built inside a system already trusted by 500+ churches
Scales infinitely, cares about none of them White-glove intake, human curation, verified against fabrication before it ever reaches a congregation

The one-line positioning:

"We don't ask you to prompt an AI. We come alongside you, organize thirty years of your preaching into a living library, and turn it into the small group series, devotionals, and books your church already trusts us to produce."

This is the sentence that needs to anchor the brochure, the sales deck, and every conversation. Everything else in the white paper (the five intelligence layers, the workflow diagram) is supporting evidence for this claim — not the headline itself.

2. The Market

Total addressable market: ~300,000 churches in the U.S. that are actively looking to "do something better" with their teaching content — your framing, and directionally consistent with commonly cited counts of U.S. congregations.

Three concentric rings, not one market:

  • Ring 1 — Warm base (immediate pilot pool): the 500+ churches already in relationship with LifeTogether. Fastest path to a signed pilot; lowest trust barrier.
  • Ring 2 — Primary ICP (the real business): senior pastors at established churches (roughly 250–2,000 in attendance) who have 10+ years of preaching behind them, feel the "hidden library" problem acutely, and have budget authority. This is who the white paper is actually written for.
  • Ring 3 — Long-tail digital market: smaller churches and church plants who can't afford full concierge onboarding but want a self-serve or lighter-touch version. This is a later-phase product, not the launch offer — don't let it dilute the initial positioning.

3. Messaging Pillars

  1. "Your library already exists." The content isn't generated — it's excavated. This is the emotional hook; it respects the pastor's years of work instead of implying an AI will out-write them.
  2. "We do it with you, not to you." Concierge intake, human curation, a real team — not a self-service upload portal. This is the direct answer to "AI feels impersonal."
  3. "From intelligence to ink." The differentiator competitors structurally cannot copy: an actual publishing and print production pipeline on the other end. This is the paragraph currently missing from the white paper, and it should probably be its own section titled something like "The Last Mile Nobody Else Has."
  4. "Proven inside 500 churches, not a lab." Credibility from scale and church-world trust, not tech-world hype.

4. Offer Architecture (Packaging)

Suggested three-tier structure — placeholders for you to price, not final numbers:

Tier 1 — Foundation Engagement Discovery + digitization + tagging of an initial sermon set (e.g., last 3–5 years); one small-group series and one devotional produced from it; digital access to their tagged library.

Tier 2 — Legacy Library Full archive ingestion (entire preaching history where available); multiple churchwide campaign outputs across formats (40-day, small group, devotional); print production run included; ongoing quarterly additions as new sermons are preached.

Tier 3 — Dynasty Partnership Everything in Legacy, plus: staff/ministry team training on using the live library, integration into their church's ongoing content calendar, priority production slots, and co-branded published works (books, curriculum) for wider distribution — this is the Casey Crawford / flagship-case-study tier.

(Naming above is a starting point to match your navy/gold, Playfair/Cormorant editorial voice — swap in whatever fits the existing brand language for tiers elsewhere in the system.)

5. Sales Motion

  1. Prove it once. Pick 2–3 pastors inside the 500-church base who are (a) prolific preachers, (b) well-known enough that a case study carries weight, (c) warm relationships already. Run Tier 2 or 3 as a founding pilot, likely at a discount in exchange for a testimonial and usage rights.
  2. Package the proof. Before/after: "300 sermons, unsearchable" → "a tagged library that produced X small group series and a published devotional in 90 days." This becomes the centerpiece of the sales one-pager.
  3. Roll out to the warm base. Direct outreach to the 500 churches using the case study, not a cold pitch.
  4. Conference and network expansion. Once 5–10 case studies exist, this becomes a stage presentation / booth offer at pastor conferences.
  5. Only then, the long tail. A lighter self-serve digital tier for Ring 3, once the concierge model is proven and repeatable.

6. Marketing Assets Needed

  • Revised white paper — with "From Intelligence to Ink" made explicit, not implied
  • One-page sales brochure, matching the existing dark navy/gold editorial design system
  • A single "sample library" showcase — anonymized or with permission, showing an actual before/after from a pilot pastor
  • ROI/case-study one-pager per completed pilot
  • Email nurture sequence for Ring 1 warm outreach
  • A short workshop/webinar pitch deck for conference use

7. Launch Sequence

  • Phase 0 (internal): Build the intake and tagging process; recruit 2–3 pilot pastors from Ring 1
  • Phase 1: Execute pilots; produce case study assets
  • Phase 2: Soft launch to the full 500-church relationship base
  • Phase 3: Conference circuit and network expansion
  • Phase 4: Self-serve/lighter tier for the broader 300,000-church market

PART TWO: BUILD OUTLINE — WHAT IT TAKES TO ACTUALLY DELIVER THIS

1. What "Pastor's Library Intelligence" Actually Is, Operationally

A managed process where LifeTogether staff (not the pastor, not a self-serve tool) collects a pastor's accumulated teaching material, digitizes and organizes it against your existing taxonomy (campaign formats, Edge Categories, biblical purposes framework), verifies it against fabrication, and routes the output into your existing production and print pipeline.

It is half service business, half software — that combination is the moat. A pure-AI competitor can copy the software half. They cannot easily copy 25 years of production infrastructure or a done-with-you service model.

2. The End-to-End Process

Stage 1 — Discovery & Rights Agreement Initial conversation with the pastor; inventory what exists (manuscripts, audio, video, notes going back how many years); simple rights agreement — pastor retains authorship/ownership, LifeTogether receives license to organize, produce, and (where agreed) publish/distribute.

Stage 2 — Intake & Digitization Collect raw material in whatever form it exists. This is the most operationally variable stage — churches will hand over everything from clean manuscript files to boxes of old cassette tapes.

Stage 3 — Transcription & Cleanup Convert audio/video to text; clean up transcription errors; standardize formatting.

Stage 4 — Tagging & Taxonomy Map every sermon against categories that already exist in your system: campaign format, Edge Category, biblical purpose, life stage, Scripture passage, recurring themes and stories. This is where the "library" becomes searchable rather than just archived.

Stage 5 — Human Curation & Verification Non-negotiable step, tied directly to the verification discipline already central to your workflow: every extracted quote, story, and Scripture reference gets checked against the source material before anything moves downstream. No fabricated citations reach a congregation. This is a human editorial role, not something to automate away.

Stage 6 — Integration into LifeTogether Systems Tagged, verified content flows into the existing module system (the shared Python pipeline already producing your campaign and brochure content), so a pastor's own material can populate the same templates already built for the 574 small group series and five campaign formats.

Stage 7 — Production Output Small-group curriculum, devotionals, books — produced through your existing design system and print pipeline. This is the step generic AI tools simply cannot offer.

Stage 8 — Ongoing Growth The library isn't static. New sermons get added on a cadence (quarterly/annually), keeping the relationship (and the revenue) ongoing rather than one-time.

3. What Needs to Be Built Internally

Technical - Intake tooling (upload/transfer mechanism for large audio/video/document sets) - Transcription pipeline (vendor or in-house) - Tagging schema that maps 1:1 onto your existing categories, so ingestion doesn't create a parallel taxonomy - A retrieval/search layer so a pastor (or your team) can actually query "everything I've said about grief" or "every illustration about fatherhood" - Integration hooks into the existing common.py/data.py/transform.py system so tagged content can populate existing templates rather than requiring new ones

Human process - A written intake checklist and rights agreement template - A verification/QC protocol — specifically, a checklist for catching fabricated or misattributed quotes and citations before anything is produced - A defined handoff point between "raw tagged content" and "production-ready curriculum"

Legal - Simple, standard rights language: pastor retains ownership of their own words; LifeTogether is licensed to organize, produce, and publish under the terms of the tier they purchase

Production - A clear map of how library output enters your existing brochure/print pipeline, so this isn't a new production line but an extension of the one you have

4. Team & Roles

  • Brett — project and strategic lead, pastor relationship, product direction
  • Hannah — content production; likely coordinates transcription/curation workflow
  • Likely new/contracted roles as volume grows:
  • Tagging/taxonomy specialist (can start as Hannah's responsibility, split off once volume justifies it)
  • Verification editor (the fabrication-check role — this should not be skipped even in an early pilot)
  • Client success/onboarding lead (manages the discovery conversation and rights agreement with each pastor)

5. What a Pastor Actually Walks Away With

Concrete, sellable deliverables — this list should live in the sales brochure almost verbatim:

  • A private, searchable, tagged library of their own sermons and teaching
  • One or more small-group series and/or devotionals generated from their own material
  • Print-ready, and optionally physically printed, curriculum
  • Ongoing digital access as the library grows

6. Risks & Safeguards

  • Fabrication risk: the single biggest reputational risk in this offer. Stage 5 (human verification) has to be treated as mandatory, not optional, in every tier — this is worth stating explicitly in sales conversations as a feature, not hidden as internal QA.
  • IP/rights ambiguity: solved by a simple, standard agreement at intake, before any material is collected.
  • Scope creep: tiering exists specifically to prevent "unlimited archive, unlimited outputs" from being implied at every price point.

7. Suggested Timeline

  • Weeks 1–4: Finalize intake process, rights agreement template, and tagging schema; recruit 2–3 Ring 1 pilot pastors
  • Weeks 5–12: Run pilots end-to-end; produce first case study assets
  • Months 4–5: Soft launch to the 500-church base using pilot proof
  • Month 6+: Conference circuit, expanded team hiring, tier refinement

Open Decisions (don't block moving forward, but worth flagging)

  • Actual pricing per tier
  • Whether Stage 5 verification is done in-house from day one or partially outsourced during pilot
  • Which 2–3 pastors from the 500-church base are the right founding pilots
