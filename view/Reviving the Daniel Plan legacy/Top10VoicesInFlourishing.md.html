THE TOP 10 VOICES IN HARVARD FLOURISHING RESEARCH

Academic Researchers, Public Intellectuals, and Commercial Authors Shaping the Flourishing Conversation

TIER 1: HARVARD FLOURISHING RESEARCH TEAM (The Scientific Foundation)

1. Tyler J. VanderWeele, PhD

Role: Director, Human Flourishing Program at Harvard
Title: John L. Loeb and Frances Lehman Loeb Professor of Epidemiology, Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health

Core Keywords & Big Ideas: - "Flourishing as a multidimensional construct" — not happiness alone, but integration of physical health, mental health, meaning, purpose, character, virtue, close relationships, financial stability - "The promotion of human flourishing" (foundational 2017 PNAS paper) - Causal inference methodology — rigorous distinction between association and causation (his specialty before flourishing) - Religion and health integration — co-director of Initiative on Health, Spirituality, and Religion - The five domains framework — universal human desires that constitute ends in themselves

Books: - A Theology of Health: Wholeness and Human Flourishing (2024) — his most recent, bridges theology and epidemiology - Measuring Well-Being (2021) - Handbook of Religion and Health (2023) - Explanation in Causal Inference (2015)

Problems He's Solving: - Fragmented wellbeing research (people study income OR happiness OR health in isolation) - Narrow metrics that miss what actually makes humans thrive - The disconnect between modern epidemiology (disease prevention) and human flourishing (what makes life good) - How to measure flourishing across 22 countries with cultural validity

Public Platform: - Monthly newsletter for Human Flourishing Program (Psychology Today) - Over 600 published papers - 2017 COPSS Presidents' Award (highest honor in statistics)

Why He Matters for Your Work: VanderWeele is the methodological foundation. He built the flourishing framework itself. His work validates what you're trying to do: physical health is not separate from meaning, relationships, and spirituality. He provides the research credibility.

2. Byron R. Johnson, PhD

Role: Co-Director, Global Flourishing Study (Baylor)
Title: Distinguished Professor of the Social Sciences, Founding Director of Institute for Studies of Religion at Baylor University

Core Keywords & Big Ideas: - "Religion is vital to flourishing" — his signature finding (the research gradient is "quite shocking," he says) - "Faith-based organizations as social interventions" — evidence on criminal justice, prisoner reentry, addiction recovery - "Prosocial behavior" — how faith communities build social resilience - "The death of religion?" (ironically) — his recent book challenges secularization narratives - $43.4 million longitudinal study — 200K+ participants, 22 countries, 5+ years

Books: - The Faith Factor and Social Welfare: Rethinking Evidence, Practice, and Polity (2026) - The Restorative Prison (2021) - Objective Religion: Problems, Prosociality, Progress (2022) - More God, Less Crime (2011) - The Angola Prison Seminary (2016)

Problems He's Solving: - The "faith gap" in social science (most wellbeing research ignores religion) - Efficacy of faith-based programs (often dismissed by secular institutions; he's proving their impact) - Why young people are flourishing less (despite wealth) — loss of religious/community participation - Criminal justice and rehabilitation through faith communities

Public Platform: - National Association of Evangelicals podcast/speaker - Regular media appearances - Speaking at major faith organizations - Baylor Connections podcast series

Why He Matters for Your Work: Johnson brings the faith integration piece that VanderWeele's epidemiology can't alone provide. He proves that religious community participation is one of the strongest predictors of flourishing across all domains. This validates the church-centered approach of your curriculum.

TIER 2: PUBLIC INTELLECTUALS ON HAPPINESS & FLOURISHING (The Translators)

3. Arthur C. Brooks

Role: Professor of Leadership & Happiness; Public Intellectual
Titles: Parker Gilbert Montgomery Professor of Public Leadership at Harvard Kennedy School; Professor of Management Practice at Harvard Business School

Core Keywords & Big Ideas: - "The science of happiness is a learnable skill" — 40% of happiness is personal effort (not genetics/circumstance) - "From Strength to Strength" — second-half-of-life flourishing (challenges the youth-obsessed culture) - "The four pillars of happiness: family, friendship, work, faith" - "Emotional self-management" (with Oprah Winfrey in Build the Life You Want) - "Calculated risk-taking as key to flourishing" — fighting risk-avoidance through fear of regret - "The pursuit of meaning over pleasure" — why happiness research has shifted

Books: - Build the Life You Want (with Oprah Winfrey) — #1 NYT bestseller - From Strength to Strength: Finding Success, Happiness, and Deep Purpose in the Second Half of Life — #1 NYT bestseller - The Meaning of Your Life: Finding Purpose in an Age of Emptiness (2025) - The Happiness Files (2025) - 15+ books total, all focused on translating science into practice

Problems He's Solving: - The hedonic treadmill (why more money/stuff doesn't make us happier) - How to build happiness intentionally (counterintuitive practices) - The "crisis of meaning" in affluent societies - Second-half-of-life purpose (aging as opportunity, not decline) - Why success doesn't guarantee fulfillment

Public Platform: - #1 bestselling author (multiple times) - Harvard professor teaching "science of happiness" - CBS News contributor - Column at The Free Press ("The Pursuit of Happiness") - Podcast: "Office Hours with Arthur Brooks" - Speaking at Fortune 500 companies, universities, faith communities globally

Why He Matters for Your Work: Brooks is the commercial translator. He takes academic flourishing research and makes it accessible to millions. His "four pillars" framework (family, friendship, work, faith) aligns perfectly with your small-group curriculum approach. He shows that flourishing content sells and resonates broadly.

4. Tal Ben-Shahar, PhD

Role: Positive Psychology Pioneer & Educator
Title: Professor of Psychology; Co-founder of Happiness Studies Academy

Core Keywords & Big Ideas: - "Positive Psychology" — he popularized this movement after Martin Seligman pioneered it - "Five elements of happiness: spiritual, physical, intellectual, relational, emotional wellbeing" - "Integration of ancient wisdom and modern research" — bridges Aristotle, Tao Te Ching, Helen Keller with neuroscience - "Happiness is a skill, not a destination" — Happier mindset - "Permission to be human" — his signature phrase; normalizes struggle as part of flourishing - The most popular course in Harvard history — 900 students/semester rated it "life-changing"

Books: - Happier: Learn the Secrets to Daily Joy and Lasting Fulfillment (2007) — international bestseller - Being Happy — translated into 25+ languages - Teachings on Personal Transformation (many workbooks/programs)

Problems He's Solving: - The "perfectionists' trap" (happiness is not perfection) - Integration of multiple wellbeing dimensions (not just mental or physical) - Making positive psychology teachable (he created the "Certificate in Happiness Studies") - Bridging Eastern and Western conceptions of wellbeing

Public Platform: - Founded Happiness Studies Academy (certificate and MA programs) - "Happier School" initiative for educators - International keynote speaker (20+ countries) - Cited by Martin Seligman as "the Master teacher of Happiness"

Why He Matters for Your Work: Ben-Shahar shows how to scale happiness education. He proved that you can teach flourishing as a curriculum (his Harvard course). His five-element framework (spiritual, physical, intellectual, relational, emotional) is directly applicable to your 15-category library structure.

TIER 3: FOUNDATIONAL POSITIVE PSYCHOLOGY PIONEERS (The Scientists)

5. Martin E. Seligman, PhD

Role: Founder of Positive Psychology Movement
Title: Director, Positive Psychology Center at University of Pennsylvania

Core Keywords & Big Ideas: - "Positive Psychology" — pivoted psychology from "what's broken" to "what works" - "PERMA model" — Positive emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, Accomplishment - "Authentic Happiness" vs. "Well-being" — important distinction (engagement/flow matters more than pleasure) - "Learned optimism" — people can train themselves toward resilience - "Strength-spotting" vs. only problem-solving

Books: - Flourish: A Visionary New Understanding of Happiness and Well-Being (2011) - Authentic Happiness (2002) - Learned Optimism (1991)

Problems He's Solving: - The "disease model" dominating psychology (fixing problems vs. building strengths) - Measuring positive states (not just absence of illness) - Making wellbeing science rigorous and testable - Military resilience (Pentagon contracted him post-9/11)

Public Platform: - Founded PERMA model that shapes institutional wellbeing initiatives globally - University of Pennsylvania's Positive Psychology Center (training ground for the field)

Why He Matters for Your Work: Seligman is the grandfather of this entire field. Everything in the Harvard flourishing framework traces back to his 1998 pivot to positive psychology. His PERMA model is foundational vocabulary for understanding the cascade you're building.

6. Barbara Fredrickson, PhD

Role: Leading Expert on Positive Emotions
Title: Kenan Distinguished Professor at UNC Chapel Hill; President, International Positive Psychology Association

Core Keywords & Big Ideas: - "Broaden and build theory" — positive emotions expand your cognitive and social capacity - "The positivity ratio" — the 3:1 ratio of positive to negative emotions for thriving - "Positivity as survival mechanism" — emotions evolved to do specific things (love leads to connection, joy leads to play) - "Love 2.0" — positivity extends beyond romance; it's micro-moments of connection - "Positivity resilience" — how to build it through practice

Books: - Positivity: Top-Notch Research Reveals the Upside of Your Inner Life (2009) - Love 2.0: Finding Happiness and Connection in a Disconnected World (2013)

Problems She's Solving: - How emotions function physiologically (not just psychologically) - Why small moments of connection matter for health - The neuroscience of flourishing (how emotions rewire the brain)

Why She Matters for Your Work: Fredrickson provides the neuroscience for the emotional/relational pieces of your cascade. Her research on how positive emotions expand capacity directly supports your pathway: physical health → mental wellbeing → relational capacity.

7. James O. Pawelski, PhD

Role: Philosopher of Flourishing & Humanities Integration
Title: Professor of Practice, Director of Education, Positive Psychology Center at University of Pennsylvania; Adjunct Professor of Religious Studies

Core Keywords & Big Ideas: - "Humanities and Human Flourishing" — philosophy, poetry, literature are essential to wellbeing (not just neuroscience) - "Eudaimonic turn" — from pleasure-based to virtue-based flourishing - "Integration of philosophy, theology, and psychology" — his signature interdisciplinary approach - "Human flourishing as collective/relational" — not just individual - "On Human Flourishing: A Poetry Anthology" — bridges arts and science

Books: - The Dynamic Individualism of William James (2007) - Happy Together: Using the Science of Positive Psychology to Build Love That Lasts (with wife Suzann) - Editor: Oxford Handbook of Happiness (philosophy section) - Co-editor: The Eudaimonic Turn: Well-Being in Literary Studies

Projects: - Humanities and Human Flourishing Project — designated NEA Research Lab - $2.5M Templeton Religion Trust grant on humanities and wellbeing

Problems He's Solving: - The "science vs. humanities" false dichotomy - How virtue and character shape flourishing (not just happiness metrics) - Why art, poetry, and stories matter for wellbeing (not just data) - Integration of spirituality/religion into academic positive psychology

Why He Matters for Your Work: Pawelski validates the theological and humanistic dimensions of your curriculum. He's proven that you can't reduce flourishing to neuroscience or metrics alone; narrative, virtue, and meaning-making are essential. His work supports your white paper's use of biblical theology alongside Harvard research.

TIER 4: SPECIALIZED VOICES (The Deep Experts)

8. Ken Pargament, PhD

Role: Expert on Religion and Spirituality in Psychology
Research Focus: How people cope with stress through spiritual means

Core Keywords: - "Spiritual coping" — how faith communities process grief, trauma, loss - "Religious struggle as growth opportunity" — doubt and deconstruction can strengthen faith - "Meaning-making through spirituality" — not just belief, but lived practice

Why Matters: Provides research validation for spiritual formation components of your curriculum, especially in grief/recovery categories.

9. Corey Keyes, PhD

Role: Leading Researcher on Flourishing and Mental Health
Research Focus: How "flourishing" and "mental illness" are distinct dimensions (not opposites)

Core Keywords: - "Languishing vs. flourishing" — you can be mentally healthy but not flourishing - "Social coherence, integration, contribution" — relational elements of flourishing - "Public health approach to flourishing" — not just clinical

Why Matters: Keyes' framework validates that health curricula can't just prevent illness; they must actively cultivate flourishing. This supports your platform's approach.

10. Jonathan Haidt, PhD

Role: Social Psychologist of Morality and Wellbeing
Research Focus: Why humans flourish in community; moral psychology

Core Keywords: - "Hive hypothesis" — humans are "hive creatures" who flourish through belonging - "Moral foundations" — different cultures/groups prioritize different virtues (but all seek some form of meaning) - "The anxious generation" — his recent work on why young people are struggling (phone addiction, loss of in-person community)

Books: - The Righteous Mind: Why Good People Are Divided by Politics and Religion - The Anxious Generation: How the Great Rewiring of Childhood is Causing an Epidemic of Mental Illness (2024)

Why Matters: Haidt explains the relational/community foundation of flourishing. His work on why young people are anxious (despite material wealth) directly supports your pitch for community-based flourishing curriculum.

THEIR SHARED PROBLEMS THEY'RE SOLVING

Across all 10:

  1. The fragmentation problem — Researchers study one domain (mental health) ignoring others (relationships, meaning, physical). Solution: integrated frameworks
  2. The materialist blind spot — Wealthy nations aren't happier. Solution: meaning, virtue, connection matter more than stuff
  3. The youth crisis — Young people report lowest flourishing despite digital connection. Solution: in-person community, purpose, spiritual anchoring
  4. The reduction problem — Can't reduce flourishing to just neuroscience or just virtue or just happiness. Solution: multidimensional integration
  5. The faith gap — Secular institutions ignore religion; religious organizations ignored by science. Solution: integration (VanderWeele, Johnson, Pawelski leading this)
  6. The translation problem — Brilliant research stays in journals; doesn't reach people. Solution: Arthur Brooks, Ben-Shahar model (academic rigor + accessible teaching)
  7. The implementation gap — People know what flourishing looks like but can't sustain it alone. Solution: community, small groups, scaffolding (your curriculum angle)
