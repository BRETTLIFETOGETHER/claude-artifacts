PRIORITY 1 — Leadership Pipeline & Succession Multiplication

Building an organization that develops leaders faster than it loses them Corporate parallel to: Category 54, Church Multiplication & Reproducing Leaders

The single most common reason a growing company stalls is not a market problem but a leadership-supply problem — there's no bench when the company needs the next VP, plant manager, or country lead. This category builds reproduction into the org chart itself, from first-line manager to the C-suite succession plan. Highest priority — this is the leadership-development foundation every other business capability depends on, and it's the most common limiting factor on growth regardless of industry.

PRIORITY 2 — New Venture Launch & Product Development

Assessment, launch strategy, founding team formation, and the first three years of a new venture, product line, or division Corporate parallel to: Category 51, Church Planting & New Church Development

Launching a new venture, product, or division remains the highest-leverage growth move most companies can make, yet most launches run on enthusiasm rather than infrastructure — no real assessment, no sequencing, no plan past the launch date. This category builds the same launch-to-year-three discipline for a business venture that a church plant needs for a congregation. Very high priority — this is the direct growth engine of a business, translating strategic ambition into new revenue.

PRIORITY 3 — Multi-Location & Franchise Operations

Remote offices, franchise units, regional managers, culture transfer, and leading one company in many locations Corporate parallel to: Category 52, Multisite & Campus Ministry

The multi-location model solves a real capacity problem, but creates a new one: how does one company's culture and standards transfer faithfully across locations leadership can't be in every week? This category addresses the leadership and systems questions that determine whether a multi-location company stays genuinely one company or slowly fragments into disconnected outposts. Moderate-high priority — essential for companies actively expanding physical footprint, less relevant to single-location businesses.

PRIORITY 4 — Corporate Turnaround & Organizational Renewal

Leading a plateaued, declining, or struggling company back to health, focus, and momentum Corporate parallel to: Category 53, Church Revitalization & Turnaround

Most mature companies eventually plateau or decline, and most leadership training assumes a growth trajectory that never arrives. Turnaround leadership gives the executive of a struggling company a genuine path forward — one that honors what built the company while calling it back to its founding value proposition. Situational priority — critical when a company needs it, but relevant only in decline or crisis rather than as an everyday leadership need.
