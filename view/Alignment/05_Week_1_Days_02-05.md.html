Day 2

Money Changes People

They don't become better, they become worse

"No one can serve two masters. Either you will hate the one and love the other, or you will be devoted to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve both God and money." Luke 16:13

Money changes people, and it doesn't change them for the good in most cases. When there's a bunch of money involved, people become different. They don't become better, they become worse.

I say that flatly because I have watched it happen enough times to stop hedging it. I am not talking about criminals. I am talking about decent families with grown children who loved each other perfectly well until there was a number on the table, and then something moved that nobody in that family would have predicted about themselves.

Here is why this belongs in a session about financial statements, which is the last place you would expect to find it.

A lot of advisors do one of two things. Some deal with the relational issues in a family and forget about the fact that there is money associated with them. Others deal with the money and never look at the family at all. Both of those are half a job, and the half that gets left out is usually the half that determines the outcome.

If you are the relational one, you can sit with a family for two years, understand every dynamic in the room, and completely miss that the plan on the table is going to detonate in the third generation. If you are the technical one, you can build something elegant and hand it to people who will tear each other apart over it.

So you do not just want to know where the family is relationally, how they are doing over on that side. You also want to know what kind of stuff they have, and how that stuff is going to affect the relationships going forward.

That last phrase is the one to hold. How the stuff is going to affect the relationships going forward. Not how the relationships will affect the plan. The other direction. The assets are going to act on these people, and the acting has not started yet, and you can see the shape of it before they can.

This is why I want the whole picture and not the part I am paid on. It is not thoroughness for its own sake. It is that a number I have not seen is a number I cannot warn anybody about.

You can also see the softer version of the same thing, and you will see it more often than the dramatic version. A son who starts doing arithmetic about his mother's spending. A daughter who becomes careful in a way she never used to be. A brother who begins keeping score of things that were never scored before. None of these people would tell you money changed them. Most of them have not noticed.

Jesus does not say you should not serve both. He says you cannot. It is a statement about capacity rather than permission, and it is the reason the change happens so quietly. Nobody sits down and elects to serve money. The devotion moves first and the person catches up to it later, if ever.

Which means the families you serve are not choosing between two masters in any conscious way. They are drifting toward one, and the drift accelerates when the numbers get larger, and there is generally nobody in the room whose job includes noticing.

That job is available. Almost nobody has taken it.

I want to be careful here, because this cuts toward you as much as toward them. An advisor is not immune to the thing he is watching for in a client. You are handling other people's money all day and forming opinions about it, and the same quiet reordering that happens to a family with a large number in front of them can happen to the man who spends thirty years standing next to large numbers.

Watch for it in them. Watch harder for it in you.

Reflect

  1. Where have you seen money change someone in a family you serve? What was the first sign, and did anyone name it at the time?

  2. Are you the relational advisor who forgets the money, or the technical advisor who forgets the family? Answer honestly rather than diplomatically.

  3. For your largest client, how is what they have going to affect the relationships going forward? Write the specific prediction, not the general concern.

  4. Money changes people quietly. What has thirty years around other people's money done to how you think about your own?

Practice

Take one client family with adult children. For each person, write what the current plan hands them, and then what you think it will do to them.

Person What the plan hands them What you think it will do

Now the harder line. Which of these people is the plan most likely to damage?

Day 3

You're Going to Collaborate With Other People

That are better than you

"Plans fail for lack of counsel, but with many advisers they succeed." Proverbs 15:22

One of the important things if you are doing legacy coaching is a collaboration mindset. You are going to collaborate with other people that are better than you in areas where you do not have the expertise that you would like to have.

I want to be concrete about what that means, because collaboration is the sort of word people nod at without changing anything.

I am a CPA. I have spent decades in tax and estate work and charitable planning, and I am good at it. I do not hold an insurance license. Not one. I know a man who does, who knows that industry inside and out, and when a client needs a policy evaluated I call him rather than pretending. I am also not the expert on business succession and governance. I have somebody for that too, a man who understands family businesses and knows how to navigate the family circle, the ownership circle, and the management circle.

That is not a confession. It is the design.

What I need to know is where my knowledge ends and who I call when I reach the edge. That is a much smaller thing to know than everything, and it is achievable this quarter, which everything is not.

So you just create a team, quite honestly, to help the family get where they want to go. Some of those people are already on their team, because they are advisors the family is already using and already paying. That is the cheapest expertise available to you and almost nobody uses it. Sometimes you have to go out and find more expertise than what they currently have.

Now here is the part that matters more than the mechanics.

Whether you build a team or cover the gap yourself is a culture decision before it is a competence decision. In a practice where saying I do not know that, let me bring in someone who does is a normal sentence, the families get better outcomes and the staff tell the truth about problems earlier. In a practice where that sentence sounds like an admission, people bluff. They bluff to clients and they bluff to each other, and the bluffing gets discovered in the worst possible circumstance, which is usually a hospital or a funeral.

You cannot ask a family to be honest about what they do not understand if honesty about what you do not understand is not modeled in your own office.

There is a second reason to work this way that has nothing to do with humility.

When I got into this I asked people who their advisors were. They would list them. An insurance man, a CPA, a lawyer, a couple of money managers, somebody for property insurance. Then I asked whether any one of those people did three things: knew their goals and objectives clearly, understood everything they had, and had helped them build a strategy to accomplish the first using the second.

Most people said no. I have got all these other people, but they are all living in their silo doing the things that they do well.

That is the market. Not a shortage of expertise. A surplus of expertise with nobody standing above it. The seat is empty in almost every family you will ever meet, and it is empty precisely because taking it requires admitting you cannot personally fill every lane underneath it.

Solomon does not say plans fail for lack of intelligence. He says lack of counsel, which is a different deficiency with a different cure. Intelligence you either have or you do not. Counsel you go and get.

The man who will not go and get it usually is not lazy. He is protecting something, and what he is protecting is the impression that he already knew.

Find out this week what you have been covering for.

Reflect

  1. Which technical lane comes up most often in your book that you are least equipped to handle alone?

  2. Do you have a name for that lane, or have you been covering it? Be specific about which.

  3. Of the professionals already serving your top client, which one knows something you need and have never asked them for?

  4. In your practice, does saying I do not know that sound like strength or like an admission? What does the answer tell you about your culture?

Day 4

That Would Be Horrible

People don't understand

"An inheritance claimed too soon will not be blessed at the end." Proverbs 20:21

I said this to a man once. Do you realize your kids, if you died today, your son is going to get a check for two and a half million dollars?

He said no.

I said yes.

He said, that would be horrible.

He was not objecting to the amount. He was picturing his son. He knew exactly what would happen, which was three or four cars, and he knew it in about a second and a half, because he had known it for years without ever having been asked the question in a form that required an answer.

But the point is, people don't understand. They don't understand. And in many cases, they haven't thought through this.

There is nothing wrong with that man's documents. I want to be clear about that, because the instinct in the room is to reach for the estate plan, and the estate plan is working. It is doing exactly what it was drafted to do. It will deliver a check on schedule to a named person, which is what it was built for.

The gap is not in the instrument. The gap is in the person receiving it.

I separate these into two kinds and I want you to hold them apart, because they get treated as one thing and they need different work. A structural gap is something missing or wrongly placed, a policy inside the estate that should be outside it, a beneficiary designation that points somewhere nobody intended. That gets fixed with documents. Then there is the other kind, where the structure is fine and the preparation is missing, and no amount of legal drafting touches it.

You might have a situation where everything is structured well. Accounts are set up, plans are in place, documents are done. And there is a next generation issue that has not been addressed.

So the question I ask is this. What are you going to do to prepare your kid to be able to handle the ten million dollars that you are going to give him? Because that is what your plan says right now.

That last sentence does the work. Not the number. The fact that the plan already says it. The parent has been carrying a vague intention to sort this out someday, and the document has been carrying a specific instruction the whole time, and the document does not know that someday has not arrived.

The two projects are separate and only one of them is written down.

Solomon is describing a transfer that happens correctly and turns out badly. Nothing is stolen and nobody is defrauded. The inheritance simply arrives before the person is ready to hold it, and the arriving is what does the damage.

That is not an argument for withholding. I have clients who have given their children everything they intend to give them, on purpose, early, because the children were prepared and the money could do more at thirty than at sixty. The variable is never the amount. It is whether anybody did the work.

And here is what makes this urgent rather than merely important. The father in my story said no, then he said that would be horrible, and both of those happened while he was alive and healthy in a conference room. That is the whole opportunity. He has time.

Most families do this arithmetic in a lawyer's office after a funeral, and by then the check is already written.

Reflect

  1. Which of your clients has excellent documents and an unprepared recipient? Name the client and name the recipient.

  2. What would that recipient do with the money if it arrived on Friday? Write what you actually think, not what you would say to the parents.

  3. Have you ever asked a client the question about preparing a child? If not, what has stopped you?

  4. Where in your book have you treated a preparation gap as though it were a structural one?

Day 5

Family Harmony and Wise Wealth Transfer

It's very simple

"How good and pleasant it is when God's people live together in unity." Psalm 133:1

We're just trying to help them to get family harmony and wise wealth transfer. It's very simple.

Two things. That is the entire enterprise, and I have never found a reason to add a third.

They are not the same thing, and that is the part people miss. A family can achieve one and lose the other, and in my experience they usually do. I have watched families transfer wealth with technical brilliance and destroy the relationships doing it. I have watched families stay close and hand the next generation a mess that took a decade to unwind. Neither of those is a success, and both of them were competently executed by somebody.

Here is what it looks like when the two get worked together.

I had a man with three children whose plan was already in place. Good documents, competent counsel, nothing obviously broken. When we assembled the numbers and put the estate tax exposure alongside them, the picture came into focus in a way he had never seen it. Each of his three children would receive twenty million dollars. The federal government would receive twenty million dollars.

He looked at it and said he did not like that.

So I showed him another plan. Ten million to each of the kids. Nothing to the government. Fifty million to charity.

He said, I want that plan.

Nothing had been added to his balance sheet between the first picture and the second. He had a charitable heart when he walked in and he did not want to hand the government that much money, and neither of those facts changed while we were sitting there. What changed is that he could see all of it at once.

He had a charitable heart, but he did not want to give that much money to his kids, and he certainly did not want to give that much to the government.

Notice what that decision accomplished on both counts. The transfer got wiser, obviously, by twenty million dollars. But the harmony piece moved too, and less obviously. Three children who were going to receive an amount their father was privately uncomfortable with are now receiving an amount he chose on purpose, and he will be able to explain why, and they will not spend the rest of their lives guessing at what he meant.

That is what I mean by simple. Not easy. Simple in the sense of having only two objects.

The Psalm is short enough that people treat it as a sentiment, and it is not. It is an observation about a condition that is rare enough to be worth remarking on. Unity is being described as good and pleasant, which are modest words, and the modesty is the point. Nobody is claiming it is dramatic. They are claiming it is better than the alternative, and anyone who has sat with a family after a badly handled transfer knows exactly how much better.

What I usually see, once a family finally has the picture, is not alarm. It is relief.

They are surprised at first, because they do not know what the gaps are. Then they find out they also do not know what could be done about the gaps. So we give them two or three things they could do right away that would make their situation better, and something shifts. There is a sense of relief. A sense of peace. It is not a no-win situation. We can fix this, and we can make it the way we want it, and we can prepare our kids for whatever it is we are going to give them.

Then gratitude, usually later, once they have lived with it for a while.

You will be tempted to make this more complicated than two things, because two things does not sound like enough to build a practice on.

It is enough. I have not needed anything else.

Reflect

  1. In your book, name a family with harmony and a poor transfer plan. Now name one with a good plan and no harmony.

  2. Which of the two are you better equipped to work on? What does that mean for the families who need the other one?

  3. What are the two or three things you could give your largest client right away that would make their situation better?

  4. When a family finally sees the whole picture, will they feel relief or alarm? What does your answer say about how you plan to present it?
