Day 11

It Will Not Happen

If it's not written down and codified

Watch the Day 11 video before you read.

"Write down the revelation and make it plain on tablets, so that a herald may run with it." Habakkuk 2:2

[Key verse recommendation. No scripture appears in the Session 3 material and none in the companion books attaches to Mandatory Documents. This one matches the teaching and needs Tom's approval.]

This particular one represents the M in FAMILY, and it's mandatory documents. Keyword, mandatory. The ones that you have got to have.

Your primary goal with the mandatory documents is you need to help them understand where they are. A lot of people, they have a will, and they did it ten years ago. Well, their family situation was very different ten years ago. They had little kids running around the house. They don't have them anymore. Now the kids are out there creating their own families.

So the documents are often outdated.

Outdated is the right word and it matters, because it is not the same as defective. The document did not fail. It was correct on the day it was signed, drafted by competent counsel, and it has gone on saying exactly the same thing while the family it described moved out of the house and had children of their own.

And then even if they have documents, some people do not have documents. My son is a lawyer. It took me three years of badgering him before he made his will, and he had three kids.

A lawyer. Three children. Three years of his father, who does this for a living, telling him to sit down and do a thing that takes an afternoon.

I tell that on my own family because it kills the explanation everybody reaches for first, which is that people do not do this because they do not understand it. My son understood it completely. He had the technical knowledge, the access, and three reasons sitting in car seats. He still did not do it for three years.

So it is not ignorance. It is that this task has no deadline attached to it. Every other important thing in a professional's life arrives with a date. This one arrives with a feeling that you ought to get to it, and a feeling loses to a calendar every single time.

Which means your job with a client is not primarily to persuade. It is to supply the date.

People don't realize how important the documents are, because what you say you want to have happen, if it's not written down and codified, it will not happen. And the state of whatever state you're in, they have a document for you, and I'll guarantee you're not going to like it.

That is not a scare tactic. It is a description of what the law does in the absence of instruction. Somebody is going to decide. If it is not the person who owned the assets, it will be a statute written for the average case by people who never met this family.

Now, what actually has to be written down.

From an estate planning standpoint, we have got wills, and you might have trusts. You need a power of attorney, who's going to make your decisions if you're not able to make them. You need healthcare directives. What if you're in the hospital? That needs to be all decided.

Those are just the basic estate documents. Then other people may have trusts, they may have irrevocable life insurance trusts. And if you have a business, there's another whole school of documents. You have shareholder agreements, you have operating agreements. You may have buy-sell agreements.

So we want to look at all these documents so that we understand where you are right now today.

Habakkuk is told to write it down and make it plain, and then given the reason, which is so that a herald may run with it. The writing is not for the man who received the vision. He already knows it. The writing is so that somebody else can carry it at speed, later, without him.

That is exactly what an estate document is for. It is not for the person who signed it. It exists so that people who are grieving and hurried and not thinking clearly can pick it up and run.

Make it plain enough that they can. Then put a date on the calendar, because nothing here happens without one.

Day 12

That Might Be 50% Correct

The three myths of estate planning

Watch the Day 12 video before you read.

"Do not boast about tomorrow, for you do not know what a day may bring." Proverbs 27:1

I tell people the three myths of estate planning.

First myth is, I'm never going to die.

Nobody says that out loud. Everybody plans as though it were true, which amounts to the same thing with better manners. A man who genuinely believed he had a limited number of years would not leave a document untouched for a decade.

The second myth: I've got the highest priced attorneys in town, certainly my estate is in good shape.

I say, that might be 50% correct. You might have the highest priced attorneys in town.

That is the only joke in this session and it is not really a joke. Price is evidence of price. It is not evidence of currency, it is not evidence that anybody has looked at the document since the day it was signed, and it is certainly not evidence that the document describes a family that still exists. That's no guarantee your estate is in good shape.

And then third is, it's my kids' money.

I say, God didn't give it to your kids. He gave it to you.

That sentence stops rooms, and I have never once had to explain it. Everybody understands it immediately, including people who do not share my faith, because at the moment it lands it is not really a theological argument. It is a question about who is responsible.

If it is the children's money, then the parent is a temporary custodian of an inheritance already spoken for, and his job is to protect it and hand it over with as little friction as possible. Nothing about that job requires him to think about who his children actually are.

If it was given to him, then he is answerable for what happens to it, including what it does to the people who receive it.

And so if you're going to pass it on to your kids, you need to make sure they're prepared to be able to handle it.

Now, the practical consequence of all three myths together.

A lot of people think, I have a will, that's a one-and-done experience for the rest of life. I tell people, you're probably going to change your will three, four times, five times in your life, depending upon your situation as it changes.

Set that expectation early, because it removes the sting from everything that follows. A client who hears at the outset that revision is normal will not experience a review as criticism of the attorney he hired and probably likes. He will experience it as maintenance, which is what it is.

And when they're not aligned, that's when confusion takes place.

A lot of families couldn't sit down and clearly explain what their documents actually say and how they function in the family, or what would happen if they were to put it in motion.

Read that as three separate failures, because they are. Not knowing what the document says. Not knowing how it functions. Not knowing what would happen if it were triggered tomorrow. A family can fail any one of those while passing the others, and most fail at least two.

Solomon is not telling anyone to be morbid. He is telling them not to boast, and the boast in question is a quiet one. It is the assumption of available time.

A document written ten years ago is a bet that nothing important would change. Most of your clients have already lost that bet and have not gone back to check the ticket.

Go check one this week.

  3. Could your largest client explain what his documents say and how they function? Which of the three would he fail?

  4. Where have you assumed something was handled because a competent professional was involved?

Day 13

He Died Three Years Ago

It takes a lot of work

Watch the Day 13 video before you read.

"You do not even know what will happen tomorrow. What is your life? You are a mist that appears for a little while and then vanishes." James 4:14

A lot of people don't have wills. And that means that the state is going to have a will for you, and you're not going to like it.

So a family, again, they just need to know what it is that they have, and they need to know what it says.

A lot of times, if it's an old will, you named your uncle to be your executor. Well, he died three years ago. And you didn't name anybody else.

That is not a drafting error. On the day it was written, the uncle was a sound choice and the attorney did good work. The document did not fail. It simply kept saying the same thing while the world around it moved, and nobody went back.

The exposure is straightforward and worth stating without diplomacy. If the client dies tonight, nobody he chose is in charge. The appointment falls to a court applying the rules of his state, which is precisely the outcome he thought he had prevented by signing something.

So we just need to make sure they're up to date, and that the people who are going to do these responsibilities for your estate are aware that they're going to get to do it. And that they're willing to do it for you, because it takes a lot of work to be an executor over the estate.

Aware. Willing. Both.

That is the step almost everybody skips, and it is not a formality. A name written into a document by somebody who never asked the person is not a plan. It is a hope with a signature on it. Serving as executor of a substantial estate is months of work, often years, frequently in conflict with people who are grieving, and the person named may be seventy-four years old, or living overseas, or estranged from half the family, or simply unwilling.

Nobody finds that out until it is being asked of them at the worst possible moment.

So the documents are there, but it doesn't reflect what you really want, so we're going to redo your documents so that they're aligned with what you say you want to do with your stuff.

Now, why this cannot wait for a convenient quarter.

Unfortunately, this confusion doesn't really show up in a calm moment. Somebody finds out they've got cancer, or somebody finds out that there's a car accident, or somebody loses a company. There's just always something out there that happens.

When something unexpected happens, the emotions are already high, and now people are trying to interpret decisions instead of just follow what has already been written.

That is the sentence to hold. Interpret rather than follow.

A document reviewed in March is maintenance, and it is boring, and nobody's feelings are involved. The same document read for the first time in a hospital corridor becomes an object of interpretation among people who are frightened and exhausted, and every ambiguity in it gets read through whatever they already suspect about each other.

James calls a life a mist that appears for a little while. He is not being grim. He is correcting a specific error, which is the confident sentence about what a man will do next year, and he writes it to people making ordinary business plans.

The estate document is the most confident sentence most people ever write about the future, and it is usually written once.

Go find out whether somebody's named executor is alive, aware, and willing. That is three questions and one phone call.

Day 14

Who's Running the Business When You're Not There?

Somebody has to be in charge

Watch the Day 14 video before you read.

"Go to the ant, you sluggard; consider its ways and be wise! It has no commander, no overseer or ruler, yet it stores its provisions in summer." Proverbs 6:6-8

People talk about, you need a succession plan if you have a business. Well, you need a continuity plan.

People say, what's that mean?

Let's say you get in a car wreck and you're in the hospital for thirty days getting repaired. Who's running the business when you're not there? If you don't have a plan for that, who knows. Everybody's running around, nobody knows who's in charge.

Somebody has to be in charge.

Here is the distinction stated as plainly as I can make it, because these two words get used interchangeably by people who should know better.

Succession is what eventually happens to the business. Continuity is who has authority during the thirty days you are unavailable.

Succession is the one everybody plans for, because it is the interesting problem. It involves valuation and structure and the future, and it can be worked on pleasantly over several years with people you enjoy.

Continuity is the boring problem. It is a list of names and a document saying who signs what. Nobody enjoys building it and nobody feels sophisticated afterward.

It is also the one that actually happens. Most business owners will be temporarily unavailable at some point, from an accident or an illness or a surgery. Comparatively few will die suddenly at the peak of an unresolved succession question.

The likely event is the unplanned one.

And again, when a tragic event happens, which often happens for people, that's when people start to look at this, and that's really way too late. We need to get this stuff figured out in advance.

Too late has a specific meaning here and it is not about expense. During the thirty days, decisions still arrive. A contract is pending. A bank wants an answer. Somebody has to sign. If nobody has authority, the company does not stop, it simply proceeds badly, because capable people guess rather than decide and nobody wants to be the one who signed something the owner would have handled differently.

So nothing, in many cases, is technically missing, but it still doesn't work the way it was intended or that you had originally hoped for.

That describes almost every failure in this entire module, and it is worth noticing that it appears here in its purest form. Nothing missing. Nothing broken. It simply does not work.

And now you're not just dealing with the situation, you're dealing with the uncertainty inside the situation.

Now, the framing that gets a client to act.

It isn't just a legal exercise to get you a new will. It's getting you the will that you want to have. You've already gone through the decision-making. You now have clarity. And you know where you want your stuff to go. We just need to make sure that your documents reflect that clarity so that your family will be served in the way you want them to be served.

That reframe matters because it removes the drudgery from the ask. You are not sending a man to a lawyer to do paperwork. You are helping him make a decision he has already made take effect.

Solomon points at an insect with no commander, no overseer, no ruler, and says look at what it manages anyway. The ant stores in summer for a season it cannot see, with nobody instructing it.

Your clients have the opposite problem. They have commanders and overseers and a leadership team, and no provision at all for a season they can see coming.

Ask one of them this week what happens during the thirty days.

Day 15

Do You Have Anyone That Knows and Does Three Things for You?

They're all living in their silo

Watch the Day 15 video before you read.

"For lack of guidance a nation falls, but victory is won through many advisers." Proverbs 11:14

There's complexity that can creep in, because each of these documents may have been created in different times with different advisors for different reasons, and no one has really stepped back to see how they all connect. And most advisors have a piece of it.

When I first got into this, I started by asking people a few questions.

The first question is, tell me who your advisors are. Well, I've got an insurance guy, I've got a CPA, I've got a loan manager, I've got a couple of money managers, I've got a property insurance guy.

Then I said, do you have anyone that knows and does three things for you?

Number one, they clearly know what your goals and objectives are. Number two, they understand everything you have. And number three, have they helped you to develop a strategy to accomplish your goals and objectives based upon the resources that you have?

Most people said, no. I've got all these other people, but they're all living in their silo, doing the things that they do well.

That is the market. Not a shortage of expertise. A surplus of expertise with nobody standing above it.

The seat is empty in almost every substantial family in the country, and it is empty for a specific reason. Occupying it requires you to admit that you cannot personally fill every lane underneath it, and most professionals would rather stay in a lane they can defend than stand above lanes they cannot.

Notice also that the three questions are diagnostic rather than persuasive. I am not telling anybody they have a problem. I am asking three questions and letting the answers accumulate, and by the third one the man has discovered something about his own life that has been true for twenty years.

That is a much better position to build from than any presentation.

And then the last question was, do your advisors share your biblical worldview?

That was often no. I've got an attorney. He's a great guy. He's really good at his estate planning. But he gets nervous when I try to give away money. He just doesn't like it. He tells me, oh, that's for your kids. You shouldn't be giving that to charity.

[Attribution note. The advisor is identified by religion on the recording. The teaching point is worldview alignment and survives without the identifier, so it has been removed here. Confirm before print.]

That is not incompetence and it is not grounds to fire anybody by itself. It is a worldview mismatch, which belongs in a different category than skill, and a family that cannot tell the two apart will keep taking advice about generosity from somebody who thinks generosity is the problem.

Character, competence, and worldview are three separate evaluations. A man can be excellent on two and unusable on the third, and the third is the one nobody assesses because it feels impolite to ask.

So it's really when I realized that a lot of people don't have these answers that we really actually got to create what we do right now.

Solomon puts guidance and many advisers in the same sentence, and the pairing is deliberate. Many advisers is not the same as guidance. A nation can have a great many advisers and still fall for lack of guidance, because advisers produce advice and guidance requires somebody to integrate it.

Your clients have advisers. Almost none of them have guidance.

Ask the three questions of one prospect this month. Then be quiet and let them answer.
