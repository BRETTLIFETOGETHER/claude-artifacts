A Welcome From Tom

When I started this work, I did not have a process. I had a question.

I would sit down with someone and ask who their advisors were. They would tell me. An insurance person, a CPA, an attorney, a couple of money managers, somebody for property and casualty. Good people, most of them, and competent in what they did. Then I would ask whether any one of them did three things. Did anyone clearly know their goals and objectives. Did anyone understand everything they had. Had anyone helped them build a strategy to accomplish the first using the second.

The answer was almost always no. Everyone was living in a silo, doing well the thing they did well.

I asked one more question after that, which was whether their advisors shared their biblical worldview. That answer was usually no too. One man told me his attorney was excellent at estate planning and grew visibly nervous whenever the conversation turned toward giving money away, because in his view that money belonged to the children.

I built what I built because of those answers.

Here is what I want you to understand before you begin. Two things matter to me in this work, and everything else serves them. The first is family harmony. The second is wise wealth transfer. They are not the same thing, and a family can achieve one and lose the other, and generally does. Most of the families I sit with have excellent documents and no shared understanding, or a great deal of money and a next generation nobody has prepared to receive it. Neither of those is a legal problem.

You do not need to be a CPA to do this. I am one, and it helps, and it is not the requirement. I hold no insurance license. I am not the expert on business succession. I know people who are, and I call them, and the families I serve are better off for it. What you need is the willingness to look at the whole picture rather than the part you are paid for, and the humility to bring in someone who knows more than you when you reach the edge of what you know.

One more thing, and I say it to clients as often as I say it to advisors. God did not give it to your children. He gave it to you. Whatever you do with that, do it on purpose. A family builds its legacy by design or it inherits one by default, and default is not neutral. Default is simply the accumulation of decisions nobody revisited.

This guide covers the second of three movements in the work: clarity first, then alignment, then communication. Alignment is where you find out whether what a family has actually matches what a family wants. It is unglamorous, it is mostly gathering, and it is the part almost nobody does.

I am glad you are here.

Tom Conway

Using This Guide

This works alone, in a pair, or with a team

Some of you are going through this by yourself. Some of you are two partners working through it together. Some of you are a team of eight, meeting on Wednesday mornings. All three work, and the guide has been built for all three from the beginning rather than adapted to them afterward.

Wherever a prompt is designed to be shared, you will see this line beneath it:

Team or pair: discuss. Solo: write it down.

That is the whole mechanism. If there are other people in the room, talk it through. If there are not, write your answer in the space provided. Nothing in this guide requires another person in order to work, and nothing loses its value when you have one.

Where a section is private by design, it says so and there is no fork. The Exercise in every session is yours alone. Do not discuss it, even if you are working with a team, and particularly if you are working with a team.

If you are meeting as a group, there is a section at the back called If You're Doing This With a Group containing a team agreement, a roster, and a calendar. If you are working alone, you can skip it entirely and lose nothing.

The privacy standard

You are never required to disclose the name of a client, a specific figure, a family circumstance, or any detail you would not put in an email.

This matters more in this series than it would in most, because you are protecting two sets of confidences at once. Your clients' information is not yours to bring into a room. Your own financial situation is nobody's business either, and several sections of this guide will ask you to look at it honestly. Honest and disclosed are different things.

Where a prompt asks about a client, answer it in whatever form protects them. A client, a family I work with, someone in my book. That is sufficient and it is the norm rather than the exception.

Six sessions, six letters

The six sessions of this guide walk the six letters of the F.A.M.I.L.Y. Review, one per session and in order. Financial Statement. Assets Under Management. Mandatory Documents. Insurance Coverage. Liabilities and Taxes. Yearly Cash Flow.

Each session pairs with a teaching video. Watch it when you reach the Learn section rather than before, since the questions there are built to be answered while the conversation is fresh.

The videos are conversations between Brett and Tom rather than lectures, so there are no blanks to fill in. Instead, each prompt in the Learn section names what they covered and asks you to apply it to your own book of business. That is a harder ask than filling in a blank and it is the reason the material sticks.

Answer before you look

Every session contains three scenarios drawn from situations Tom has actually handled. The responses are collected at the back of the book rather than printed alongside the scenarios, and that is deliberate.

Write your answer first. Then read his. The distance between the two is the entire point, and there is no distance to measure if you have already read the answer.

How long this takes

Plan on ninety minutes per session if you are working through everything, including the video. The Exercise adds another thirty and is worth protecting rather than compressing, since it is the only section that turns the material on you rather than on your clients.

Six weeks is the natural rhythm. Six days is not.

Outline of Each Session

Every session follows the same seventeen sections, in this order.

1. Session Divider The number, the letter, and the theme.

2. Introduction The key verse, then a short framing of what this letter of the review is actually asking.

3. Opening Story One story per session, drawn from Tom's work with real families and de-identified.

4. Get Started A privacy reminder and two questions to open the session.

5. Building a Healthy Culture Half a page in Session 1 about your own team and practice. A one-line reminder in the sessions that follow.

6. Learn Watch the teaching video, then four capture prompts. Each names what Tom and Brett covered and asks you to apply it to your own clients.

7. Reflect Seven questions. The seventh is always about you rather than about a client. A quotation from Tom follows.

8. The Framework The session's named framework, in the exact language used across the Family Legacy by Design material, with one application question underneath.

9. Scenarios Three situations, with room to write. Responses are collected in the Appendix.

10. The Tool One of Tom's actual instruments per session, described the way he runs it.

11. Exercise Private, in three parts. The first applies to a client, the second goes deeper, the third turns it on you. Closes with a five-item self-check.

12. Circles of Influence Two names. An invitation, not a commitment.

13. Next Steps Three or four actions with checkboxes.

14. Practice Conversation Four questions to take to a colleague, a partner, a mentor, or a family you serve.

15. Going Deeper Three passages, two questions each.

16. This Week's Readings Five readings from the companion book, one per day, each with a note on how to use it in practice.

17. Final Thoughts Two prompts and a closing thought.
