Day 21

It's Worth 600,000

It says it's worth a million dollars

Watch the Day 21 video before you read.

"Give back to Caesar what is Caesar's, and to God what is God's." Mark 12:17

Up until this point we've been building a picture from different angles. It's almost like a diamond that has different facets. What a family has, how it fits together, how it's structured, how it's protected.

And there's another side to that picture, and it's essentially what we owe.

Families naturally focus on what they've built and what they can see, what they can track, what they can grow, what's in the bank. The liabilities and taxes are always there in the background, shaping what actually is available and what decisions truly can be made.

That is the honest description of why this section gets skipped. It is not that anybody is avoiding it. It is that one side of the picture generates statements and the other side does not.

And if that part of the picture isn't clear, it can create a gap between perception and reality.

So what are you really trying to help a family understand? You're really helping them to understand the full picture of just where they are.

Because a lot of people will have assets on their balance sheet. But they don't realize if they sell those assets, forty percent of that could go to the government.

And so, it says it's worth a million dollars. They sell it, it's not worth a million dollars. It's worth 600,000.

[Rate figure is Tom's on tape. Rates vary by asset and circumstance and are subject to change. Use current figures with an as-of date, or present illustratively.]

That takes four seconds to say and I have watched it reorganize an entire plan.

The balance sheet is not wrong. It is incomplete. It reports what is owned without reporting what is attached to it, and a family that has been reading that page for fifteen years has been building expectations on a number that will not survive contact with a transaction.

They get Caesar what is Caesar's. You give to Caesar his portion.

I say that without complaint and I want you to notice the absence of complaint, because it sets the tone for everything else in this week. The obligation is real. Nothing here is about avoiding what is owed. It is about knowing the number before you make a promise against it.

Here is where the incompleteness does its damage.

A generous man decides on a gift, sized against the balance sheet figure. He sells the position to fund it. The tax arrives, and the gift he can actually make is materially smaller than the one he announced, and now he is renegotiating a commitment with people he wanted to bless.

That is entirely preventable. It is prevented by one calculation, performed in the right week, on a number he has never questioned because nobody ever put a second number beside it.

Jesus is handed a coin and asked a trap question, and his answer refuses the trap by dividing the field. Some of this is Caesar's, and you should give it to him without resentment. Some of it is God's, and no arrangement with Caesar touches that.

What that does for a family looking at a large tax number is remove the drama from it. The portion that is owed is owed. The energy that would go into being aggrieved about it is better spent finding out what the number actually is.

Run the after-tax figure on three holdings for one client this week. Bring all three, so it reads as a category they have been missing rather than one unlucky position.

  3. What plans has he made against the higher number?

  4. Where has a client of yours built an expectation on a figure that will not survive a sale?

Day 22

Shackled Because of Their Debts

Not worth what they think they're worth

Watch the Day 22 video before you read.

"The rich rule over the poor, and the borrower is slave to the lender." Proverbs 22:7

Debt is really important, too. Because a lot of people are shackled because of their debts. They owe a lot of money to someone, to the bank or whatever, and until they get that debt brought down, they really are not worth what they think they're worth.

Shackled is a strong word and I use it on purpose, because the families I am describing do not look shackled. They look successful. That is exactly the problem, and it is the reason nobody raises it.

So when we get into liabilities and taxes, we want to be real clear on who they owe, how much they owe, what the interest rates are. When does it need to be paid off? Is it a balloon payment, or is it something they're going to pay over the next thirty years?

Those are all things that are important when you're putting together this big picture of where they are.

Four items, and most advisors capture two. Balance and rate get recorded because the statement supplies them. Term and structure do not, because they require asking, and those two are the ones that determine what actually happens.

A balloon is not a balance. It is a date. And a date that arrives during a soft market or a slow year in the business is a forced decision, which is the worst kind of financial event, because every party to it can see that you have no choice.

Thirty years of amortization is a different animal entirely. Same balance, same rate, completely different exposure. Nobody who records only the first two numbers can tell the difference between them.

Then you get into taxes. An income tax return, a personal tax return, is the most revealing document that you can find out, because all your income goes into your tax return. So even if they didn't tell you they owned a certain thing, it'll show up on their tax return.

That is where this week turns, and tomorrow is entirely about it. For today, notice only that the two halves belong together. What a family owes and what a family earns are both on the underside of the picture, both invisible on a statement, and both discoverable in documents the family already has.

Now the reason I care about debt in a legacy conversation, which is not the reason you would expect.

It is not that debt reduces net worth, though it does. It is that debt constrains generosity, and it constrains it invisibly. A family with a genuine commitment to giving and a heavy debt load has a plan that cannot execute, and nobody has ever told them the two facts are connected.

You can watch it happen across years. The intention stays constant. The capacity shrinks. The family experiences it as a series of individual decisions to give a little less this year, each of which feels reasonable on its own.

Solomon's language is not moral. He does not say the borrower is wicked. He says the borrower is a servant, which is a statement about who gets to decide. A man carrying significant debt has handed a portion of his future decision-making to somebody else, and the portion grows quietly while he is busy being successful.

That is what shackled means. Not poverty. Reduced authorship.

Get all four items on every obligation for one client this week. Look hardest at the two nobody records.

Reflect

  1. Which of your clients looks wealthier than he is? What is the evidence?

  2. For your most leveraged client, can you state the rate and the term, or only the balance?

  3. Where is a stated commitment to generosity being quietly constrained by a debt load nobody has connected to it?

  4. What would it mean to treat debt as a matter of authorship rather than arithmetic?

Day 23

It Shows a Face

But a lot of times they miss things

Watch the Day 23 video before you read.

"There is nothing concealed that will not be disclosed, or hidden that will not be made known." Luke 12:2

[Scripture note. This passage concerns the eventual visibility of hidden things and is spoken about hypocrisy. It is used here for the principle rather than the context. Flagged for Tom's judgment.]

Tax returns are one of the most important documents, because it shows the income, it shows the structure, it shows how different pieces of the financial picture are functioning.

It often shows how much they own of a particular asset, because in order to prepare a tax return, you have to get a K-1 that generally says what percentage of the asset or the company they actually own.

Read that one again. A client tells you he owns a piece of a partnership. The K-1 tells you exactly how much. He may not remember. He may be wrong. The form is not wrong.

You can look at a tax return and begin to see where all their income is coming from, whether it's earned income, passive income, business-related income, investment income. These are things that are all structured in their estate plan somewhere, and you're just learning how it all relates together.

And once you start to understand that, you're not just looking at numbers, you're starting to see how the whole system works together. Because all the assets flow into the income tax return.

That is the sentence that should change your Tuesday. Every asset a family owns eventually announces itself on a schedule somewhere. The return is not a record of what happened last year. Read properly, it is a map of the entire system, drawn by the family itself, that they will hand you if you ask.

But most people, they don't look at it that way. They file them, and they move on, and they assume someone else is handling it, and that they're good, because the top line is good.

Now, the part that explains why things get missed even when everyone is competent.

Quite honestly, many of the income tax preparers, they don't understand what the family owns either. All they're doing, they go to you, and they say, send me all your information. You don't know what to send. You don't know what things you should have kept and didn't keep. You send them whatever you have, and they take that like a puzzle. And they put it together and it shows a face.

But a lot of times they miss things.

It shows a face. That is the whole diagnosis and it is the best phrase in this session.

A puzzle assembled from incomplete pieces still produces a picture. The picture is coherent. It is internally consistent. Nothing about it announces which pieces are absent, and that is precisely why nobody catches the omission. An obviously broken return gets questions. A plausible one gets filed.

So the failure mode here is not error. It is omission producing a plausible result, and there is only one position from which that is visible.

Somebody has to be reading the document who knows the family rather than only the paperwork. Not a better accountant, and not a second opinion on the arithmetic. Somebody holding the whole picture, reading a return prepared by somebody holding a partial one, and noticing what is not there.

That seat is the one this entire module has been describing for five weeks, and it is empty in almost every family you will ever meet.

Jesus says nothing concealed will not be disclosed. He is not talking about tax returns and I will not pretend otherwise. What the principle gives you is this: the information you need about a family is not hidden from you. It is sitting in a document they already have and already signed.

Ask for two years from one client this week. One year is a photograph. Two years is a direction.

Then actually read them.

Day 24

Taxes Often Become Most Significant During Transitions

Nothing has felt like a problem until it is

Watch the Day 24 video before you read.

"Suppose one of you wants to build a tower. Won't you first sit down and estimate the cost to see if you have enough money to complete it?" Luke 14:28

Taxes often become most significant during transitions.

Everything can look strong while the assets are growing, but when something changes, when assets are sold, transferred, or passed on, that's when the full impact shows up. And if that hasn't been thought through ahead of time, it can change the outcome significantly for a family.

And that's where things catch families off guard, because nothing has felt like a problem until it is.

That sentence explains a great deal, and it is worth slowing down on.

Growth conceals this problem perfectly. A growing asset generates no taxable event, therefore no correction to the number in anyone's head, therefore no signal that anything needs attention. A family can be wrong by a substantial margin for fifteen years and receive no feedback of any kind.

Then a sale happens, or a death, and the number corrects all at once, at the least convenient moment available.

And this is where clarity changes how decisions are made. Once you understand what's attached to what you've built, you start planning differently. You think ahead, you make adjustments earlier, and you avoid having to react later under pressure.

Reacting under pressure is the actual cost, more than the tax itself. A family that knows its numbers chooses the timing. A family that finds out at closing has no choices left, only consequences, and every party across the table can see it.

This also connects to how assets are transferred. What can be given annually, because you can make transfers annually to children and others. What fits with lifetime exclusions, because you have a lifetime exclusion.

These are tools, but only if they're clearly understood. And if they're not, opportunities are missed, and more pressure gets pushed into the future.

[Exclusion amounts are indexed and subject to legislative change. Print current values with an as-of date.]

That phrase, more pressure gets pushed into the future, describes something families never see happening. An annual exclusion unused is not a loss anybody notices. It leaves no hole in a statement. It simply does not appear, year after year, and the amount that could have moved out of an estate quietly stays in it, compounding, until it becomes a problem the next generation inherits along with everything else.

And that's where this becomes more than just awareness. Now you're talking about decisions, and how and when to move assets, and how to plan ahead, and how to reduce pressure on the next generation.

And all this comes back to alignment. When you understand what you owe, both now and in the future, you can begin to align strategy accordingly.

Jesus asks about a man building a tower, and the question is not whether he wants the tower or whether the tower is a good idea. The question is whether he sat down first and estimated. The failure being described is not ambition. It is arithmetic performed too late, in public, with a half-built structure standing behind you.

Every family you serve is building a tower. Most of them have never sat down and estimated what it will cost to finish it, because while it is going up nothing feels like a problem.

Do the estimate for one family this week, before anything changes.

  3. Which of your families has an annual exclusion sitting unused? For how long?

  4. What decision would that family make differently if they knew the number now?

Day 25

We Saved a Family Probably $10 Million

There's so many tools

Watch the Day 25 video before you read.

"Houses and wealth are inherited from parents, but a prudent wife is from the Lord." Proverbs 19:14

Many people don't know all the implications, and that's why it's helpful to come to different people to be able to get that insight. You may know it as a financial planner or as a business leader, but you also know you have got to grab your phone and call somebody who knows somebody.

In the last couple of weeks, we saved a family probably ten million dollars by a transaction that we did with them.

Here is what we did.

They have a new company, and we've taken that company, and we've moved it into a trust for the benefit of the next generation. And that new company went and bought a bunch of real estate. Of course, it didn't have any money, so what mom and dad did is they lent money to this new company.

So now the company has no value, because they took their ten million dollars, they bought a piece of real estate for ten million dollars. So they have no value. There's zero. There's a ten million dollar asset, ten million dollar liability, zero value.

Now, all the future growth of that real estate is going to go to the next generation. And over time, they're going to pay rent on that property. Over time, they're going to pay back the ten million dollars.

But if that property grows to be worth twenty million dollars ten years from now, that twenty million is out of the parents' estate. It's now in the kids' estate, and you have transferred ten million dollars of value at no cost by doing that.

Read that slowly, because the mechanism is elegant and the elegance is the point.

Nothing was given away. Nothing was gifted, so nothing consumed an exclusion. The parents lent money and will be repaid. The company they funded holds an asset exactly offset by a liability, which is why moving it into the trust cost nothing at the moment of transfer.

What moved was not value. What moved was future growth, and future growth is the only thing in an estate plan that is genuinely free to relocate, because at the moment you move it, it does not yet exist.

That is the whole art of this. Everybody looks at what a family has. Almost nobody looks at what a family is about to have.

There's so many tools. There's so many different things you can do to help families save money, and using the tax laws that exist, and doing it in a very effective way, and accomplish a lot of your long-term goals and objectives.

[Structural note. This is a description of one transaction for one family, not a recommendation. Structures of this kind depend on valuation, interest rates, documentation, and the specific facts of a family, and they require competent counsel. Confirm the treatment of this passage before print.]

I want to name the honest condition attached to all of it, because I said it before I told the story.

You have got to grab your phone and call somebody who knows somebody. I did not do that transaction alone and I would not have known how. What I knew was that a gap existed, roughly what shape it was, and who to bring in.

That is the job. Not knowing every tool. Knowing that tools exist, seeing the situation clearly enough to know one applies, and having the humility and the phone numbers to go get help.

Solomon separates two kinds of inheritance in a single verse. Houses and wealth come from parents. Something else comes from somewhere else. He is not diminishing the houses. He is locating them accurately, as the part a family can arrange, alongside the part no family can arrange at all.

You spend your career on the first half. Do it well, because doing it badly costs people a great deal.

Just do not confuse it with the second half.
