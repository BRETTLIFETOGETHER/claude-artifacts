Separate layout specification for the designer, kept out of the manuscript

LAYOUT SPECIFICATION

Legacy Coaching by Design: Thirty Days of Reading

For Hannah. Separate from the manuscript so the manuscript stays clean.

STRUCTURE

Thirty entries. Six week dividers. Front matter is title page, copyright, How to Use This, and contents.

Every entry carries the same nine elements in the same order, with no horizontal rules or dividers between them:

  1. Day number
  2. Title
  3. Subtitle
  4. Scripture in bold, then the reference
  5. Body, roughly seven hundred to seven hundred fifty words
  6. Reflect, four numbered questions
  7. Practice, a written exercise with rules or a table
  8. One Question to Carry, a single bold question with write-in lines
  9. The Bucket, three or four private write-in prompts
  10. Prayer, one or two italic lines

The prayer is always a direction rather than a written prayer. Set it in italic and do not add quotation marks, which would make it read as something to recite.

HEADING HIERARCHY

The markdown maps directly.

Markdown Element
# WEEK ONE Week divider, full page
## SESSION 1 · ... Session name on the divider
### *subtitle* Session subtitle on the divider
# DAY 1 Day number
# Title Entry title
## Subtitle Entry subtitle
## REFLECT etc. Section headings inside an entry

The day number, title, and subtitle share the same page opening and should be treated as one typographic unit rather than three separate headings.

WRITE-IN RULES

Runs of underscores are write-in rules and should be set as ruled lines rather than as literal underscore characters.

Three lengths appear:

  • Full width. The standard line in One Question to Carry, The Bucket, and most Practice sections.
  • Short. Numbered lines in a list, and inline fields such as a name or a date.
  • Inline. A short blank inside a sentence, for a count or a figure.

Where two or three rules appear in sequence with no text between them, they are a block of writing space and should be set with consistent leading.

TABLES

Roughly two thirds of the Practice sections use tables. Column headers are set. Row content is blank by design and the rows are for the reader to complete.

Blank rows should be given enough height to write in by hand. A table with four blank rows should occupy meaningfully more depth than the same table would if it were displaying data.

Several tables use a 1 to 5 scoring column. Where that appears, consider setting five small boxes rather than an open cell.

SCRIPTURE

Every entry opens with a scripture in bold followed by the reference in small caps on its own line. All thirty are set in the New International Version.

No verse repeats across any two entries. If a verse appears to repeat, it is an error and should be flagged rather than corrected in layout.

One entry, Day 25, refers back to a verse that appeared earlier in the book in a shorter form. That reference is intentional.

RUNNING HEADS AND FOOTERS

Recommend the week and session on the verso and the day number and title on the recto. The entry titles run long, so the recto head may need the title only rather than the title and subtitle.

THINGS TO WATCH

No em-dashes appear anywhere in this manuscript. That is deliberate and it is a series rule. Do not let a layout pass introduce them, and do not let a spellcheck convert a double hyphen.

No horizontal rules between sections. Sections are separated by typography rather than by lines.

The Bucket is private. It should feel visually distinct from the rest of the entry, quieter rather than louder, and it should not be given a treatment that invites sharing.

Entry titles are quotations. Every title and subtitle in this book is a phrase spoken by Tom Conway. They are not descriptive headings and should not be edited for grammar or parallelism.

OUTSTANDING BEFORE PRINT

  • ISBN
  • Author photographs, if the devotional carries them
  • Confirmation of the New International Version permission and the exact copyright line required by the publisher
  • Whether the devotional carries a foreword, which the curriculum does
