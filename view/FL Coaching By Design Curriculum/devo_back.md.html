THE QUESTIONS

Every reading in this book ended with one question to carry into a client conversation. Gathered here, they are the working set. Fifteen questions, and between them they open almost anything.

None of these are ours in the sense that we invented them. They are the questions Tom has asked families for thirty years, and they are plain on purpose. A question that sounds impressive gets an impressive answer. A question that sounds ordinary gets a true one.

To open

What matters most to you?

What do you want to be true of your family over time?

What do you want your family to be known for?

What has shaped how you think about life and resources?

To draw something out

How are you seeing this?

How are you thinking about this?

How are you thinking about the future?

What feels most important here?

What concerns you?

To test alignment

What matters most to us?

Does this align with what we've said matters most?

To turn it on yourself

What am I building into the lives around me?

What are we actually passing on?

What else are we passing on?

Ask one. Then be quiet longer than is comfortable.

WHAT'S NEXT

You have finished thirty days. The honest question now is not whether any of it was useful.

It is which name you wrote down.

Somewhere in The Bucket you named a family whose next generation you have never met, a conversation you have not started because it matters too much, a forecast you are holding without data, or something you have seen clearly for a year and done nothing about. Nobody read it and nobody is going to.

That is the item. One family, one conversation, this month.

The deeper work is waiting whenever you want it, and the credential is worth having. Neither one is what makes this real. What makes it real is a call this week to a client who has no idea it is coming, asking a question nobody has asked him.

Do that first. The rest follows.

ABOUT FAMILY LEGACY BY DESIGN

Three words sit at the heart of this work: clarity, alignment, and communication.

We help families gain clarity around their goals in five areas of legacy, personal, family, financial, business, and charitable. We review the existing plan to see how well it matches that clarity. Then we help them communicate and discuss the plan with the next generation, which happens in family meetings.

All of it runs through the Multi-Generational Legacy Coaching Process, which helps families gain confidence about what they believe God wants them to do with their resources. It helps them quantify what they need for the future, what they wish to leave to their family, what they will do with their business, and how the remainder can be released to organizations that reflect their values.

The conviction underneath the work is that a lack of clarity around legacy keeps many families from making full use of what they have been entrusted with, and that many families do not know whether their existing plans match what they actually intend.
