There are eighteen scenarios in this guide, three in each session. All eighteen responses are gathered here, in session order, rather than at the back of each session.

These responses are not the only correct handling of each situation. They are how Tom approaches them, and the reasoning matters more than the move.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

I am the only person in my firm doing this. Does it still work?

Yes, and this guide was built that way. Every exercise is designed for one advisor working alone. The prompts that can be discussed carry a fork so you can write instead. Nothing here requires a partner, a team, or a facilitator.

How long does this actually take?

Ninety minutes per session and about fifteen minutes a day for the readings, across six weeks. The Practice Conversation and the Exercise happen between sessions. If you are moving faster than that, you are skipping the writing, and the writing is where the work happens.

Do I need to be a CPA or an attorney to do this?

No. Technical background helps and Tom is a CPA, but his own summary of the job puts the relational skills ahead of the technical ones. What the work does require is a willingness to bring in genuine experts where you are not strong.

My clients are not people of faith. Does this apply?

Most of it, directly. Tom adapts his own language for this. In the fifth area of legacy he uses charitable or philanthropic rather than Kingdom, and the substance of the conversation does not change. The scripture in this guide is for you rather than for the meeting.

What do I charge for this?

Tom charges a flat fee for the Family Legacy Review as its own engagement, and his reasoning is diagnostic rather than commercial. Nobody can scope a plan before knowing where the gaps are, and doing the review without charge quietly converts it into a sales process, which changes what the client tells you. His range appears in the companion book. [Fee guidance for certified coaches pending.]

Will this take time away from my existing practice?

It redirects hours rather than adding them. Advisors who try to run legacy coaching as additional service volume on top of a full book tend to burn out within a year. Advisors who treat it as a different posture inside hours they were already spending tend to sustain it.

What if a family refuses to have the conversation?

Some will. Your job is to make the first attempt more likely to go well, not to compel it. A family that declines this year has often moved by the third time it is raised, and raising it a third time is only possible if the first two times were handled without pressure.

What if I open something I cannot handle?

Know your limit in advance and name it in the room when you reach it. Serious family conflict, addiction, mental illness, and estrangement are outside the scope of legacy coaching. Referring well is part of the work, not an admission of failure.

Can I use this with families I do not manage assets for?

Yes. The process is independent of the asset relationship, and Tom's own entry into this work came through a role that had no asset management in it at all.

Do I have to do the personal parts?

Part Three of every Exercise turns the session on you, and it is the section most likely to be skipped. It is also the reason the material lands with clients. A conversation about legacy that you have never had yourself is a conversation you will run at arm's length, and families can tell.

Where do I go after this guide?

The five deeper titles are Clarity, Alignment, Communication, Meetings, and Next Generation. Each takes one session of this guide and gives it six of its own. See Next Steps.

How does certification work?

Completing this guide counts as one module. Module completion is self-attested. The Certified Family Legacy Coach credential is a separate instrument issued after all modules and the Training Manual are complete. [Requirements, timeline, and fees pending.]

I secure permission and set confidentiality before engaging a next generation
I advocate for the next generation with the first generation
I am preparing rather than exposing the people who will follow me

Reading Your Score

Total the thirty items. The number matters less than where it is concentrated.

Section totals are the useful figure. Any section under 15 marks where your practice is thin, and it tells you which of the five deeper titles to take next.

Compare the two administrations. Movement of five points in a section over six weeks is substantial. Movement in Section One or Section Six is the strongest predictor that this will hold.

One caution. A high score on the first administration usually means the items were read as aspirations rather than as descriptions of last month. Take it again after Session 6 and be less generous.

TOP 10 COACHING EXERCISES

The ten instruments Tom uses most, with where each one is documented. Six of the ten appear in this guide. The remaining four belong to the deeper titles and are listed here so you know the full set.

1. The Reality Check The five realities of life, read aloud, ending on the only decision available. Removes the option of treating legacy as hypothetical. Session 1 of this guide. Workbook Legend, Session 1.

2. The Three Kinds of Legacies What you received, what you will pass on, what you are living out today. Run it on a client, then on yourself. Session 1 of this guide. Workbook Legend, Session 1.

3. The Goals Conversation The five areas of legacy, worked in order, personal first. Step one of Stage 1. Session 3 of this guide. Coaching Book, Chapter 7 and Appendix A. Family Book, Appendix B.

4. The FAMILY Review Structured inventory of what a family actually has: financial statement, assets under management, mandatory documents, insurance, liabilities and taxes, yearly cash flow. Step two of Stage 1. Coaching Book, Chapter 8 and Appendices C and D. Family Book, Appendix A.

5. The Family Legacy Goals and Gaps Analysis Sets what the family says it wants against what the current plan does. The deliverable of Stage 1. Session 4 of this guide. Coaching Book, Chapter 9 and Appendix E.

6. The Next Generation Interview Nine areas, one to one, with permission secured and confidentiality established first. Session 6 of this guide. Workbook Legend, Session 9. Coaching Book, Chapter 11. Family Book, Appendix F.

7. The Family Meeting Four types, four categories of issue, rules set before the agenda. Coaching Book, Chapter 12. Workbook Legend, Session 10. Full treatment in Meetings.

8. Developing Family Vision, Mission, and Values Every generation in the room, every voice counted. The process matters more than the statement. Coaching Book, Chapter 13.

9. Legacy Letters and Family Blessings Written by the client, in their own hand, saying what the documents cannot. Coaching Book, Appendix B. Family Book, Appendix D.

10. The Five Uses of Money Living expenses, giving, debt repayment, taxes, savings and investment, with the five money management principles underneath. Workbook Legend, Session 6. Coaching Book, Appendix G. Family Book, Appendix E.

IF YOU'RE DOING THIS WITH A GROUP

Optional. Nothing in the six sessions requires any of this, and if you are working alone you can skip the section entirely.

Team Agreement

Read this aloud at the first meeting and agree to it out loud rather than by silence.

  • What is said here stays here. This matters more in this group than in most, because we are discussing families we serve.
  • No client is identified. Use initials or a description. If someone in the room would recognize the family, change the detail.
  • Nobody is required to disclose personal financial information, their own or a client's.
  • We speak from our own practice rather than about anyone else's.
  • We do not fix each other. Advice is offered when asked for.
  • Silence is allowed. Nobody is drawn out.
  • We arrive having done the reading and the writing.
  • We finish on time.

A note on running it

There is no facilitator layer in this series and no leader's guide, by design. Rotate who opens. The opener's only job is to start the first question and keep time.

Ninety minutes divides reasonably as fifteen for Get Started, twenty for the video, twenty for Learn and Reflect, twenty for the Framework and Scenarios, and fifteen for Next Steps and Final Thoughts. The Exercise is done alone between meetings and is never discussed as a group.

NEXT STEPS: COURSES & CURRICULUM

This guide is the foundation. Each of the five titles below takes one session of this guide and gives it six of its own.

Clarity Helping families define what matters most across the five areas of legacy. Opens from Session 3.

Alignment Moving a family from individual clarity to shared understanding, and closing the gap between stated goals and existing plans. Opens from Session 4.

Communication Guiding the conversations families avoid, and building the trust that lets them happen again. Opens from Session 5.

Meetings Designing and running the family meeting, from the first one through the annual rhythm. Opens from Session 5 and Session 6.

Next Generation Preparing heirs to receive both the wealth and the wisdom. Opens from Session 6.

The Legacy Coaching Training Manual The full technical and process reference underneath all six titles. Required for the credential.

Recommended order. Foundation, Clarity, Alignment, Communication, Meetings, Next Generation. Take them in that order unless the Readiness Assessment points somewhere else, in which case start with your weakest section.

Family Legacy by Design, the family-facing companion, is the volume you place directly in a client's hands. It follows the same structure at a level built for the family rather than the advisor.

ABOUT FAMILY LEGACY BY DESIGN

Three words sit at the heart of this work: clarity, alignment, and communication.

We help families gain clarity around their goals in five areas of legacy, personal, family, financial, business, and charitable. We review the existing plan to see how well it matches that clarity. Then we help them communicate and discuss the plan with the next generation, which is done in family meetings.

All of it runs through the Multi-Generational Legacy Coaching Process, which helps families gain confidence about what they believe God wants them to do with their resources. It helps them quantify what they need for the future, what they wish to leave to their family, what they will do with their business, and how the remainder can be released to organizations that reflect their values.

The conviction underneath the work is that a lack of clarity around legacy keeps many families from making full use of what they have been entrusted with, and that many families do not know whether their existing plans match what they actually intend.

WHAT'S NEXT

You have finished the guide. The honest question now is not whether you found it useful.

It is which name you wrote down.

Somewhere in these six sessions you identified a family whose next generation you have never met, a conversation you have been leaving alone, an area of legacy you have never once raised with a client who has trusted you for a decade. You wrote it in the margin of an exercise nobody will ever read.

That is the whole of the assignment. One family. One conversation. This month.

The five deeper titles will be there when you want them, and the credential is worth having. Neither one is what makes this real. What makes it real is a phone call this week to a client who has no idea it is coming, asking a question nobody has asked him.

Do that first. The rest follows.
