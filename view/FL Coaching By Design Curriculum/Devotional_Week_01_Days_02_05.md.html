DAY 2

It's Being Formed Right Now

In How You Live, What You Prioritize

"Impress them on your children. Talk about them when you sit at home and when you walk along the road, when you lie down and when you get up." DEUTERONOMY 6:7

Most people think of legacy as something that happens later. That is the assumption I have spent thirty years working against, and it is worth being precise about why it is wrong, because the reason is mechanical rather than inspirational.

Legacy is being formed right now, in how you live, what you prioritize, and how you relate to others.

Take those three separately, because they are not the same thing and a family can be strong in one and empty in another.

How you live is the part people think of first, and it is the part most families feel reasonably good about. Honest dealing, hard work, showing up. It is also the part that is hardest to teach on purpose, because it is transmitted by observation rather than instruction and the observer is doing the interpreting.

What you prioritize is different, and it is the one I find most useful in a client meeting, because it is measurable. Priority is not what a person says matters. It is what gets the hour when there is only one hour. A calendar is a values statement that nobody edits for presentation, which is why it is more reliable than anything a client tells me in a first meeting.

How you relate to others is the third, and in family work it is the one that decides whether the first two survive. A person can live well and prioritize correctly and still pass on almost nothing, because the relationships were not built to carry it. What travels between generations travels along a relationship or it does not travel.

Now the part that makes this urgent rather than merely true.

If legacy is being formed right now, then it is being formed at the current rate whether or not anyone is managing it. Every year a family waits is not a neutral year. It is a year of formation that happened by default, and default formation is not nothing. It is a completed transfer of whatever was actually operating.

I have sat with families who genuinely believed they were in a holding pattern. The business was consuming everything, the children were young or busy or far away, and the plan was to get to the family work once things settled. What they did not see was that the holding pattern was itself the curriculum. The children were learning something specific during those years, which was that the business comes first and the family conversation waits for a season that has not arrived in a decade.

That is not a moral failure and I do not present it to clients as one. It is a sequencing error, and sequencing errors are correctable.

The correction is smaller than people expect. It does not require a retreat or a family constitution or a change in how anyone spends their week. It requires that the thing already being transmitted gets named out loud, once, to the people receiving it. A father who says to his adult son, here is why I built this and here is what I hope it does, has just converted twenty years of ambient observation into something the son can actually hold.

There is a principle in Deuteronomy 6. The instruction is to talk about these things when you sit at home and when you walk along the road, when you lie down and when you get up.

I notice what is not in that verse. There is no occasion in it. No annual meeting, no setting, no moment set aside. The instruction locates the whole thing inside ordinary movement through a day, which is the same place the formation is already happening.

Most families are waiting for the right setting to have these conversations. The verse suggests there is not one, and thirty years of watching families supports that. The families who do this well are not the ones who found a better venue. They are the ones who stopped waiting for a venue.

What is being formed in the families you serve is being formed this month, at the rate it has always been forming, and none of it is waiting for your engagement letter.

REFLECT

1. Of the three, how you live, what you prioritize, and how you relate to others, which one is strongest in the families you serve, and which one is being neglected?

2. A calendar is a values statement nobody edits for presentation. What would a client's calendar tell you that his goals conversation would not?

3. Where have you watched a family treat a delay as neutral when it was actually teaching something?

4. What are you currently waiting for the right setting to say, and to whom?

DAY 3

Not What Was Intended

It's What Was Lived

"We loved you so much that we were delighted to share with you not only the gospel of God but our lives as well." 1 THESSALONIANS 2:8

When I got started in this industry I thought I understood my job. Help a client get clear on what he wanted to do with what he had been entrusted with. Make sure the documents matched those intentions. Communicate the plan to the people who would inherit it. If the strategy was sound and the paperwork was tight, I had done good work.

It took me years to notice the cracks, and longer to admit what they meant.

Families with what looked like airtight plans were coming apart anyway. Siblings stopped speaking. A business sold within months of a transition that everyone had prepared for. Heirs received more than they had any framework for holding, and instead of freedom they found conflict, or isolation, or a quiet resentment that never surfaced and never went away.

For a while I assumed the problem was with the families. That is the comfortable conclusion and it is available to any advisor who wants it. Eventually I had to admit it was also with us.

Here is what I had missed. Often, what gets passed on most clearly is not what was intended. It's what was lived.

There are two records in every family. One is documented. It has been drafted, reviewed, signed, and stored, and it represents the client at his most deliberate. The other record is not written anywhere. It is the accumulated observation of the people who lived alongside him, and it was compiled without his participation or consent.

Only one of those records is being read.

I want to be careful here, because this sounds like an accusation and it is not. The gap between intended and lived is not usually hypocrisy. It is far more often a father who never said the thing out loud because he assumed it was obvious. He built the business to give his family security. He did not say that, because to him it did not need saying. His son watched him work through two Christmases and concluded that the business mattered more than the family, which is a completely reasonable reading of the available evidence.

Nobody lied. Nobody failed. The intended record simply never got published, and in its absence the observed record was all anyone had.

This is why I stopped treating communication as the last step. In the model I was trained in, you clarify, you plan, you document, and then you communicate the plan. Communication sits at the end, after the real work. What I now believe is that the communication is not the delivery of the work. It is the mechanism by which the work becomes real to anyone other than the person who did it.

There is a practical version of this you can use tomorrow. When a client tells you what he wants his children to understand, ask him when he last said it to them in those words. Not whether they know. Whether he said it. The answer is almost always that he has not, and the gap between what he assumed was obvious and what has actually been stated is the entire working space of this profession.

There is a principle in 1 Thessalonians 2. The writer describes sharing not only a message but his own life alongside it.

That pairing is the whole thing. A message without a life is unconvincing. A life without a message is uninterpretable, and uninterpretable is the more common failure in the families I work with. These are people of real integrity whose children could not tell you what any of it was for.

I do not think most advisors need to become better communicators. I think we need to stop treating the client's own communication as something that happens after we are finished.

The plan was never the legacy. It was the record of an intention that somebody still has to say out loud.

DAY 4

From the Outside, Everything Looked Strong

The Deeper Elements Hadn't Been Clearly Passed On

"A person may labor with wisdom, knowledge and skill, and then they must leave all they own to another who has not toiled for it." ECCLESIASTES 2:21

I worked with a family where the parents had done very well financially. They had built significant resources over decades. The structures were in place, the documents were current, and from the outside everything looked strong.

Then we spent time with the next generation, and there was a different picture.

There wasn't a clear sense of shared values. There wasn't a strong connection around purpose. And there was some uncertainty about how to think about what had been built. The resources were there, but the deeper elements of legacy hadn't been clearly passed on.

I have told that story many times and I want to be careful about what it is and is not.

It is not a story about a bad family. These were people any advisor would be glad to serve, and the parents had done nothing that would look like a mistake in any file review. If you had graded that engagement on the criteria our profession actually uses, it would have graded well. That is the point of the story rather than an aside to it.

It is also not a story about money ruining children. I hear that reading offered constantly and I think it is lazy. The children in that family were not spoiled. They were uninformed, which is a different condition with a different remedy.

What was actually happening was that a set of questions had formed and nobody had answered them. What is this for. What are we supposed to do with it. How do we think about it. Those are reasonable questions from anyone standing in front of something substantial that somebody else built. They had never been asked out loud, and they had never been answered, and in the absence of answers the children were quietly writing their own.

That is the mechanism that makes this urgent. An unanswered question does not stay open. It closes on its own, using whatever material happens to be available, and what is available is usually observation plus guesswork. Once a child has settled on his own explanation of what the money means, that explanation is very hard to displace, and it will shape how he handles the money long before anyone consults him about it.

So we shifted the conversation. Instead of starting with assets, we started with purpose. What matters most. What do you want your family to be known for. What values have shaped your life.

Those questions did not produce a document and they did not resolve anything in one meeting. What they did was slower and more useful. Over time the next generation started to see the bigger picture, not just what existed, but why it existed.

I want to name the professional cost of missing this, because I do not think advisors weigh it accurately. In that family, every professional in the room was working the technical side and doing it competently. Not one of them had looked at the other side, and the other side was where the failure was going to originate. When that transition eventually came, whatever went wrong was going to look like a family problem, and the advisors were going to describe it that way, and they would have been wrong.

There is a principle in Ecclesiastes 2. A person may labor with wisdom, knowledge and skill, and then leave all he owns to another who has not toiled for it.

The complaint in that passage is not about losing the wealth. It is about not knowing who will hold it or how. That anxiety is real and it sits underneath a great many client relationships without ever being named, and a client will rarely raise it with you because raising it sounds like a criticism of his own children.

You can name it for him. Most of them are relieved when someone does.

The file looked complete. It was missing the only part that had to travel through a person.

DAY 5

Something Is Always Being Passed On

What Else Are We Passing On?

"One generation commends your works to another; they tell of your mighty acts." PSALM 145:4

You have to step back and ask, what are we actually passing on. Because whether it's intentional or not, something is always being passed on.

I sat with a client worth roughly forty-five million, most of it in real estate, with four married children. His documents left five hundred thousand to each child and the rest to charity. I asked him whether the children knew. They did not. He had told them only that they should not expect anything.

So we held a meeting. His wife, it turned out, had never agreed with the number, and he moved it to a million each. Ten people were in the room, and he had one vote out of ten rather than running it. He told them what he intended, that the gifts would come over the next few years rather than at his death, and that the bulk of the estate was going to charity.

The part of that meeting I care about is not the tax outcome. It is what did not happen afterward.

Had he died with none of it said, four children would have received a number and built a story around it. Did he not trust us. What did he think we would do with it. Was there something he never told us. I call those vain imaginations, the explanations people construct when they have a fact and no context, and families produce them constantly. Once one takes hold it is very hard to dislodge, and the person who could have corrected it is not available.

The number was going to be passed on either way. What he chose was whether the meaning went with it.

That is the whole distinction and it applies well below forty-five million. A modest estate transmits an explanation just as reliably as a large one. So does a business that goes to one child and not another. So does a father who says nothing, which is itself a message and usually a louder one than he intends.

I will put it more plainly for the advisors reading this. There will be a family meeting. The only question is whether your client is going to be in it. People gather when someone dies and they talk about the stuff, and the difference between the families who handle that well and the families who do not is almost entirely whether the meeting happened once while the person was alive.

Start by asking what else are we passing on. Not just assets, but attitudes, values, and patterns.

Attitudes are the posture toward money that a child absorbs without instruction. Whether it is a source of anxiety or of freedom. Whether it is discussed or avoided.

Values are the commitments that decisions are supposed to reflect, which children can usually name if the family has said them out loud and cannot name if it has not.

Patterns are the ones nobody tracks. How this family handles conflict. Whether the hard conversation gets had or postponed. Who is allowed to disagree. Those transfer with more reliability than anything in the estate plan, because they were demonstrated a thousand times.

There is a principle in Psalm 145. One generation commends your works to another; they tell of your mighty acts.

Both of those verbs are active. Commends and tell. Nothing in that verse happens by proximity, and the transmission it describes requires someone to open their mouth.

That is what the man with the real estate did in that meeting. Not the gift. The telling.

Something is moving to the next generation this year in every family on your list. The only open question is whether anyone is going to say what it means.

REFLECT

1. Which of your clients has a number their children have never been told, and what story will those children build if it is never explained?

2. Tom says there will be a family meeting either way. What changes for a client when you put it to him in those terms?

3. Of attitudes, values, and patterns, which one transfers most reliably in the families you serve, and which one gets discussed least?

4. Both verbs in Psalm 145 are active. Where in your process does anyone actually tell rather than report?
