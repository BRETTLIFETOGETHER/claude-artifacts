SESSION 2

Your Personal Legacy

What Do You Want to Be Remembered For

INTRODUCTION

KEY VERSE

"For we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand."

EPHESIANS 2:10

It surprises people that a series about family legacy starts with one person. But a family cannot get clear together if the people in it have never gotten clear themselves. You can put five people around the same table, ask them the same question, and get five answers that have nothing to do with each other, because each of them is in a completely different place internally and nobody has ever noticed.

So this session is about you. Your values, your purpose, your priorities, and the spiritual life underneath all of it. Not because you matter more than the family, but because you are the one part of this you can actually work on today without anyone else's permission.

Notice what the verse says. Prepared beforehand. Which means the thing you are trying to get clear about was already decided about you. You are finding it, not inventing it.

OPENING STORY

The Four-Second Answer

He was in his late fifties and had been successful at nearly everything he had put his hand to. Tom asked him what he wanted to be remembered for.

He answered in about four seconds. It was a good answer. Solid, decent, the kind of thing you would be glad to hear a man say. And it was completely prepared. He had said it before, probably many times, and had never once gone underneath it.

Tom said, that is good, tell me more. And then he waited.

What came out on the third pass was something much more specific and much more personal, and by his own admission he had never said it to anyone. Not his wife. Not his children. It had been sitting there for thirty years, fully formed, and nobody had asked a second time.

GET STARTED

Take these before you begin. With your spouse or family, take them together. On your own, take them with a pen.

The reminder from Session 1 still stands. You are never required to say a number out loud, and nothing in a private exercise has to be shared. Some of what comes up here will be more personal than last week. Go at the pace that is right for the people in the room.

1. What is one thing about you that the people closest to you would probably get wrong? With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write it down.

2. When was the last time somebody asked you a question about your life that you had to think about? With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write it down.

BEFORE YOU BRING THE FAMILY IN

A short reminder. This session is the one most likely to surface something you have not said to your spouse.

If that happens, say it there first, before it goes anywhere else.

LEARN

Watch the Session 2 teaching video.

Capture what you heard, then put it against your own life. Each prompt names what Tom and Brett covered. Your job is the second half.

Tom says the genius of this work is not in the answers, it is in the question asking, and that most people have never been asked so they have never had to think it through.

What question about your own life have you never been asked?

Tom says do not accept the first answer, because the first answer is the one people have already prepared. The second or third question is where you find out what is actually going on.

Take your own answer to what matters most and go one layer under it. What is there?

Tom asks what has shaped you, what you are good at, what you love, and what you believe God has entrusted to you.

Answer the one of those four you have the least ready answer for.

Brett names the gap between what we say matters and how we actually spend our time and money, and Tom says that gap is almost never a character problem. It is a clarity problem.

Where is your gap, and how long has it been there?

Tom asks people directly how much time they spend with God in a week, what they are reading, and whether anyone in their life actually knows how they are doing.

Answer all three, honestly, and only for yourself.

REFLECT

With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write your answers. There are more here than you need. Take the ones that catch.

1. What has shaped you most, and does anyone in your family know that?

2. What are you good at, and what do you love? They are not always the same thing. Which one has been driving your decisions?

3. What do you believe God has entrusted to you specifically, as opposed to what you happen to have?

4. If you gave a four-second answer to what you want to be remembered for, what would it be? Now go underneath it.

5. Where is the gap between what you say matters and where last year actually went?

6. Who in your life knows how you are actually doing, spiritually? If nobody does, what would it take to change that?

7. Answer this one about someone else in your family. What do you think they would say they were made for, and have you ever asked?

"Don't accept the first answer. The first answer is almost never the real one."

TOM CONWAY

THE FRAMEWORK

The Personal Legacy Questions

Tom does not run a personality assessment. He asks questions and then he asks again. These are the ones he keeps coming back to.

  • What has shaped you? The things that made you who you are. Some are wonderful and some are hard, and both of them count.

  • What are you good at? Not what you are known for. What you are actually good at.

  • What do you love? The thing that gives you energy rather than the thing that earns you approval.

  • What has God entrusted to you? Not what you own. What you have been made responsible for.

  • What do you want said? The two or three sentences you would want spoken about your life, by people who knew you well.

Take the one you answered fastest. That is probably the one you have prepared. Go underneath it.

CASE STUDIES

Two people. Read each one, answer what is going on, then answer the turn.

CASE STUDY ONE. A man in his late fifties, successful at nearly everything. Asked what he wants to be remembered for, he gives a good answer in about four seconds, and you can tell he is repeating something.

What is the four seconds telling you?

Now the turn. What is your prepared answer, and what is underneath it?

CASE STUDY TWO. A woman who has spent thirty years pouring into her family. Asked about her personal legacy, she cannot answer. Not because she has not thought about it, but because she is not sure she is allowed to have one that is separate from everybody else's.

What does she need to hear?

Now the turn. Is there a part of what you were made for that you have set aside because it seemed like it belonged to somebody else?

See the appendix for what Tom has seen in situations like these.

THE TOOL

The Goals Conversation, Personal Legacy

Tom uses this at the front of every engagement, and it is built out of open questions rather than a form.

Set aside forty-five minutes. Not fifteen. Answer these in writing, in order, and do not move to the next one until the previous one has more than one sentence under it.

  • What has shaped my life most, for better and for worse?
  • What am I actually good at?
  • What do I love doing, regardless of whether I am good at it?
  • What has God entrusted to me that I am responsible for?
  • What would I want said about me by the people who know me best?
  • What would have to change in the next year for that to become more true?

Then do the part most people skip. Read your answers to one person.

EXERCISE

Private. Complete this alone.

Part One. Your own five areas. Return to your Session 1 ratings. Look only at the Personal row. Write what you would want said about you in that area, in full sentences rather than words.

Part Two. Your marriage. Write what you believe your spouse would say they were made for. Then circle it if you are guessing. If you are not married, use the person who knows you best.

Part Three. The eulogy. Write the two or three sentences you would want spoken about your life. Not your accomplishments. What kind of person you were, and what you gave yourself to.

Then answer this. What in your current calendar and current spending would have to change for those sentences to be true?

Self-check. Mark the ones that are true today.

  • I could answer all five personal legacy questions without stalling.
  • I know the difference between my prepared answer and my real one.
  • I have named the gap between what I say matters and how I live.
  • Somebody knows how I am actually doing spiritually.
  • One person has heard what I want said about my life.

FOR THE NEXT GENERATION

These questions are not for the second half of life. The only difference between asking them at thirty and asking them at sixty is thirty years.

You are allowed to want something specific. You are allowed to want something different from what your family built. What you are not allowed to do, if you want this to go well, is leave it unsaid and hope somebody guesses.

And you are allowed to not know yet. Trying something and finding out it is not for you is not a failure. That is how most people find the thing.

Answer the five personal legacy questions on yourself. Then ask one parent or grandparent to answer them, and listen to what you have never heard.

TOGETHER

Read your eulogy sentences to one person before the next session.

Not to get feedback. Just so they exist outside your own head.

CIRCLES OF INFLUENCE

Two names.

Someone who would tell you the truth about yourself. Not the person who agrees with you. The one who has earned the right to say something you would not enjoy hearing.

Someone whose own clarity you admire. Ask them how they got it. Most people have never been asked and will tell you more than you expect.

NEXT STEPS

  • [ ] Answer the six Goals Conversation questions in writing.
  • [ ] Write the eulogy sentences.
  • [ ] Read them to one person.
  • [ ] Name the gap between what you say matters and where last year went.

FAMILY CONVERSATION

Four questions for one person in your family. Start with the one you could actually ask.

1. What do you think you were made for?

2. What has shaped you most, and have I ever heard the whole story?

3. What would you want said about you?

4. Is there anything you have wanted to do that you never told anyone about?

GOING DEEPER

Ephesians 2:10. We are His workmanship, created for good works, prepared beforehand.

What does prepared beforehand change about how you approach this question? If the works were prepared, what is your job?

Psalm 139:23-24. Search me, God, and know my heart. Test me and know my anxious thoughts.

David asks to be searched. What would you rather not have found? What would it mean to invite it anyway?

Proverbs 4:23. Watch over your heart with all diligence, for from it flow the springs of life.

What is currently shaping your heart that you did not choose?

Five readings from Family Legacy by Design, chapter six, one per day.

Day 1. What a personal legacy is.

Day 2. Values, purpose, and priorities.

Day 3. Spiritual legacy.

Day 4. Calling and gifting.

Day 5. The gap between stated values and daily life.

FINAL THOUGHTS

What did you write down this week that you have never said out loud?

Who needs to hear it, and when?

You are not going to reach the end of your life and wish you had been less clear. The work of this session is not to produce a better answer. It is to stop giving the prepared one.

SESSION 3

Your Family Legacy

Defining What Your Family Stands For

INTRODUCTION

KEY VERSE

"Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brothers to dwell together in unity!"

PSALM 133:1

Notice what the verse does not say. It does not say easy.

Last session was about one person. This one brings people back together, and now you have got different perspectives in the room. Different priorities. Different histories. Different ways of thinking about the future. None of that is wrong, and all of it is normal. But if it is never brought together, a family starts to drift, and drift is never dramatic. It is subtle and it is significant over time.

Tom wants two things for every family he works with. Family harmony, and the wise transfer of both wealth and wisdom. This session is about the first one, and about the word that makes it possible. Shared. Not agreed. Shared.

OPENING STORY

They Would Have Told You They Were Close

They were close, and they were not pretending. Holidays, birthdays, a family group chat that actually got used. Three adult children who genuinely liked each other. By any measure available from the outside, this family was doing well.

Tom asked each of them separately what the family stood for.

The father talked about hard work and providing. The mother talked about faith. One daughter talked about being there for each other no matter what. The son talked about not making a big deal out of things. The son-in-law, who had been in the family for nine years, said he had honestly never thought about it and was not sure it was his place to say.

Every one of those answers was true. None of them was wrong. And not one person in that family had any idea the others would have said something different.

GET STARTED

Take these together where you can.

The reminder stands. Nobody has to say a number, nothing private has to be shared, and this session in particular may surface things people have thought for years and never said. That is the point, and it is also why the pace matters.

1. If your family had a motto that nobody wrote down but everybody follows, what would it be? With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write it down.

2. Who is in your family? Say the whole list out loud, including the people who married in. With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write it down.

BEFORE YOU BRING THE FAMILY IN

A short reminder. Mom's view of a child and Dad's view of the same child are often different, and most couples have a sense of that without ever having said it.

Say it to each other before this session goes any further.

LEARN

Watch the Session 3 teaching video.

Tom says the two things he wants for every family are family harmony and wise wealth transfer, and that your family is the most important thing. Money is great. It is not the most important thing.

If you had to choose which of those two your family is currently better at, which is it?

Tom says you have to know the family. Who the children are, who the grandchildren are, who the parents are if they are living, and who the outside people are who affect the family.

Name one person who affects your family that you would not have listed.

Tom asks how each person is doing spiritually, relationally, vocationally, and financially, and whether they carry debt.

Take the four areas and one person in your family. Which of the four could you not answer with confidence?

Brett names the word underneath all of it. Shared. Without it, everyone is operating off their own understanding, and it feels like alignment right up until a decision has to be made.

Where has your family assumed something was shared that turned out not to be?

Tom says when your children marry, that person brings a whole other set of family values into your family, and you have to understand what they brought.

What did the people who married into your family bring, and has anyone ever asked them?

REFLECT

With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write your answers.

1. What has your family legacy been so far? Not what you want it to be. What it has actually been.

2. How is each person in your family really doing? Take them one at a time.

3. Where is there conflict in your family that has not been dealt with?

4. One person wants to protect what you have. Another wants to take more risk. Another wants time together. Which is which in your family, and has that ever been named?

5. What did the in-laws bring, and how well do you actually know it?

6. What do you want this family to be known for?

7. Answer this one about someone else. Who in your family would say they are on the outside of it, and would you have guessed?

"Your family is the most important thing. Your money is great. It's great to have money. But that's not the most important thing."

TOM CONWAY

THE FRAMEWORK

Knowing Your Family

Tom asks about four areas for each person, one at a time, and he does it before he does anything else. Not to evaluate anyone. To understand.

  • Spiritually. Where is this person actually, not where do you hope they are.

  • Relationally. Who are they close to, who are they distant from, and is there anything unresolved.

  • Vocationally. What are they doing, do they like it, and are they where they want to be.

  • Financially. How are they managing, and do they carry debt.

Then two more that sit across the whole family. What do we believe as a family? And what do we want to be known for?

Take one person in your family and answer all four. Then circle the ones where you are guessing.

CASE STUDIES

CASE STUDY ONE. A family that would tell you they are close, and they are. Holidays, birthdays, real affection. Ask each person what the family stands for and you get very different answers, and nobody knows it.

What is the risk in that family?

Now the turn. If you asked everyone in your family the same question this week, how many different answers would you get?

CASE STUDY TWO. A father whose daughter is getting serious about someone. It is heading toward a proposal. He is happy for her and also has a knot in his stomach, and he has not asked a single real question because he does not want to be the man who interfered.

What is he risking by staying quiet?

Now the turn. What is the question you have not asked because you did not want to be the one who asked it?

See the appendix for what Tom has seen in families like these.

THE TOOL

The Family Health Survey

Tom uses this to find out how a family is actually doing rather than how it appears to be doing. It is not a test and there is no score.

Take each person in your family, one at a time, and answer the four areas. Spiritually. Relationally. Vocationally. Financially.

Then answer two questions about the family as a whole.

  • Is there anyone in this family who has something unresolved with someone else?
  • Is there anyone who would say they are on the outside of it?

Circle every answer you are guessing at. Those are not failures. They are the list of conversations you have not had yet.

Do this privately first. It is not a family exercise until you have done it alone.

EXERCISE

Private. Complete this alone.

Part One. Your family, person by person.

Person Spiritually Relationally Vocationally Financially

Circle every cell where you are guessing.

Part Two. Your marriage. Write the one thing you and your spouse see differently about one of your children. Then write whether you have ever said it to each other in those words.

Part Three. What this family stands for. Write three to six values you believe your family actually lives by. Not aspires to. Lives by.

Then write the one you wish were on the list and is not.

Self-check. Mark the ones that are true today.

  • I could name everyone in my family, including the people who married in.
  • I know how each of them is doing in at least three of the four areas.
  • I know where the unresolved thing is.
  • I could say what this family stands for in one sentence.
  • Somebody else in my family would say the same sentence.

FOR THE NEXT GENERATION

Your parents may have a picture of you in their head that is a few years out of date. That is not a criticism of them. It is what happens. And the only person who can update it is you.

You are also allowed to want to know what this family is actually about. Not what it has. What it is for. That is a fair question and you are allowed to ask it out loud.

And if you married into this family, your values came with you. The family is better when they are on the table rather than politely kept to yourself.

TOGETHER

Ask each person in your family how they think the family is actually doing.

Not what needs fixing. Just how they would describe it. Then listen without correcting anybody.

CIRCLES OF INFLUENCE

Two names.

The person in your family who has said the least. Not the difficult one. The quiet one. What would it take to hear from them.

A family a few years ahead of yours that has clearly done something right. Ask them what they did on purpose.

NEXT STEPS

  • [ ] Complete the Family Health Survey privately.
  • [ ] Circle every answer you are guessing at.
  • [ ] Write the three to six values your family actually lives by.
  • [ ] Ask one person how they think the family is doing.

FAMILY CONVERSATION

1. How do you think this family is doing?

2. What do you think we stand for?

3. Is there anyone in this family you have something unresolved with?

4. What do you wish we talked about that we never do?

GOING DEEPER

Psalm 133:1. How good and how pleasant it is for brothers to dwell together in unity.

Unity is described as good and pleasant, not as easy or automatic. What has unity in your family actually cost, and who paid it?

Genesis 17:7. God's covenant is with Abraham and his descendants after him, throughout their generations.

The promise has a horizon longer than one lifetime. How would your decisions change if you were planning on that horizon?

Ephesians 4:2-3. With all humility and gentleness, with patience, showing tolerance for one another in love.

Which of those four is hardest in your family, and with whom?

THIS WEEK'S READINGS

Five readings from Family Legacy by Design, chapters two and five.

Day 1. The purpose of family.

Day 2. Independence and interdependence.

Day 3. Family legacy within the five areas.

Day 4. Knowing your family.

Day 5. What a family stands for.

FINAL THOUGHTS

What did you learn about your own family this week that you did not know on Monday?

Who is the person you need to ask something?

Take your family from where it actually is, not from where you wish it were. Most families are healthier than they think. They have just never talked about it.

SESSION 4

Your Financial Legacy

A Blessing and Not a Curse

INTRODUCTION

KEY VERSE

"A good man leaves an inheritance to his children's children."

PROVERBS 13:22

Two generations out. Which means the question was never just about your kids.

This is where things get more sensitive for most families, and there is no shame in that. Even in families that are close, even in families that communicate well, there are assumptions. People think they understand what exists and they really do not. What you end up with is a gap between what is real and what is understood, and that gap does not close on its own.

The question underneath this whole session is not how much. It is what for. What do you want this to accomplish in the life of each person who receives it? Because if you do not know what you hope it will do, it raises the real question of why you are leaving more, or less, than you actually could.

OPENING STORY

We Wish We Had Known More

Brett knows a family whose relationships have almost been blown apart, and what they told him is that it was the extra resources that did it.

Not a business failure. Not a scandal. The complexity of what they had built challenged their relationships with their children. In one case there is now a lawsuit with one of them. In another they are not in relationship with the children or the grandchildren at all, and it is heartbreaking to them.

Brett asked whether they would be better off without it.

They said they would not go that far. But they said this. We wish we had known more of the kinds of things you are talking about.

GET STARTED

Take these before you begin.

This session touches money, and money touches everything. You are never required to say a number out loud, to anyone, at any point. If you are doing this with family in the room, agree on that before the first question.

1. What did you learn about money from the generation before you, on purpose or otherwise? With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write it down.

2. What do you assume everyone in your family already knows about your finances? With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write it down.

BEFORE YOU BRING THE FAMILY IN

A short reminder, and this one matters more than usual.

Frequently one spouse does not have the full picture. Not because anything was hidden, but because that was the arrangement and it worked. Both of you looking at the same information, at the same time, is the whole first step.

LEARN

Watch the Session 4 teaching video.

Tom says most families do not have the full picture of what they have. They know parts of it. Nobody has ever put it on one page.

Could you put it on one page today? Could your spouse?

Tom says a significant transfer is coming, and the question is not what is being transferred but what it will do to the people receiving it.

What do you think it will do to each person who receives something from you?

Tom asks what amount of inheritance would be a blessing and not a curse, and says that more is not always better.

Is there a number above which this stops helping the people you love? Have you ever asked yourself that?

Tom says you love your children equally, but that does not necessarily mean you treat them the same. And that if you are going to treat them differently, you have to communicate that you are, and why.

Are you planning to treat anyone differently? Does that person know?

Tom says if you can afford to, give some of it now and watch how they steward it, because that will tell you more than guessing will.

What could you give now, at any size, that would tell you something?

REFLECT

With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write your answers.

1. What do you actually have? Not roughly. Specifically.

2. Does your spouse know? Does anyone else?

3. What do you want this to accomplish in the life of each person who receives it?

4. Is there anyone who is not ready? What would ready look like?

5. Are you planning to treat anyone differently, and have you communicated why?

6. What is the timing? All at once, in stages, or while you are living?

7. Answer this one about someone else. What do you think each of your children assumes they are getting, and where did that assumption come from?

"You love your children equally, but that doesn't necessarily mean you treat them the same. Because they're not the same."

TOM CONWAY

THE FRAMEWORK

Love Equally, Treat Uniquely

This is Tom's line and it does more work than any other sentence in this session.

  • Equal is not the same as wise. Equal feels fair because it requires no judgment. You divide by the number of children and nobody can accuse you of anything. Wise requires you to know your children.

  • Every child is different. Some have needs the others do not. Some are ready and some are not. Some built something and some are still finding it.

  • Different treatment requires communication. This is the part families skip. If you are going to do something unequal, the unfairness is not the difference. The unfairness is finding out about it at the reading of a will.

  • Readiness, timing, and structure are three separate questions. How ready is this person. When should it arrive. And what shape should it take when it does.

Which of your children would be helped by receiving something differently, and what would it cost you to say so out loud?

CASE STUDIES

CASE STUDY ONE. A couple who has decided. They know exactly what each child is getting and why. It is thoughtful, generous, and right for their family. They have never told the children a word of it.

What happens in that family the day it is read?

Now the turn. What have you decided that nobody else knows?

CASE STUDY TWO. Three children. Two are steady, capable, doing well. The third has struggled for years and there is real reason to worry. The parents are stuck, because equal does not feel wise and unequal does not feel loving.

What are they actually stuck on?

Now the turn. Is there someone in your family that this describes, and what have you been doing with the question so far?

See the appendix for what Tom has seen in families like these.

THE TOOL

Blessing or Curse

One line per person. That is the whole tool, and it is harder than it sounds.

For each person who will receive something from you, write one sentence answering this. What do I hope this does in their life?

Not the amount. Not the mechanism. What it is for.

Person What I hope this does in their life

Then read what you wrote and ask whether the amount, the timing, and the structure you currently have in mind would actually produce it.

Most families discover the answer is no, and that the fix is not more money. It is a different shape, a different time, or a conversation that has not happened.

EXERCISE

Private. Complete this alone.

Part One. What you have. Write down what you own and what you owe. Not to the dollar. Closely enough that the number in your head is the real one.

Part Two. Your marriage. Write what you believe your spouse would put on that same page. Then plan the evening where you compare the two lists.

Part Three. Readiness. For each person who will receive something, answer this in one word. Ready, nearly, or not yet. Then for anyone who is not ready, write the one thing that would move them.

Self-check. Mark the ones that are true today.

  • I know what we actually have.
  • My spouse knows.
  • I know what I hope it does in each person's life.
  • If I am treating anyone differently, they know and they know why.
  • I have thought about timing, not just amount.

FOR THE NEXT GENERATION

You may have a number in your head. Most people do, even if they would never say it, and that number came from somewhere other than your parents, because your parents have probably never told you.

The most useful thing you can do is let it go and ask a better question. Not what am I getting. What is this for.

And ask a second one. What would I need to know in order to handle this well, and who could teach me? Ask that out loud, before anything arrives. It will change how your family thinks about the whole thing, and it will change how they think about you.

TOGETHER

Complete the Blessing or Curse sheet, then read one line of it to the person it is about.

One line. One person.

CIRCLES OF INFLUENCE

Two names.

Someone who has done this well. A family a generation ahead who transferred something and stayed close. Ask them what they did on purpose.

Someone who could help you see it. If you own a business, hold property in more than one place, or could not explain your own structure out loud, this is where an outside set of eyes earns its money. Not to decide anything. To see it accurately.

GOING DEEPER

Proverbs 13:22. A good man leaves an inheritance to his children's children.

Two generations. What would change if you planned for the grandchildren rather than the children?

He was not condemned for the harvest. What exactly was the problem?

Ecclesiastes 2:18-19. Solomon hated his labor because he had to leave it to someone who came after him, and he did not know whether that person would be wise or a fool.

Is that fear in your family? Is it named?

Who has not been told something they will eventually find out?

THE FRAMEWORK

Continuity and Succession

Two different questions. Most families have only ever thought about one.

  • Continuity is tomorrow. If you are unavailable for thirty days, who decides? Who signs? Who do the employees go to? This is not a long-term question and it has a short-term answer, and most owners have never written it down.

  • Succession is eventually. Who leads it next. And underneath that, is that person prepared, and if not, what are you doing between now and then to get them ready.

  • Readiness is developed, not inherited. If the next person is not ready, they may not be for five years. That is a plan, not a verdict.
