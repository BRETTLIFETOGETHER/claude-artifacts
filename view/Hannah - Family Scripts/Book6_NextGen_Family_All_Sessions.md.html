🎬 SESSION 1 — FROM HEIRS TO STEWARDS

From Receiving to Being Responsible

Source: filmed Next Gen S1; Family Book ch. 11

BRETT: CENTER

Well, welcome to Family Legacy by Design.

My name is Brett, and this is the one and only Tom Conway. The man who has been in the trenches with more families navigating their legacy than probably anybody over the last thirty years, and in a way that tries to bring out the heart and the hope of a family so it can flourish fully. Not in one or two areas. Fully.

Tom, thank you for your life, your leadership, and the legacy God has already built through you.

We are kicking off a brand new series on preparing the next generation. And in this first session, here it is. Moving from just being heirs, or receivers, to being stewards.

And one thing before we begin. This book is for two people at once. If you are the parent or the grandparent, this is for you. And if you are in the next generation, this is for you too, and not as a subject. As a reader. Some of what we say is going to be said directly to you.

BRETT: TURN

So Tom, why has this captured your heart so much?

TOM: CENTER

Honestly, because most of the families I serve are older. Which means the future of their legacy is in the next generation.

And a lot of times the oldest generation is not listening to the younger generation, or is not trusting them.

We have talked about the term adult children. And it is really an oxymoron. Are they adults, or are they children? The more you can get a family to treat their kids as adults and not as children, the more progress you are going to make.

So my passion is helping the next generation be prepared to take the mantle and then grow it even more. Because if they just manage what was handed to them, it is not going to grow much, and probably it dissipates over time.

BRETT: CENTER

And Tom, you have a conviction about this generation that I want people to hear, because it is not the common view.

TOM: CENTER

I have no doubt the next generation is going to have a greater impact than the first generation.

People talk about the wonderful baby boomers. Well, that is because they have not seen the next one coming along. The children those baby boomers raised.

In many cases these kids care a lot more about people than their parents did. Their parents are more transactional. The kids are more relational.

And I think my own kids will probably have a greater impact on the world than I will, because I did not start until I was twenty. I became a Christian at twenty. They started coming out of the box. So they have a lot more time to do good things.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, let's name the difference plainly. Heir and steward.

TOM: CENTER

An heir receives. A steward is responsible.

And the Bible does not call us heirs. It calls us stewards.

What is being transferred is not just assets. It is the things that go along with the money. Responsibility. Multiplication. The impact you could have with those resources if they were given to you and you managed them well.

There is a principle that from everyone who has been given much, much will be required. So the more you get, the more responsible you are.

It is not pressure. It is stewardship.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, why does this break down?

TOM: CENTER

There are reasons legacy does not transfer well.

About sixty percent of the time it breaks down because of a lack of communication and trust. About twenty-five percent of the time it is because the heirs are unprepared.

So if you can knock out those two things, you have got about an eighty-five percent chance of passing it from one generation to the next.

And notice what is not on that list. It is not the plan. It is not the taxes. It is not the investments. It is the two things nobody schedules.

Scripture. 1 Corinthians 4:2. It is required of stewards that one be found trustworthy. Required. Not hoped for.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, let me give you a scenario.

SCENARIO ONE. A father with a capable adult son. The son has proven himself. And the father keeps saying, not yet. He is not ready. And what is actually happening is the father is not ready.

BRETT: TURN

TOM: CENTER

[TOM: the son in private equity who was about to walk away, and the call to his father, and what the father finally did. Keep the outcome. Lose the valuation.]

It was not that the boy was not ready. It was that the father was not ready.

And this happens everywhere. How many companies and ministries have you seen just peter out because the founder was afraid to hand it over? So he stays longer than he should. He does not develop anybody underneath him, because he does not want them taking his job.

[TOM: the ministry leader who hired three or four people to take over and never let any of them.]

He still had an office there. He still needed to make the last decision.

BRETT: CENTER

Let me give you another version of this.

SCENARIO TWO. A family where the parents have built something substantial and the children genuinely do not know what exists. Not the whole picture. They know about one piece because they work in it.

BRETT: TURN

TOM: CENTER

[TOM: the family who finally laid out everything they owned, and the parts the children had no idea about, and what the kids said when they saw the whole picture.]

And once they heard it, it was, we are going to be responsible for that at some point. We had better pay attention when they tell us things.

It should be exciting. And honestly it should be a little overwhelming too.

Because communication and trust are the whole thing. And the only way you build trust is by communicating. If you are keeping all your cards in your hand, close to the vest, there is no communication happening. Once you open your hands and show people, good things can begin.

BRETT: CENTER

Now, to the next generation reading this.

You may have been waiting to be told. Waiting for the conversation to start, because it seems like the kind of thing your parents are supposed to open.

You do not have to wait. And here is what Tom finds when he sits with people your age.

TOM: CENTER

They are ready to get in the game. They really are.

Give it to me so I can begin to do something with it. Do not wait until you are eighty-five and I am sixty-five, because I may not have the energy then. I am ready now. Just help me get started.

BRETT: CENTER

THIS WEEK. If you are a parent, tell one adult child something about your family they do not currently know. Not everything. One thing.

If you are in the next generation, ask one question you have been holding.

BRETT: TURN

Tom, bring us home.

TOM: CENTER

You need to stop thinking of them as your kids and start thinking of them as growing adults. Key word, growing.

They are growing into adulthood, and they are going to keep growing. But you can involve them now. And as you involve them more, they grow more.

BRETT: CENTER

Next session, the thing that does not transfer through any document.

We will see you there.

🎬 SESSION 2 — PASSING ON FAITH AND VALUES

What Does Not Transfer Automatically

Source: filmed Next Gen S2; Family Book ch. 11

BRETT: CENTER

Well, welcome back to Family Legacy by Design. My name is Brett, and I am here with Tom.

We are in a series on preparing the next generation, and this is session two. The topic is developing faith and values.

Last session we talked about the shift from heirs to stewards. And for a lot of families that is where something clicks, and then comes the question. Yes, but how?

Because before someone can steward anything well, they have to understand what actually matters.

BRETT: TURN

So Tom, give us the big idea here.

TOM: CENTER

When we are raising our kids, we want them to go out and be okay in life. We want to raise them to independence. But we often think about that as, I just do not want them living in the basement. I hope we do not have to support them the rest of their lives.

It is much more than that. What are the spiritual values your kids have learned living in your house? What have you done to help them grow spiritually?

Because I believe legacy coaching is very simple. You need to love Jesus. You need to teach your kids to love Jesus. And hope they teach their kids to love Jesus.

That is it. And if that happens, they will probably be good stewards. If they know God's word, they will know God's principles, they will seek to live by them, and they will eventually create their own good families.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, and you say it is not about the money, which is a strange thing to say in a series about wealth transfer.

TOM: CENTER

We get so confused in this space, because we think it is all about how much money am I going to leave them.

It is not about the money. It is the values. It is the character. It is the commitment you have to God that you are going to pass on to your kids.

BRETT: CENTER

So Tom, what are we really helping families understand?

TOM: CENTER

That what matters most does not transfer automatically.

Money can be transferred through documents and planning. That part is pretty straightforward. Faith and values are different. They are passed on through intentionality, through conversations, and through example.

Probably more through example than anything else. I tell people modeling is the most important thing you can do for your kids.

If a family does not communicate what they believe and why they believe it, the next generation is left to figure it out themselves. They see parts of it. They do not fully understand it.

Paul says it this way. The things that you have learned and received and heard and seen in me, practice these things, and the God of peace will be with you.

So we have to practice it. We have to model it.

BRETT: CENTER

And Tom, they are always watching.

TOM: CENTER

They are always watching. And unfortunately they get to see all of it, because they live in your house.

At church you have got your church face on. At home you have got your real face out. And they get to see it.

Which is why I say there are three parts of your family legacy. The one you received, which you did not have much to do with. The one you are going to leave. And the one you are living right now. That last one is the most important, because that is the one people are actually watching.

Scripture. Deuteronomy 6. These words shall be on your heart, and you shall teach them diligently to your sons. When you sit down and when you walk. Ordinary moments, not a scheduled event.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, there is a competition happening for your children that I want people to name out loud.

TOM: CENTER

We live in a culture that is trying to disciple your kids. It is trying to disciple them with worldly values. Money. Popularity. Likes on an account.

And that does not matter at all. What matters is what God thinks about what you are doing.

Your kids are being fed things all day long. And unless they are compensating by adding the truth of God's word to their life, that is what is forming them.

That is why I have a quiet time every day. Because I am desperately in need of God's perspective. Otherwise I am just going to listen to whatever somebody else says, which is not true.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, let me give you a scenario.

SCENARIO ONE. A family where the parents are genuinely intentional. You can see it in how they make decisions, how they give, how they treat people. And you ask the next generation what matters most in this family, and there is a hesitation. Not because they do not care. Because nobody ever connected the dots for them.

BRETT: TURN

TOM: CENTER

That is very common, and it is fixable, and the fix is embarrassingly simple.

You start by talking. You share. You talk about your faith. You talk about your experiences. You talk about decisions that shaped your life.

Stories are so important in legacy. That is where the transfer actually begins.

Because families assume it comes by osmosis. They have been around it, they have seen it, they will pick it up. And that is a very different thing than understanding it and applying it and having it show up in their own life.

BRETT: CENTER

Let me give you another version of this.

SCENARIO TWO. A family that has done the church part faithfully for twenty years. Every Sunday. Youth group. Camps. And the parents have never once said out loud, in their own words, at their own table, why any of it matters to them personally.

BRETT: TURN

TOM: CENTER

[TOM: the worldview class taken with a son-in-law, where everybody had the right answers and the practical part was missing.]

It is easy to know this stuff in your head. The question is whether we are living it out.

And one thing people do not think about enough is service. How do you get your kids to serve other people? When our kids were growing up, you just take them with you. You are going to go serve some people, you take your kids. They are playing while you are working, but they know what you are there for.

I have read a lot of books about raising kids. I have not found one that talks about the importance of teaching your kids to serve others. And I think it is critical.

BRETT: CENTER

To the next generation reading this, here is what I would say about this session.

You may have received a faith that came to you fully formed, from people who worked out the hard parts before you were born. That is a gift and it is also a problem, because a faith you have never had to think about is not yet yours.

Ask your parents why. Not what they believe. Why they believe it, and what it cost them. Most of them have never been asked and would give almost anything to be.

BRETT: CENTER

THIS WEEK. Tell one story about a decision that shaped your faith. Out loud, to your family. Not a lesson. A story.

BRETT: TURN

Tom, bring us home.

TOM: CENTER

Look around at the families you know who have done this well. Go to them and ask for tips.

I have lots of friends who have done a great job raising their families. I listen to them, I ask them questions, and I learn from them. We are all trying to help each other be successful.

Do not look at one person or one example. Look around you, see who has done it well, and pick their brains.

BRETT: CENTER

Next session, the thing underneath all of it. Trust.

We will see you there.

🎬 SESSION 3 — BUILDING TRUST ACROSS GENERATIONS

Why Assumptions Fill the Silence

Source: filmed Next Gen S3

BRETT: CENTER

Welcome back to Family Legacy by Design. My name is Brett, this is Tom, and we are grateful you have joined us.

Last session we talked about passing on faith and values. And underneath all of that there is something that determines whether any of it takes hold.

Trust.

A family can have clear values. Good intentions. The right structures, all the documents, every box checked. If trust is not there, it becomes very difficult for any of it to hold across generations.

There may be enough to keep a family together for one generation. It is destined to crack in the ones that follow.

BRETT: TURN

So Tom, give them the one thing.

TOM: CENTER

The reason legacy does not move from one generation to the next is lack of communication and trust. Sixty percent of the time.

So let's talk about trust. And trust is not a one-time thing. It is something you build on over time.

How much do you trust your brother and your sister? How much do you trust your mother or your father? We just want there to be trust, because that is what adds up to family harmony. You are not going to have harmony without it.

BRETT: CENTER

And Tom, when communication is missing, something takes its place.

TOM: CENTER

Assumptions. A friend of mine used to call them vain imaginations.

Here is what that looks like. You and I are talking, Brett. And Joe walks up. And we stop talking.

Now Joe is wondering, I wonder if they were talking about me. Is that why they stopped?

And the reality is we just finished whatever we were saying. But Joe now has the vain imagination that it was about him.

And there is a cousin to it, which is catastrophe thinking. My parents just hate me. My sister always discounts me. Whatever it is.

People fill the gaps with their own interpretation. So we just need to be aware of that.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, give them what that looks like in a real family.

TOM: CENTER

[TOM: the family where the parents thought the adult child was disengaging, and the child thought she was not trusted or included, and neither one said it, and specifically the daughter who never told her parents she did not think they were listening.]

So assumptions filled the gap. She assumed they were not listening. And over time that created distance between a mother and a daughter.

And when you create space for a conversation just to understand, not to fix everything, something shifts. Because understanding makes people feel that they matter and that what they say matters.

Trust is something that is built, and it falls down, and then it has to be rebuilt. Over and over again.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, there is a question you ask that I want people to have, because it changed how I think about walking into a family.

TOM: CENTER

I ask, is there anyone in your family that you have an issue with?

Because I want to know that before I end up talking with that person.

BRETT: CENTER

And Tom, I have asked that. It was a little scary for me. I asked some family members and one of them looked at me and said, yeah, my mom.

And you think, okay, where am I going to go with this?

But in almost every case there is somebody. And once it is named, you can actually do something about it.

Scripture. Ephesians 4:2. With all humility and gentleness, with patience, showing tolerance for one another in love. That is the posture trust grows in.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, say more about humility, because you make it central.

TOM: CENTER

Trust is not just built through what is said. It is built through what is consistently demonstrated. It is built through body language.

If parents expect openness and do not model openness, it creates confusion. If they expect honesty and avoid difficult conversations, it creates distance.

But when people see humility, when a father apologizes for how he behaved, that is what builds trust.

And when you mess up, you have got to admit you messed up. And you need to ask people to forgive you for the way you acted.

BRETT: CENTER

That last ten percent. Would you forgive me.

It is a hard thing to say. I need to say it more in my own family. But it empowers the other person enormously.

BRETT: TURN

Tom, what about conflict?

TOM: CENTER

Conflict is going to happen, and conflict is not always bad. Sometimes conflict brings out the truth.

Differences in perspective are normal. Your sister is not you. Your wife is not you.

But if people do not know how to walk through it in a healthy way, they either avoid it or they handle it poorly. And peacemaking training can be very helpful here.

I just reread a book called Peacemaker. It gives great insight into how to deal with difficult situations. It gives families tools to listen well, communicate clearly, and work through conflict.

What goes wrong is avoidance or escalation. Fight or flight. What works is learning to engage with the right posture. And it starts small, one conversation at a time.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, let me give you a scenario.

SCENARIO ONE. A family that genuinely cares about each other. Nobody has bad intent. And there is tension anyway, because one person believes they are being excluded and another believes they are protecting them. Neither one has said a word about it.

BRETT: TURN

TOM: CENTER

Both of them are reacting to a story that has never been confirmed.

And the answer is not to solve the problem first. You start by helping them understand each other. Ask a question, then a follow-up question. The second one usually matters more than the first.

Because most families jump straight to fixing without understanding what is going on underneath the water line.

BRETT: CENTER

Let me give you another version of this.

SCENARIO TWO. A family where a child said something honest once, years ago, and a parent reacted to it. And nothing that honest has been said at that table since.

BRETT: TURN

TOM: CENTER

There is a concept from Patrick Lencioni that applies. The absence of trust is the foundation of dysfunction in a team.

And part of building trust is allowing people to say what they think, even when you do not like what they say.

If a child says something and dad reacts, they are not going to say anything else. And neither is anybody else at that table who saw it happen. You just shut off communication for the whole room.

Safety in communication is really important. When a person feels safe, they will say what they think. If they do not feel safe, they back off and they do not engage.

BRETT: CENTER

To the next generation reading this, here is the one that is yours.

You are almost certainly carrying an assumption about someone in your family that you have never checked. Most people are. And you have probably built a fair amount of your behavior on top of it.

Checking it costs one uncomfortable conversation. Not checking it costs years.

BRETT: CENTER

THIS WEEK. Ask each person in your family whether there is anyone in the family they have something unresolved with.

Then listen to the answer without defending anyone, including yourself.

BRETT: TURN

Tom, bring us home.

TOM: CENTER

If people trust you, they will listen to what you have to say. Without it, communication is limited, assumptions increase, and alignment becomes very difficult.

And trust does not happen automatically. It is built through consistent interaction over time. And not just interaction. Following through on it.

BRETT: CENTER

Next session, we get practical. Teaching them to handle what is coming.

We will see you there.

🎬 SESSION 4 — TEACHING THEM TO HANDLE IT

Stewardship Means Management, Not Giving

Source: filmed Next Gen S4; Training Manual S6

BRETT: CENTER

Welcome back to Family Legacy by Design. My name is Brett, and this is Tom.

This session we are going to talk specifically about teaching financial stewardship. And it can feel a little counterintuitive to talk about stewardship with a family that has resources. But this is exactly where the opportunity is, to equip the next generation for, keyword, responsibility.

BRETT: TURN

Tom, this is one you live in all the time. Set it up.

TOM: CENTER

The church has ruined the word stewardship. Because they have stewardship campaigns and stewardship Sundays.

If you look in the dictionary, the word stewardship means management. It does not mean giving.

Stewardship is managing all that you have. Managing your money. Managing your time. Managing your relationships. Managing all kinds of things.

So when we say financial stewardship, we are talking about managing money.

BRETT: CENTER

And Tom, where does it start?

TOM: CENTER

I go back to a man named Larry Burkett. Larry was way at the beginning of Christian financial management. And he talked about three things you teach kids at the very beginning.

You have three jars. One is for giving. You get an allowance, you take ten percent, you put it in the giving jar. Then another portion goes in the long-term savings jar. And the rest you can spend.

Pay God first. Pay yourself second. Live on the rest.

And honestly, even in my business today, that is how I run it. Ten percent goes to giving. Ten percent goes to savings. And we run the business on the rest.

It is a simple thing. But when the numbers get bigger, if you are still operating off those same principles, it makes an enormous difference.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, and then there are the five.

TOM: CENTER

There are only five things you can do with your money.

You can live on it. You can give it away. You can pay taxes with it. You can repay debt if you owe somebody. Or you can save and invest it.

That is all of them. Everything fits into one of those five.

So I have a little chart with those five, and I ask people, this year, how much do you want to allocate to each one? Because you are going to allocate it whether you decide or not.

And what goes wrong is people are never taught the framework. So money becomes, who knows what I am going to do with it.

BRETT: CENTER

And the line you use that connects all five.

TOM: CENTER

There is no such thing as an independent financial decision.

They are all connected. If you say yes to A, you are saying no to B and no to C. Because you have limited resources. Everybody has limited resources.

Scripture. Luke 16:10. He who is faithful in a very little thing is also faithful in much. The little thing comes first. It always comes first.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, tell them about your father.

TOM: CENTER

[TOM: your father matching every dollar you saved from age ten, and the first car at fifteen, and what it cost, and paying cash. Tell it the way you tell it.]

But the point is I had a goal in mind. My goal was that I did not want to ride the bus to school anymore.

And that is why it is important to help your kids set goals.

[TOM: the granddaughter who just bought her first car, and how she got there.]

As opposed to a birthday coming and the keys just showing up.

BRETT: CENTER

And Tom, your father was not a finance guy.

TOM: CENTER

No, he ran a franchise of a sewer service.

But he paid fifty percent of my college and I worked all the way through and paid the other fifty. Part of that was just necessity. He did not have a lot of money.

But it was valuable to me. And I did not get involved in a lot of bad things because I did not have any time. I had to go to work.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, and you have a strong opinion about the alternative.

TOM: CENTER

When you have the resources and you can do everything for your kids and get them everything they want, it is a bad decision for you to do that.

That is strong. I think it is a bad decision.

There needs to be some kind of co-ownership.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, let me give you a scenario.

SCENARIO ONE. A family with real resources, and children who have never had to make a financial decision with any consequence attached to it. Everything has been handled. And the parents are quietly wondering whether their kids could run their own lives.

BRETT: TURN

TOM: CENTER

Then give them something real to manage.

[TOM: the college food budget, the empty refrigerator, the ramen noodles, and where the money was actually going, and how that story ends.]

You need to give people responsibility. That is why I believe in an allowance, because it gives people the opportunity to practice.

[TOM: Ron Blue's allowance approach, where they stopped paying for things directly and the kids had to earn the difference for what they wanted.]

That is teaching a kid responsibility for himself. And if you teach it when they are young, they remember it.

BRETT: CENTER

Let me give you another version of this.

SCENARIO TWO. A family where the money is professionally managed by people outside the family. Well managed. And no one in the next generation has ever had to think about how any of it works.

BRETT: TURN

TOM: CENTER

That is the problem with handing it all to someone else. You have not taught your kids how to manage money.

[TOM: the game with your son and his friends. The fake five thousand dollars, the six months, and the prize for the best portfolio.]

And you do not wait until responsibility shows up to start preparing. You start earlier and you give them opportunities when they are younger.

Give them ways to make money. If you are paying somebody to cut your grass, why not have your kids cut it and pay them? And maybe help them buy a lawn mower so they can go cut the neighbor's too.

Because you want them to have a work ethic. Most people want their kids to have a work ethic. And you want them to be able to make their own money.

BRETT: CENTER

To the next generation reading this, one thing.

Tom's first question to anyone in the next generation is not what are you going to inherit. It is whether you are living within your means right now, on what you actually have.

Because the system you use today is the system you will use later. Habits do not scale up when the numbers do. They just get bigger.

And his second question is about giving. Not someday. Now, at whatever level you are actually at.

BRETT: CENTER

THIS WEEK. Give one real financial responsibility to someone in the next generation. Real, meaning there is a consequence if it goes badly.

BRETT: TURN

Tom, bring us home.

TOM: CENTER

It is better to start when you have a dollar and you are putting ten cents in, than to wait until you have a hundred thousand and you are trying to figure out what to do with ten.

Your kids are not going to learn this without somebody guiding them. So do not just explain the principles. Show them how they apply in real life.

BRETT: CENTER

Next session, giving them the freedom to be different from you.

We will see you there.

🎬 SESSION 5 — HELPING THEM FIND THEIR CALLING

Freedom to Be Different From You

Source: filmed Next Gen S5; Family Book ch. 11

BRETT: CENTER

Well, welcome back to Family Legacy by Design. My name is Brett, and this is my partner in crime.

This session is about guiding purpose and calling. How do you help someone discover their God-given purpose, and the path God actually has them on?

Because last session we talked about stewardship. Learning responsibility over time. But there is something deeper that shapes how that responsibility gets lived out.

Responsibility without purpose feels like pressure. It feels like expectation and obligation. But when responsibility is connected to calling, it becomes something else entirely. It becomes personal.

And this is where a lot of families feel real tension. They want the next generation to succeed and carry things forward, and they are not sure how to help them discover what they are actually wired to do. And sometimes without realizing it, they start steering instead of guiding.

BRETT: TURN

So Tom, what do you most want families to understand here?

TOM: CENTER

First, you want to give your children the freedom to be different than you are.
