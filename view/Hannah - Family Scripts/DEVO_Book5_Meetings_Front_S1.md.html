HOW TO USE THIS

Thirty readings. Five per session, six sessions, with two days off between. Ten minutes each.

A word about the phrase. If family meeting already sounds heavy to you, you are not alone. For a lot of families it sounds like something serious is about to be announced, and that association alone is why most families never have one. Nothing in these thirty days requires a boardroom or an agenda with roman numerals. A family meeting is a time when a family comes together on purpose to talk about something that matters.

This works completely on its own. No videos required, no guide required, nobody else in your family required. If you are the only one reading this, you are still the person who can put a date on a calendar.

It also runs alongside the guide. If your family is working through Family Meetings That Bring You Together, these thirty days are the reading plan. The five readings in each group go with the matching session.

Each day has six parts. The reading. Reflect. Practice. One Question to Ask. The Bucket, which is private and which nobody reads. And Prayer, which is a direction rather than a written prayer.

One warning. More than any other book in this series, this one is going to ask you to schedule something. If you get to Day 30 without a date on a calendar, the book did not work, however good the reading was.

Day 1

There Is Going to Be a Family Meeting

It's just a matter of whether you're going to be there or not

"Without consultation, plans are frustrated, but with many counselors they succeed." PROVERBS 15:22

I say this to families all the time, and it lands differently depending on how old they are.

There is going to be a family meeting. It is just a matter of whether you are going to be there or not.

That is not a threat and it is not a sales line. It is a description of what actually happens.

Somewhere in the future of your family there is a room. Your children are in it. Possibly their spouses. There are documents on the table, or a house to deal with, or a decision that cannot wait. And they are talking about what you wanted.

That meeting is going to happen. Nothing about it is optional. The only variable is whether you were present for it, or whether it happened without you and everyone in the room had to reconstruct your intentions from fragments.

I have watched both versions. The difference is not subtle.

In the first version, the family has questions and there is somebody who can answer them. Somebody says, why did you decide that, and the answer arrives in a voice everyone recognizes rather than through a lawyer reading a paragraph. And sometimes, more often than people expect, the person who made the decision hears something in that room and changes their mind, which is only possible while they are still in it.

In the second version, everyone is guessing. And they are guessing under pressure, with grief in the room, about a person who is not there to be asked. The guesses are usually kind and they are frequently wrong, and once made they are very hard to revise, because now revising them feels like betraying somebody who has died.

So the question is not whether your family will have this conversation. It is when, and whether you will be at the table.

The proverb says that without consultation plans are frustrated. Notice it does not say the plans are bad. A plan can be excellent and still be frustrated, and the thing that frustrates it is that nobody was consulted.

I want to say one more thing to whoever is reading this and is not the patriarch or matriarch of the family.

You can call the meeting. There is no rule that the oldest person has to be the one who says let us sit down. In a lot of the families I have watched do this well, the person who started it was a daughter, or a son-in-law, or somebody who simply got tired of everyone guessing.

Somebody goes first. It might as well be whoever is holding this book.

Reflect

1. Picture the room. Who is in it, and what are they trying to figure out?

2. What would they get wrong about what you wanted?

3. Has your family ever sat down on purpose to talk about anything that mattered?

4. If somebody has to go first, who is it going to be?

Practice

Write down who will be in the room when that conversation happens.

Now write, in one sentence, what you would most want them to understand that they currently do not.

That sentence is the reason to have the meeting while you are here.

One Question to Ask

Take this to one person in your family this week.

"Have we ever actually sat down and talked about where this family is headed?"

Who I will ask _______

When _______

What came back, in their words rather than my summary

The Bucket

Private. Nobody reads this.

What my family would get wrong if I were not there to explain it

Whether that is actually me

Prayer

Ask to be present for the conversation rather than remembered in it. Then ask for the specific nerve to raise it.

Day 2

It's Really About Creating Space

Space that normally doesn't happen on its own

"Come away by yourselves to a secluded place and rest a while." MARK 6:31

A family meeting is not complicated and it is not overly formal. It is simply a time when a family comes together intentionally to talk about things that matter to them.

What it really is, is creating space. Space that does not happen on its own.

I want to sit on that phrase, because it explains something families find confusing about their own lives.

Most families talk constantly. There are texts all day. There are calls. There are dinners and holidays and a group chat with photographs in it. If you measured contact, you would conclude these people are in close communication.

And yet the things that matter most never come up.

That is not a contradiction. It is what happens when all your communication is unstructured. Unstructured time fills with whatever is easiest to talk about, and what is easiest is logistics, weather, sports, the grandchildren, and whatever is currently in the news. None of those are bad. They are just what floats.

The deeper things do not float. They sink, and they stay down unless somebody deliberately goes after them.

So creating space is not about adding more time with your family. Most of the families I work with have plenty of time together. It is about designating some of that time for something that would never surface in the rest of it.

Here is the practical shape of it, and it is smaller than people assume.

It does not have to be long. An hour is plenty for a first one, and thirty minutes at the end of a dinner you were already having is a completely legitimate way to start.

It does not have to be everyone. Whoever can be there is who you start with.

And it does not have to be heavy. The first one can be about something almost pleasant. What do we want this family to be about. What has this family been good at. Those are not confrontational questions and they open more than people expect.

Jesus tells the disciples to come away to a secluded place. What strikes me is the reason. They had been so busy that they had not had a chance even to eat. That is not a spiritual failure, it is a schedule problem, and the answer was to go somewhere on purpose.

Your family probably does not need more contact. It needs one hour that has been set aside for something the other hours cannot hold.

Reflect

1. How much does your family talk, and how much of it is logistics?

2. What has never come up in all those hours together?

3. When was the last time your family gathered on purpose for something other than a holiday or a crisis?

4. What is the smallest version of this you could actually do?

Practice

List the last five times your whole family, or most of it, was together.

1. _______

2. _______

3. _______

4. _______

5. _______

Now mark each one. O if the occasion was already set by something else, a holiday, a birthday, a funeral. C if somebody chose it.

How many C's?

One Question to Ask

"Could we set aside an hour together sometime soon? Nothing is wrong. I just want to talk about something properly."

The second sentence is not optional. Without it, everyone assumes bad news.

Who I will ask _______

When _______

What came back _______

The Bucket

Private.

What never comes up when my family is together

Why I think that is

The smallest gathering I could actually make happen

What I would want to talk about if I had one hour

Prayer

Ask for one hour rather than for a transformed family. The hour is the thing you can actually give.

Day 3

They Kick the Can Down the Road

Not because it's unimportant, but because it feels unclear

"Do not boast about tomorrow, for you do not know what a day may bring forth." PROVERBS 27:1

Families think about having a meeting and then everybody gets a little nervous, because they start anticipating what might happen.

They know the personalities. They know the dynamics. They are not sure how it is going to go. So instead of stepping into it, they kick the can down the road.

Not because it is unimportant. Because it feels unclear.

That distinction matters more than almost anything else in this session, because it changes what you are actually dealing with.

If a family were postponing because they did not care, the problem would be motivation, and motivation is hard to manufacture. But that is not what is happening. They care a great deal. What they lack is a picture of how the thing goes, and the human mind, in the absence of a picture, supplies the worst one available.

So somebody imagines the meeting and what they see is their brother saying the thing he always says. Or their father getting quiet in the particular way he gets quiet. Or a sister-in-law who will take something personally. And having watched that imaginary meeting go badly, they decide, reasonably, not to hold it.

The meeting that gets cancelled is almost never the meeting that would have happened.

I have sat in a great many of these now, and I will tell you plainly that the anticipated disaster almost never arrives. What arrives instead is usually awkwardness for about ten minutes, and then somebody says something honest, and the room changes.

That does not mean nothing hard comes up. Hard things do come up, and they should, because they were already there. But there is a difference between a hard thing being said in a room where everyone is present and a hard thing sitting unsaid for another five years while everyone privately assumes something.

The proverb warns against boasting about tomorrow, and it is worth asking what boasting looks like here. It does not look like arrogance. It looks like the quiet confidence that there will be a better moment, later, when things have settled and everyone is in a good place.

That confidence is a claim about a day you do not control.

So here is the honest question for today. What are you actually imagining? Because whatever it is, you have probably never checked it against reality, and it is currently making a decision for your entire family.

Reflect

1. When you imagine your family sitting down together, what goes wrong?

2. Whose reaction are you most anticipating?

3. Have you ever checked that anticipation with the person?

4. How long has the imagined version been running your decision?

Practice

Write the version you have been imagining. Be specific. Who says what.

Now write the most likely version. Not the best case. The likely one.

Which one have you been planning around?

One Question to Ask

"If we all sat down together, what do you think would happen?"

Ask someone who would be in the room. Their answer will tell you whether your imagined version is shared or invented.

Who I will ask _______

When _______

What came back _______

The Bucket

Private.

What I am afraid would happen

Who I am most afraid of in that room

How long I have been putting it off

What has changed in my family while I waited

Prayer

Name the imagined version out loud. Then ask whether it is discernment or fear, and be willing to hear that it is fear.

Day 4

If It's Not Clear, It's Going to Drift

The agenda becomes a tool

"We must pay much closer attention to what we have heard, so that we do not drift away from it." HEBREWS 2:1

Before you think about structure, you have to be clear on why you are getting together.

Are you trying to communicate something? Solve a problem? Teach the next generation something? Build understanding?

If that is not clear, the meeting is going to drift.

I have watched families do everything else right and still come away disappointed, and almost every time the diagnosis is the same. Good people, right room, real intentions, no purpose. So the conversation went wherever the most talkative person took it, and at the end nobody could say what had happened.

Purpose is not the same as a topic. A topic is what you are talking about. A purpose is what you want to be different when you leave.

Those produce very different meetings. If the topic is the lake house, you can talk about the lake house for two hours and accomplish nothing. If the purpose is we want everybody's honest opinion before we decide about the lake house, the conversation has a shape, and you can tell whether it worked.

Once purpose is clear, then structure helps, and this is where the agenda comes in. An agenda is not formality for its own sake. It tells people what you are trying to accomplish, how the conversation will move, and roughly how long it will take.

That matters because people are wired differently. Some of your family are naturally structured and some are not at all. The structured ones need to know the shape or they cannot relax. The unstructured ones will drift if there is none. An agenda serves both, and it costs you four lines on a piece of paper.

I would add one thing that families skip. Send it ahead.

If people know what the meeting is about before they walk in, two things happen. The nervous ones stop imagining disasters, because they can see the actual subject. And the thoughtful ones arrive having actually thought, which is worth more than anything that happens in the first twenty minutes.

Hebrews warns about drifting, and the image is worth holding. Drifting is not a decision. Nobody turns the boat. You drift by not paying attention, and you notice much later that you are somewhere you did not choose.

That is exactly what happens to a family meeting without a purpose, and it is what happens to a family without meetings.

Reflect

1. What would the purpose of your first meeting be? Not the topic. The purpose.

2. How would you know whether it worked?

3. Who in your family needs structure, and who does not?

4. What has drifted in your family because nobody was paying attention?

Practice

Write the purpose of your first meeting in one sentence, starting with the words we want.

Now write the agenda. Two items maximum.

1. _______

2. _______

How long _______

One Question to Ask

"If we got together, what would you want to make sure we actually talked about?"

Ask it before you set the agenda rather than after. It changes what you put on it.

Who I will ask _______

When _______

What came back _______

The Bucket

Private.

What I actually want to be different afterward

What I would put on the agenda if nobody would be upset

What has drifted in my family

When I first noticed it

Prayer

Ask for clarity about the purpose before you ask for a good meeting. The second one depends on the first.

Day 5

If Something Is Worth Doing, It's Worth Doing Poorly

The way you get better at it is by doing it

"Who has despised the day of small things?" ZECHARIAH 4:10

I heard a man speak once who contested the phrase everybody knows, that if something is worth doing, it is worth doing well.

He said he did not believe that at all.

If something is worth doing, it is worth doing poorly. Because even if it is not perfect the first time, the second time it will be a little bit better, and the third time better still.

The way you get better at this is by doing it. The way you never get better at it is by not doing it.

I have thought about that a great deal in this work, because perfectionism kills more family meetings than conflict does.

Families wait until they have the right agenda. Until everyone can be there. Until the difficult person is in a better season. Until they have read more, or found a facilitator, or figured out what they actually want to say. Each one of those is reasonable and each one is a reason to wait, and in the meantime the years go by and there has never been a first meeting.

So let me describe what a poor first meeting looks like, so you can stop being afraid of it.

It is a bit awkward at the beginning. Somebody makes a joke about it being a board meeting. The agenda is too ambitious and you get through half of it. One person talks more than their share and you do not know how to redirect them. Somebody says something slightly raw and you handle it clumsily. It runs long. At the end you are not sure it accomplished anything.

That is a successful first meeting. Every family that does this well has one of those in their history.

The second one is better, because you know what to cut. The third one, people arrive having thought about it beforehand. By the fourth, somebody who said nothing the first time says something that changes the family.

None of that is available to the family still waiting for the right conditions.

Zechariah asks who has despised the day of small things. It was said to people rebuilding a temple that looked nothing like the one they remembered, and who were embarrassed by how modest the beginning was.

The answer implied in the question is that God does not despise it. Only we do.

So do it badly. That is the entire instruction for this week.

Reflect

1. What have you been waiting for before you start?

2. Is that a reason or a permission slip?

3. What would a poor first meeting actually cost you?

4. What is it costing to keep waiting for a good one?

Practice

Put a date on the calendar. Not a plan. A date.

The date _______

Who I will invite _______

The one thing we will talk about _______

Now tell one person the date. That is what converts it from an intention into an appointment.

One Question to Ask

"Would you come if I set something up? It does not have to be anything formal."

Who I will ask _______

When _______

What came back _______

The Bucket

Private.

What I have been waiting for

Whether that is real

The date I just wrote down

What I am willing to have go badly

Prayer

Ask for a willingness to begin small and imperfectly, and ask for it to be this month rather than at some better time.
