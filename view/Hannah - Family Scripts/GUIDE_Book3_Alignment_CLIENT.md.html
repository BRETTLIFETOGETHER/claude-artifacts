A WELCOME FROM TOM AND BRETT

This is the most practical book in the series and the one people most want to skip.

It is about documents, accounts, coverage, debt, and where the money actually goes. If that sounds like the part you were hoping somebody else would handle, you are exactly the person we wrote it for.

Here is why it matters more than it looks like it should.

Tom has spent about thirty years sitting with families, and Brett has spent most of his career building material that helps people have conversations they would not otherwise have. And what we have both watched, more times than we would like, is a family that did the financial work well and came apart anyway.

Siblings stopped speaking over a document nobody had read. A business was sold under a deadline that had been visible for a decade. Somebody's spouse discovered what the family had on the worst week of her life, from a stranger.

None of those were financial failures. Every one of them was a family that had the pieces and had never put them together.

So one thing before you begin.

Everything in these six sessions is a conversation before it is a document. If you get the paperwork organized and skip the conversation, you have done the easy half and left the part that actually determines whether your family holds together.

And one more thing.

Most families never do this.

Not because they do not care. Because there is no natural moment when it comes up, and because the work has no deadline attached and produces no visible result for a long time.

So if you are opening this and feeling behind, you are not. You are ahead of nearly everyone.

You do not have to do it perfectly. You do not have to do it with everyone. And you do not have to finish it today.

USING THIS GUIDE

This works alone, with your spouse, or with your whole family

You can do every part of this by yourself. But it is better together, and it is meant to end up there.

With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write it down.

Who leads this

Somebody has to press play and read the questions out loud. That person is a host, not a teacher.

The host does not need to know more than anyone else and is not responsible for anyone's answers. Start the video. Read the question. Then be quiet long enough for somebody to answer it.

It should probably not be the person who usually decides things in your family, and it should rotate if you can manage it.

Three rules for whoever has the chair. Read the question and then stop talking. Answer last, not first. And you are not responsible for fixing anything that comes up.

A word about numbers

Nothing in these six sessions requires you to say a figure out loud to anyone.

That is worth agreeing to before the first session, out loud, especially if there are adult children in the room. Every question here works without a number attached, and the moment somebody feels obligated to disclose one, you will get less honesty rather than more.

The private exercises are where the real numbers go, and the private exercises are yours.

If your family does not look like the one in this book

If there is no spouse. Widowed, divorced, single, or married to somebody who will not engage. Every session has a section built around a husband and wife. Read it as being about the first person who will know. Somebody eventually has to understand what you decided.

If a child is estranged. Do not skip the session. Do the work about the people you can reach, and answer the questions about your whole family anyway. Being able to name what you would say if you could say it is worth something on the day you can.

If your family is blended. Remarriage, stepchildren, children from two marriages. Your questions in Sessions 3 and 4 are genuinely harder than the ones described here, not easier. Where this guide says children, read the specific people in your situation and let the difference stand.

If your resources are modest. Almost every story in this material involves families with a great deal. Every question in these six sessions works at any scale. The only thing that changes is the number, and the number is the least important variable in this book.

When to bring someone in

This book is more technical than the others in the series, and some of it will be outside what you can handle alone.

Every session ends with a line naming the point at which a family should get professional help. That is not a sales pitch and it is not a failure. It is the honest answer.

But there is a difference between needing help to act and needing help to understand, and the professions have not always been careful about that distinction. You are allowed to understand your own situation. In fact you are responsible for it, and nobody else can carry that.

What you need each week

The session video. About twenty minutes.

This guide. Bring something to write with.

The companion book. Family Legacy by Design. Each session assigns five short readings.

And one honest note about the questions. There are more in each session than you will answer. Each session marks one question as the one that matters. If you answer only that one, the session worked.

Where this sits in the series

This is the third book in the Family Foundation Series Curriculum. Family Clarity comes before it, and most families should do that one first. What follows is Family Communication, then Family Meetings, then Next Generation.

Clarity is what you want. Alignment is whether your life reflects it.

If you are in the next generation

Each session has a section addressed to you directly.

This is the book where your questions are most likely to be dismissed as premature. They are not. Everything here will eventually be yours to handle, and the time to understand it is before you are handling it in a difficult week.

SESSION 1

F · FINANCIAL STATEMENT

What You Own and What You Owe

Seeing Your Whole Picture for the First Time

HOST NOTE. Agree out loud, before the first question, that no numbers get said in the room. Then keep that agreement even if somebody volunteers one. If a person shares a figure and the room reacts, nobody will share anything again.

INTRODUCTION

Here is the question most people bring to this session, and almost nobody asks it out loud because it sounds like an admission.

Do I even know what we have?

Not roughly. Specifically. Could you list it right now, without looking anything up, and would your spouse produce the same list.

KEY VERSE

"For which one of you, when he wants to build a tower, does not first sit down and calculate the cost?"

LUKE 14:28

A lot of times, families do not know what they have.

That surprises people, and then we sit down and go through it, and it turns out to be true of nearly everyone. Not because anything is hidden. Because a family builds over decades, in pieces, and every piece is known by somebody and nobody knows all of them at once.

So there is an abundance of information and no picture. And you cannot make a decision from information. You make decisions from a picture.

Notice what the verse actually describes. A man wants to build a tower and the first thing he does is sit down. Not gather advisors. Not draw plans. He sits down and calculates whether what he has will reach where he is going.

That is this session, and it comes before every technical question in the other five.

THE ONE QUESTION

If something happened to you tomorrow, what would your spouse not know?

Answer that one and the session worked. Everything else is optional.

OPENING STORY

I Can't Believe We Have All This

A lot of times the wife does not have a clue.

She knows he goes to work every day. She knows there is a business. She has no idea what it is worth, because that was the arrangement, and the arrangement worked, and nobody ever revisited it.

Tom put the statement in front of both of them. She looked at it and said something close to, my goodness, I cannot believe we have all this.

And just that alone is like marital counseling.

It was not the number. It was that for the first time in a long marriage, two people were looking at the same information at the same time and making the same observations about it. Up to that point, one person had been holding the whole thing.

What nobody says out loud about that arrangement is that it is lonely for the one holding it.

GET STARTED

You are never required to say a number out loud, to anyone, at any point in this book.

1. Who in your household holds the picture, and how did that get decided? With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write it down.

And one more thing, before anything else

There is a conversation your family has not had.

You know what it is. It might be about what exists, or about a decision made years ago, or about what happens when you are gone.

Write it down here, in one sentence. Plain language, no softening.

The conversation we have not had is

And it is really with

Every session from here will come back to it, and by Session 6 you will decide what to do about it.

BEFORE YOU BRING THE FAMILY IN

This is about the two of you, and in this book it matters more than anywhere else.

One day, and neither of you knows the day, one person is going to have to understand all of this at once, while grieving, with people around them offering advice. That is the worst possible moment to be learning it for the first time.

You can spare them that on a Tuesday evening this month.

If you are the one holding it, the reason to share is not fairness. It is mercy.

If you are the one who has never been shown it, you are allowed to ask. Not as an accusation. As a request. I would like to understand what we have, so I am not learning it in a hard week.

Almost nobody refuses that when it is asked that way.

2. What have you told yourself about why you have not put it all on one page?

3. If your spouse had to call your advisor tomorrow, could they ask an intelligent question?

4. Answer this one about someone else. What do your adult children believe about what this family has, and where did that belief come from?

THE FRAMEWORK

What You Own Minus What You Owe

The definition intimidates people who have no reason to be intimidated. A financial statement is two lists and some subtraction.

  • What you own. The house. The accounts. The retirement. The business. The policy with cash value. The property that came from somebody's mother.

  • What you owe. The mortgage. The line of credit. Anything owed on the business. The obligation nobody counts because it is owed to a family member.

  • The difference. That is your net worth, and it is not a grade. It measures one thing, at one moment, and its only purpose is to tell you where you are standing.

  • Who owns each piece. Individually, jointly, in a trust, inside an entity. This one matters more than people expect and it is the bridge into Session 2.

Approximate is fine for now. The purpose today is not precision, it is possession of the picture.

WHERE YOU ARE STARTING

If one person has always handled this. That is most households and it is not a problem to be blamed on anyone. It became an arrangement in year three and nobody revisited it. The fix is one evening, not a restructuring.

If you both know and have never sat down together. You are closer than you think. What you are missing is not information, it is the shared moment of looking, and that moment is what produces the conversation.

If your situation is genuinely complicated. Business interests, multiple properties, entities. You may not be able to complete the page alone and you can still complete most of it. Fill in what you can and let the blanks be the list you take to somebody.

THE TOOL

The One-Page Picture

The whole tool is one page and two lists, done separately and then compared.

Both spouses write down, from memory and without looking anything up, everything the family owns and everything it owes.

Then compare the two pages.

The gap between the two lists is the exercise. Not the total. The gap.

That is where the conversation starts, and it is usually more revealing than the number at the bottom.

If you are doing this alone, write your page and then write what you believe the person who would eventually handle things could produce. That second page is your list of what needs explaining.

Circle the row with the widest gap.

Self-check. Mark the ones that are true today.

  • I could list what we own without looking it up.
  • My spouse could too.
  • Nothing significant is known by only one person.
  • We have looked at the same page at the same time.
  • I know where the widest gap is.

FOR THE NEXT GENERATION

You are not entitled to know what your parents have, and asking about the amount will land badly in most families.

But there is a different question you are entitled to ask, and it is the one that actually matters.

If something happened, would anybody besides you know where everything is?

That is not a question about money. It is a question about whether your mother or your father will be alone in a hard week with a filing cabinet and no map.

Ask that one. It is a gift rather than an intrusion, and almost no parent hears it as prying.

TOGETHER

Both of you write the page separately this week. Then set a time to compare.

That is the whole assignment. Not a plan. One evening.

CIRCLES OF INFLUENCE

The person who would handle things. A spouse, an adult child, an executor. Do they know they are that person?

Somebody who could help you see it. Not to decide anything. To look at it with you once.

SESSION 2

A · ASSETS UNDER MANAGEMENT

How It All Fits Together

Making Sure Everything Pulls the Same Direction

HOST NOTE. This session produces the most surprise of any in the book, usually around beneficiary designations. If somebody realizes something mid-session, let them sit with it rather than moving on. That realization is the whole point.

INTRODUCTION

Last session was about what you have. This one asks a harder question, and it is the one families arrive with once the first page exists.

Is any of this actually working?

Everything looks fine. The statements arrive. Nothing is obviously wrong. And there is a quiet suspicion that the pieces are not connected to each other, and nobody can name what is producing it.

KEY VERSE

"By wisdom a house is built, and by understanding it is established."

PROVERBS 24:3

Most families do not build everything at once. It develops over time. Different decisions, different seasons, different people advising along the way.

Each piece can make sense on its own, and when you step back it still feels like a collection of separate systems that are not connected.

Building and establishing are two different verbs. Most families have done the first one.

THE ONE QUESTION

For each significant thing your family holds, who owns it and where does it go?

Two facts per item. Answer that and the session worked.

OPENING STORY

Three Directions at Once

You have got one part of your assets trying to grow aggressively. Another part trying to generate income for the family. And a third part trying to minimize taxes.

Each one of those makes complete sense by itself.

But if they are not coordinated, you have three strategies pulling in three directions, and nobody is asking whether they work together.

Tom has sat with families where three different money managers were running three portions and all three were using essentially the same strategy. The family could have had one person put a third in each place and gotten the identical result.

Nobody did anything wrong. Nobody was even inefficient in isolation.

It was only visible from above, and nobody had ever looked from there.

GET STARTED

1. How many separate people advise your family, and do any of them talk to each other? With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write it down.

BEFORE YOU BRING THE FAMILY IN

A short reminder. This session frequently reveals that one spouse assumed something about how things pass that is not true.

If that happens, sit with it together before it goes any further. It is not anybody's fault and it is much easier to fix now than later.

THE FRAMEWORK

How Assets Actually Pass

Different assets move in different ways, and which way depends almost entirely on who owns it.

  • Held individually. Moves one way.

  • Held jointly. Moves another.

  • Held in a trust. Moves a third way.

  • Held inside a business entity. A fourth.

  • With a beneficiary named. Moves before all of those, and does not wait for a will.

Families assume ownership is a technicality. It is the single largest determinant of what actually happens.

Two facts per item. Who owns it. Where does it go. Most families cannot complete that table on the first attempt, and the incomplete rows are the most useful output of this session.

WHERE YOU ARE STARTING

If everything is in one place with one advisor. You have simplicity, which is worth a great deal. Your risk is concentration rather than fragmentation, and the questions to ask are about whether one strategy is serving several different objectives.

If things are spread across several people. This is the most common shape and it is where fragmentation hides. Nobody is doing anything wrong and nobody can see the whole. Your work is assembling a view that currently does not exist anywhere.

If you inherited some of this. Assets that came from a previous generation frequently carry structures nobody in your family chose and nobody has examined. Those are worth looking at first, because they are the most likely to be doing something you did not intend.

THE TOOL

Who Owns What, and Where Does It Go

Two facts per item. That is the entire instrument.

What Who owns it Where it goes

Fill it in for everything significant. Then circle any row where the second and third columns surprise you.

Then do the part that takes an afternoon and changes the most. Check every beneficiary designation you have. Retirement accounts, policies, anything with a named beneficiary.

Those forms were filled out at a moment in time, they never expire, and they override everything else.

SESSION 3

M · MANDATORY DOCUMENTS

The Documents That Speak for You

Making Sure They Say What You Would Say

HOST NOTE. This session touches death directly and some people will go quiet. That is not resistance. Give it room, and do not fill the silence with logistics.

INTRODUCTION

Here is the question underneath this session.

Would my family know what to do?

Not eventually. On the day. Would they know where things are, who is in charge, what you would have wanted, and whether the paper says any of it.

KEY VERSE

"Now go, write it on a tablet before them and inscribe it on a scroll, that it may serve in the time to come as a witness forever."

ISAIAH 30:8

Keyword, mandatory. These are the documents you have to have so the plans line up with the priorities.

Most families assume that because they have documents, they are covered. They may have a will they did a long time ago, when their family situation was very different. Little kids running around the house who are now out there starting their own families.

The documents are often just out of date.

And here is the hard part. What you say you want to have happen, if it is not written down, will not happen.

THE ONE QUESTION

Do the people you have named know they are named, and are they willing?

Answer that one and the session worked.

OPENING STORY

The Executor Who Died Three Years Ago

Tom was reviewing an old will with a client and found the named executor was an uncle who had died three years earlier.

Nobody had noticed. Not the family, not anybody. The document was sitting in a drawer doing exactly what it said it would do, and what it said had stopped being possible.

People think a will is one and done for the rest of your life. Tom tells them they will probably change it three, four, five times, depending on how their situation changes.

And it is not only the will. It is who is named, whether they know, and whether they are willing. Being an executor is a great deal of work, and a surprising number of people find out they have the job at the worst possible moment.

GET STARTED

1. When were your documents last looked at, and what has changed in your family since? With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write it down.

BEFORE YOU BRING THE FAMILY IN

A short reminder, and this one is specific.

Most couples have not read their own documents out loud to each other. They signed them, in an office, some years ago.

Before anything else in this session, get them out and read who is named. That is the conversation.

THE FRAMEWORK

The Three Myths of Estate Planning

  • I am never going to die. Nobody says it. Many people plan as though it were true, which is the same thing operationally.

  • I have the highest priced attorneys in town, so my estate is in good shape. That might be fifty percent correct. You may well have the highest priced attorneys. That is no guarantee at all that your estate is in good shape, because a document is a snapshot of what you told somebody at a moment.

  • It is my kids' money. God did not give it to your kids. He gave it to you. Which means you are the one accountable for what happens to it, including whether the people receiving it are prepared.

Which of the three have you been operating under, and what has it cost you so far?

WHERE YOU ARE STARTING

If you have no documents at all. You are in more company than you think, and knowing better has almost nothing to do with it. The task has no deadline, produces no visible benefit, and requires an afternoon of thinking about your own death. Start by naming the date rather than the attorney.

If yours are more than five years old. They are describing a family that may no longer exist. You do not need to start over. You need to read who is named and check it against your family today.

If yours are current and you have never read them. This is more common among families who have done the work. Get them out and read them in plain language. More than once Tom has shown somebody their own documents and heard, I had no idea that is what it said.

THE TOOL

The Document Locator

Three steps, and the third is the one families skip.

Find them. Where are the actual documents. Not where they should be. Where they are. Write it down somewhere a grieving person could find it in an afternoon.

Read who is named. Executor. Power of attorney. Healthcare decisions. Guardian, if that applies. Trustee, if there is a trust.

Call those people. Tell them they are named. Ask whether they are willing. Ask whether they know where the documents are.

That third step takes four phone calls and it is the difference between a plan and a piece of paper.
