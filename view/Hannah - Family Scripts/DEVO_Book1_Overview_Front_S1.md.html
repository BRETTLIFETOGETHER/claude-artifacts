HOW TO USE THIS

Thirty readings. Five per session, six sessions, with two days off between. Ten minutes each.

This is the front door. Everything else in the Family Foundation Series comes after this book, and this book has one job. To make sure everyone in your family means the same thing by the word legacy before anyone spends time on it.

If you have already started somewhere else in the series, you have not done anything wrong. Most families should begin with Clarity. This one is for the family that wants to know what they are getting into first, or for the person who needs to be able to explain to somebody else why any of it matters.

This works completely on its own. No videos required, no guide required, nobody else in your family required.

It also runs alongside the guide. If your family is working through Family Legacy by Design, these thirty days are the reading plan. The five readings in each group go with the matching session.

Each day has six parts. The reading. Reflect. Practice. One Question to Ask. The Bucket, which is private and which nobody reads. And Prayer, which is a direction rather than a written prayer.

What this book will not do. It will not tell you what to put in your will, how to structure anything, or what to say to your children. Those come later and they come in the other five books. This one is about the question underneath all of them, which is what you are actually trying to pass on.

What Is a Legacy?

Understanding what you are already passing on

Day 1

Not Just What You Leave Behind

It's what you pass on every day

"So I hated all the fruit of my labor for which I had labored under the sun, for I must leave it to the man who will come after me. And who knows whether he will be a wise man or a fool?" ECCLESIASTES 2:18-19

Most people think of legacy as something that happens later.

That is not a criticism. Everything about the way this subject is usually handled reinforces it. The documents are drafted for a moment none of us will be in the room for. The plan is built around a date nobody can name. The whole apparatus points at the future.

But legacy is not just what you leave behind. It is what you pass on every day.

Those are two different verbs and they run on completely different terms.

Leaving is a transaction. It happens once, it happens after you are gone, and it is administered by people who were hired to administer it. There is a closing date and a signature page.

Passing on is not a transaction at all. It is happening right now, in how you live, what you prioritize, and how you treat the people around you. Nobody was hired to handle it. There is no date and no signature, and it does not stop while you get organized.

Which changes the question entirely. Not what will I leave. Something more like what am I building into the lives around me.

Here is the part that takes longest to accept. What gets passed on most clearly is frequently not what was intended. It is what was lived.

The intended legacy is the one in the documents. It is careful, it was reviewed, and it usually reflects a person at their best. The lived legacy is what your children actually observed over thirty years, and nobody reviewed that at all.

No child in the history of this work has ever read a trust document and concluded something about their father. They concluded it from watching him.

And you cannot pause it. That is the uncomfortable part. There is no version of this where a family suspends the transfer of values for two years, gets the planning in order, and then resumes. Whatever is true in your house this year is being passed on this year.

Solomon says something bleak at the start of this, and I do not think he is being dramatic. He hated the fruit of his labor because he had to leave it to somebody who came after him, and he did not know whether that person would be wise or a fool.

That anxiety is under nearly every family I have sat with. It is rarely stated that plainly, and it is almost always there.

But notice what he does not consider. He never asks whether the person could be prepared.

That question is the whole reason for this series.

3. What is the distance between your intended legacy and your lived one?

Day 2

It's Being Formed Right Now

In how you live, what you prioritize

"So teach us to number our days, that we may present to You a heart of wisdom." PSALM 90:12

If legacy is being passed on rather than left, then it is being formed right now, and it is being formed by ordinary things.

Not by the decisions you think of as significant. By how you live, what you prioritize, and how you handle a Tuesday.

I want to make that concrete, because it is easy to nod at and hard to actually see.

Your children learned what your family thinks about money by watching what happened when money got tight, not by anything you said about stewardship. They learned what your family thinks about work from whether you came to the thing you said you would come to. They learned what your family thinks about forgiveness from what happened after the argument, and whether anyone ever went back.

None of those were teaching moments. Nobody sat anyone down. They were Tuesdays, and Tuesdays are what a legacy is made of.

Here is why this is good news rather than bad.

If legacy were formed by grand gestures, most people would be out of options by sixty. But if it is formed daily, then every day is available, including this one, and including whatever repair might be needed.

I have watched families change direction late. Not erase what came before, which nobody can do, but change what is currently transmitting. And what did it was never a dramatic conversation. It was a person deciding to live differently in small ways that other people could see.

The psalm asks God to teach us to number our days. It is a strange request when you sit with it. Not to extend them. Not to make them count in some vague sense. To number them, which means to count them and know there is a limit.

And notice what the counting produces. A heart of wisdom. Not anxiety. Not urgency. Wisdom.

That is the posture this whole book is after. Not panic about how little time is left, but the particular clarity that comes from knowing the number is finite and today is one of them.

So the question for today is small and it is not rhetorical.

What did yesterday teach your family about what matters to you?

Day 3

Not What Was Intended

It's what was lived

"Be imitators of me, just as I also am of Christ." 1 CORINTHIANS 11:1

There are two legacies running in every family, and only one of them has started.

The intended legacy is the one you have in mind. It may be written down. It may be quite specific. It is careful, it reflects your best thinking, and it is aimed at a moment in the future.

The lived legacy is what people are actually observing. It has been running the whole time, without review, and it is the only one currently transmitting.

I want to be precise about the mechanism, because this is where people misunderstand it.

It is not that the lived legacy competes with the intended one and sometimes wins. It is that the intended one has not begun yet. A family can spend a decade perfecting a document that has not moved a single thing, while the thing that is actually moving goes entirely unexamined.

And the two are often far apart without anyone lying to anyone.

A man intends to pass on generosity. He has arranged significant charitable giving at the end of his life, carefully structured, and he means every bit of it. His children have never once seen him give anything, because he has always given quietly and privately, on principle.

What is he passing on? Not generosity. Discretion, maybe. Privacy. Possibly the impression that this family keeps money to itself.

Nothing about his intention was wrong. It simply never got transmitted, because transmission requires witnesses.

Paul says something in this verse that sounds arrogant until you understand what he is doing. Be imitators of me. He is not claiming to be exemplary. He is acknowledging something about how people actually learn, which is that they copy somebody, and the only question is who.

Your family is imitating something. If you have not decided what, they are copying whatever is most visible, and what is most visible is not your intentions.

So here is the practical move, and it is smaller than it sounds. Where there is a gap between your intended legacy and your lived one, you do not have to change the intention. You have to make it visible.

Let them see the giving. Let them be in the room for the decision. Say why, out loud, once.

The intention was never the problem. The privacy was.

2. What is your lived one, as somebody watching would describe it?

4. What have you kept private that would have been worth letting your family see?

Day 4

From the Outside, Everything Looked Strong

The deeper elements hadn't been clearly passed on

"God sees not as man sees, for man looks at the outward appearance, but the Lord looks at the heart." 1 SAMUEL 16:7

He had built a substantial company and handed it to his son. By every measure available, the file was closed. The business had grown. The succession was done, and done well. Nothing in the numbers needed attention.

From the outside, everything looked strong.

Then, in conversation, he described a set of projects he had taken up in some struggling neighborhoods near where the company was headquartered. Places that stood to gain far more from his presence and his leadership than from a check.

He said the company was no longer his day job. This was his day job. This was his passion.

There was nothing in any standard process that would have surfaced that. There is no line on any form for what a person intends to do with the second half of their influence.

I tell that story at the front of this because it makes a point that is otherwise hard to hear.

A family can look completely successful and still not have passed on the things that matter most. Not because anything went wrong. Because the things that look like success are all measurable, and the deeper elements are not, so nobody checks.

The business grew. The plan is sound. The children are educated and employed and pleasant at Christmas. Every visible indicator is green.

And nobody in that family could tell you what it is for.

I have come to think that the outwardly strongest families are actually at the greatest risk here, and the reason is simple. Nothing forces the conversation. A family in difficulty gets pushed into honesty by circumstance. A family where everything is working can go thirty years without anybody raising a question, because raising one would seem ungrateful.

Samuel is told that God does not see as people see, that people look at the outward appearance and the Lord looks at the heart. It is said at a moment when Samuel is about to pick the wrong person for exactly that reason, because the obvious candidate looked right.

Your family has an outward appearance. Most likely it is a good one, or you would not have picked up a book like this.

The question is whether anyone has looked underneath it.

Day 5

Something Is Always Being Passed On

What else are we passing on?

"We will not conceal them from their children, but tell to the generation to come the praises of the Lord." PSALM 78:4

Here is where the first session lands.

Something is always being passed on. That is not a goal to work toward. It is a description of what is already happening, and it happens whether or not anyone is paying attention.

So the useful question is not whether you have a legacy. You do. The question Tom asks families early is simply this.

What else are we passing on?

Not just assets. Attitudes. Values. Patterns. The things nobody put in a document because nobody thought of them as transferable.

And they are the most transferable things in the house.

Attitudes transfer almost perfectly. How your family talks about people who are not in the room. What gets laughed at. Whether disagreement is safe. Those get absorbed completely and they are almost never discussed.

Patterns transfer even more reliably, because they are practiced rather than taught. How your family handles conflict. Whether hard things get said or avoided. What happens after somebody is wrong. Your children have run those patterns a thousand times and most of them will run the same ones.

Values are the interesting category, because families usually do have values and usually have never named them. They exist as a set of assumptions everyone operates from and nobody has said out loud, which means they get passed on in a slightly different form to each person.

That is the whole reason this series exists. Not to help you create a legacy. You have one. To help you look at it, name it, and decide which parts you want to continue.

The psalm says we will not conceal them from their children but will tell the generation to come. Notice the verb. Tell. Not model, not hope, not arrange. Tell.

Modeling is doing most of the work already, whether you meant it to or not. What is usually missing is the telling, which is the part that turns a pattern into something a person can choose rather than just inherit.

That is the first session. Legacy is not later, it is now. It is not what you intended, it is what was lived. And it is already happening.

The rest of this book is about what to do with that.

END OF SESSION 1

Five days on what a legacy actually is. If you can now say the difference between what you intend to leave and what you are currently passing on, the session did its job.

Next session goes underneath that and asks a question most families have never been asked. Not what is your family passing on, but what is your family actually for.

BUILD NOTES

Front matter and Session 1 complete. Approximately 4,400 words. Days average 740, in line with the other five books.

Days 1 through 5 carry from the coaching journey. All five are family-viable as written and they are the strongest stretch in that book.

Days 6 through 30 were rebuilt. Two reasons. The coaching Overview previews the other five titles, so its Sessions 3 through 6 are compressed versions of Clarity, Alignment, Communication, and Next Generation, which would duplicate four books a family already owns. And the family Overview guide deliberately does not follow that structure. It builds a foundation instead: what legacy is, what a family is for, what you are living now, wealth and wisdom, why families drift, and the path ahead. The devotional now matches the guide.

This is the weakest-sourced book in the series and it should be said plainly. The Overview was never filmed, so unlike Clarity, Alignment, Meetings, and Next Generation, there is no transcript behind it. The coaching titles are descriptive fragments rather than Tom quotes, which is a visible drop in quality against Deep Waters, The Golden Goose, and The Fifth Child. The replacement titles for Days 6 through 30 draw on the Workbook Legend, which is sourced, and on filmed material from the other four books where the teaching overlaps.

Five collisions were avoided. Not to Force Agreement But to Build Understanding is Communication Day 6. Uncomfortable With Silence overlaps Communication Day 13. A Blessing and Not a Curse is Clarity Day 16. Shirt Sleeves to Shirt Sleeves is Clarity Day 17. Three Parts of Your Family Legacy is Next Generation Day 8.

Scripture. NASB. Ecclesiastes 2:18-19, Psalm 90:12, 1 Corinthians 11:1, 1 Samuel 16:7, Psalm 78:4. No collisions with the other five books.

A translation conflict to settle. The coaching Overview journey's copyright page states that Scripture quotations are NIV. The published Family Book states NASB. All six family devotionals and all six family guides have been built NASB. One of those two has to change.

Day 4 is the same story as Clarity Day 4, told from a different angle. Clarity uses it for the man who found his real work. Here it is used for the family that looked strong from the outside. Same source, different point, and worth confirming you want it twice across the series.
