SESSION 4

Teaching Them to Handle It

Stewardship means management, not giving

Day 16

The Church Has Ruined the Word Stewardship

It means management. It doesn't mean giving.

"As each one has received a special gift, employ it in serving one another as good stewards of the multifaceted grace of God." 1 PETER 4:10

I am going to say something that sounds harsh about the church, and I say it as somebody who loves the church.

The church has ruined the word stewardship.

We have stewardship campaigns. We have stewardship Sundays. And because of that, an entire generation hears the word and thinks it means giving, specifically giving to the church, specifically right now.

Look it up. Stewardship means management.

It does not mean giving. Giving is one thing a steward does with what they manage, and it is not the whole job.

Stewardship is managing everything you have been entrusted with. Your money. Your time. Your relationships. Your health. Your influence. All of it.

I care about this distinction because of what it does to how families teach their children.

If stewardship means giving, then teaching stewardship means teaching a child to put something in an envelope. That is good and it is one lesson.

If stewardship means management, then teaching stewardship means teaching a child to handle what they have been given, which is a far larger curriculum and one that occupies years rather than an afternoon.

Here is the practical consequence. Families who hold the narrow definition teach generosity and assume competence will develop on its own. It does not. Generosity without management produces people who give impulsively and cannot sustain it.

And management without generosity produces something worse, which is competence in the service of accumulation.

Peter says each has received a gift and should employ it as a good steward. Notice that the gift is described as received rather than earned, and the response is described as employment rather than preservation.

That is stewardship in a sentence. Something was given. Do something with it. You will be asked about what you did.

So when this session talks about teaching your children stewardship, do not hear it as teaching them to tithe.

Hear it as teaching them to manage, which begins long before there is anything significant to manage.

Day 17

Only Five Things You Can Do With Your Money

That is the whole list

"There is precious treasure and oil in the dwelling of the wise, but a foolish person swallows it up." PROVERBS 21:20

There are only five things you can do with money.

You can live on it. You can give it away. You can pay taxes with it. You can repay debt. You can save and invest it.

That is the complete list. There is no sixth.

I teach this to families because it makes an overwhelming subject tractable. Five categories is a number a person can hold in their head, and a young person who learns the five at twenty has a framework for the rest of their life.

Here is what the framework does that a budget does not.

A budget tells you where money went. The five tell you what money is for. Those are different, and the second one is what produces decisions rather than reports.

And it makes the trade-offs visible. Every dollar goes into exactly one of the five, which means increasing any one requires decreasing another. That is obvious when stated and it is invisible in practice, because families experience the categories separately.

Somebody wants to give more and does not want to spend less. That is not a plan. It is a wish, and the five make it apparent immediately.

Now, for teaching a young person, there is a specific advantage. The five work at any scale. A twelve-year-old with an allowance has exactly the same five categories as a family with a business, and the proportions they establish at twelve tend to persist.

That is the whole reason to teach it early. Not because the amounts matter at twelve. Because the ratios do, and ratios are what scale.

Proverbs contrasts a wise household with precious treasure and oil against a foolish person who swallows it up. The word swallows suggests consumption without thought rather than extravagance.

That is what happens in the absence of a framework. Not reckless spending. Just everything flowing into lifestyle by default, because lifestyle is the category that fills whatever the others do not claim.

So teach the five. And then let a young person actually allocate something, so the framework has a chance to become a habit rather than an idea.

Day 18

Pay God First, Pay Yourself Second

And live on the rest

"Honor the Lord from your wealth and from the first of all your produce; so your barns will be filled with plenty." PROVERBS 3:9-10

Larry Burkett was at the beginning of Christian financial teaching, and he described something simple enough for a child.

Three jars. Giving. Saving. Spending.

You get an allowance. Ten percent goes in the giving jar. Some portion goes in long-term savings. The rest you can spend.

Pay God first. Pay yourself second. Live on the rest.

I still run my own business that way. Ten percent to giving. Ten percent to savings. The company operates on the remainder.

It is not sophisticated and that is the point. The reason it works is the order, not the percentages.

Here is why order matters more than amount.

If giving comes last, it comes from what is left, and what is left is determined by everything else, which means giving is the variable that absorbs every other decision. In a good year it grows. In a tight year it disappears. And over time the pattern communicates that giving is optional in a way that lifestyle is not.

If giving comes first, everything else is calibrated to what remains, and lifestyle becomes the variable rather than the constant.

The same is true of savings, one position down.

Now I want to be honest about the difficulty. Reordering this in an established household is hard, because lifestyle expands to fill available space and contracting it is genuinely painful.

Which is exactly why teaching it to a young person is worth so much. At twenty, the order can be set before anything has expanded. They will not experience it as sacrifice because they will never have known the alternative.

Proverbs says honor the Lord from the first of your produce. The word first is doing all the work in that sentence. Not from what remains after the operation is funded.

And there is a promise attached, which I hold loosely rather than transactionally. It is not a guarantee of returns. It is a description of how a life ordered that way tends to go.

Three jars. Any age. Start with whoever is youngest.

Day 19

Every Dollar You Save, I Will Match It

But the money was there

"Wealth obtained by fraud dwindles, but the one who gathers by labor increases it." PROVERBS 13:11

My father ran a franchise of a sewer service. He was not a wealthy man and he was not a finance person.

But he made me an offer when I was young. Every dollar you save, I will match it.

So I saved. And at fifteen I bought my first car with cash. A Renault, three hundred and forty-two dollars.

I want to draw out what that arrangement actually taught me, because it was not primarily about money.

The first thing is that saving produced something visible. Not a number in an account. A car in the driveway that I had bought.

The second is that I had a goal. I did not want to ride the bus anymore. That is not a noble motivation and it worked, and I have come to think that the nobility of the goal matters far less than the existence of one.

The third is that my father participated without providing. He did not buy me a car. He made my own effort go further, which is a completely different lesson than generosity.

That distinction is the whole day.

There is an enormous difference between giving a young person something and increasing the return on what they do themselves. The first teaches that things arrive. The second teaches that effort compounds, and it teaches it experientially rather than as a lecture.

My father also paid fifty percent of my college. I worked and paid the other half. Part of that was necessity, because he did not have much. But it was valuable to me, and I did not get into a lot of bad things because I did not have time. I had to go to work.

Proverbs contrasts wealth obtained quickly with wealth gathered by labor, and it says the second increases while the first dwindles.

It is not a moral claim about the money. It is an observation about the person. Somebody who gathered something has become a gatherer, and the becoming is what persists.

The matching arrangement is available at any income. It costs whatever you decide it costs, and it can start this month.

Day 20

The More You Do for Them, the More You Take Away

There needs to be some kind of co-ownership

"For each one will bear his own load." GALATIANS 6:5

I am going to say something strong, and I mean it as strongly as it sounds.

When you have the resources and you can do everything for your kids and get them everything they want, it is a bad decision for you to do that.

That is not a warning about spoiling anybody in the ordinary sense. It is about what capability is and how it develops.

Every task you do for a young person is a task they do not learn. Every problem you solve is a problem they do not develop the capacity to solve. And when the resources are ample, the temptation to solve is constant, because solving is easy and watching somebody struggle is not.

So the more you do for them, the more you take away from them.

What you are removing is not comfort. It is competence, and competence only develops through the friction you are relieving.

There needs to be some kind of co-ownership.

That phrase is worth sitting with, because it names the middle position. Not doing everything, which produces dependence. Not withholding everything, which is neither loving nor necessary.

Co-ownership means they have a real stake and so do you. They carry something and you carry something, and the something they carry has actual weight.

The practical shape varies enormously with age and circumstance. A share of the cost. A portion of the work. The consequence if it goes badly. What matters is that it is real rather than symbolic, because young people can detect a symbolic stake instantly and it teaches them nothing.

Paul says each will bear his own load. It sits a few verses after an instruction to bear one another's burdens, which appears to contradict it and does not.

Two different words. A burden is something crushing that a person cannot carry alone. A load is the ordinary weight of your own life, and Paul says each person carries that one.

Removing somebody's load in the name of love is not what that verse describes. It is a category error with good intentions.

2. What capability has never developed because you relieved the friction?

3. Where could you set up co-ownership instead?

4. What is a burden and what is a load in your family?

SESSION 5

Helping Them Find Their Calling

Freedom to be different from you

Day 21

Freedom to Be Different Than You

Not the box mom and dad created

"I will praise You, for I am fearfully and wonderfully made." PSALM 139:14

The first thing I want to say about calling is the thing parents find hardest.

Give your children the freedom to be different than you are.

Not trying to put them in the box mom and dad created.

How many people say, well, dad was a lawyer, so I need to be a lawyer. Maybe you are not wired to be a lawyer.

I want to be fair to the parents in this, because the box is rarely built deliberately.

It is built by what got praised. By what was described as a real job. By the stories told at dinner about which paths were serious. By a hundred small signals delivered over twenty years, none of which was intended as an instruction.

And then a child chooses accordingly and everyone believes it was their own decision.

Here is what happens when somebody is in the wrong box. They comply for a while, because compliance is easier than the conversation. And then at some point they step back and say, I should have done this five years ago, because I knew it was not for me.

I see this most in family businesses. The parents want the child in it, and the child does not want to be, and he is only there because he feels he has to. That is not going to help the business, and it is going to cost him years.

It is better to find somebody who has a passion for that business, even if he is not a family member, and let him lead it. The family can still own it.

The psalm says I am fearfully and wonderfully made. It is usually read as reassurance and it is also a statement about specificity.

Made. Deliberately, particularly, as this rather than as something else.

Which means a child who does not fit your box is not failing to become what they should be. They are becoming what they were made to be, and the box was never the standard.

Your job is to find out what that is. Not to assign it.

Day 22

What Are They Naturally Drawn To

Observation before direction

"Train up a child in the way he should go, and even when he is old he will not depart from it." PROVERBS 22:6

If your job is not to assign a calling, then what is it?

Observation.

What are they good at? What do they love? Those are not always the same thing and both matter. Are they an introvert or an extrovert? What kind of things interest them? What are they naturally drawn to?

That last question is the most useful one I know, and it is answered by watching rather than by asking.

Here is why watching beats asking, especially with young people. Ask a fifteen-year-old what they want to do and you will get an answer shaped by what sounds acceptable. Watch a fifteen-year-old for six months and you will see what they do when nobody has assigned anything.

That is the data.

What do they do with unstructured time. What do they talk about without being prompted. What are they doing when they lose track of the clock. Who do they gravitate toward.

I have a granddaughter who went to school to write songs. Her father is a lawyer. Nobody in that family had a template for what she was doing, and her father understood something about her that let him back it rather than redirect it.

He had watched.

Now the verse, because it is one of the most misused in Scripture.

Train up a child in the way he should go. That is usually read as train a child in the right way, meaning the correct moral path, with a promise attached that it will stick.

There is another reading that Hebrew scholars have argued for a long time, and it is closer to train a child according to his way. According to his bent. The particular way he is made.

I am not competent to settle the translation and I will say this. The second reading matches what I have observed in families for thirty years far better than the first one does.

Children trained against their bent depart. Children trained according to it tend to hold.

So watch first. The training comes after the observing, and it is shaped by what you saw.

Day 23

Their Biggest Cheerleader

It takes courage and faith in your child

"Therefore encourage one another and build one another up, just as you also are doing." 1 THESSALONIANS 5:11

Once you have observed, there is a decision, and it takes more than most parents expect.

You have to have the courage, and the faith in your child, to let them go a different direction if that is the way God is leading them.

And then you come alongside as their biggest cheerleader.

I want to be honest about why this is hard, because it is not usually stubbornness.

You want them to have a better life than you had. And you have an idea in your mind of what that looks like, built from your own experience, which is the only data you have. It is not necessarily their idea and it may not be right for them, and none of that makes your version unloving.

But there is a difference between wanting good for somebody and wanting a specific good for them, and the second one can prevent the first.

Here is what cheerleading actually requires, and it is more than approval.

It requires you to be enthusiastic about something you would not have chosen. Not tolerant. Enthusiastic. Children can distinguish those instantly and permission with reservations attached is not permission.

It requires you to defend the choice to other people. Extended family, friends, whoever asks what he is doing now. How you answer that question in a room your child is not in gets back to them.

And it requires you to keep doing it when it is going badly, which is when cheerleading actually matters and when the temptation to say something is strongest.

Paul says encourage one another and build one another up. The word build implies construction over time rather than a single act of affirmation.

That is what a cheerleader is in a family. Not somebody who praised a decision once. Somebody who kept building through the part where it was not obviously working.

Most young people can name whether they had one.

Day 24

Maybe He's Never Going to Find Your Purpose

God cares more about your kids than you do

"For My thoughts are not your thoughts, nor are your ways My ways." ISAIAH 55:8

Here is something I say to worried parents, and it usually lands as a relief rather than a rebuke.

Parents always want their kids to end up better than themselves. That may not be God's plan.

God's plan may be to use your child in a totally different direction than how you have been used. It might be that He wants them working among poor people. And you have planned your whole life so your kids would never be poor.

Well, that may be the best thing for them.

I say that carefully because it can sound like a dismissal of legitimate concern, and I do not mean it that way. Parents worry for reasons and the reasons are usually good.

But there is a specific worry I want to address, and it is the one that sounds like this. He is never going to find his purpose.

Maybe he is never going to find your purpose for his life. He may well find the one God has for him, and those may not be the same thing, and you may not recognize the second one when it arrives.

That is the sentence I would want a worried parent to hold.

And here is the thing underneath it that produces the actual relief.

God is involved with your kids. He cares more about your children than you do, which is a difficult sentence to accept and is true. Your care for them is enormous and it is finite and it is not the primary care operating in their life.

That does not remove your responsibility. It relocates the weight of it.

Isaiah says my thoughts are not your thoughts and my ways are not your ways. It is spoken as comfort in a passage about mercy rather than as a rebuke about presumption.

The relevance here is direct. Your assessment of what a good life looks like for your child is a human assessment, made with partial information, by somebody who loves them and cannot see the whole thing.

It might be right. It might be small.

Either way it is not the only assessment being made, and it is not the governing one.

Day 25

Sometimes God Tells You What You Shouldn't Be Doing

The thing you ought to be doing is praying

"The mind of a person plans his way, but the Lord directs his steps." PROVERBS 16:9

This is the last day of the calling session, and it corrects an assumption most people carry.

Sometimes God tells you what you should not be doing, more clearly than He tells you what you should be doing.

How many pastors said to their father, I do not know what I want to do, but I do not want to be a pastor? And how many of them ended up being pastors?

I raise it because young people are frequently paralyzed waiting for a positive answer, and the negative answers they are receiving do not register as guidance.

But they are guidance, and they are usually clearer.

I had a friend whose son agreed to work with him for two years as a test, with an agreement at the front that either of them could conclude it was not right. At the end of two years he decided it was not, and everybody was fine with it, and he went and did the other thing.

Nothing failed there. He learned something definitive that he could not have learned any other way, and the arrangement made the learning safe.

Another friend's son spent a single day with a real estate developer and came away knowing it was not for him. One day. That is not indecision, that is data.

So the practical guidance for a young person is to try things with the explicit understanding that finding out it is not for you is a successful outcome rather than a failure.

And the practical guidance for a parent is to design the trying so that it can end without shame.

Now the last piece, and it is the most important one in this session.

The thing you ought to be doing for your kids is praying. Praying that God would make it clear to them what they are supposed to do.

I believe God answers prayer. And if a child has an interest, you can say to them, can we pray about that? See if that is really what God has for you.

That is a great form of listening. It takes their interest seriously enough to bring it to God rather than to evaluate it.

Proverbs says a person plans his way and the Lord directs his steps. Both are in the sentence. Planning is not futile and it is also not final.

SESSION 6

Growing the Whole Person

Six areas, not one

Day 26

Six Areas of My Life

I can handle six. I can't handle nine.

"And Jesus kept increasing in wisdom and stature, and in favor with God and men." LUKE 2:52

I have lived my whole life around six areas.

My spiritual life. My family life. My financial life. My physical life, which is health and exercise. My vocational life. And my social life, which is friendships and relationships.

Some people have more areas than that. For me, I can handle six. I cannot handle nine.

I offer that not as a system but as an example of something every person needs and most do not have, which is a frame for looking at a whole life at once.

Here is what happens without one.

You track the area that gives feedback. For most people that is vocational, because work produces measurable results on a schedule. Financial is second, because there are statements.

The other four produce almost no feedback at all. Nobody sends a quarterly report on your spiritual life. Your friendships do not generate a statement. Your health gives feedback slowly and then suddenly.

So attention flows to the areas that report, and the areas that do not report quietly deteriorate, and the deterioration is invisible until it is not.

A frame fixes that. Not because it produces feedback, but because it forces you to look at things that would not otherwise ask to be looked at.

And here is why this belongs in a book about the next generation.

Families prepare children financially and vocationally, because those are the two areas with visible outcomes. A child who is doing well in those two is described as doing well.

Which means a young person can be entirely unprepared in four of six areas and receive no signal about it from anyone, until something breaks.

Luke says Jesus increased in wisdom and stature and in favor with God and men. That is four areas, named as growing simultaneously in a single sentence about a young person.

Not one. Four, and the writer thought it was worth recording that they moved together.

That is the standard for whole-person development, and it is older than any of our frameworks.

Day 27

Rate Yourself Zero to Ten

Then we look at it again next time

"Test yourselves to see if you are in the faith; examine yourselves." 2 CORINTHIANS 13:5

Here is the tool, and it is one sheet of paper.

I give it to somebody every time we get together. Rate yourself on a scale of zero to ten in each of the six areas.

Then the next time we meet, I give them another blank sheet. Rate yourself again.

And I say, last time you said you were a four. Now you say six. What changed? Let us celebrate that.

And they tell me. Well, I started listening to this Bible app and it really helped.

That is the whole instrument. There is nothing else to it.

I want to explain why the second sheet matters more than the first, because families who try this usually do it once.

A single rating is a snapshot and it is not very useful. People do not know how to calibrate, and a four means different things to different people, and there is no way to interpret it.

The second rating creates a comparison, and the comparison is where everything happens. Not because the number moved, but because the person has to explain the movement, and the explanation is the actual content.

And notice the direction of the conversation. I am not assessing them. They rated themselves. I am asking about their own account of their own change, which means nothing is being imposed and nothing has to be defended.

That is why it works with young people who would resist anything that felt like evaluation.

There is one rule I would add. Do not respond to a low number with concern. The moment a four produces alarm, the next sheet will not have any fours on it.

Ask about it the same way you would ask about an eight.

Paul says test yourselves and examine yourselves. Both verbs are reflexive. He does not say examine one another, which he could have.

Self-examination is being described as a normal practice rather than an emergency response, and it is being asked of everybody rather than of the struggling.

One sheet. Six numbers. Then again in six months.

Day 28

Everything Flows Out of Your Spiritual Life

It's not the church's job. It's your job.

"Beloved, I pray that in all respects you may prosper and be in good health, just as your soul prospers." 3 JOHN 2

If I had to pick one of the six to start with, I would dip into the spiritual life.

Because everything else flows out of it. If God is not part of your life, you are basically living for the wrong stuff.

And I ask about it directly. Do you have devotions? How many times a week? What are you reading?

I asked a high school student once whether he was a morning person or a night person, and he told me when he read, and that told me more than a general question about his spiritual life ever would.

The specificity is the point. General questions get general answers.

Now here is the part that lands hardest with parents, and I say it without softening.

It is not the church's job. It is your job.

Families delegate this more thoroughly than they realize. There is a youth pastor. There is a school, possibly a Christian one. There are programs, and the programs are good, and the cumulative effect is that a parent can feel the area is covered while never having had a spiritual conversation with their own child.

I am not against any of those institutions. I am saying they were never designed to carry the primary load and they cannot.

The verse in 3 John is a greeting, and it contains a striking assumption. I pray you may prosper and be in health, just as your soul prospers.

The soul's prosperity is the benchmark against which the rest is measured. Not the other way around.

Read that against how most families assess how somebody is doing. We look at the work, the finances, the health, the relationships, and we conclude a person is doing well. The soul is the one we would have to ask about, and asking feels intrusive even inside a family.

So ask. Specifically rather than generally.

And notice that you cannot ask somebody about a thing you are not doing yourself, or rather you can, and they will know.

Day 29

He Knew All the Answers and Still Asked

That's how you build a relationship

"They found Him in the temple, sitting in the midst of the teachers, both listening to them and asking them questions." LUKE 2:46

How do you build a relationship? You ask people questions.

I want to end the practical part of this book there, because after thirty years it is the thing I would most want a family to take.

Not a framework. Not a structure. Questions.

Here is the observation underneath it. People love to talk about themselves and hardly anybody asks them to. Ask somebody a thought-provoking question and they think, why is he asking me that. And they conclude that you care. That you want to know what they have to say.

The question does that work before a single answer arrives, which means even the questions that go nowhere are not wasted.

Now the verse, because it is the strangest illustration of this in Scripture.

Jesus at twelve, in the temple, sitting among the teachers. And Luke says he was doing two things. Listening to them, and asking them questions.

Consider who is asking. If anyone in that room had grounds to skip the questions and simply teach, it was him.

He asked anyway.

I do not think that is modesty or technique. I think it tells us something about what questions are for. They are not primarily a way of acquiring information you lack. They are how you are present with somebody.

Which reframes what it means to ask your adult children things. You are not conducting an assessment. You are not gathering data to act on. You are demonstrating, in the only way available, that you want to know them.

And here is what makes it urgent rather than merely nice.

The window is finite. Your children are forming a view of whether you were interested in them, and they are forming it now, from the questions you ask and the ones you do not.

That view will be settled long before you are gone, and it will be very hard to revise afterward.

So ask. About their work, their marriage, their doubts, what they are hoping for, what they are afraid of.

You know most of the answers. Ask anyway.

Day 30

Who Is Developing Your Children?

God has done a good job on my kids in spite of me

"Behold, children are a gift of the Lord, the fruit of the womb is a reward." PSALM 127:3

This is the last day of the last book, and it comes down to a question.

Who is developing your children?

Is it you? Is social media developing your children? Are their friends? A boyfriend or a girlfriend?

Somebody is. Development is not optional and it does not pause while a family gets organized. The only question is whose.

I ask it as a question rather than an accusation because the honest answer in most families is a mix, and the mix is not something anybody chose.

You are doing some of it. Their peers are doing a great deal of it. The culture is doing more than anybody would like. And the proportions were set by default rather than by decision.

Naming the proportions is most of the work. A parent who knows they are contributing twenty percent can do something about it. A parent who assumes they are the primary influence and is not will do nothing, because there is no problem visible.

Now, having said all of that, I want to end somewhere other than responsibility, because responsibility alone will crush a person.

God has done a good job on my kids in spite of me.

I mean that. I have four children, I have been at this a long time, and I made a great many mistakes that I can name specifically. Some of them were significant. And they turned out well, and the arithmetic of that does not work if it was all down to me.

So hold both. Your influence is real and it matters and you should use it deliberately.

And you are not the only thing operating in their lives, and their outcome is not entirely a report card on your performance.

Long-term impact is not shaped by what somebody has. It is shaped by who they are becoming.

The psalm calls children a gift and a reward. Not a project and not an outcome. A gift, which is something you receive rather than something you produce.

That is the frame I would want you to close on. Thirty days of preparation, deliberateness, and responsibility, held inside the knowledge that they were given to you rather than made by you.

Do the work. Then trust the One who cares about them more than you do.
