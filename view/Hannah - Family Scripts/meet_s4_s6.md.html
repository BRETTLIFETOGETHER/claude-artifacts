SESSION 4

What to Talk About

Keeping the conversation on what matters

Day 16

Every Conversation Expands

You need to bring that back

"Let your eyes look directly ahead and let your gaze be fixed straight in front of you." PROVERBS 4:25

Every conversation has a natural tendency to expand.

That is not a criticism of anyone. It is a property of conversation. Somebody says something related, somebody else adds to it, a memory surfaces, and twenty minutes later you are somewhere entirely different from where you started.

And nothing felt wrong along the way. Every step was reasonable. That is precisely why drift is hard to catch.

Here is the shape of it in a family meeting.

You set out to talk about one thing. Ten minutes in, somebody mentions something adjacent, and it matters to them, so you address it. Somebody else responds to that. Now there is a second conversation running, and the first one has not been finished, and the time is going.

At the end everybody feels like the meeting was full and nobody can say what was accomplished.

So somebody has to bring it back, and the timing is the whole skill.

If I were leading, we would not be twenty minutes into a sidetrack. We would be about five minutes in, and then I would bring it back.

Five minutes is enough to honor what somebody raised. Twenty minutes means you have changed the subject.

Now, the reason families do not do this is not that they cannot see it happening. They can. The reason is that bringing it back feels rude, especially if the person who took it sideways cares about what they raised.

Which is why the move has to be gracious rather than corrective. That is an important point and I want us to come back to it, and right now I want to make sure we finish this.

That sentence does the whole job. It acknowledges rather than dismisses, and it returns.

Proverbs says let your eyes look directly ahead and your gaze be fixed in front of you. The next verse says to watch the path of your feet.

It is written about moral direction and it describes attention. Something is in front of you, and a great deal else is available in the periphery, and the instruction is to keep looking at the thing.

Not because the periphery is bad. Because you cannot arrive anywhere while looking at it.

Reflect

1. How quickly do your family conversations drift?

2. Who tends to take them sideways, and do they know?

3. Have you ever brought one back, and how did it land?

4. What has never been finished because a conversation expanded?

Day 17

If You Ignore It, People Feel Dismissed

If you follow it too far, you're going to lose focus

"Let your speech always be with grace, seasoned as it were with salt." COLOSSIANS 4:6

There are two ways to handle a sidetrack and both of them are wrong.

If you ignore it, people feel dismissed. If you follow it too far, you lose focus.

Most people have a default, and the default is usually determined by temperament rather than by judgment.

Some of us ignore. We are focused, we know what we came to do, and when somebody raises something off-topic we move past it politely. What we do not see is what that costs. The person who raised it now knows that their contribution was an obstacle, and they will contribute less next time, and eventually not at all.

Some of us follow. We are relational, we care about the person, and following the tangent feels like honoring them. What we do not see is that the meeting becomes a series of tangents and everybody leaves having accomplished nothing, and over time people stop coming because these gatherings do not go anywhere.

The middle is not a compromise between the two. It is a specific move.

You acknowledge it, and then you bring it back.

That is an important point and I want to make sure we come back to it. Let me note it down.

Then you write it down, visibly, where people can see you doing it. That last detail matters more than it should. When somebody watches their point get written down, they let it go, because it has been received rather than deflected.

And then you actually come back to it, either later in that conversation or in the next one. If you do not, you have used a technique on somebody, and they will notice the second time.

Paul says let your speech be with grace, seasoned with salt. Salt preserves and it also has flavor and bite. It is not a description of blandness.

Gracious speech in this context is not softness. It is the ability to redirect somebody without diminishing them, and it requires both the grace and the salt.

Brett describes it as being a nice pain in the neck, which is about right. You are trying to get people back where they need to go, and doing it in such a way that they do not resent you by the end.

Day 18

Being a Nice Pain in the Neck

So you don't hate me by the time it's over

"Faithful are the wounds of a friend, but deceitful are the kisses of an enemy." PROVERBS 27:6

Brett has a phrase for what holding a family conversation actually requires, and I have adopted it.

Being a nice pain in the neck.

I am trying to get you back where you need to go, and I am doing it in a way where you do not hate me by the time it is over.

Both halves of that are load-bearing.

The pain in the neck part is unavoidable. Somebody in the room has to be willing to interrupt, redirect, ask the follow-up, and say we are not finished with that. None of those are comfortable and all of them cost you something in the moment.

The nice part is what makes it sustainable. If the redirecting is done sharply, you can only do it a few times before people stop showing up. If it is done graciously, you can do it indefinitely and people will thank you afterward.

Here is what I want to say to whoever is going to be that person in your family, because it is usually one person and frequently they did not volunteer.

You will be, at times, mildly resented in the moment and appreciated afterward. That gap is uncomfortable and it does not close. Somebody has to be willing to live in it.

And you will occasionally get it wrong. You will redirect something that should have been followed, or follow something that should have been redirected. That is not a reason to stop. It is a reason to say afterward that you got it wrong, which incidentally does more for your standing than getting it right would have.

Proverbs says the wounds of a friend are faithful while the kisses of an enemy are deceitful. It is a startling pairing, because it puts wounding on the side of loyalty.

The claim is not that hurting people is good. It is that a friend who will say the uncomfortable thing is more valuable than somebody who will only ever affirm you, and the second one is described as an enemy.

Most families have plenty of the second and very little of the first.

So if you are going to be the nice pain in the neck in your family, understand what you are signing up for. It is a form of faithfulness and it will not always feel like one.

Reflect

4. Who has wounded you faithfully, and were you grateful later?

Day 19

Said in Public, Addressed in Public

Being direct, truthful, and respectful

"If your brother sins, go and show him his fault in private." MATTHEW 18:15

There is a principle about where a thing gets handled, and families get it backwards constantly.

What is said in public needs to be addressed in public. How you handle a person is often better done in private.

Those are two halves and both matter.

The first half. If somebody says something in a family gathering that needs a response, respond there. Do not let it sit unaddressed and then handle it in the car afterward, because everyone in that room heard it and everyone will draw a conclusion from the silence.

The conclusion they draw is usually that it was acceptable, or that nobody was willing to engage it, and both of those shape what happens next time.

The second half. If the issue is with a person rather than with a statement, take it to them privately.

I had a situation where somebody was consistently taking a meeting off course. The right move was not to address it in the room. It was to take a break, pull him aside, and ask him to help me get the others engaged.

Now he had a job. And the job was the opposite of dominating.

That got it off the hot seat entirely. Nobody was confronted in front of the family, and the behavior changed, and he did not spend the rest of the day defending himself.

Jesus is explicit about the private version. If your brother sins against you, go to him privately. Not to the family, not to a sibling first. To him.

The instruction is not primarily about kindness, though it is kind. It is about preserving the possibility of resolution. A thing raised privately can be settled. A thing raised publicly requires somebody to lose face, and people who are losing face do not settle, they defend.

Families reverse this constantly. They handle statements privately, which teaches the room that difficult things go unaddressed, and they handle people publicly, which guarantees defensiveness.

Getting the two the right way round costs nothing and changes a great deal.

Reflect

1. What has been said in your family gatherings and never addressed?

2. What conclusion did the room draw from the silence?

3. Who have you addressed in front of others when it should have been private?

4. Which way round does your family default?

Day 20

Five Minutes Into the Sidetrack

The purpose is the anchor

"Commit your works to the Lord and your plans will be established." PROVERBS 16:3

This is the last day of the session, and it comes back to the thing everything else depends on.

The purpose is the anchor.

If I were leading, we would be five minutes into the sidetrack, not twenty. And the reason I would know is that I would have the purpose in front of me.

Without a purpose, there is no way to tell whether a conversation is drifting or developing. Those two look identical from inside. Something new has come up and it is interesting and people are engaged.

The only difference is whether it serves what you came to do, and you cannot answer that unless you named what you came to do.

So the discipline is upstream of the meeting entirely.

Before people arrive, you answer one question. What do we want to be different when we leave?

Not what will we discuss. What will be different.

Then the whole thing becomes navigable. Somebody raises something. You hold it against the purpose. If it serves it, follow it. If it does not, acknowledge and return.

That is the entire skill and it is unavailable to a family that has not defined the purpose.

Here is a second use of the anchor that families miss.

The purpose tells you when to stop. Meetings run long because nobody knows what finished looks like, so they continue until people are tired. A meeting with a defined purpose can end early, and ending early is one of the great gifts you can give a family.

Nothing makes people more willing to come to the next one.

Proverbs says commit your works to the Lord and your plans will be established. The order in that sentence is worth noticing, because it is the same order as everything else in this session.

Commit first. The establishing follows.

Most families try to establish first, which is to say they schedule the meeting, gather the people, and then work out what it is for in the room. That is backwards and it is why so many family gatherings feel like effort without result.

Decide what it is for. Then everything else has something to serve.

Reflect

1. What do you want to be different after your next gathering?

2. Could you tell drift from development in your last one?

3. Have you ever ended a family conversation early?

4. What would finished look like?

SESSION 5

Your Values, Mission, and Vision

Keeping the family grounded

Day 21

It's Not All About the Money

Families naturally move toward urgent decisions

"For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also." MATTHEW 6:21

If I could say one thing about this whole subject, it is this.

It is not all about the money.

I say it knowing that most of what I do involves money, and that families come to me because of money, and that a great deal of this series deals with it directly.

And it is still not what this is about.

Here is what happens over time in a family that gathers regularly. The meetings become more tactical. More transactional. More financial. Decisions need making, opportunities arrive, responsibilities have to be assigned, and all of those are legitimate and urgent and they crowd out everything else.

Nobody decides that the family's values will stop being discussed. They simply get displaced, one urgent item at a time, until a year has passed and every gathering has been about a decision.

That is drift at the level of the whole family rather than within a single conversation, and it is much harder to notice.

The corrective is not to talk about money less. It is to make sure something else has protected time.

Because here is what a family loses. The decisions get made and the reasons behind them stop being spoken. And a family that makes good decisions without articulating why is passing on outcomes rather than values, and outcomes do not survive a generation.

Jesus says where your treasure is, your heart will be also. I have quoted it elsewhere in this series and I want to use it differently here.

Read it as a warning about attention rather than about spending. Whatever your family gives its gathered attention to is what your family's heart will follow.

If every time you gather, the subject is money, then over twenty years you have formed a family whose shared attention is entirely financial, regardless of what anybody would say about their values.

That is not a moral failure. It is what happens when urgent things and important things compete for the same hour, which they always do.

So this session is about the hour that is not for decisions.

Day 22

It Requires a Different Kind of Focus

Values aren't clarified in a short segment

"Where there is no vision, the people are unrestrained." PROVERBS 29:18

Values and vision do not get clarified in a short segment of a broader meeting.

They require a different kind of focus, and families consistently underestimate what that means practically.

Here is what usually happens. A family decides that values matter, and they add twenty minutes to the end of an existing meeting. Everyone is already tired. The financial items ran long. And twenty minutes produces a list of words that nobody feels much about.

The exercise is then judged to have not worked, and the conclusion is that this family is not the sort of family that does this.

What actually failed was the container.

This needs its own gathering. A minimum of three hours and probably closer to four or five. A Saturday. A retreat. An overnight if you can manage it.

That sounds like a lot and here is why it takes that long.

The first hour is people being polite. Nothing real happens and it cannot be skipped, because it is the price of the second hour.

The second hour is where people start saying things they actually think, usually because somebody went first and it was received well.

The third hour is where the family finds out something about itself.

You cannot compress that and you cannot start at hour three. Every family that has done this well has sat through the first hour.

The other thing a longer container permits is everyone speaking. In twenty minutes, three people talk. In four hours, the person who never says anything says something, because there is enough space that they are not competing for it.

Proverbs says where there is no vision the people are unrestrained. The word is not about perishing, which is the common rendering. It is closer to being cast off, going in whatever direction was already underway.

Which is exactly what a family without a stated vision does. Not disaster. Just individual drift, at different speeds, in different directions, with nothing holding the shape.

So put a real container around it. It is the single most valuable half-day your family will spend.

4. What would it take to get a Saturday?

Day 23

Write Down the Vision and Make It Plain

It needs to be visible if it's going to guide behavior

"Record the vision and inscribe it on tablets, so that the one who reads it may run." HABAKKUK 2:2

Once a family has done the work, there is a step that determines whether any of it survives.

Write it down and make it plain.

I put the emphasis on plain, because families write things down all the time and produce documents nobody can use.

Plain means somebody who was not in the room could read it and know what your family means. Not evocative. Not clever. Plain.

Here is the test. Take what you wrote and give it to a fourteen-year-old in your family. If they can tell you what it means without asking a question, it is plain. If they cannot, it is a memory aid for the people who were present, which is a different and much less useful thing.

And then the part families skip entirely.

It has to be visible.

A vision in a drawer is not guiding anything. It is like a budget. People make one, put it away, and look at it next year, and in the meantime every decision is made without reference to it.

So decide where it lives. On a wall. In the front of something people open. Wherever your family actually looks.

I have seen a family put their five values on a card and hand one to every household. That is not a design decision. It is the difference between a document and an instrument.

Habakkuk is told to record the vision and inscribe it on tablets, and the reason is given directly. So that the one who reads it may run.

Not so it is preserved. Not so it is official. So that somebody who was not present can act on it without you.

That is the whole point. You are not creating a record of a good weekend. You are making it possible for your grandchildren to know what this family decided, when nobody who was in the room is available to explain it.

Which means the writing is not the end of the exercise. It is the mechanism by which the exercise outlives the people who did it.

Reflect

3. Where does it live, and does anyone see it?

4. Could a fourteen-year-old in your family explain it?

Day 24

Are You Living What You Said You Would Live?

They put it in a drawer

"Prove yourselves doers of the word, and not merely hearers who delude themselves." JAMES 1:22

Here is the question that makes a written set of values worth anything.

Are you living what you said you were going to live?

Families create these and then never look at them again. It is exactly like a budget. Somebody makes one, files it, and comes back a year later to find that nothing was measured against it.

So when the family is together, pull it out. Say, this is what we said we wanted our family to be about. Is that still true? Is there anything we would change?

A lot of the time there is nothing to change. And then comes the real question.

How are we living this out?

I want to describe what that question does in a room, because it is the most productive uncomfortable moment in this whole series.

Say your family named family as a core value. And this year somebody had a health problem, and two of the siblings reached out and two did not.

You raise that. Not as an accusation. This is what we said we believed. How did we do?

Something happens in that moment that does not happen any other way. The value stops being a word and becomes a standard, and standards can be missed, and missing one in the open is how a family actually changes.

Now the caution, and it matters. This is not done from a judgmental position. The moment it becomes a scorecard for who failed, it will not survive its second use, and nobody will ever again agree to write anything down.

The framing is collective. How did we do. Not how did you do.

And whoever raises it should be prepared to be the first example of falling short, because that is what makes it safe for everyone else.

James says be doers and not hearers only, and the phrase he uses for hearers is deluding themselves. Not deceiving others. Deceiving yourself.

A family that has written down beautiful values and never measured against them is in exactly that position, and the delusion is comfortable, which is why it lasts.

Reflect

1. What did your family say it valued?

2. How did you actually do this year?

3. What happened that ran contrary to something you said you believed?

4. Could you raise that without it becoming a scorecard?

Day 25

This Is What We Said We Believed

You're not doing it from a judgmental standpoint

"With all humility and gentleness, with patience, showing tolerance for one another in love." EPHESIANS 4:2

This is the last day of the session, and it is about tone rather than content.

You are not doing this from a judgmental standpoint.

Everything in the last four days can be done in a way that damages a family rather than forming it, and the difference is entirely in how it is held.

Let me describe the failure, because it is common and it is well-intentioned.

A family does the work. They name their values. Somebody, usually the person who cared most about doing it, becomes the keeper of the list. And thereafter every family gathering includes an assessment of how everyone has been measuring up.

Within two years nobody wants to talk about values, and the family concludes that this kind of thing does not work for them.

What went wrong is not the exercise. It is that a shared standard became one person's instrument.

Here is how the same material is held well.

The question is always we, never you. This is what we said we believed, how are we doing. Not, you said you believed this, and look at you.

The person raising it goes first and names their own shortfall. That single move converts the whole thing from an audit to a confession, and confessions are contagious in the right direction.

And the goal is stated out loud, because people will assume the worst if you do not. We are not doing this to catch anybody. We are doing it because we said something and we would like it to be true.

Paul lists humility, gentleness, patience, and tolerance, and then says these are how unity is preserved. It is not a list of nice qualities. It is the specific set required to hold a group of different people together without anyone being crushed.

Notice that patience is on the list. Which implies that people will fall short repeatedly and that the falling short is expected rather than exceptional.

A family that expects to fall short can measure itself honestly. A family that does not will stop measuring in order to stop failing.

That is the end of this session. The last one is about what makes any of it continue.

SESSION 6

Building a Rhythm That Lasts

Staying connected over time

Day 26

A Meeting by Itself Is Just an Event

Rhythm turns an event into something that continues

"Let us consider how to encourage one another in love and good deeds, not forsaking our own assembling together." HEBREWS 10:24-25

A meeting by itself is just an event.

Rhythm is what turns an event into something that continues.

I want to be exact about the distinction, because families hear it as a call to meet more often and that is not quite it.

An event is complete in itself. It happened, it went well or badly, and it is over. Whatever it produced begins decaying immediately.

A rhythm is a set of events that reference each other. The second one picks up something from the first. The third assumes the second. And because they reference each other, they compound rather than decay.

That is the whole difference, and it does not require a high frequency. Two well-connected gatherings a year is a rhythm. Six disconnected ones are six events.

So what connects them?

Three things, and none of them are complicated.

Something carried forward. Somebody says at the start, last time we said we would do this. Even if it did not get done, naming it connects the two.

Something scheduled at the end. The next date is set before people leave. Not proposed. Set.

Something the same. A question that gets asked every time, or an order things happen in, or a place you always meet. Sameness is what lets people arrive knowing how to participate.

Families underestimate that third one badly. Novelty is exhausting and it puts the burden on whoever is organizing. Sameness lets a gathering run itself.

Hebrews says consider how to encourage one another and do not forsake assembling together. The word forsake is strong and it implies abandonment rather than a missed occasion.

What actually happens in most families is not abandonment. It is one postponement, then another, and then a year, and nobody ever decided to stop.

That is why the date at the end matters more than anything else in this session.

Reflect

4. When did your family last set a date before leaving?

Day 27

Not Meant to Be a Burden

The thing is, you need to have at least one

"My yoke is easy and My burden is light." MATTHEW 11:30

Family meetings are not meant to be a burden.

I say that because families read material like this and immediately imagine an obligation they will fail at, and the imagining is enough to prevent them from starting.

So let me be clear about the minimum.

You need to have at least one. That is the requirement. One gathering where you communicate where things are and what the situation is, so that everybody is aware.

Everything beyond that is good and none of it is required.

What a family actually needs varies enormously and it depends mostly on complexity. If there is a business in the middle of it, you will need to meet more often, because there are decisions. If there is not, an annual gathering may be entirely sufficient. Maybe you are together in the summer and again at Christmas, and one of those includes thirty minutes of substance rather than three hours.

That is a legitimate rhythm and it will serve a family well.

Here is what I want to guard against, because I have seen it.

A family becomes ambitious about this, schedules something quarterly, does two, misses the third, feels bad, and quietly stops. Six months later there is nothing, and the family concludes they are not the kind of family who does this.

They would have been much better off with one annual gathering they never missed.

The same is true of length. A thirty-minute conversation that happens is worth more than a four-hour one that gets postponed twice and then cancelled.

Start smaller than you think you should.

Jesus says his yoke is easy and his burden light. It is said in a passage about people who are weary and heavy-laden, which is to say people already carrying too much.

I do not think it is a promise that nothing will be asked of you. It is a statement about proportion.

Whatever your family takes on here should be proportionate to what your family can actually sustain, and if it is not, it will not be sustained, and you will have taught your family that this is something they fail at.

2. Have you been ambitious in a way that will not hold?

4. What would you never miss?

Day 28

Seventy-Five Percent of the People

We can at least get started

"And let us not become weary in doing good, for in due time we will reap if we do not become weary." GALATIANS 6:9

I have three families right now who have been trying for two years to find a date when everybody can be there.

Two years.

And what I finally said to them was, let us see if we can get seventy-five percent of the people there. We can at least get started.

Because waiting for everybody is how two years becomes ten.

I want to acknowledge the instinct, because it is a good one. If this is a family conversation, it should include the family. Somebody missing means somebody was left out, and being left out of something significant has consequences.

All of that is true and it is still not worth two years.

Here is the arithmetic. If you have six adults with jobs and children in three time zones, the probability of all six being available on any given weekend is genuinely low. And the more times you try and fail, the more the gathering acquires the character of something that never happens, which makes people less responsive to the next attempt.

Momentum matters more than completeness at the beginning.

So gather who can come. And then do two things that make the absence survivable.

Tell the missing person beforehand. Not an apology. An invitation and an explanation. We are getting together on this date, we know you cannot make it, here is what we will be talking about, and we would like to know your thoughts in advance.

Brief them afterward, personally. Not a group message. A call, from somebody who was there, telling them what happened.

Those two steps take twenty minutes and they convert an exclusion into an inclusion that happened at a different time.

Paul says do not become weary in doing good because there is a harvest in due time. The weariness he names is specific. It is the exhaustion of continuing without visible result.

Two years of failed scheduling is exactly that kind of weariness, and the remedy is not more determination.

It is a lower bar.

Day 29

Your Top Three Prayer Requests

Give me your top three for the next year

"Confess your sins to one another, and pray for one another, so that you may be healed." JAMES 5:16

I want to describe the single best practice I have seen a family adopt, and it costs nothing.

A client asked each of his children for their top three prayer requests for the coming year.

And then, the next time the family was together, he asked for a report.

That is it. That is the whole practice.

Think about what it does.

It requires each person to say what they actually hope for, which most family members have never articulated to their parents. Not what they are working on. What they are asking God for. That is a different and much more revealing question.

It gives the parent something to do all year that is not advice. This is the part I would emphasize to anybody in an older generation. You will be tempted to respond to what your children tell you with counsel. The requests are not an invitation to counsel. They are an invitation to pray.

And the report is what makes it a rhythm rather than an exercise. Somebody has to remember and follow up, and being followed up on tells a person that what they said was actually retained.

I have watched this become the spine of a family's year. Not the meetings. This.

There is a version for families who would find the explicitly spiritual framing difficult. Ask for the three things each person most hopes will be true a year from now. It is a weaker version and it still does most of the work.

But I would encourage the stronger one if your family can carry it, because there is something particular that happens when a father tells his adult son that he has been praying about a specific thing for eleven months.

James says confess to one another and pray for one another so that you may be healed. Both halves are mutual. Confess to one another. Pray for one another.

Not a parent praying for children, which is where most families stop. One another, which means the children are praying for the parent too, and that requires the parent to have said what they need.

Which is harder, and it is the thing that makes it a family practice rather than a parental one.

Day 30

Our Family Loves Pamela

You want to create interdependence in your family

"So we, who are many, are one body in Christ, and individually parts of one another." ROMANS 12:5

This is the last day, and I want to end with my daughter.

Pamela has Down syndrome. She is a grown woman and she will always need care, and for most of her life that care has come from her mother and me.

At some point it will not be able to.

So her three siblings have made a plan. They have decided where she will live and who will be responsible and how it will work. Not because anyone asked them to write something. Because they talked about it, and they are her family.

She will never be institutionalized. That was not a decision anybody had to argue for.

What I did not expect, and what I still find difficult to describe, is that they compete over it. Over who gets her. That is not a burden being divided. It is something else entirely.

She called me this morning. She calls most mornings. And at the end of it she said what she always says, which is that I am her hero.

I have had a career. There are things in it I am proud of. None of them are that.

I tell you this on the last day because everything in these thirty days was aimed here.

You can plan a meeting well. You can set an agenda, hold a room, keep a conversation on track, name your values, and build a rhythm. All of that is worth doing and none of it is the point.

The point is a family where somebody who cannot advocate for herself has three people arguing over who gets to take care of her.

That does not happen by accident and it does not happen through documents. It happens because a family talked, over years, about who they were and what they were for, until the answer was obvious enough that nobody needed to be persuaded.

Paul says we are individually parts of one another. Not members of an organization. Parts of one another.

The word for what you are building is interdependence, and it is the opposite of what most of us were taught to raise our children toward.

Go build it. And set the next date before you close this.
