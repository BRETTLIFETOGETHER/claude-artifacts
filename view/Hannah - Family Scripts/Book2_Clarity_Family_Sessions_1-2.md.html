Teleprompter scripts. Brett Eastman and Tom Conway, two-chair format. Six sessions. Sessions 1 and 2 below.

The pattern. Coaching titles lead with the gerund and name the advisor's work: Clarifying the… Family titles lead with the possessive and give the family ownership: Your … Legacy. That mirrors the you/they pivot the book titles already make, and it carries the distinction down to the session level without either edition sounding like a translation of the other.

What is missing on the coaching side. Sessions 1 and 5 have no name at all, in any document or on camera. They need locking before Clarity can go to layout, packaging, or the certification pathway. Everything else in the coaching column is what was said on film.

DEFAULTS SET FOR THIS BUILD

Four items were open. I set each one rather than hold the build. Any of these can be reversed cheaply now and expensively later.

Scripture: NASB. The published Family Book uses NASB. The family line should match the family book. The three coaching scripts quote NIV, which means the two editions will differ until someone decides otherwise.

Tom addresses families directly. He speaks to the viewer as a family member, not about clients to an advisor. He still tells stories from thirty years of work, but "my clients" becomes "families I have sat with," and the second person is the family.

Session titles as written in the outline. Independent family naming rather than a tight echo of the coaching titles. Flagged in the outline as your call; this build assumes independent. Reversible.

Scale rule. Relational stories carry over intact. Financial stories keep the shape and lose the figure.

STORY CUES

[TOM: ...] marks a slot where Tom improvised on camera in the coaching film and should improvise again. The scripted line around it holds the teaching. Meetings proved the delta between script and film is three to one and that nearly all of it is story, so these slots are reserved rather than hoped for.

🎬 SESSION 1 — THE FIVE AREAS OF YOUR LEGACY

Bringing Focus to What Matters Most

Source: filmed Clarity S1; Family Book ch. 5; Workbook Legend S4

BRETT: CENTER

Well, welcome to Family Legacy by Design.

My name is Brett, and I am here with Tom Conway, who has come alongside more families in this particular space than probably anyone over the last thirty years. Tom, I thank you for your life, your leadership, and your legacy, and I am glad to be starting this one with you.

We are very excited to kick off a brand new series on family clarity. What it takes to define what matters most to you and to your family.

Over the next six sessions we want to help you get clear. Not clear on a plan, not yet. Clear on what actually matters, and where all of this is supposed to be going.

And before we start, one thing. Some of what comes up in these sessions is going to be personal. You may be watching alone. You may be watching with your spouse. You may have your whole family in the room. Wherever you are, go at the pace that is right for the people you are with. Nobody has to say everything out loud today. What matters is that you start.

BRETT: TURN

So Tom, as we begin, why don't you just open up and share your heart on this. When a family sits down and starts talking about legacy, what are they really trying to figure out?

TOM: CENTER

Yeah. A lot of times when people think about legacy, it is a very big, broad term. And people do not focus it.

So what we have done is we have come up with five areas that we think are really critical for you to think about.

The first is your personal legacy. What do you want to be remembered for? And that includes your spiritual legacy. What is your spiritual legacy going to be?

The second is your family legacy. Tell me about your kids. Your grandkids. Your parents, if they are still alive. What has your family legacy been, and what do you want it to be going forward?

The third is your financial legacy, and this is the wealth transfer part. What is the inheritance you are going to leave to your kids or your grandkids that is going to be a blessing and not a curse?

The fourth is your business legacy. That is for those of you who own a business, and also for those of you who lead somewhere in your work.

And the fifth is your charitable legacy. What are you going to do for the kingdom, both while you are living and at the end of your life?

BRETT: CENTER

Five areas. And I want to slow down on the word focus, because I think that is the whole thing right there.

Because most families are not unclear because they do not care. They are unclear because legacy is such a big word that nobody knows where to put their hands on it. It is easier to talk about the estate. It is easier to talk about the will. Those feel like tasks you can finish.

This is different. This is asking what all of it is for.

BRETT: TURN

Tom, you have sat across the table from a lot of families. When you ask a successful family what matters most to them, what actually happens in the room?

TOM: CENTER

Well, a lot of times you get five different answers.

And here is the thing. These are people who love each other. They have been around the same table for thirty years. They would tell you they are on the same page. But nobody has ever actually asked them the question, so they have never had to say it out loud.

[TOM: the room where each person gave a different answer. Keep the family shape and the surprise on their faces. No numbers.]

And what we find is there is usually a gap. There is a gap between where a family says they want to be and where they actually are right now. That gap is not a failure. It is just information. But you cannot close a gap you have never named.

BRETT: CENTER

That is really good. And I think that is what this series is.

Because there are three words that we come back to over and over in this work. Clarity. Alignment. Communication. And they go in that order for a reason.

You cannot align a family around something that has never been made clear. And you cannot have a real conversation about something nobody has defined. So clarity comes first, and clarity is where we are going to live for the next six sessions.

BRETT: TURN

Tom, when a family does this well, when they actually get clear, what are you hoping happens for them?

TOM: CENTER

Two things. And these are the two things we want for every family.

We want family harmony. And we want wise transfer, not just of the wealth, but of the wisdom.

Because you can pass on everything you have built and still not pass on the thing that built it. And if that happens, the money does not help. It usually makes it worse.

So we are after both. We want your family to be close. And we want what you have built to land well in the people who receive it.

BRETT: CENTER

And here is the phrase that runs through this whole series, and Tom says it all the time.

Legacy by design, or legacy by default.

Every family is passing something on. That is not the question. The question is whether you chose it.

Because if you do not decide, it still gets decided. The state has a plan for you. Your family will fill in the silence with their own assumptions. Something is going to happen. The only question is whether you were part of choosing it.

Scripture. Proverbs 20:5. A plan in the heart of a man is like deep water. The rest of the verse says a person of understanding draws it out. That is what these six sessions are trying to do. The answers are already in you. They just have not been drawn out yet.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, let me give you a scenario, because I want people to see themselves in this.

SCENARIO ONE. Here is a couple in their sixties. They have done well. The business is stable, the kids are grown, there is more than enough. And if you asked either one of them what matters most, they would both say family. But they have never once sat down and said what that means. And so every decision they make is a good decision made in the dark.

BRETT: TURN

What is going on there?

TOM: CENTER

That is most families. That is not a broken family, that is a normal one.

And what I would say to them is, you are not behind. You just have not started. The conversation you are avoiding is not going to be as hard as you think it is going to be. It is the anticipating that is hard.

Start with the two of you. Before you ever bring the kids into it, the two of you need to be able to say what you want. If you cannot say it to each other, you certainly are not going to be able to say it to your family.

BRETT: CENTER

Let me give you another version of this.

SCENARIO TWO. A father who has thought about it. He has thought about it a lot. He knows exactly what he wants and he could tell you in one sentence. But he has never told his children, because he is waiting for the right moment, and the right moment has not come in eleven years.

BRETT: TURN

TOM: CENTER

Yeah. And that is a different problem, but it ends up in the same place.

[TOM: the family that waited too long, and what it cost. Relational shape, no figures.]

Here is what I would say to him. Your kids are already forming an opinion about what you want. They are doing it right now, without you. Every year you wait, they are writing the story themselves, and it is probably not the story you would have written.

You do not have to have the whole conversation. You just have to start it.

BRETT: CENTER

If you are watching this and you are in the next generation, let me speak to you for a second, because this is not only for your parents.

You may be thinking this is their conversation and you are just waiting to find out what they decided. It is not. What your family becomes in the next thirty years has as much to do with you as with them. And you are allowed to ask. You are allowed to say you want to understand what your family is about, not what your family has.

That is a different question, and it is the one worth asking.

So here is what we want to leave you with.

Five areas. Personal, family, financial, business, and charitable. Over the next five sessions we are going to walk through them one at a time, and we are going to slow down in each one.

THIS WEEK. Rate yourself in each of the five areas, one to ten. Not how you are doing. How clear you are. Which one is clearest to you right now, and which one is the most undefined?

Do not fix anything. Just find out where you actually are.

BRETT: TURN

Tom, bring us home. If a family only heard one thing today, what would you want it to be?

TOM: CENTER

That this is worth doing.

Most families never do this. Not because they do not love each other, but because nobody ever asked them to sit down and think about it. And someday every one of us is going to answer for what we did with what we were given.

Next session we go to the first of the five, and it may be the one people expect least. Before we talk about your family, we are going to talk about you.

🎬 SESSION 2 — YOUR PERSONAL LEGACY

What Do You Want to Be Remembered For

Source: filmed Clarity S2; Family Book ch. 6

BRETT: CENTER

Welcome back to Family Legacy by Design. My name is Brett, this is Tom, and we are grateful you are back with us.

We have been going in a series on family clarity, on being able to clarify those things that really matter to you and to your family.

In our last session we laid out the five areas of legacy. Personal, family, financial, business, and charitable. And we said clarity comes before alignment, and alignment comes before the conversation.

So in this session we start with the first of the five, and it is the one that surprises people. Before we talk about your family, we are going to talk about you. Your values. Your purpose. Your priorities. And your spiritual life, which sits underneath all of it.

And here is why the order matters. You can have a whole family sitting around the same table, hearing the same question, and every single person is in a completely different place internally. One is clear. One is still working it out. One is not sure what any of this is about. And that shows up in how they respond, how they engage, and whether anything moves.

A family cannot get clear together if the people in it have never gotten clear themselves.

BRETT: TURN

So Tom, when you sit with someone and you are trying to help them get clear about their own life, where do you actually begin?

TOM: CENTER

Yeah. I start with questions, and I ask a lot of them.

Because the genius of this work is not in the answers. It is in the question asking. Most people have never been asked, so they have never had to think it through.

I want to know what has shaped you. What are the things in your life that made you who you are? Some of those are wonderful and some of those are hard, and both of them count.

I want to know what you are good at, and what you love. Those are not always the same thing.

And I want to know what you think God has entrusted to you, and what you are supposed to do with it. That is the real question underneath all of this.

BRETT: CENTER

And Tom, you have said something to me many times that I want people to hear, because it changes how you do this.

Do not accept the first answer.

TOM: CENTER

That is right. The first answer is almost never the real one.

The first answer is the one people have already prepared. It is the socially acceptable version. So you have to ask again. You say, tell me more about that. Or, why is that important to you?

And that second or third question is where you find out what is actually going on. [TOM: the moment someone's real answer came out on the third question.]

You have to give people room. And you have to be willing to sit in the quiet for a minute, because most of us fill the silence too fast, and the answer was coming.

BRETT: CENTER

Let me put a hard one on the table, because I think this is where a lot of people get uncomfortable.

There is often a gap between what we say matters and how we actually live. We will say family is the most important thing, and then look at where the time went last year. We will say generosity matters, and then look at where the money went.

That is not hypocrisy. That is just being human. But you cannot fix a gap you will not look at.

BRETT: TURN

Tom, how do you help somebody look at that honestly without shame?

TOM: CENTER

Well, the first thing is you have to be careful about judgment. That is the thing you have to watch in yourself.

Because people are living their lives, and the gap is usually not a character problem. It is a clarity problem. They never decided, so the calendar decided, and the calendar is not evil, it is just loud.

So I do not come at it as, look what you have done. I come at it as, here is what you said matters, and here is what your life currently reflects. What do you want to do about that?

And most people, when you put it that way, they already know.

BRETT: CENTER

And Tom, the piece under all of it for you is the spiritual piece.

TOM: CENTER

It is. And I will ask people directly.

How much time do you spend with God in a week? What are you reading? Is there anybody in your life who actually knows how you are doing?

I am not asking to grade anybody. I am asking because your spiritual legacy is the one thing that will still be working in your family a hundred years from now, and it is the one nobody schedules.

Scripture. Ephesians 2:10. We are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works. Prepared beforehand. Which means the thing you are trying to get clear about was already decided about you. You are finding it, not inventing it.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, let me give you a scenario.

SCENARIO ONE. A man in his late fifties. He has been successful at everything he has put his hand to. And you ask him what he wants to be remembered for and he gives you a good answer, a solid answer, in about four seconds. And you can tell he has never actually thought about it. He is repeating something.

BRETT: TURN

What do you do with him?

TOM: CENTER

I ask him again.

Not aggressively. I just say, that is good, tell me more. And then I wait.

Because a four second answer to a question that big means he has an answer he uses, and he has never gone underneath it. And underneath it is usually something much more specific and much more personal. Sometimes it is something he has never said to anyone.

That is the one worth getting to.

BRETT: CENTER

Let me give you another version of this.

SCENARIO TWO. A woman who has spent thirty years pouring into her family, and you ask her about her personal legacy, and she cannot answer. Not because she has not thought about it. Because she is not sure she is allowed to have one that is separate from everybody else's.

BRETT: TURN

TOM: CENTER

Yeah. And I see that a lot, and I think it is one of the more important conversations in this whole series.

Because you were given something individually. Not just as a spouse, not just as a parent. And the family is better when you know what that is.

[TOM: a wife or mother who found her own answer, and what it changed. Keep it relational.]

I would tell her, your family does not need less of you. They need you to know what you were made for, because they are watching you either way.

BRETT: CENTER

And to the next generation watching this, this one is for you as much as for anybody.

You may think these questions belong to the second half of life. They do not. The difference between asking them at thirty and asking them at sixty is thirty years.

You are allowed to want something specific. You are allowed to want something different from what your family built. What you are not allowed to do, if you want this to go well, is leave it unsaid and hope somebody guesses.

BRETT: CENTER

So here is where we want to leave you.

THIS WEEK. Write the two or three sentences you would want said about your life. Not your accomplishments. What kind of person you were, and what you gave yourself to.

Then show it to one person. That is the part that makes it real.

BRETT: TURN

Tom, bring it home for us.

TOM: CENTER

You are not going to get to the end of your life and wish you had been less clear.

Do the work now while you can still do something about it. And do not do it alone. Tell somebody what you wrote down, because the moment you say it out loud to another person, it stops being an idea and starts being a direction.

BRETT: CENTER

Next session we move from you to your family. Who is actually in it, how they are really doing, and what you want this family to be known for.

We will see you there.
