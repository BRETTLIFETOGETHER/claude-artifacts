🎬 SESSION 3 — YOUR FAMILY LEGACY

Defining What Your Family Stands For

Source: filmed Clarity S3; Family Book ch. 5

BRETT: CENTER

Welcome back to Family Legacy by Design. My name is Brett, I am with Tom, and we are excited about you jumping into this particular session.

We have been going in a series on family clarity. And in the last session we worked on the individual. What matters to you, what has shaped you, what you want to be remembered for.

But once you do that, you bring people back together. And now you have got different perspectives in the room. Different priorities. Different ways of thinking about the future. None of that is wrong. But if it is never brought together, a family starts to drift.

And that is the difference between legacy by design and legacy by default. Drifting in different directions.

So this session is about defining what your family stands for.

BRETT: TURN

So Tom, as we begin, why don't you just share off the top of your heart about these families. What is it you would say matters most here?

TOM: CENTER

Well, in all of our work we want two things for every family. We want family harmony, and we want wise wealth transfer. Every question we ask is trying to get people there.

And your family is the most important thing. Your money is great. It is great to have money. But that is not the most important thing. The most important thing is the relationships you have with the people in your family.

So I want to know the family. Who are the kids? Who are the grandkids? Who are the parents, if they are still around. Who are the outside people who affect your family?

Because once you understand who the family is, then you can start asking better questions. How are your kids doing spiritually? How are they doing vocationally? How are they doing financially? Do they have debt?

You just want to know your family. Not the version of them you carry around in your head. The actual people.

BRETT: CENTER

And Tom, there is a word underneath all of that which I think is the whole thing.

Shared.

Because without that, everyone is operating off their own understanding of what is true. And it can feel like you are aligned, right up until a decision has to be made. And then you find out you were not.

TOM: CENTER

That is right. And you see it pretty quickly in real life.

You have got one person thinking, we need to protect what we have. And another one thinking we should take more risk. And someone else thinking we should focus on relationships and time together.

None of those are wrong. Those are just different temperaments and different personalities. But if they are never brought together, they start pulling in different directions.

And here is one more thing. Mom's view of a son and Dad's view of that same son can be very different. Most families have a sense of that, but it has never been said out loud, so it has never been dealt with.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, let me raise the piece that surprises people, because it surprised me.

When your kids get married, that person brings a whole other set of family values into your family.

TOM: CENTER

They do. And that is a big deal.

They were not raised the way your kids were raised. They may fit in beautifully and they may not. But they are here now. It is not a matter of, well, we are going to get rid of that one. No. We work with them the way they are.

[TOM: the family with the hundred year multigenerational vision, and the in-laws who could not grasp it yet. Keep the shape, no figures.]

And you have to understand what they brought, because that is how you serve your son and his wife, or your daughter and her husband, well.

BRETT: CENTER

And this is where a family gets to look in the mirror a little bit. Not to judge. Just to get an honest picture.

Because it is one thing to talk about planning. It is another thing to stop and ask, how are we actually doing as a family? And when you ask that, people usually pause. Because they realize they have never talked about it directly.

Scripture. Psalm 133:1. Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brothers to dwell together in unity. Notice it does not say how easy.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, let me give you a scenario.

SCENARIO ONE. A family that would tell you they are close. And they are. They love each other, they pray for each other, they are there for the holidays and the birthdays. Things look good on the surface. But you ask each person what the family stands for, and you get very different answers.

BRETT: TURN

What do you do with that?

TOM: CENTER

You get them all together and you talk about it.

I have done a lot of family meetings around vision, mission, and values, and what happens in those is not just that the family gets aligned. The family starts to understand itself. You find out why she thinks that way, and why he thinks that way, because it comes out in the values they name.

And then they talk about all of it together, and they eventually settle on five or six that really matter to them. And that is what moves a family together in unity.

BRETT: CENTER

Let me give you another version of this.

SCENARIO TWO. A father whose daughter is getting serious about someone. It is heading toward a proposal. And Dad is happy for her, and also has a knot in his stomach, and has not asked a single real question because he does not want to be the man who interfered.

BRETT: TURN

TOM: CENTER

Yeah. I would tell him to ask.

I have six questions I asked my sons-in-law before they got permission to marry my daughters. And I told them, you just need to answer these six questions the way I want them answered.

[TOM: the client who adapted the six questions, and the daughter who was nervous it would scare him off, and what actually happened.]

And what I have found is that it does not scare the right young man away. It does the opposite. He rises up to it.

Somebody said once, it is better to be single than to be married to the wrong person. That is a powerful statement if you sit with it. And most of the pain comes from not being serious enough on the front end.

BRETT: CENTER

If you are in the next generation watching this, here is what I would say to you.

Your parents may have a picture of you in their head that is a few years out of date. That is not a criticism of them, it is just what happens. And the only person who can update it is you.

You are also allowed to want to know what this family is actually about. Not what it has. What it is for. That is a fair question, and you are allowed to ask it out loud.

BRETT: CENTER

So here is the defining question we want to leave you with, and it is the one that shapes everything that comes after.

What do you want this family to be known for?

THIS WEEK. Ask each person in your family how they think the family is actually doing. Not what needs fixing. Just how they would describe it. Then listen without correcting anybody.

BRETT: TURN

Tom, bring us home.

TOM: CENTER

Every family is unique. So take your family from where it actually is, not from where you wish it were, and help it go where you want it to go.

And do not be afraid of what you find out. Most families are healthier than they think they are. They have just never talked about it.

BRETT: CENTER

Next session we go to the one people brace for. We are going to talk about money.

We will see you there.

🎬 SESSION 4 — YOUR FINANCIAL LEGACY

A Blessing and Not a Curse

Source: filmed Clarity S4; Family Book ch. 7

BRETT: CENTER

Welcome back to Family Legacy by Design. My name is Brett and this is Tom, and we are grateful you have joined us.

We have talked about clarity at the personal level and at the family level. But there is a place where, for most families, things start to feel a little more sensitive. A little more care is required.

No shame in it. But this is where the conversation turns to money.

And even in families that are close, even in families that communicate well, there are assumptions. People think they understand what exists, and they really do not. It might be a property. It might be the business. It might be some corner of the family's life nobody has walked through out loud.

What you end up with is a gap. A gap between what is real and what is understood.

And sometimes families avoid it altogether. Not because they do not care, but because it feels complicated or uncomfortable.

BRETT: TURN

So Tom, when you talk about getting clear on the financial reality, this can be a stick of dynamite, or it can be the thing that greases the wheels and takes a family to the next level. What are you really trying to help a family step into?

TOM: CENTER

You are helping them understand what they actually have. Clear, complete, and summarized.

Most families do not have the full picture. They know parts of it. Yeah, I have an insurance policy. I think I have a will, it is a little bit old. But nobody has ever put it all on one page.

And we do that for a simple reason. You cannot change where you are if you do not know where you are.

And you have got to start with Mom and Dad. A lot of times Mom does not understand what they really have. Getting the two of them looking at the same information, making the same observations, that alone changes a marriage.

BRETT: CENTER

And Tom, this is where it stops being about assets and starts being about people.

Because there is a significant transfer coming over the next several years from one generation to the next. And the question is not just what is being transferred. It is what it is going to do to the people receiving it.

TOM: CENTER

That is right. You give a lot of money to someone who did not have a lot of money, it is going to affect their life. Think about lottery winners. Everybody knows those stories.

[TOM: a family where the transfer went badly, and what it cost the relationships. Relational shape. No figures.]

Most legacy families know the phrase, shirt sleeves to shirt sleeves. By the third generation it is gone. And they hate that idea, and they do not want their kids to be part of it. They just do not know how to keep it from happening.

BRETT: CENTER

I want to say something here, because I have watched this up close.

I know a family whose relationships have almost been blown apart, and what they told me recently is that it was the extra resources that did it. The complexity of it challenged their relationships with their kids. In one case there is a lawsuit with one of their children. In another they are not in relationship with the children or the grandchildren at all, and it is heartbreaking to them.

I asked them, would you be better off without it? And they said, we would not go that far. But we wish we had known more of the kinds of things you are talking about.

BRETT: TURN

So Tom, what is the question that changes this?

TOM: CENTER

The question is, what amount of inheritance are you going to leave your kids that is going to be a blessing and not a curse?

Because more is not always better. If someone is not prepared, it creates problems instead of solving them.

So instead of asking how much, you ask what for. What do you want to see this accomplish in the lives of your children? What kind of impact do you want it to have?

Because if you do not know what you are hoping it will do in their life, it really raises the question of why you are leaving more, or less, than you actually could.

BRETT: CENTER

And Tom, that leads to something you and I have both lived.

Every child is different.

TOM: CENTER

Yeah. We use the phrase, you love your children equally, but that does not necessarily mean you treat them the same. Because they are not the same. Some have needs the others do not have.

But here is the part people miss. If you are going to treat them differently, you have to communicate that you are going to treat them differently, and why.

BRETT: CENTER

And Tom, in both your life and mine, we each have a child with special needs. And the way things get distributed matters enormously, whatever the size of the estate. And it is absolutely vital that it gets communicated. Not just thought about. Not just written down between the two spouses.

Because equal feels fair. You just divide it down the middle. But wise can look very different depending on the family.

Scripture. Proverbs 13:22. A good man leaves an inheritance to his children's children. Two generations out. Which means the question was never just about your kids.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, let me give you a scenario.

SCENARIO ONE. A couple who has decided. They know exactly what each child is getting and why. It is thoughtful, it is generous, and it is right for their family. And they have never told the children a word of it.

BRETT: TURN

TOM: CENTER

That is a disaster about to happen.

[TOM: the family who communicated it while he was alive, what the kids' reaction actually was, and what he changed afterward. Keep the shape and the outcome. Lose the numbers.]

And here is what I would say to them. There is going to be a family meeting. It is just a matter of whether you are going to be there or not.

If you are there, they can ask you questions. They can hear why. And you might even change your mind once you hear them, which happens more than people expect.

BRETT: CENTER

Let me give you another version of this.

SCENARIO TWO. Three children. Two of them are steady, capable, doing well. The third one has struggled for years and there is real reason to worry. And the parents are stuck, because equal does not feel wise and unequal does not feel loving.

BRETT: TURN

TOM: CENTER

Yeah, and there are ways to handle that.

For the two who are ready, you might leave it to them outright. For the third, you might put it in a trust, and the siblings serve as trustees, so there is always enough that he has a place to live and food to eat, without handing him something that could be gone in six months.

Every family is unique. Two families with exactly the same amount of money should often do completely different things.

And then you have to think about timing. Some families do a third at thirty, a third at thirty-five, a third at forty. You are just asking how ready this person is to handle it.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, one more before we go, and I think it is the most hopeful thing in this session.

TOM: CENTER

If you can afford to, give some of it to them now.

Give it to them now and watch how they steward it. That will tell you more about what to leave at the end of your life than any amount of guessing will.

Because if you are sixty-five, you might not die for twenty years. That is twenty years they do not see any of it. But if they get something now that they can build on, they will be in far better shape when the rest arrives.

And Ron Blue talks about this in terms of splitting heirs. The goal is that this does not break the family up.

BRETT: CENTER

To the next generation watching this, one thing.

You may have a number in your head. Most people do, even if they would never say it. And that number came from somewhere other than your parents, because your parents have probably never told you.

The most useful thing you can do is let it go and ask a better question. Not what am I getting. What is this for.

BRETT: CENTER

THIS WEEK. For each person who will receive something from you, write one sentence about what you hope it does in their life.

Not the amount. What it is for.

BRETT: TURN

Tom, bring it home.

TOM: CENTER

Most people do not have forty-five million dollars. But a hundred thousand, five hundred thousand, that can be a huge amount of money to a normal family.

So it is not about the number of zeros. It is about the impact of the zeros. On your kids, and on your family.

But the point is, you have got to tell people. And then you have got to listen to what they say back.

BRETT: CENTER

Next session we go to the fourth area, and for a lot of families it is where the story is and where the wealth came from. Your work and your business.

We will see you there.

🎬 SESSION 5 — YOUR WORK AND BUSINESS LEGACY

Understanding What You Have Built

Source: filmed Clarity S5; Family Book ch. 8

BRETT: CENTER

Welcome back to Family Legacy by Design. My name is Brett and this is Tom, and we are grateful that you are back.

We are in a series on family clarity, working through the five areas. Personal, family, financial, business, and charitable. And in this session we come to the fourth one.

Now, this one is for two groups. If you own a business, this is obviously for you. But if you lead somewhere in your work, if you carry influence and responsibility even though your name is not on the door, this is for you too. We are talking about both.

Because for a lot of families, this is not just another asset on a page. This is where the story is. This is where the wealth came from, or where it is headed.

And not everyone in the family sees it the same way. One person sees their life's work. Another sees a job. Another does not want to be involved at all. And those differences almost never get talked about clearly.

BRETT: TURN

So Tom, give a quick overview and then bring home why this one matters so much to you.

TOM: CENTER

Well, a lot of times people who are in business are focused on the business. How it is doing, how it is growing. And they do not realize the impact their business is having on their family.

Because the business affects everything else. It affects your personal legacy. It affects your family, because your family may be connected to it. It affects your giving. It affects your marriage. It affects the roles people play.

So we dig into it. How healthy is it? Who are the people around you? Do you have a strong leadership team, or not? And what kind of impact are you trying to have through it?

Because a lot of business owners are trying to impact their employees and their customers through the way they do business. Their employees become a kind of extended family. They care about that family.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, you have said something to me that reframed this completely, and I want you to say it here.

TOM: CENTER

I tell my clients, your business is like a church.

Think about it. In a church you have people who are not believers. You have cultural Christians. And you have people who are very committed.

In your business you have got exactly the same three groups. And so part of what you are doing, whether you ever say it out loud, is helping people move. You would love for some of them to come to Christ. You would love for the cultural ones to get serious. And with the committed ones, you are already aligned on values.

But you treat all of them the same way, even though some are easier to deal with than others.

BRETT: CENTER

And Tom, the simplest place to start is the story.

TOM: CENTER

It is. How did it get started? If it is multi-generational, tell me about your dad. How did he start it? And what does it mean to you?

And then, what role does your spouse play in the business? Because for some owners the wife plays no role at all. I have one client whose wife plays a huge role. He does not make a decision without her input, and everybody in that company knows it.

That matters, because here is a situation I have seen. The wife knows nothing about the business. And if he dies, she gets all of his stock. She does not know how to run it.

So what protection have you put in place so this thing keeps going if the person who built it is not here?

BRETT: CENTER

And that raises the question underneath this whole session, and Tom draws a distinction that most families never make.

Continuity is what happens if you are gone tomorrow.

Succession is who leads it next.

They are not the same question, and most families have only ever thought about one of them.

TOM: CENTER

That is right. And you want to know who the leaders actually are.

Because sometimes you have someone leading it in title, and the decisions are happening somewhere else. Or leadership has technically transitioned, but not really. [TOM: the founder who left but kept the office.]

And then, is the next person prepared? If he is not, he may not be for five years. So what are you doing in the meantime to get him ready?

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, give people the reality check on how hard this is.

TOM: CENTER

There is a reason that seventy percent of businesses fail going from the first generation to the second. Ninety-seven percent fail from the second to the third.

I tell clients right at the beginning, your chances of success are really low. You have got about a three percent chance.

And that is not to discourage anybody. It is so we can ask the right question. What are we going to do to be in that three percent? [TOM: the friend in the sixth generation.]

Scripture. Colossians 3:23. Whatever you do, do your work heartily, as for the Lord. Which puts the business in a different category than an asset.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, let me give you a scenario.

SCENARIO ONE. Three adult children. One of them has no interest in the business at all and says so plainly. The other two are in it and want to run it. And the two in it do not want their sibling to own any part of something she does not work in.

BRETT: TURN

TOM: CENTER

Yeah, and I have sat with exactly that, all three of them in the same room.

And I am of the opinion she should own part of it. Because who knows whether she has kids someday who want to be in it. Or whether she changes her mind. You do not want to just cut her out of the family's business.

But there are ways to handle the fairness question. The people who work in it can be compensated a lot more than the people who do not. And then at the end, when there are distributions, everyone participates.

The mistake is treating ownership and employment like they are the same thing. They are not.

BRETT: CENTER

Let me give you another version of this.

SCENARIO TWO. A son who has come into the business and is walking around with the same last name as his father, acting as if that is a qualification.

BRETT: TURN

TOM: CENTER

You do not want that. He needs humility. And if he does not have it, you make sure he gets it before the lights come on.

Because the business needs to be successful. That is the point. We are not putting somebody in charge because his last name matches the sign.

It is the golden goose. If the goose is not healthy, the eggs are going to be crummy. So you make sure the people leading it are the most competent people you have.

BRETT: CENTER

And to the next generation, especially if you are in the family business or thinking about it.

Nobody owes you a seat. And you do not owe anybody your life.

Both of those are true at the same time, and families get into trouble when only one of them gets said out loud. If you want in, earn it somewhere it is hard. If you do not want in, say so early and say so kindly. The worst outcome is the one where you spend fifteen years in something you never chose.

BRETT: CENTER

THIS WEEK. If you were unavailable for the next thirty days, who makes the decisions?

Write the name down. If you cannot, that is your answer, and that is where the work starts.

BRETT: TURN

Tom, bring us home.

TOM: CENTER

Remember that the age of the family matters here. When your kids are teenagers, you are not talking about whether they will be in the business. When they are thirty-five, you might be.

And we use the term adult children, which is really an oxymoron. Are they adults or are they children? How are you treating them?

Because every one of these decisions depends on what stage the business is in and what stage your family is in. And both of those keep moving.

BRETT: CENTER

Next session is our last in this series, and it may be the one that pulls all of it together. What all of this is finally for.

We will see you there.

🎬 SESSION 6 — YOUR CHARITABLE LEGACY

Defining a Vision for Generosity

Source: filmed Clarity S6 and S4; Family Book ch. 9

BRETT: CENTER

Welcome back to Family Legacy by Design. In this series we have been talking about family clarity.

And this is our last session together.

So let's step back and look at everything we have covered. Clarity for the individual and their personal life. Clarity for the family. Understanding what you actually have. The role of the business.

It all leads somewhere. Because at the end of the day it is not just what you have built. It is what all of it becomes.

And for a lot of families this is where the conversation shifts in a deeper way. Because now you are not talking about structure or decisions. You are talking about impact. What difference does all of this actually make? What continues beyond you? And how do you want to be remembered, not just by your family, but beyond your family?

BRETT: TURN

So Tom, when you step into this space with a family, what is it you are really dreaming that they would have?

TOM: CENTER

You are helping them think about what they want their life to represent, and how generosity fits into that.

Most families are already giving in some way. But if you ask them to describe their giving clearly, it is usually not something they have thought about much. They have done whatever came up. It tends to be more responsive than intentional.

So I like to ask a question that opens people up. What has been the most fun gift you have ever given?

And it is interesting to hear what people say. Because they light up. And then you can ask the bigger one. What are the passions in your life you would like to see accomplished with your life? What problems in the world would you like to play a part in solving?

BRETT: CENTER

And Tom, that reframes the whole thing, doesn't it. Because most people have never been asked what they are trying to accomplish. Only how much.

TOM: CENTER

That is right. People are generous. They care. They give when they are asked.

But when you ask what are you trying to accomplish through your giving, that is a different conversation. And that is where clarity comes in. Because if you have real resources to give, you want to give where you can actually see the impact.

So you start asking, what do you really care about? What kind of difference do you want to make? Why do you give?

And that moves giving from something you do to something that fits who you are.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, there is a question you ask families that I have never heard anybody else ask, and I want you to give it to people here.

TOM: CENTER

They have three kids, the estate is divided by three. Four kids, divided by four.

[TOM: the wife who said no, then not yet, and then eighteen months later said yes. Keep the timeline and her reasoning. Lose the estate size.]

BRETT: CENTER

And Tom, once a family starts, you keep pushing. Gently. But you keep pushing.

TOM: CENTER

I do. You give away ten percent, that is a good start. What about twenty?

And they say, well, I don't know. And you say, alright, let's do fifteen. Let's split the difference.

[TOM: the client who went from ten to thirty and eventually to fifty percent.]

And here is one more thing I say, because I see it constantly. People park money in a donor-advised fund and never move it out. And I tell them, it does not do God any good sitting in your donor-advised fund.

It does good when it gets out and into churches and ministries and organizations that are actually doing something for people.

Scripture. Matthew 25:21. Well done, good and faithful slave. Tom calls it the final exam. There is going to be a moment when you are asked what you did with what you were given.

BRETT: CENTER

Tom, let me give you a scenario.

SCENARIO ONE. A family that gives regularly. They support a lot of different causes. They respond when a need comes up. They would absolutely tell you generosity matters to them. But when you ask what they are trying to accomplish with it, it is not clear.

BRETT: TURN

TOM: CENTER

Yeah. And what I would do is help them find the thing they actually care about.

Some families have a heart for the Bible and want to see it translated. Some have a heart for evangelism. Some for Christian education. Some for kids.

[TOM: the man giving to thirty-five charities who could not visit them all, and what happened when he cut it down and gave bigger gifts to fewer places.]

Because it is not just how much you give. It is what kind of impact your giving is having.

BRETT: CENTER

Let me give you another version of this.

SCENARIO TWO. A couple who intends to leave a significant amount to charity at the end of their lives. It is in the documents. It is decided. And nobody has thought about who is actually going to give it away, because they will not be here.

BRETT: TURN

TOM: CENTER

That is a real question, and almost nobody asks it.

Unless you have given specific instructions, somebody else is deciding. Who is it? All of your kids together? One of them who has a real bent toward giving? Someone outside the family who shares your values?

[TOM: the friend whose best friend will give it away, because his kids do not share his values yet.]

Because you are not just passing on money at that point. You are passing on responsibility.

BRETT: CENTER

And to the next generation, this one especially is for you.

The giving decisions in your family will be yours sooner than you think. Not someday. Sooner.
