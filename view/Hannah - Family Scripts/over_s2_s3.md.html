Family Legacy Overview devotional, Sessions 2 and 3, Days 6 through 15

SESSION 2

The Family God Designed

Why your family was built to last longer than you

Day 6

Raise Them Up and Kick Them Out

The modern view, stated plainly

"Behold, children are a gift of the Lord, the fruit of the womb is a reward." PSALM 127:3

There is a view of family that almost everyone in our culture holds and almost nobody has examined.

Raise them up and kick them out.

I state it that bluntly because stated politely it sounds obviously correct. We raise our children toward independence. We want them capable, self-supporting, and able to run their own lives without us.

All of that is good and I am not arguing against any of it.

But notice what that view implies about the finish line.

If independence is the goal, then success is the day your child no longer needs you. And once you cross that line, the relationship has completed its purpose, and whatever follows is affection rather than function.

That is the assumption running underneath a great deal of modern family life, and it explains something families find puzzling about themselves.

It explains why so many parents of grown children feel obsolete. It explains why the relationship frequently thins after the launch. And it explains why families struggle to find a reason to gather that is not a holiday, because the organizing purpose was completed years ago.

Nobody chose that. It came with the water.

Now, I want to be careful about the alternative, because there is a bad version of it.

The bad version is a family where nobody ever becomes an adult, where dependence is maintained because the older generation cannot bear to be unneeded. That is not a corrective. It is a different failure and it is worse.

The psalm calls children a gift and a reward. Not a project with a completion date.

A project ends when it is delivered. A gift is something you continue to have.

That single distinction is what this session is about. Not whether to raise capable children. Of course you will.

Whether raising them was the whole point, or the first part of something longer.

Reflect

1. What has your family assumed the goal was?

2. Where did that assumption come from?

3. If independence is the finish line, what comes after it?

4. Do you feel obsolete in any part of your family's life?

Practice

Write the goal you have actually been raising your children toward.

Now write what happens after that goal is reached.

If the second line is thin, that is the finding.

Day 7

Interdependence, Not Independence

Multiple generations loving and serving one another

"So we, who are many, are one body in Christ, and individually parts of one another." ROMANS 12:5

If independence is the modern view, here is the older one.

Interdependence. Multiple generations loving and serving one another, staying connected, continuing to influence each other, and living with a shared sense of purpose over time.

That is a completely different picture, and the difference is not sentimental. It changes what success means.

Under independence, success is: they are on their own.

Under interdependence, success is: we are still building something together.

I want to say what interdependence is not, because it gets confused with two things it is not.

It is not dependence. A family where an adult cannot function without their parents is not interdependent, it is arrested. Interdependence requires capable people, which means the independence work still has to happen.

And it is not enmeshment. Interdependence does not mean everyone is in everyone's business, or that decisions require consensus, or that adult children owe an accounting.

It means people who could function separately choose not to entirely.

Here is the practical shape of it in the families I have watched do it well.

Multiple generations who are genuinely capable, who could each run their own lives without the others, and who continue to bring things to each other anyway. Advice sought rather than given. Resources shared in both directions. Grandchildren who know their grandparents as people rather than as occasions.

None of that is required by circumstance. All of it is chosen, repeatedly.

Paul says we are individually parts of one another. It is a strange construction and it is deliberately not the language of an organization.

An organization has members who belong to it. A body has parts that belong to each other.

That mutual belonging is what interdependence describes, and it is worth noticing that it says nothing about proximity, frequency, or structure.

It is a disposition rather than an arrangement, and it can be true of a family in three time zones.

Reflect

1. Is your family independent, interdependent, or something else?

2. Where does dependence exist that should not?

3. What do the generations in your family bring to each other?

4. What is chosen rather than required?

Day 8

Throughout Their Generations

The horizon was never one lifetime

"I will establish My covenant between Me and you and your descendants after you throughout their generations." GENESIS 17:7

Here is where the interdependent view actually comes from, and it is older than any of us.

When God makes His promise to Abraham, He does not stop at Abraham.

I will establish my covenant between me and you and your descendants after you, throughout their generations.

Throughout their generations.

The promise has a horizon built into it, and the horizon is longer than a lifetime.

I want to draw out what that does to planning, because it is not merely inspirational.

Most families operate on a five to ten year horizon. That is the range in which decisions feel real, plans can be verified, and outcomes are visible. Beyond that it becomes abstract.

But the entity being planned for runs on a much longer clock. Your family will exist, in some form, for a hundred years after this conversation. Decisions made now will still be operating when nobody involved in making them is available to explain the reasoning.

That is not a comfortable frame and it is the accurate one.

Here is what changes when a family adopts it.

You start asking different questions. Not what is best this year, but what would still make sense in thirty. Not who should get what, but what is being established. Not how do we solve this, but what does this teach the people who come after us about how our family solves things.

Those are the questions of a family that thinks it will continue.

And most families never ask them, not because they think they will end, but because nobody ever put the horizon in front of them.

There is a second thing worth noticing in Genesis. God makes the covenant with Abraham and with his descendants, who are not present and have not consented to anything.

They are included by virtue of being descendants.

Which is a fair description of what a family inheritance actually is, in every sense. People who were not there receive what was decided, and their only options are to carry it, revise it, or drop it.

Give them something worth carrying.

Reflect

1. What horizon does your family actually plan on?

2. What decisions will still be operating in thirty years?

3. What are you establishing rather than solving?

4. What would somebody a hundred years from now inherit from your family?

Day 9

When You Sit Down and When You Walk

The transfer happens in ordinary life

"These words shall be on your heart. You shall teach them diligently to your sons and shall speak of them when you sit in your house and when you walk by the way." DEUTERONOMY 6:6-7

If a family is going to last longer than one generation, something has to move between them. And Deuteronomy describes how.

When you sit in your house. When you walk by the way. When you lie down. When you rise up.

That is not a curriculum. It is a description of ordinary life with something running through it constantly.

Families read that passage and hear an instruction to hold family devotions, which is fine and is not what it says. It describes something less scheduled and more ambitious, which is a subject that keeps coming up in the car.

Here is why the distinction matters practically.

A scheduled transfer has a container, and containers have edges. Twenty minutes on Sunday evening is twenty minutes, and everything outside it is unavailable for this purpose. Which means the volume is capped, and the cap is low.

An unscheduled transfer has no edges. It happens whenever the subject arises, which in a family where these things are normal is constantly and briefly.

The second one moves far more over a decade, and it costs less effort at any given moment.

Now, the requirement hidden in the verse is the hard part. These words shall be on your heart.

Before the teaching, the having. You cannot mention something in the car that is not currently occupying you. The passage assumes a person who is thinking about this anyway, and describes what naturally leaks out of such a person.

Which is why most families cannot do the walking-by-the-way version. Not because they are undisciplined, but because there is nothing to leak.

So the first instruction is not about your children. It is about whether anything is on your heart to begin with.

If there is, the rest is easier than any curriculum. You just say what you are already thinking about, out loud, in the car, to whoever is with you.

Reflect

1. What is on your heart right now that your family knows about?

2. When does anything of substance come up naturally in your family?

3. Have you tried to schedule what should have been leaking?

4. What would leak out of you if you spoke freely this week?

Day 10

A Group of People Who Used to Live Together

Or is it something that still has a purpose

"How good and how pleasant it is for brothers to live together in unity!" PSALM 133:1

This is the last day of the second session and it comes down to a question that decides everything downstream.

Is your family a group of people who used to live together?

Or is it something that still has a purpose?

Both are legitimate outcomes and only one of them was chosen.

Here is what the first one looks like, and it is extremely common and not tragic. People who were once a household, who are now separate households, who have affection for each other and meet at holidays and behave warmly. Nothing is wrong. Nothing is broken.

There is simply nothing being built.

And here is what the second looks like. The same people, the same affection, the same holidays, and a shared sense that this family is for something. Not a business necessarily. Not a shared project. A sense of common direction that would show up if you asked each person what this family is about.

The distance between those two is not resources, and it is not proximity, and it is not how much anybody likes each other.

It is whether anybody ever said what the family was for.

Because a family that has never said it will default to the first, and the defaulting takes about a decade and nobody notices it happening.

I ask families a version of this early and the reaction tells me a great deal. Some people find the question strange. What do you mean, what is it for? It is a family.

That is not a wrong answer. It is an answer from inside the first option, and it is the answer most people give.

The psalm calls unity good and pleasant and treats it as something worth beholding, which is language reserved for things that are somewhat unusual.

Unity is not described there as the natural state of families. It is described as an achievement.

That is the honest frame for this session. Interdependence is not what happens if you leave a family alone. It is what happens if a family decides.

So decide, and then say it out loud, and then the next four sessions are about how.

Reflect

1. Which is your family, honestly?

2. Has anybody ever said what it is for?

3. What would each person answer if you asked?

4. What would it take to become the second one?

SESSION 3

Day 11

The One You're Living Today

It is the only one currently transmitting

"Only be careful for yourself and watch over your soul diligently, so that you do not forget the things which your eyes have seen." DEUTERONOMY 4:9

Of the three legacies, the one you are living today is the most important, and the reason is mechanical rather than moral.

It is the only one currently transmitting.

The one you received has already happened. You can examine it, decide what to carry and what to set down, and that examination is worth doing. But it is finished as an event.

The one you will leave has not begun. It is planned, it is arranged, and none of it has moved.

The one you are living is moving right now, today, into the people around you, and it will move whether or not you have given it a moment's thought.

I want to make that concrete, because it is easy to nod at.

Your children learned what your family thinks about money by watching what happened when money got tight. They learned what your family thinks about commitment from whether you came to the thing you said you would come to. They learned what your family thinks about failure from what happened after somebody failed.

None of those were lessons. Nobody sat anybody down. They were ordinary days, and ordinary days are what the third legacy is made of.

Here is the good news buried in that.

If legacy were transmitted by significant moments, most people would have very few opportunities. Significant moments are rare and you cannot schedule them.

If it is transmitted daily, then every day is one, including this one, and including whatever correction you might want to make.

I have watched families change what they were transmitting quite late. Not erase what came before, which nobody can do. Change what was currently moving.

And what did it was never a dramatic conversation. It was a person deciding to live differently in small ways that other people could observe.

Moses says be careful and watch over your soul diligently so that you do not forget what your eyes have seen. And the next clause is that you make them known to your children.

Notice the order. Watch yourself first. The making known follows from it.

Day 12

They Get to See All of It

Your family has access to a version nobody else does

"Nothing is concealed that will not be revealed, or hidden that will not be known." LUKE 12:2

Here is the uncomfortable part of the third legacy.

They get to see all of it.

At church you have got your church face on. At home you have got your real face out. And they live in your house.

I want to be fair about this, because behaving differently in different settings is not hypocrisy. Everyone modulates. A person who was identical at work, at church, and at their own kitchen table would be strange rather than admirable.

The point is narrower and it is this. Your family has access to a version of you that nobody else sees, and that version is the one doing the teaching.

Which means the formation of your children is being carried out by the least curated version of you that exists.

Here is what actually transmits most, and it surprises people.

It is not the large failures. Those are visible, they usually get addressed, and addressing them teaches something valuable. A family that watches a person fail and then repair has learned a great deal.

It is the small consistent things that nobody names. How you speak about people who are not in the room. What you say when you are tired. Whether you follow through on something minor that nobody would have noticed.

Those are the curriculum, delivered daily, without a lesson plan and without anybody registering that a lesson occurred.

Now, the relief. This does not require you to be exemplary and you cannot be.

It requires you to be real and pointed somewhere. The repair matters more than the performance, and a household where things get named and mended teaches something a flawless one could not.

Jesus says nothing concealed will not be revealed. It is said about hypocrisy and it describes something that happens in families on a much shorter timescale than eternity.

Your children already know. They have known for years. Whatever you think is concealed at home is not concealed at home.

So the question is not whether they see it. It is what they see you do about it.

Reflect

4. What do you think is concealed that is not?

Day 13

What They Replicate

People do not replicate what you say

"The things you have learned and received and heard and seen in me, practice these things." PHILIPPIANS 4:9

Here is the sentence that sits underneath this whole session.

People do not replicate what you say. They replicate what you live.

I have watched that operate in families for thirty years and I have never seen an exception.

It is not that words are useless. Words explain, and explanation matters enormously, and a value that is lived and never named gets copied as behavior without the reason attached, which does not survive a hard year.

But when the words and the life disagree, the life wins, every time, without a contest.

Here is a version I see constantly.

A father tells his children that family matters most. He means it entirely. And they watched him choose work over family events for twenty years, not carelessly, always for a good reason, each individual instance defensible.

What did they learn? Not that family matters most. They learned that family matters most in principle and comes second in practice, which is a more sophisticated and more durable lesson than the one intended.

And here is the part that should get your attention. They will replicate that, including the sincerity. They will tell their own children that family matters most and mean it, and choose the same way.

The pattern transfers with the words attached.

Paul lists four things. Learned, received, heard, and seen. Three of the four are about words and the fourth is about observation, and then he says practice these things.

The word practice is not incidental. He is not asking them to remember or agree. He is asking them to do, which puts the whole list in the category of behavior rather than belief.

Notice also that he includes himself as the example without hesitation. It is the same claim as be imitators of me, and it carries the same assumption, which is that imitation is happening whether or not anyone plans for it.

Your family is replicating something. The only question is what.

Day 14

Where the Year Actually Went

The gap is a clarity problem, not a character problem

"Consider your ways!" HAGGAI 1:7

There is usually a gap between what a family says matters and where the year actually went.

I want to name what that gap is and what it is not, because families hear the observation as an accusation and it is not one.

It is not hypocrisy. A hypocrite says one thing and privately believes another. That is rare in the families I sit with.

What is common is somebody who believes the thing sincerely and has a calendar that was built by other forces.

Nobody sat down in a particular year and decided that work would take the place family was supposed to have. It happened one reasonable yes at a time, each defensible, and the sum of them produced a life that does not match the stated values of the person living it.

That is a clarity problem before it is a character problem. Nobody decided, so the loudest thing decided.

Here is how you find the gap, and it is not by reflection.

Reflection will tell you what you value, and you already know that. You find the gap by looking at evidence.

Look at last year's calendar. Look at where the money actually went. Look at what got cancelled when something had to give, because what gets cancelled is what is genuinely optional in your life, whatever you would say about it.

Those three will tell you more in twenty minutes than a month of thinking.

And then, having found it, do not spend any time on remorse. Remorse is a way of feeling like you have addressed something without addressing it.

Haggai says consider your ways, and he says it twice in the same short chapter. The people had been building comfortable houses while the temple sat unfinished, and he does not accuse them of anything.

He tells them to look.

Looking is uncomfortable in a particular way. It is not the discovery of a disaster. It is the discovery that a hundred reasonable decisions have added up to something nobody would have chosen.

That is what a legacy by default looks like from close up. Not a failure. An accumulation.

Reflect

1. What do you say matters most?

2. What does last year's calendar say?

3. What gets cancelled when something has to give?

4. How large is the gap, honestly?

Day 15

No Shame in It

I just want you to know

"There is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus." ROMANS 8:1

This is the last day of the third session and it is about tone, because everything in the last four days can be held in a way that helps or in a way that crushes.

When I look at a family's numbers with them and something is off, I tell them plainly. And then I say what I actually mean, which is that there is no shame in it. I just want you to know.

Because a family that knows can decide. A family that does not know is being decided for.

That is the whole purpose of looking, and it is easy to lose.

Here is the failure I want to guard against, because I have seen material like this do damage.

Somebody reads a book like this, takes it seriously, looks honestly at the gap between their stated values and their actual life, and concludes that they have failed as a parent.

That conclusion is not accurate and it is not useful. It produces either paralysis or a burst of overcorrection that nobody in the family asked for and that does not last.

The gap is information. It is not a verdict on your worth or your love for your family.

And there is a specific version of this that applies to people reading this later in life, with grown children and a good deal already behind them. The material can read as a list of things you did not do.

I would say two things to that person.

The first is that the third legacy is still transmitting. Whatever is true this year is moving, which means the correction available to you is real rather than theoretical.

The second is that families are more forgiving than people expect, and an adult child hearing a parent say I got that wrong and I have been thinking about it is receiving something they will remember far longer than the original failure.

Paul says there is now no condemnation. It is written to people who had plenty to be condemned for and it is not a statement about their performance.

It is a statement about where they stand, which is prior to and independent of any assessment of how they have done.

So look honestly. Then let the looking do its work rather than turning it into a sentence.

Reflect

1. How have you been holding what you found in this session?

2. Are you using it as information or as a verdict?

3. What would you say to somebody else who found the same gap?

4. What is still available to you?
