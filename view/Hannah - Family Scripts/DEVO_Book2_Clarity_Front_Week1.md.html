HOW TO USE THIS

Thirty readings. Five a week for six weeks, with two days off. Each one takes about ten minutes to read and considerably longer to be done with.

This works completely on its own. You do not need the videos, you do not need the guide, and you do not need anyone else in your family to be doing it. If this is the only piece of the series you ever touch, it will still do its work.

It also runs alongside the guide. If your family is working through What Will You Do With What You've Been Given?, these thirty days are the reading plan. Week One goes with Session 1, and so on through Week Six.

Each day has six parts.

The reading. Ten minutes. Tom's teaching, in his own words, aimed at you rather than at your advisor.

Reflect. Four questions. You are not required to answer all four. Take the one that catches.

Practice. One thing to do, usually written, usually short.

One Question to Ask. A question to take to one person in your family that week. This is the part that changes things.

The Bucket. Private. Nobody reads it. This is the only place in the whole series where you can be completely honest without managing anyone's reaction, and it will not work if you write what you would be comfortable having read.

Prayer. Not a written prayer. A direction to take.

If you fall behind, do not restart. Pick up where the calendar is, not where you stopped. The days are not sequential arguments, and Day 17 does not depend on Day 16.

WEEK ONE

The Five Areas of Your Legacy

Bringing Focus to What Matters Most

Day 1

Deep Waters

One who has insight draws them out

"A plan in the heart of a man is like deep water, but a man of understanding draws it out." PROVERBS 20:5

Notice what this proverb does not say. It does not say the plans are absent, or shallow, or waiting to be supplied by somebody wiser. It says they are deep, and it says they need drawing out.

I have been sitting with families for about thirty years, and this verse describes the work better than anything else I know. Most of what matters to a person or a family is already there. It just has not been brought up clearly.

That is not what I expected to find. When I started, I assumed my job was to bring things to families. Answers, strategies, structures. And there is a place for that. But the thing that actually changes a family is almost never something I brought in the door. It is something that was already sitting in the room, unspoken, sometimes for decades.

So here is what I want you to consider on the first day of this.

You already know more than you think you do about what matters to you. You may not be able to say it in a sentence, and you may never have been asked, and nobody in your family may have any idea. But it is not missing. It is deep.

The problem is that deep water does not surface on its own. It has to be drawn out, and drawing takes a bucket and it takes somebody willing to lower it.

Most families never lower anything. Not out of indifference. Out of not knowing that they should, and not having anyone in their life whose job it is to ask.

Think about who has ever asked you a real question about your life. Not how are you doing. Not how is business. A question that made you stop and think before you answered it. For most people that list is very short, and for a lot of people it is empty.

Now think about your own children. When was the last time somebody asked them what they actually want their life to be about? If your family is like most, the answer is that nobody has, and everyone is assuming somebody else did.

I want to be precise about something, because this is where people get it wrong. The reason to ask is not that you will get a good answer immediately. You usually will not. The first answer is almost always the prepared one, the version a person has said before at a dinner party. The real answer comes on the second or third pass, and only if somebody stays in the room long enough to ask again.

That is the whole skill, and it is not a skill you need thirty years to learn. It is asking, and then waiting, and then asking once more.

There is one more thing in the verse worth sitting with. It says a man of understanding draws it out. Not a man of expertise. Not a professional. Understanding is available to a wife, a son, a sister, a friend. You do not need credentials to draw water out of your own family. You need to be the kind of person somebody would say something true to.

Which raises the question that this whole thirty days is going to keep coming back to.

Are you that person in your family? And if you are not, who is?

Reflect

1. When was the last time someone asked you a real question about your life, and what did you do with it?

2. What is sitting in deep water in your family right now that everyone senses and nobody has said?

3. Who in your family is most likely to have something they have never said out loud?

4. Are you a person your family would tell the truth to? What is your evidence either way?

Practice

Write down the one thing about your own life you have never said out loud to anyone in your family.

You are not going to say it today. You are just going to stop pretending it is not there.

Now write why you have not said it. Be specific. Not "there has never been a good time." What are you actually protecting?

Day 2

Three Keywords

Clarity, alignment, communication

"Commit your works to the Lord and your plans will be established." PROVERBS 16:3

There are three words we come back to in this work. Clarity, alignment, and communication. They go in that order, and the order is not a preference.

Clarity is being clear about what you actually want. Alignment is making sure your life and your decisions and your documents reflect it. Communication is telling the people it affects.

Most families fail at one of the three and cannot tell which one.

Clarity fails quietly. A family does not know what they want and nobody notices, because a lack of clarity does not announce itself. It shows up later as decisions that do not connect to each other, as different people pulling different directions, as a growing sense that things are happening to the family rather than being chosen by it.

Alignment fails visibly, but usually too late. This is the family with real clarity and documents that say something else. I have shown people what their own paperwork actually says more times than I can count, and more than once the response has been, I had no idea that is what it said. They were clear. The paper was not.

Communication fails last and it fails hardest, because by then the other two are done and everyone assumes the work is finished. The plan is right, the documents are right, and nobody has told the children anything. So the family finds out at the worst possible moment, from a lawyer, in a room where no questions can be answered by the person who made the decisions.

Here is why the order matters.

You cannot align around something that has never been made clear. You will align around something, because alignment is not optional, and what you will align around is whatever is loudest. Usually that is a deadline, a tax consequence, or the opinion of whoever pushes hardest.

And you cannot communicate something nobody has defined. You will communicate something, because families always do. It will be a version, assembled from fragments, and it will not be yours.

So this thirty days is Week One of six, and Week One is entirely about the first word. Not because alignment and communication are less important, but because they are downstream, and there is no point straightening a river above the source.

The proverb has an order in it too. Commit first. The establishing is His part.

I have watched a lot of families try to establish first. They build the structure, and then hope clarity arrives to fill it. It does not usually work that way. The structure ends up holding whatever was there when it was poured.

Start with what you want. The rest can be built.

Reflect

1. Of the three, which is thinnest in your family right now?

2. What has that cost you already, that you can actually name?

3. Where has your family aligned around something loud rather than something chosen?

4. If your documents were read aloud tomorrow, would they say what you would say?

Day 3

Legacy by Default

Something you developed over time

"The plans of the diligent lead certainly to advantage, but everyone who is hasty comes certainly to poverty." PROVERBS 21:5

Most people have a plan, and it is not the one they would have chosen. It is something they developed over time without ever deciding on it.

That is what we call a legacy by default. What we are trying to help you move to is a legacy by design.

Hear what that means, because people hear it as an accusation and it is not one. A family with documents in a drawer believes the matter is settled, and in a narrow sense it is. Something will happen when they die. The question is not whether something happens. The question is whether anyone chose it.

A legacy by default is not the absence of a plan. It is a plan that assembled itself.

Here is how it happens, and none of these steps look like a mistake at the time.

You do a will when the children are small, because someone tells you that you should. It is fine. It fits the family you had that year.

Then life moves. A child grows up in a way you did not expect. A business does better, or worse. Somebody marries. Somebody divorces. A grandchild arrives with needs nobody planned for. A parent dies and something comes to you that was not in the picture.

None of those events triggers a phone call. There is no system anywhere that says, your family changed, come back in. So the document made in year one is still governing year twenty-six, and it is describing a family that no longer exists.

Meanwhile the other half of your legacy has been transmitting the entire time, and nobody has looked at that either. Your children have been watching you for thirty years. They have drawn conclusions. They have decided what this family is about, based entirely on what they observed, and not one of them has read your estate plan.

That is the part that took me the longest to accept in my own work. The documents are for later. Everything else is already underway, and it cannot be paused while you get organized.

So the drift is not one thing. It is two. The paper drifts away from the family, and the family drifts away from what anybody intended, and both of them do it quietly.

The proverb sets diligence against haste, and the interesting word is certainly. Both roads have certain outcomes. Neither is a coin flip.

I would put it this way. Doing nothing is not neutral. Doing nothing is a decision with a known result, and the result is that somebody else decides. Sometimes that somebody is a state you happen to live in. Sometimes it is whichever of your children is most comfortable being forceful in a difficult week.

You still get to choose. That is the whole point of the six weeks in front of you. But the window in which choosing is available is not permanent, and it is shorter than most people assume.

Reflect

1. Where in your family is something forming right now that nobody chose?

2. When were your documents last looked at, and what has changed since?

3. What have your children concluded about this family from watching, rather than from being told?

4. What is the cost of one more year of default in your particular situation?

Day 4

My Day Job

This is my passion

"For we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand." EPHESIANS 2:10

He had built a substantial company and handed it to his son. By any measure a planner would use, the file was closed. The business had grown. Succession was done, and done well. Nothing in the numbers needed attention.

Then, in conversation, he described a set of projects he had taken on in some struggling neighborhoods near where the company was headquartered. Places that stood to gain far more from his presence and his leadership than from a check.

He said the company was no longer his day job.

This was his day job. This was his passion.

Sit with that for a minute, because there is nothing in a standard planning process that would ever have surfaced it. There is no line on any intake form for what a person intends to do with the second half of their influence. Everything in the process is built to capture what you have. Nothing in it asks what you are for.

He had spent decades building what the world calls a legacy, and then went looking, on his own, for the one he actually wanted.

Most people never make that turn. Not because they lack the desire. Because nobody ever asks, and because the question does not arrive on its own. Retirement arrives. A sale arrives. A diagnosis arrives. The question has to be asked.

I have a phrase I use with people approaching that stretch. You do not retire from something. You retire to something.

That distinction matters more than it sounds. Retiring from is a subtraction, and subtraction alone is a poor plan for the most experienced years of your life. Retiring to requires you to have named the thing, in advance, before the calendar empties out.

And here is what I want you to notice in the verse.

Prepared beforehand.

Whatever you were made for was decided before you got here. Which means you are not inventing it at sixty. You are finding it, and finding is a different activity than inventing. It requires attention rather than imagination.

There is also something in this for the people watching you.

Your children are forming their view of what a life is for by watching what you do with yours, and the most instructive stretch is not the building years. Anyone can look purposeful while a business is growing. What your family learns most from is what you do when you no longer have to do anything.

That is the season where a legacy either becomes visible or quietly does not.

So the question is not what you will do with your time. It is what you were prepared for that you have not started yet.

Reflect

1. What would you do if the thing that currently occupies you stopped tomorrow?

2. What are you retiring to, in one sentence?

3. What have you been prepared for that you have never begun?

4. What are your children learning from what you do with your freest hours?

Day 5

The Genius Is in the Question Asking

That's how you get clarity

"He who gives an answer before he hears, it is folly and shame to him." PROVERBS 18:13

The genius of this work is in the question asking, because that is how you get clarity. By asking the right kind of questions and really understanding what is deeply in somebody's heart.

That sentence took me a long time to earn. Early on I thought the value I brought was what I knew. I am a CPA. I had technical answers and people paid for them, and there is nothing wrong with that. But the thing that actually changes a family is not the answer I supply. It is the question that surfaces what they already have.

So how do you ask well. A few things I have learned, and none of them require training.

Ask questions that make somebody slow down. Then give them time to answer. Do not move to the next question. That last part is where most of us fail. We ask something good, the person pauses, the pause gets uncomfortable, and we fill it. The pause was the whole point. Something was rising and we interrupted it.

Then circle back. It is like the old Columbo. He would ask a simple question, come back around, and ask a follow-up. The first answer is usually the practiced one, the version a person has said before. The second answer is the real one, and you only get it by returning.

And listen for the right thing. You have got to listen, not to judge. You are listening for understanding. Why did they say that. What is behind what they are saying.

When there is a couple in the room, there is a second layer. You want to know what the other spouse thinks about what the first one just said. A lot of men, toward the end, want to help their wife accomplish what she wants, because she supported them in their vision for forty years. She is often sitting right there with something specific in mind that has never been asked for.

Here is what surprised me most. People love to talk about themselves, and hardly anybody asks them to. Ask somebody a thought-provoking question and they think, why is he asking me that. And they conclude that you care. That you actually want to know what they have to say.

The question does that work before a single answer arrives. Which means even the ones that go nowhere are not wasted.

Now, you may be reading this as the person in your family most likely to do the asking, or you may be reading it as the person nobody has asked. Both are common. If it is the second one, I would say this plainly. You are allowed to ask for the question. There is no rule that says you have to wait to be invited into your own family's conversation.

The proverb calls answering before listening folly and shame, which is stronger language than we would use. But it names something true.

Answering early feels like competence and functions as deafness.

2. Which questions in your family actually slow people down, and which just move the evening along?

3. Whose voice do you hear least in your family, and what have you done about it?

4. When did you last hear something someone in your family did not say out loud?
