SESSION 4

Your Financial Legacy

A blessing and not a curse

Day 16

A Blessing and Not a Curse

More is not always better

"A good man leaves an inheritance to his children's children." PROVERBS 13:22

Here is the question I ask families, and it reframes everything that follows.

What amount of inheritance are you going to leave your children that is going to be a blessing and not a curse?

Most people have never considered that both are possible. The assumption running underneath nearly every plan is that more is better, and that the only real question is how to maximize and protect the amount.

More is not always better.

If somebody is not prepared, more money creates problems rather than solving them. I have watched it happen enough times that I no longer treat it as an unlucky exception. It is a predictable outcome when preparation has not kept pace with the amount.

So the question is not how much. The question is what for.

What do you want this to accomplish in the life of each person who receives it? What kind of impact do you want it to have?

That question is harder than it sounds and it is the one that actually determines the number. Because if you do not know what you hope it will do in somebody's life, then you have no basis at all for deciding whether to leave more or less than you could.

I want to be careful here, because this is where people hear judgment and there is none intended. Wanting to provide for your children is right. The instinct is good. What I am asking is whether the provision has been thought through past the amount.

The proverb is worth reading slowly. A good man leaves an inheritance to his children's children.

Two generations. Not his children. His children's children.

That means whatever he leaves has to survive a handoff by somebody else. Money survives that handoff without help; it has custodians and statements and a legal system built to move it. Everything else requires a carrier, and the carrier is a person, and a person has to be prepared.

Which is why the amount is the second question and never the first.

Reflect

1. What amount would be a blessing in the life of each person who receives it?

2. Have you ever asked what for rather than how much?

3. Is there a number above which this stops helping the people you love?

4. What have you assumed about more being better?

Day 17

Shirt Sleeves to Shirt Sleeves

By the third generation

"An inheritance gained hurriedly at the beginning will not be blessed in the end." PROVERBS 20:21

Most legacy families know the phrase. Shirt sleeves to shirt sleeves in three generations.

The first generation builds it. The second maintains it, or spends some of it. By the third it is gone, and the great-grandchildren are back where the great-grandfather started.

Families hate that idea. They do not want their children to be part of it. What they usually do not know is how to keep it from happening, and the instinct is to solve it structurally, with better trusts and tighter controls.

Structure helps and structure is not the mechanism.

The mechanism is that the first generation had something the third generation never received, and it was not money. It was whatever produced the money. The habits, the judgment, the willingness to go without, the understanding of what a dollar costs to earn.

That is not transmitted by a document. It is transmitted by a person, over years, on purpose.

And here is the part that is easy to miss. The second generation usually did receive some of it, because they were there. They watched it get built. They remember the years before. The third generation has no memory of anything but the result, and so they inherit the outcome without any of the formation that produced it.

That is why the third generation is the break point. Not because they are worse people. Because they are the first ones for whom the wealth was simply the weather.

The proverb says an inheritance gained hurriedly at the beginning will not be blessed in the end. The word hurriedly is doing the work. It is not describing an amount. It is describing a pace.

Which suggests a remedy that is available to any family, at any size. Slow it down. Give responsibility before you give resources. Let them handle something small and watch. Then something larger.

You are not testing them. You are building the only thing that makes the eventual arrival survivable.

Reflect

1. What produced what your family has, and has that been transmitted?

2. What does the youngest generation in your family remember, and what have they never seen?

3. Where has anything been given hurriedly?

4. What could be given slowly instead?

Day 18

Equally, Not the Same

You've got to communicate why

"For just as we have many parts in one body, and all the parts do not have the same function, so we, who are many, are one body in Christ." ROMANS 12:4-5

You love your children equally. That does not necessarily mean you treat them the same.

Because they are not the same. Some have needs the others do not have. Some are ready and some are not. Some built something and some are still finding their footing.

Equal is easy. Equal requires no judgment at all. You divide by the number of children and nobody can accuse you of favoritism, and that is precisely why so many families default to it.

Wise is harder, because wise requires you to actually know your children and then act on what you know.

Now here is the part families skip, and skipping it is what turns a wise decision into a wound.

If you are going to treat them differently, you have to communicate that you are going to treat them differently. And why.

The unfairness is almost never the difference. Families absorb difference all the time. The unfairness is finding out about the difference in a lawyer's office, from a document, with no one available to explain the reasoning.

At that point the difference is not a decision anymore. It is a verdict, and it gets read as a statement about who was loved more.

I have seen a family where the arrangement was thoughtful, generous, and completely defensible, and it did enormous damage anyway, purely because nobody had said a word about it in advance.

And I have seen the harder-looking version go fine. A father sat down with three grown children and explained exactly what he was doing and why one of them would be handled differently. It was uncomfortable for an hour. It was not a wound.

Paul's image is a body where the parts do not have the same function. It is not making them equal. It is making them one, which is a different claim and a more demanding one.

So if you are planning something unequal, that may well be right. The question is whether the people affected will hear it from you or from a stranger.

Reflect

1. Are you planning to treat anyone differently?

2. Do they know? Do the others?

3. What is your actual reason, said plainly?

4. What would it cost to say it out loud this year?

Day 19

Where You Are Right Now

We don't know what to change if we don't know where we are

"Let's examine and search out our ways, and return to the Lord." LAMENTATIONS 3:40

Before any of this can be acted on, there is an unglamorous step.

You have to know where you are right now. Because we do not know what to change if we do not know where we are.

That sounds too obvious to say and it is the most commonly skipped step in the entire process.

Families want to start with the interesting question. What should we do about the trust. What about the business. What about the child we are worried about. Those are real questions and every one of them is downstream, and answering a downstream question from an unknown position produces a confident decision built on nothing.

Most families do not have the full picture of what they have. They know pieces. There is a retirement statement. There is a policy from 1998. There is a property that came through somebody's mother. Each piece is known by somebody. Nobody knows all of them at once.

So there is information and no picture. And you cannot make a decision from information.

Here is what I would ask you to do, and it costs nothing but an evening.

Put it on one page. What you own, what you owe, and who owns what. Approximate is fine. The point is not precision, it is possession of the picture.

And do it with your spouse, both of you looking at the same thing at the same time. In a great many marriages one person has carried this alone for thirty years, not because anything was hidden but because that was the arrangement in year three and nobody revisited it.

That single evening does more for a marriage than most people expect.

Lamentations says let us examine and search out our ways. It is written at the lowest moment in that book, and the instruction is not to fix anything. It is to look.

Looking is the whole assignment today. Not fixing. Looking, honestly, at where you actually are.

Reflect

1. Could you list what your family has, right now, without looking anything up?

2. Could your spouse?

3. What piece is known by exactly one person?

4. What have you told yourself about why you have not put it on one page?

Day 20

Goals and Gaps

Here's what you said you wanted, here's your existing plan

"The mind of a man plans his way, but the Lord directs his steps." PROVERBS 16:9

Once a family knows where they are, we hold it up against what they said they wanted.

Here is what you told us matters. Here is what is actually in place. And a good deal of the time, the honest sentence is that there is a significant gap between the two.

I want to say clearly what a gap is and is not.

It is not a failure and it is not an indictment. It is information, and most families are genuinely surprised to find one, because they were not ignoring anything. Nobody had ever put the two pictures side by side.

And a gap is not always something missing structurally. Sometimes everything is technically in order and the gap is in preparation. The documents are fine and the person who will receive what they describe has never been told anything and would not know what to do on the day.

I remember telling a man exactly what would happen the day he died. His son would receive a check, that week, for an amount his son had never handled in his life.

He said, that would be horrible.

He was right, and nothing in his plan was defective. Every document said precisely what he had asked for. The gap was between what the paperwork would do and what he actually hoped would happen to his son.

That kind of gap only appears when you put the two pictures next to each other, and most families never do, because each picture lives with a different professional and neither one has been asked to look at the other.

The proverb is honest about the limits here. A man plans his way, and the Lord directs his steps. Planning is not futile in that sentence, but it is not sovereign either.

Which is oddly freeing. You are not being asked to construct a perfect plan. You are being asked to close the gap between what you say you want and what you have actually arranged, and then to hold it loosely.

That is the end of this session. Next we go to what a family built.

Reflect

1. What did you say you wanted?

2. What is actually in place?

3. Where is the largest gap, and how long has it been there?

4. Is your gap structural, or is it in somebody's preparation?

Day 21

The Golden Goose

If the goose is not healthy, the eggs are going to be crummy

"Whoever tends the fig tree will eat its fruit." PROVERBS 27:18

If you own a business, or if you lead somewhere in your work, there is a phrase worth holding onto.

It is the golden goose. If the goose is not healthy, the eggs are going to be crummy.

I use it because families forget the order. They focus on distributions, on what the business provides, on what each person is entitled to. All of that is the eggs. And a family can spend an enormous amount of energy negotiating the eggs while nobody is looking after the goose.

The most common version I see is a family putting somebody in a role because of who they are rather than what they can do. Same last name, therefore qualified. That is not a family decision, it is an abdication, and the business pays for it first and the family pays for it second.

You want the most competent people you have leading it. Sometimes that is a family member and sometimes it is not, and being honest about which is one of the harder conversations available.

There is a second version of the same mistake, which is a family taking out more than the business can sustain because everybody's lifestyle got built around a good decade. The goose is being asked to lay faster than it can, and nobody wants to be the one to say so.

Now, if you do not own a business, do not skip this session. Substitute the word work.

You have built something through your working life. Influence, reputation, relationships, a way of doing things that people around you learned from. That is not smaller than a business, it is just harder to see on a balance sheet, and it will also outlast your involvement in it.

The proverb says whoever tends the fig tree will eat its fruit. Tending comes first in the sentence and in reality.

So the question today is simple and it is not about succession yet. It is about health.

How is the goose?

Reflect

1. How healthy is what you have built, honestly?

2. Is anyone in a role because of their name rather than their capability?

3. Is the family taking out more than it can sustain?

4. If you do not own a business, what have you built through your work that will outlast you?

Day 22

Your Business Is Like a Church

You've got three kinds of people

"Whatever you do, do your work heartily, as for the Lord and not for people." COLOSSIANS 3:23

I tell business owners something that reframes how they see the place.

Your business is like a church.

Think about a congregation. You have people who are not believers. You have cultural Christians who are there for reasons that have little to do with conviction. And you have people who are genuinely committed.

Your business has exactly the same three groups.

Which means that part of what you are doing, whether you have ever said it out loud, is helping people move. You would love for some of them to come to Christ. You would love for the cultural ones to get serious. And with the committed ones, you are already aligned on what matters.

But you treat all three the same way, even though some are easier to deal with than others.

That last sentence is the one I would underline. The temptation is to sort people and then treat the sorted groups differently. That is not what a good church does and it is not what a good business does either.

Here is why this matters for a family legacy rather than just for management.

Your children are watching how you treat the people who work for you, and they are drawing conclusions that will outlast anything you tell them about values. If the people around your business are treated as instruments, your children will learn that people are instruments. If they are treated as people you are responsible for, your children learn something else entirely.

I know an owner who told me the thing he was proudest of was not the growth. It was that a man who had worked for him for twenty years had become a different man in those twenty years. Nothing about that appears in a valuation.

Paul says to work heartily as for the Lord rather than for people. It is written to servants, which is to say to people with very little control over their circumstances, and it reframes the whole activity. The work is not primarily transactional even when it is genuinely transactional.

So the question is not only what your business produces. It is who it produces.

Reflect

1. Who are the three groups in your workplace, and can you name people in each?

2. Do you treat them the same, honestly?

3. What have your children learned about people from watching how you run things?

4. Who has become a different person because they worked with you?

Day 23

Business Continuity

Who's running the business when you're not there

"The sensible person sees evil and hides himself, but the naive proceed, and pay the penalty." PROVERBS 22:3

There are two questions that sound like one question and are not.

Succession is who leads it next. Continuity is who is in charge if you are in a car wreck and in the hospital for a month.

Most owners have thought about the first one, in a general way, and almost nobody has written down the second.

I ask this directly. If you were unavailable for thirty days starting tomorrow, who decides? Who signs? Who do the employees go to on day three when something has to be answered?

The pause after that question tells you everything.

And I want to be clear that the pause is not negligence. Continuity feels like a small administrative matter compared to succession, which is philosophical and emotional and about legacy. So it gets deferred as the lesser question.

It is the more urgent one. Succession is probably years away. Continuity applies tomorrow morning.

I worked with a man who was in the hospital for a month. Nothing had been arranged, because nothing had needed to be. And the vice president was calling around asking who actually had the authority to make a decision, and the honest answer was nobody, and the business absorbed a month of paralysis it did not need to.

There is a second layer that families miss entirely. In many cases the spouse would inherit the ownership immediately and has no idea how any of it works.

That is not a criticism of the spouse. It is a description of an arrangement that made sense for thirty years and stops making sense in one afternoon.

The proverb says the sensible person sees the evil coming and takes cover, while the naive walk on and pay for it. It is not a warning about pessimism. It is about the small cost of looking ahead versus the large cost of not.

So write the name down. That is the whole exercise. Who decides for thirty days.

If you cannot write a name, that is your answer, and it is the most fixable problem in this entire book.

Reflect

1. If you were unavailable for thirty days, who decides?

2. Do they know? Does anyone else know?

3. What would your spouse need to understand that they currently do not?

4. Why has this not been written down?

Day 24

A Secondary Succession Plan

Who's the successor to him

"Select out of all the people able men who fear God, men of truth, those who hate dishonest gain." EXODUS 18:21

Families that do think about succession usually stop one layer too early.

You have a successor. Good. Who is the successor to him?

That question lands awkwardly because it sounds like planning for a failure, and people hear it as doubt about the person they just named. It is not that.

It is arithmetic. Your successor is a human being with a lifespan, a health history, and a family of his own who may need something from him you cannot predict. He may also, entirely reasonably, decide in eight years that this is not the life he wants.

If the plan has exactly one name in it, the plan is one phone call from having no name in it.

There is a second reason this matters, and it is the one owners find more persuasive.

Naming a second layer forces you to develop a second layer. The moment you ask who comes after him, you start noticing who is being prepared and who is not, and in most family businesses the honest answer is that nobody below the named successor is being prepared for anything.

That is a bigger vulnerability than the succession question itself, because it means the whole enterprise depends on the continued good fortune of one individual.

And there is a family dimension. In a lot of families there is one obvious successor and several other capable people who have quietly concluded there is nothing here for them. Some of them leave. Some stay and disengage. Almost none of them say so.

Asking about the second layer surfaces those people, and frequently it turns out somebody was interested and had decided not to bother mentioning it.

Jethro tells Moses to select able men from among all the people, and the criteria are worth noticing. Able. God-fearing. Truthful. Not covetous. Only one of the four is about competence.

That is what you are looking for at every layer, and it is a description of character rather than credentials, which means it can be developed in somebody starting now.

Reflect

1. Who is your successor?

2. Who is the successor to him?

3. Who is being developed below the person you named?

4. Who has quietly concluded there is nothing here for them?

Day 25

Three Percent

So what are we going to do to make sure we're in that three percent

"Prepare your work outside and make it ready for yourself in the field; afterward, then, build your house." PROVERBS 24:27

I tell business owners the numbers early, and I tell them for a specific reason.

Roughly seventy percent of businesses do not survive the move from the first generation to the second. By the time you get from the second to the third, the failure rate is around ninety-seven percent.

Which means you have about a three percent chance.

People sometimes think I am trying to discourage them. I am not. If I wanted to discourage them I would stop at the number.

The question I ask next is the entire point. So what are we going to do to make sure we are in that three percent?

That reframes the whole thing from a prediction into a project. The three percent is not luck. The families that land there did specific things, and the things are not secret, and most of them are in this book.

They communicated. They prepared the next generation deliberately rather than hoping. They got clear on what the business was for beyond profit. They separated ownership from employment in their own thinking. They let capable outsiders lead when the family did not have the right person. They talked about the hard things before they were urgent.

None of that is exotic. All of it is uncomfortable, which is why the number is three and not thirty.

The proverb has an order in it that owners will recognize immediately. Prepare the work in the field first. Then build the house.

That is agricultural advice about not spending your capital on a comfortable life before the productive asset can support it. It is also a decent description of what separates families that hold from families that do not.

I have a friend whose family business is in its sixth generation. It exists. It is not a myth. And what is striking when you talk to that family is how ordinary the practices are and how relentlessly they were kept up.

So take the number seriously and then move immediately past it. The number describes families who did nothing in particular.

You are reading this, which means you are already not one of them.

SESSION 6

Your Charitable Legacy

Defining a vision for generosity

Day 26

Bear Fruit, and More Fruit

Your fruit should remain

"You did not choose Me, but I chose you and appointed you that you would go and bear fruit, and that your fruit would remain." JOHN 15:16

In John 15 there is a progression that I come back to constantly.

Bear fruit. Then more fruit. Then even more fruit.

It is not a rebuke of the person bearing some. It is a description of a direction, and the direction does not have a stopping point where a person has finally done enough and can stand still.

I find that clarifying rather than exhausting, and here is why.

Most people who have built something arrive at a season where the building is finished and nobody has told them what comes next. The company is sold, or handed over. The career is behind them. And there is a quiet assumption underneath a lot of retirement planning that the productive part of a life is over and what remains is maintenance.

John 15 does not describe a life that has a maintenance phase.

Now notice the last clause, which is the one people skip. That your fruit would remain.

Remaining is a different standard than producing. Plenty of good things are produced and do not remain. Money gets given and consumed. Buildings get built and repurposed. A gift can accomplish something real this year and leave nothing behind it.

Fruit that remains implies something planted rather than something spent.

That distinction changes how a family thinks about giving. Responsive giving meets a need, and needs are real and meeting them is right. But responsive giving rarely remains, because it was never aimed at anything past the need in front of it.

Giving that remains usually has three characteristics. It is aimed at something specific. It is sustained long enough for compounding. And somebody in the next generation knows why it is being done.

That third one is what makes it remain past you, and it is the one families almost never do.

So this session is not about giving more. It is about giving in a way that is still working after you are gone.

Reflect

1. What have you produced that will remain?

2. Is your giving planted or spent?

3. What season are you in, and have you assumed the productive part is behind you?

4. Does anyone in the next generation know why you give to what you give to?

Day 27

The Most Fun Gift

What has been the most fun gift you have ever given

"Each one must do just as he has purposed in his heart, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver." 2 CORINTHIANS 9:7

I ask families a question that opens people up faster than anything else I have found.

What has been the most fun gift you have ever given?

It is a strange question to ask a serious person about a serious subject, and that is exactly why it works. Everybody expects to be asked about amounts, or percentages, or what they have committed to. Nobody expects to be asked what was fun.

And they light up. Every time.

What comes out is almost never the largest gift. It is usually something modest that they got to see. A family they helped directly. A young person they put through something. A building they can drive past. A moment where they were in the room when it landed.

The pattern is not the size. It is proximity and specificity.

Which tells you something important about how most family giving is structured. It is arranged to be efficient, anonymous, and at a distance, and every one of those decisions is defensible and all three of them together remove the joy.

I am not arguing against giving quietly. There are good reasons for it and Scripture has things to say about not announcing yourself. But there is a difference between not publicizing a gift and never getting close enough to see what it did.

There is a second thing in the answer people give. Nearly always, somebody else was with them. A spouse. A child. The joy is not solitary, and giving that is done entirely alone, from an account, by transfer, is missing a component the giver would not have been able to name.

Paul says God loves a cheerful giver, and the word for cheerful is closer to hilarious than to willing. It is a strong word for what he is describing, and it sits at the end of a sentence about deciding in your own heart rather than giving under compulsion.

So the diagnostic today is not whether you give. It is whether any of it has been fun.

If none of it has, something in the arrangement needs to change, and the change is probably about getting closer rather than giving more.

2. What made it fun?

3. Is any of your current giving fun, honestly?

4. Who was with you the last time giving was a joy?

Day 28

The Fifth Child

Have you thought any more about that fifth child

"Honor the Lord from your wealth and from the first of all your produce." PROVERBS 3:9

Here is a question I ask, and the way I ask it matters as much as the question.

Is there any charitable giving in your estate plan?

Most of the time, at the beginning, the answer is none. Three children, the estate divided three ways. Four children, divided four ways.

So I say, why do we not add charity as another child? If you have three, now you have four, and the fourth one is called giving.

I want to tell you how that usually goes, because it is not what people expect.

I had a couple where the husband liked it right away and the wife said no. Not hostile. Just no.

I did not press. I brought it up again some months later and she said not yet. I brought it up again after that.

Eighteen months from the first conversation she said yes. And what she told me was that she had needed the time to be sure it would not take something away from her children.

That is the actual objection nearly every time, and it is a good objection, and it deserves the months rather than an argument.

So the practice underneath this is patience. Do not accept the first answer, and do not push against it either. Ask, let it sit, ask again later. The second answer is usually different from the first and the third is different again.

Now the reason the fifth child framing works, when it works. It converts giving from a subtraction into a relationship. A percentage feels like something taken off the top. A child feels like somebody you are providing for, which is exactly how most families think about their own.

And it makes the amount discussable in a way a percentage never is. Nobody argues about whether the fourth child gets exactly what the others get.

The proverb says honor the Lord from your wealth and from the first of your produce. The word first is doing the work. Not from what is left.

Which is a different arrangement than most estate plans have, where charity receives whatever survives everything else.

Reflect

1. Is there any charitable giving in your estate plan?

2. What would it mean to add one more child called giving?

3. Where are you and your spouse on this, and are you in the same place?

4. What is the actual objection, said plainly?

Day 29

A River, Not a Reservoir

It does no good sitting in your donor-advised fund

"There is one who scatters, and yet increases all the more, and there is one who withholds what is justly due, but it results only in want." PROVERBS 11:24-25

I say something to families that occasionally lands harder than I intend, and I say it anyway.

It does not do God any good sitting in your donor-advised fund.

I am not against donor-advised funds. They are useful, they solve real problems, and I have recommended them many times. What I am against is the thing that happens afterward, which is that money goes in and does not come out.

The account grows. The statement arrives. There is a warm feeling attached to the number, and the number is doing nothing.

That is a reservoir. Water goes in, water stays, and eventually something in it goes still.

A river is different. The water is moving through, and the moving is the point. A river that stops being a river becomes a lake, and a lake is a fine thing to look at and a poor thing to drink from.

Money does good when it gets out and into churches and ministries and organizations that are actually doing something for people. Parked, it is potential energy, and potential energy is not a category God appears to grade on.

Now, I want to be fair to the people I am describing, because I have sat with them and the reasons are rarely miserly.

Some are waiting for clarity about where to give and the clarity never quite arrives, so waiting becomes the permanent condition. Some are worried about giving to the wrong thing, which is a real risk and a smaller one than they think. Some are enjoying the flexibility and have not noticed that flexibility has become the entire relationship.

The proverb is one of the stranger economic statements in Scripture. One person scatters and increases. Another withholds what is due and ends up in want.

It is not describing a technique. It is describing a disposition, and the disposition it warns against is not greed. It is holding.

So the question today is plain. Is anything of yours parked rather than working?

And the follow-up. What are you actually waiting for?

Day 30

The Final Exam

What did you do with what I gave you

"Well done, good and faithful slave. You were faithful with a few things, I will put you in charge of many things." MATTHEW 25:21

This is the last day, and it ends where the whole thing has been pointed.

There is going to be a moment when you are asked what you did with what you were given. I call it the final exam.

And the question at that moment is not what you accumulated. It is not what you built or what the number was. God is not going to ask you what your children did with their money either.

He is going to ask what you did with what He gave you.

That is a narrower question than most of us prepare for and a more personal one. It removes the comparison entirely. You are not being measured against your brother-in-law or the family down the road or the version of yourself you thought you would be at this age.

You are being asked about what was actually handed to you, which for some people is a great deal and for some people is not much, and the amount is not the variable being tested.

Look at what the master says in the parable. Faithful with a few things.

A few. Not many. The commendation is for faithfulness at small scale, and the reward is more responsibility rather than rest, which tells you something about how God thinks about capacity.

So here is where the thirty days land.

You have looked at what a legacy actually is. You have looked at your own life, and at your family, and at what you have, and at what you built, and at what all of it is finally for. That is the work of clarity, and clarity is the first of the three words. Alignment and communication come after it, in the books that follow.

But if you never went any further than this, you would still be further along than most families ever get. Because most families never do this. Not because they do not love each other, but because nobody ever asked them to sit down and think about it.

Somebody asked. That was the point of thirty days.

Now the only question left is what you do with the next thirty.
