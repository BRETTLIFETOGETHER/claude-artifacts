Family Next Generation devotional, Sessions 2 and 3, Days 6 through 15

SESSION 2

Passing On Faith and Values

What does not transfer automatically

Day 6

You Just Need to Love Jesus

Legacy is very simple when you get it down to it

"But as for me and my house, we will serve the Lord." JOSHUA 24:15

I have made this work complicated for thirty years and I want to say the simple version out loud.

You need to love Jesus. You need to teach your kids to love Jesus. And you hope they teach their kids to love Jesus.

That is it. That is the whole thing.

And if that happens, most of the rest takes care of itself. Because if they know God's word, they will know God's principles. If they seek to live by them, they will be reasonable stewards. And they will eventually create their own good families, which is the only mechanism by which any of this continues past two generations.

Now I want to be careful, because a sentence like that can be heard as dismissive of everything else in this series. It is not.

The documents matter. The preparation matters. The conversations matter. I have spent a career on them and I would not have if they did not.

But they are downstream. They are what a family does with what it already believes, and a family that believes nothing in particular will produce documents that reflect that.

Here is what I have observed and I offer it as observation rather than doctrine. The families that hold together across three generations are not the ones with the best structures. I have seen excellent structures dissolve. They are the ones where something was believed and the believing was passed on deliberately.

Structures without shared conviction get litigated. Conviction without structure gets muddled. Both together tend to hold.

Joshua says as for me and my house, we will serve the Lord. It is said at the end of a long life, to a nation being asked to choose, and it is notable for what it is not.

It is not a prediction and it is not a hope. It is a declaration about a household, made by the person responsible for it, out loud, in front of witnesses.

Most families have never made one. They have a general orientation that everyone assumes is shared, and nobody has ever stated it.

Stating it is not a formality. It is the difference between a family with a faith and a family where several individuals happen to believe things.

Reflect

1. Has anybody in your family ever said out loud what this house serves?

2. What do your children believe you believe?

3. Is your family's faith stated or assumed?

4. What would it cost to say it plainly, once?

Day 7

It's Not About the Money

It's the values. It's the character.

"Better is a little with the fear of the Lord than great treasure and turmoil with it." PROVERBS 15:16

We get confused in this space, because we think it is all about how much money we are going to leave them.

It is not about the money. It is the values. It is the character. It is the commitment you have to God that you are going to pass on to your kids.

I say this in a series about wealth transfer, to families who have wealth to transfer, and I mean it entirely.

Here is what makes it more than a pious sentiment.

Money is the only part of this that transfers without any effort on your part. It moves through documents, administered by professionals, on a timeline set by law. You could do nothing at all and it would still arrive.

Everything else requires you. Values do not transfer through a will. Character is not administered by anyone. Faith has no custodian.

Which means that the part requiring your deliberate attention is precisely the part most families give the least attention to, because it does not have deadlines attached and nobody sends a reminder.

There is a second reason this matters, and it is more practical.

If the values do not transfer and the money does, you have handed something significant to somebody without the framework that makes it useful. That is the mechanism behind every story you have heard about wealth damaging a family.

It is not that money is corrupting. It is that money arrived and wisdom did not, and the arrival was scheduled while the wisdom was left to chance.

Proverbs says better a little with the fear of the Lord than great treasure with turmoil. It is not romanticizing poverty. It is making a comparison about what accompanies the amount.

A little with something. Much with something else. The amount is not the variable being evaluated.

So the question for a family with resources is not whether to pass them on. Of course you will.

It is whether anything is being passed on alongside them, and whether that transfer has had a fraction of the deliberate effort the financial one has received.

Reflect

2. How much has gone into the other one?

3. What is the ratio, honestly?

4. What would change if they were equal?

Day 8

Three Parts of Your Family Legacy

The one you're living right now is most important

"One generation shall praise Your works to another, and shall declare Your mighty acts." PSALM 145:4

There are three parts to your family legacy, and families almost never separate them.

There is the one you received. You did not choose it. Some of it is good and some of it is hard, and both are shaping you whether you have examined them or not.

There is the one you are going to leave. That is the one everybody thinks about, and it is mostly out ahead of you.

And there is the one you are living right now.

That third one is the most important, and here is why. It is the only one currently transmitting.

The received one has already happened. The one you will leave has not started. The one being lived is moving, today, into the people around you, and it is doing it whether or not you have thought about it.

I find this frame useful because it changes what a person can act on.

You cannot revise what you received. You can examine it, which is worth doing, and you can decide which parts to carry, but you cannot change that it happened.

You cannot control what you will leave, not entirely. You can plan for it and other people will handle it.

But the one you are living is entirely available. Today. Without anybody's permission.

And it is the one your children are reading. No child has ever formed a view of their father from a document. They formed it from thirty years of watching, and the watching is ongoing.

Now, the uncomfortable part. If you have been living something you would not choose to pass on, that is currently being passed on. Not as a punishment. As a mechanism.

The good news attached to that is symmetrical. If you change what you are living, the transmission changes too, and it changes immediately rather than at the end.

The psalm says one generation shall praise your works to another. The verb is active and it is done by a generation rather than by an institution.

That is the third legacy described in one line. Not what was arranged. What was declared, by people who were there, to people who were watching.

Reflect

1. What did you receive that you did not choose?

2. What have you examined, and what have you carried without looking at?

3. What are you living right now that is being transmitted?

4. What would you change if you knew it was transmitting today?

Day 9

Modeling Is the Most Important Thing

At church you've got your church face on

"Be imitators of me, just as I also am of Christ." 1 CORINTHIANS 11:1

I tell people that modeling is the most important thing you can do for your kids.

And then I say the uncomfortable half, which is that they get to see all of it.

At church you have got your church face on. At home you have got your real face out. And they live in your house.

That is not an accusation. Everybody has a church face and there is nothing hypocritical about behaving differently in different settings. The point is narrower.

Your children have access to a version of you that nobody else sees, and that version is the one doing the teaching.

Here is what makes this heavier than it first sounds. It is not the big failures that transmit most. Those are visible, and visible failures usually get addressed, and addressing them teaches something good.

It is the small consistent things nobody names. How you talk about people who are not present. What you say when you are tired. Whether you follow through on something small when nobody would notice if you did not.

Those are the curriculum, and they are being delivered daily without a lesson plan.

Now I want to offer some relief, because this can be paralyzing.

You do not have to be exemplary. You have to be real and pointed in the right direction. A family that watches a person fail and then repair has learned something more useful than a family that only ever saw a polished version.

The repair is the teaching. Possibly more than the behavior.

Paul says be imitators of me as I am of Christ. It sounds arrogant until you notice the second half, which relocates the whole claim. He is not saying be like me. He is saying I am pointed at something and you can follow the direction.

That is a lower bar than being exemplary and a more honest one.

Your children are going to imitate somebody. They will imitate you in specific ways whether or not you deserve it.

The question is whether you are pointed at anything.

Reflect

Day 10

The Culture Is Discipling Your Kids

That's why I'm desperately in need of God's perspective

"Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind." ROMANS 12:2

We live in a culture that is trying to disciple your kids.

It is trying to disciple them with worldly values. Money. Popularity. Likes on an account.

And that does not matter at all. What matters is what God thinks about what you are doing.

I want to be careful with this, because it can be said in a way that produces fear rather than clarity, and fear is not useful here.

The point is not that culture is uniquely wicked in this generation. Every generation has had a discipling culture and every generation of parents has found it alarming.

The point is arithmetic. Your children are receiving a formation from somewhere, all day, in volume. If nothing is being added deliberately, the formation is whatever they are absorbing.

That is not a moral judgment about them. It is what happens to anybody who receives a continuous input and no counterweight.

Here is what strikes me most about this. I have a quiet time every day, and it is not primarily for the sake of my children.

It is because I am desperately in need of God's perspective. Otherwise I am just going to listen to whatever somebody else says, which is not true.

I am not immune to the discipling. Nobody is. And the version of me that has not been in Scripture for two weeks is measurably more susceptible to nonsense than the version that has.

Which means the first move is not managing your children's inputs. It is having any of your own.

A parent who is being formed by the same culture and hoping to counteract it in somebody else is in a difficult position.

Paul says do not be conformed but be transformed by the renewing of your mind. Notice both verbs are passive. You do not conform yourself and you do not transform yourself. Something is being done to you.

The only active part is which process you submit to.

That is available to you today and it is the precondition for everything you would want for your children.

Reflect

2. What is being added deliberately?

3. What is forming you?

4. Are you being conformed or transformed, honestly?

SESSION 3

Building Trust Across Generations

Why assumptions fill the silence

Day 11

Trust Is Not a One-Time Thing

Something you build on over time

"He who is faithful in a very little thing is also faithful in much." LUKE 16:10

Trust is not a one-time thing. It is something you build on over time.

I say that because families treat trust as a status rather than a process. Somebody is trusted or they are not, and the judgment gets made once and then rarely revisited.

That is not how it works and it is not how it develops.

Trust is a conclusion drawn from evidence, and it accumulates. Each small thing handled well adds to it. Each small thing handled badly subtracts. And the accumulation happens in both directions between every pair of people in a family, continuously, whether anybody is paying attention.

Which means two things follow.

The first is that trust can be built deliberately, and the mechanism is small and boring. Give somebody something to handle. Let them handle it. Notice that they did.

That is the whole procedure and most families skip the third step, which is the one that actually registers.

The second is that trust can be rebuilt after it falls, and this matters more than the first. Trust gets built, it falls down, and then it has to be rebuilt. Over and over again.

Families that do not understand this treat a breach as permanent. Somebody let somebody down eight years ago, and the conclusion drawn then is still operating, and there has been no mechanism for revising it.

But trust is not a verdict. It is a running total, and running totals can change.

The practical implication is that if you want to rebuild trust with somebody in your family, you do not do it with a conversation. You do it with a series of small things over a period of time, and the conversation may not be necessary at all.

Jesus says whoever is faithful in a very little is faithful also in much. It is said about money and it describes a general principle about scale.

Character shows up small and it is developed small. Nobody becomes trustworthy at large scale who was not first entrusted at small scale by somebody willing to risk it.

Somebody has to go first, and going first is the whole of what a parent does here.

Day 12

What Somebody Else Is Thinking

They stop talking and you fill in the rest

"Take every thought captive to the obedience of Christ." 2 CORINTHIANS 10:5

Here is what happens in families when communication stops.

People start having ideas about what somebody else is thinking, and those ideas harden into facts.

The mechanism is simple. Two people are talking. A third walks up. They stop talking.

Now the third person wonders whether they were being discussed. And the reality is that the sentence had simply ended.

Multiply that over thirty years in a family and you get a dense web of conclusions, all of them confidently held, almost none of them checked.

I want to be specific about how this operates between generations, because that is where it does the most damage.

A parent notices an adult child has become distant. The parent concludes the child is pulling away, possibly rejecting something, possibly upset about a decision.

Meanwhile the child has concluded that the parent does not trust them, based on a decision that was made without consulting them.

Neither has said a word. Both are behaving on the basis of a conclusion. And each new piece of evidence gets sorted into the existing frame, so the frames strengthen rather than correct.

I sat with a family exactly like that. The parents thought the daughter was disengaging. The daughter thought her parents were not listening and had not been for years. She had never told them, because saying it out loud felt like an accusation.

When they finally talked, it took about twenty minutes to dissolve something that had been running for the better part of a decade.

Paul says take every thought captive. The image is of something that would otherwise be running free, generating and multiplying.

An unexamined conclusion about a family member does exactly that. It recruits evidence. It resists contradiction. Eventually it stops feeling like an interpretation at all.

Taking it captive means holding it long enough to ask a single question, out loud, of the person it is about.

Which almost nobody does, because the asking exposes that you have been carrying it.

Reflect

4. What might they have concluded about you?

Day 13

If Dad Reacts, They Won't Say Anything Else

You just shut off communication

"Fathers, do not exasperate your children, so that they will not lose heart." COLOSSIANS 3:21

Part of building trust is allowing people to say what they think, even when you do not like what they say.

If a child says something and dad reacts to it, they are not going to say anything else. And neither is anybody else at that table who watched it happen.

You just shut off communication, and not only with the person who spoke.

I want to describe the reaction I mean, because it is usually not anger.

It is a change in the room. A slight stiffening. A correction offered immediately. An explanation of why that is not quite right. Any of those, delivered in under three seconds, tells everybody present what the cost of honesty is.

And children are extraordinarily good at reading it. They have been reading it since they were four.

The consequence is not that they stop talking. They keep talking. What stops is the category of thing they will say, and the narrowing is invisible from your side, because the conversation continues and feels perfectly normal.

You just no longer hear anything that would produce a reaction.

Here is the practical version of what to do instead, and it is one skill.

When somebody says something you do not like, your only job in the next five seconds is to not respond. Not to agree. Just to not respond.

After five seconds, ask a question rather than making a statement. Tell me more about that. Or, how long have you thought that.

You have not conceded anything. You have simply not spent the five seconds that would have closed the subject permanently.

Paul tells fathers not to exasperate their children so they will not lose heart. Losing heart is not anger and it is not rebellion.

It is a quiet giving up. The conclusion that being honest here is not worth what it costs.

That conclusion gets reached gradually and it is almost never announced, and by the time a parent notices it has usually been true for years.

Day 14

You've Got to Admit It

And ask them to forgive you for the way you acted

"Confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, so that you may be healed." JAMES 5:16

When you mess up, you have got to admit that you messed up.

And you need to ask people to forgive you for the way that you acted.

Brett calls that last part the last ten percent, and he is right that it is where most people stop short.

The first ninety percent is real and it is not nothing. I am sorry that happened. I did not handle that well. I should not have said it like that.

All of that is good. None of it requires anything from the other person, which is why it is easier.

The last ten percent is a question. Would you forgive me?

A question hands something over. Now they have a decision, and you have to wait, and they might say something other than yes.

That vulnerability is the point and it is why we avoid it.

I want to say what this does between generations specifically, because it is disproportionate to the effort.

When a parent asks an adult child for forgiveness, something reorders in the relationship. Not because the offense was necessarily large. Because the asking establishes that this is a relationship between two adults where accounts can be settled.

Without it, a parent's actions function as weather. They happen, they are absorbed, and there is no mechanism for addressing them. Children of every age adapt to weather rather than responding to it.

The asking creates the mechanism.

There is a caution. This will not work as a technique. If it is used to close a subject rather than to open a relationship, people can tell, and it costs more than it gains.

James says confess to one another and pray for one another so that you may be healed. Both halves are mutual, and the healing is attached to the mutuality rather than to the confessing.

Which means somebody has to go first, and in most families the person with the most standing to go first is the one who finds it hardest.

If that is you, that is the assignment.

Day 15

Ask a Lot of Questions

That's why we ask

"A plan in the heart of a man is like deep water, but a man of understanding draws it out." PROVERBS 20:5

This is the last day of the trust session, and the practice underneath everything else is a single one.

Ask a lot of questions.

Not because questions are a technique. Because in a family, being asked something is the primary evidence a person has that somebody wants to know them.

I want to make that concrete, because it sounds soft and it is not.

Think about the people in your family. How many of them have been asked a real question by you in the last year? Not how are you, which is a greeting. A question with an actual answer attached.

For most families, the honest count is low, and it is not because anybody stopped caring. It is because ordinary life supplies plenty of logistics and no occasion for anything else.

There is one question I ask families that opens more than any other, and it is uncomfortable.

Is there anyone in your family that you have an issue with?

I ask it because in almost every family the answer is yes and nobody has ever been asked. Brett tried it and one family member looked at him and said, yeah. My mom.

And then you are in it, which is the point.

Here is why questions build trust rather than merely gathering information. When you ask somebody something and then actually listen, you have communicated that their answer matters to you. That communication is not in the words. It is in the asking.

And a person who has been genuinely asked something will tell you more the next time, because they have evidence that it goes somewhere.

Proverbs describes a plan in the heart as deep water that a person of understanding draws out. The image is a well.

There is something down there. It will not come up on its own. Drawing takes a bucket and somebody willing to lower it more than once.

That is the whole of the third session. Trust is built by communicating, communicating requires asking, and asking requires you to actually want to know.

Reflect

1. How many people in your family have you asked a real question this year?

2. Is there anyone in your family you have an issue with?

3. Has anyone ever asked you that?

4. Who would say yes if you asked them?
