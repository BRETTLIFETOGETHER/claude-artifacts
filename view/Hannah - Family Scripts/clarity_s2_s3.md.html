SESSION 2

Your Personal Legacy

What do you want to be remembered for

Day 6

Who Am I

What really matters to me

"Search me, God, and know my heart; put me to the test and know my anxious thoughts." PSALM 139:23-24

Before a family can get clear together, the people in it have to get clear alone.

That sounds obvious and it is routinely skipped, because starting with yourself feels self-indulgent when the whole point is the family. So people jump straight to the family conversation and it stalls, and nobody can say why.

Here is why. You can put five people around a table, ask them all the same question, and get five answers that have nothing to do with each other, because every one of them is somewhere different internally and nobody has noticed.

So the question at the front of everything is a plain one. Who am I, and what really matters to me?

I ask people that and the pause is usually long. Not because the question is hard to understand. Because it has been decades since anyone asked, and the answer they used to have has not been checked in a while.

What I find underneath is almost never emptiness. It is sediment. Things that mattered at thirty and were never revisited. Things somebody else wanted for them that got adopted so early they now read as their own. Things that genuinely matter and have never been said, because there was no occasion.

The psalm is the shortest instruction I know for how to do this well. David does not say I have examined myself and here is my report. He asks to be searched, by someone else, including the parts he would rather not have found.

That is a different posture than self-assessment. Self-assessment produces the version of you that you have already agreed to. Being searched produces something you did not author.

And notice what he asks to have surfaced. Anxious thoughts. Not accomplishments, not gifts. The things that worry him, which is where a person is most honest and least composed.

So today is not about producing a good answer. It is about being willing to have the real one found.

Reflect

1. Who are you, in a sentence that is not your job or your role in the family?

2. What matters most to you, and when did you last say it out loud?

3. What do you carry that somebody else wanted for you?

4. What anxious thought have you never let anybody search?

Day 7

Put Yourself in Your Own Eulogy

What would you want people to say about you

"It is better to go to a house of mourning than to go to a house of feasting, because that is the end of every person, and the living takes it to heart." ECCLESIASTES 7:2

I ask people to do something that makes them uncomfortable, and I ask it early.

Put yourself in your own eulogy. What would you want people to say about you?

Not what would they say. What would you want them to say. Those are different questions and the distance between them is the whole exercise.

People resist this and I understand why. It feels morbid, and there is a superstition underneath it that thinking about the end invites it. But that is not what happens. What happens is that a person who writes down what they want said discovers that they have been living toward something slightly different, and that the correction available is smaller than they feared.

Here is what I notice about the answers.

Almost nobody says anything about money. In thirty years I have never had a person say they wanted to be remembered for a net worth or a transaction. They say things about character. They say things about how they treated people. They say things about their family, and specifically about whether their children turned out well and whether they knew they were loved.

Which raises an obvious question that I let sit rather than ask directly. If that is what you want said, does your calendar reflect it? Does your spending? Would somebody watching this year guess that is what you were building toward?

The verse is one of the strangest pieces of advice in Scripture. Better to go to a funeral than a party, because that is where everyone ends up and the living take it to heart.

It does not say the funeral is more pleasant. It says it is more useful.

Something happens in that room that does not happen anywhere else. The measures we normally use fall away, and what is left is a very short list of things that actually held. Everybody notices it. Almost nobody writes any of it down afterward.

So write it down. Two or three sentences. Not accomplishments. What kind of person you were, and what you gave yourself to.

Then read it and ask what would have to change for it to be true.

Reflect

1. What would you want said about you by the people who knew you best?

2. How is that different from what would currently be said?

3. What in your life this year points toward those sentences?

4. What points away from them?

Then show what you wrote to one person. That is the step that makes it real.

Day 8

Not Living in Alignment With It

That's where the gap shows

"But prove yourselves doers of the word, and not merely hearers who delude themselves." JAMES 1:22

Here is what I see constantly, and it is not hypocrisy.

A person can be genuinely clear about what matters to them and not be living in alignment with it. That is where the gap shows.

I want to defend the people in that gap for a moment, because the word hypocrite gets used too fast.

A hypocrite is somebody who says one thing and privately believes another. That is rare in the families I sit with. What is common is somebody who believes the thing sincerely and has a calendar that was built by other forces.

Nobody sat down in 2011 and decided that work would take the place family was supposed to have. It happened one reasonable yes at a time, each of them defensible, and the sum of them is a life that does not match the stated values of the person living it.

That is a clarity problem before it is a character problem. Nobody decided, so the loudest thing decided.

The way you find the gap is not by introspection. Introspection will tell you what you value, and you already know that. You find the gap by looking at evidence.

Look at last year's calendar. Look at where the money actually went. Look at what got cancelled when something had to give, because what gets cancelled is what is actually optional in your life, whatever you would say about it.

Those three sources will tell you more in twenty minutes than a month of reflection.

James says something sharp here. Be doers, not merely hearers, and the phrase he uses for the alternative is deluding themselves. Not deceiving others. Deceiving yourself.

That is precisely the mechanism. The person in the gap is not fooling their family. Their family can see it clearly. They are fooling themselves, and they can only keep doing it as long as they never look at the evidence.

So look at it. Not to feel bad. To find out what is true, which is the only place a change can start from.

Reflect

2. What does last year's calendar say?

3. What gets cancelled in your life when something has to give?

4. What is the honest size of the gap?

Day 9

Listen, Not to Judge

You're listening for understanding

"Everyone must be quick to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger." JAMES 1:19

You have got to be able to listen, not to judge.

You are listening for understanding. You are trying to understand why they say that, or why they say this. It is almost what is behind what they are saying.

I put this in the personal week rather than the communication book on purpose, because most people think listening is something you do for somebody else. It is also how you find out what you think.

Here is what I mean. When you listen to judge, you are running an evaluation. Is this right, is this reasonable, do I agree. That process is fast and it is loud, and it takes up the whole channel.

When you listen to understand, you are running a different question. Why would somebody say that. What is underneath it. What would have to be true for this to make sense from where they are sitting.

That second question is slower and quieter, and it leaves room for something to arrive that you were not expecting, including about yourself.

I notice this most with couples. A man will describe what he wants for his family, and I will watch his wife's face, and there is frequently something there. So I ask her. And a good portion of the time what comes out is not disagreement. It is something adjacent that he has never heard, and once he hears it, his own answer changes.

He did not know he thought that until she said something else.

James gives the order and the order is the instruction. Quick to hear. Slow to speak. Slow to anger. Most of us have those exactly reversed and would defend the reversal as decisiveness.

There is one practical piece I would add, and it is the hardest part. Give people time to answer. Do not just move to the next question.

The pause after a real question is where the thinking happens, and most of us end it in about two seconds because silence is uncomfortable. Whatever was rising in that moment does not come back.

Sit in one uncomfortable pause this week without ending it. That is the whole practice.

Reflect

1. When you listen, are you evaluating or understanding? Be honest.

2. Who in your life do you listen to least well?

3. When did you last learn something about yourself from listening to somebody else?

4. How long can you sit in silence before you fill it?

Day 10

Write It Down

When you write it down, it forces clarity

"Record the vision and inscribe it on tablets, so that the one who reads it may run." HABAKKUK 2:2

There is a reason this book has lines on it and not just paragraphs.

When you write it down, it forces clarity. Thinking about something produces a feeling of understanding. Writing it produces actual understanding, and the difference between the two is usually embarrassing.

Try it with something you are certain about. Take a value you would say your family holds and write it in one sentence, on a line, in words somebody else would understand.

Most people cannot do it on the first pass. Not because they do not hold the value. Because they have held it as an atmosphere rather than a statement, and an atmosphere cannot be handed to anyone.

That is the mechanism this whole series runs on. A thing that has never been written down cannot be passed on with any precision. It can be absorbed, partially, by the people close enough to observe it. That is not nothing, and it is not transfer.

Habakkuk is told to record the vision and inscribe it, and then comes the reason. So that the one who reads it may run.

Not so it is preserved. Not so it is official. So that somebody else, later, who was not present when it was received, can act on it without you.

That is the whole case for writing anything down in a family. You are not creating a record. You are making it possible for people to move without you in the room.

Now, a warning that comes from watching families do this well and then badly. Written things go in drawers.

Somebody has a good weekend, real clarity arrives, three pages get filled, and then it is filed and never looked at again. A year later nobody could quote a line of it.

So write it down, and then decide where it lives and when you will read it again. Habakkuk's instruction was to inscribe it on tablets, which is to say make it visible and hard to lose. A vision that is not visible is not guiding anything.

This is the end of the second session. Five days on you, before anything about the family.

Next session brings the people back in.

SESSION 3

Your Family Legacy

Defining what your family stands for

Day 11

Family Harmony and Wise Transfer

That's what we want for every family

"Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brothers to live together in unity!" PSALM 133:1

There are two things we want for every family we work with, and everything else serves them.

Family harmony. And the wise transfer of both the wealth and the wisdom.

I want to say a word about the order, because people assume the second one is the point and the first one is a nice outcome if you can get it.

It runs the other way.

If a family transfers everything perfectly and the relationships do not survive it, nobody would call that a success. I have watched families execute a technically flawless transition and end up with siblings who do not speak. The plan worked. The family did not.

And it is not a trade. Harmony is not something you sacrifice for a better structure. In practice the families with the most harmony end up transferring most successfully, because trust makes preparation possible and preparation is what determines whether the transfer holds.

So the two are not competing objectives. One is the soil the other grows in.

Now, harmony is a word that gets misread, so let me be precise. It does not mean agreement. It does not mean the absence of conflict, and a family with no conflict is usually a family where somebody has stopped speaking rather than a family at peace.

Harmony in the musical sense is different notes that work together. That is closer to what we are after. Different people, genuinely different, holding together.

The psalm calls unity good and pleasant. Notice what it does not call it. It does not call it easy, and it does not call it automatic. It is described as a thing worth beholding, which is language reserved for something a little rare.

So the question for this session is not whether your family gets along. Most do, at holidays.

It is whether your family has enough shared understanding that it could survive a hard decision.

That is what the next four days are about.

Reflect

2. Where is there conflict in your family that has never been dealt with?

3. Is your family peaceful, or has somebody stopped speaking?

4. Could your family survive a hard decision together?

Day 12

Know the Family

You just want to know all the people

"Do not merely look out for your own personal interests, but also for the interests of others." PHILIPPIANS 2:4

Before I do anything with a family, I want to know the family.

Who are the kids. Who are the grandkids. Who are the parents, if they are still living. Who are the outside people who affect this family. You just want to know all the people.

That sounds so basic it is easy to skip, and it is the step families most consistently skip about themselves.

Here is what I mean. Every family has a version of itself that it carries around, and that version is usually about ten years out of date. The children are fixed at the age when they last did something memorable. Somebody's marriage is understood as it was before the hard year. A person who went through a genuine change is still described the old way.

So the family is operating from a picture, and the picture is not current, and nobody has updated it because updating requires asking and asking requires an occasion.

What I actually ask about each person is four things. How are they doing spiritually. How are they doing relationally. How are they doing vocationally. How are they doing financially, and do they carry debt.

Try that on your own family, one person at a time, and notice how quickly you run out of things you are sure about.

That is not a failure of love. It is what happens when people become adults in different cities and the conversation becomes logistics.

There is one category people leave off entirely, and it matters more than they expect. The outside people who affect your family. The in-laws, obviously, but also the friend somebody listens to, the pastor, the business partner, the person whose opinion carries more weight than yours does on a particular subject.

Those people are shaping your family and most families could not name them.

Paul says to look out for the interests of others, not merely your own. The word he uses is about attention rather than sacrifice. It is asking you to actually know what somebody else's interests are, which requires having found out.

So today is not about doing anything for anyone. It is about admitting how much of your own family you are currently guessing at.

Day 13

Six Questions

Answer them the way that I want you to answer them

"Charm is deceitful and beauty is vain, but a woman who fears the Lord, she shall be praised." PROVERBS 31:30

I have six questions I asked my sons-in-law before they got permission to marry my daughters.

And I told them plainly. You just need to answer these six questions the way I want them answered.

People smile when I say that, and then they get uncomfortable, because it sounds like an overreach. Let me tell you what actually happens.

It does not scare the right young man away. It does the opposite. He rises up to it.

What I have watched, more than once, is a man who had never been asked anything serious by anybody, sitting across from a father who wanted to know what he believed, and finding that the question raised him rather than tested him. Nobody had ever thought he was worth vetting.

The daughters are usually the ones who are nervous. They are afraid it will drive him off. It has not yet.

Now, I am not going to tell you what the six are, because your six should be yours. But I will tell you the principle underneath them, which is that the most consequential decision affecting your family's next generation is one your children will make while you are standing off to the side deciding whether it is your place to say anything.

It is your place. Kindly, early, and once.

There is a bigger idea here than in-laws, and it is this. Families are usually clear about their standards internally and completely silent about them at the boundary. We know what we believe. We have never told a single person marrying into this family what any of it is.

And then that person arrives with a full set of values from a different house, which is normal and unavoidable, and nobody has ever had a conversation about how the two sets fit together.

The proverb ends a long passage about a woman of excellence, and what it says at the end is that charm is deceitful and beauty is vain. It is not against either one. It is saying they are the things most easily seen and least predictive.

Which is exactly why you have to ask questions. What matters most is not visible at the wedding.

Day 14

Every Family Is Unique

This is a new family

"Since we have gifts that differ according to the grace given to us, each of us is to use them accordingly." ROMANS 12:6

Every family is unique.

That sounds like something you say to be nice, and I mean it operationally. Two families with exactly the same amount of money should frequently do completely different things, and a solution that transformed one family will do nothing for another.

The reason is that the variables that matter are not financial. They are how many children there are and what the gaps are between them. Whether the parents are still married and to whom. Who is capable and who is struggling. Who lives nearby. Who married whom. Whether there is a business, and whether anybody wants it. What happened in 2009 that nobody talks about.

None of that appears on a balance sheet and all of it determines the answer.

There is a second thing in this phrase that people miss, and it is the more useful one.

When your children marry, this is a new family.

Not an extension of yours. A new one, with its own values, its own way of handling money, its own decisions about faith and holidays and how much to tell the grandparents. They are doing what you did thirty years ago, which is trying to build harmony in a household that combines two histories.

Families get into trouble when they treat that new family as a branch office. And the new family gets into trouble when it treats the older one as an authority it has to either obey or escape.

Both are the same mistake, which is failing to notice that there are now two families rather than one enlarged one.

Paul says the gifts differ and each is to be used accordingly. The instruction is not to make everyone the same. It is to notice the difference and act on it.

So the practical question for today is a little uncomfortable. Are you trying to run one family or are you related to several?

Because the answer changes almost everything about how you communicate, what you can decide for people, and how much you are entitled to expect.

Day 15

Vision, Mission, and Values

What do you want this family to be known for

"Where there is no vision, the people are unrestrained." PROVERBS 29:18

Here is the question this whole session has been building toward.

What do you want this family to be known for?

I run vision, mission, and values sessions with families, and I want to describe what actually happens in them, because it is not what people expect.

They expect an exercise. Somebody writes words on a board, everyone nods, there is a document at the end that nobody looks at again.

What happens instead, in the good ones, is that the family finds out about itself.

Everyone speaks into it. Not just the parents. In one family I worked with there were ten people in the room and the parents had two votes out of ten, deliberately, so they could not control the outcome. And what came out was not something the parents disagreed with. These children had lived in this culture their whole lives. But it was in their words, and that changed everything about whether they owned it.

The married-ins matter here more than anywhere. They came from different houses and they bring things nobody else in the room would have thought of, and it is frequently the daughter-in-law who names the thing everyone recognizes immediately.

Families usually land on five or six values. Not twenty. Five or six they would actually defend.

Now the proverb, because it is routinely misquoted. It does not say that without vision people perish in the sense of dying out. The word is closer to unrestrained, or cast off. Without a vision, people go in whatever direction they were already going, because nothing is holding the shape.

That is exactly what drift is. Not disaster. Just an absence of restraint, and everyone gradually pursuing their own thing because there was never a shared one.

So the point of naming what your family stands for is not to produce a document. It is to give your family something to measure a decision against, three years from now, when a decision arrives that nobody anticipated.

That is the end of this session. Next we go to the area families brace for.
