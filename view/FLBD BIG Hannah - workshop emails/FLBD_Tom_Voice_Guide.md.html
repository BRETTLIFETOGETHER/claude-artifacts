Tom Conway — Real Voice Guide (v2)

Built from the workshop transcript (Tom's actual spoken voice) — not the spring agency emails. Use this for August.

The correction this version makes

The spring emails were written in a polished agency house style — short choppy stacked lines, faith stripped out, third-person ("Tom walks through…"). That is not how Tom talks. The real Tom is warm, flowing, story-driven, first-person, and openly faith-rooted. August uses this voice.

The feel in one sentence

A trusted, seasoned friend who's done this work for years — warm, personal, story-driven, faith in the posture, talking with you, not marketing at you.

1. Voice & point of view

  • First person. Tom says "I," "my heart," "here's what I've come to believe," "I've learned the hard way." Biggest shift from the spring emails (which avoided "I"). It's what makes him trustworthy.
  • Flowing, connected sentences that build an idea — not stacked one-line fragments. Tighter than a live transcript, but warm and unhurried.
  • Teaches through conviction and story: "After years of sitting with families of wealth, here's what I've come to believe…"
  • Generous and humble — credits others by name (Randy, Jim, Jerry, John Warnick, Jay Hughes), admits he's "a slow learner," jokes "that's why I'm so smart." Never self-important.

2. Faith — in the posture, not the pulpit (for cold-ish sends)

  • Tom is deeply faith-rooted: discipleship, stewardship, "God gave you everything you have," quotes Paul ("impart not only the gospel but our lives").
  • For August's re-engagement (faith advisors, but lukewarm): lead with the faith posture — stewardship, "heart for the next generation," serve-before-you-ask — and let explicit scripture/God language deepen later in the sequence. Don't open a cold-ish inbox on overt God language unless Tom wants to.
  • His real anchor line: "Figure out what you can do for a family before you ask anything from them." Use it.

3. Tom's actual teaching (real content for value-add blocks)

Four characteristics of a legacy planner (his words): 1. Heart preparation — genuinely care about the soft-side work, even though it takes time. 2. A multi-generational mindset — think beyond your client and spouse to their kids and grandkids. 3. Skills — the relational/conversation skills you can actually learn. 4. A collaboration mindset — "you don't live in a silo"; bring in others' perspective.

The three questions that started it all (he'd ask wealthy clients): 1. "Tell me who your advisors are." 2. "Do you have anyone who: (a) clearly knows your goals, (b) knows everything you have — not just the pot they manage, and (c) built you a strategy to accomplish those goals with what you've been entrusted?" 3. "Do your advisors share your biblical worldview?" → The answer was almost always "no, no, no." That gap is why Family Legacy by Design exists.

"The right side of the table" (Scott Fithian's image Tom uses): the trusted advisor sits next to the client — not scattered among the CPA, attorney, and insurance reps. The goal is to be that person.

4. Recurring vocabulary & ideas

  • families of wealth · high-capacity families · the next generation · multi-generational
  • "two sides of legacy planning — technical and relational"
  • clarity · alignment · communication · steward · entrusted · heart
  • "It's not about the money" / "beyond the money"
  • "serve the family before you ask anything of them"

5. CTAs — soft, personal, inviting

  • "I'd love to show you…" · "Here's the way we've learned to do it" · "See how this works" · "Learn more about the workshop" · "Explore the framework"
  • Save "Reserve Your Seat" for the workshop card and genuine final calls only.

Hero + warm subhead → an insight or story in his voice → a teaching block (four characteristics / three questions / right side of the table) → video or article → workshop card → testimonial (faith carried here) → soft next step.
