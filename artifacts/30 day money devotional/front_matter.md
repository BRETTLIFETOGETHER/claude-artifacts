**Welcome**

Dear friend,

I am glad you are here. For nearly fifty years I have sat across from people at every income level, from those wondering how to make it to the end of the month to those managing more than they ever imagined they would have, and I have watched the same thing again and again. The people who find real peace with money are almost never the ones who simply have the most of it. They are the ones who have settled a deeper question underneath the numbers, a question most of us were never taught to ask.

Over the next thirty days, I want to walk that question with you, along with five others that grow out of it. We will start where everything has to start, with the question of who really owns it all, because until that one is settled, every other financial decision carries a weight it was never meant to carry. From there we will ask what the Bible actually says about money, and find that it offers a handful of ancient, reliable habits rather than the endless supply of clever shortcuts the world keeps selling. We will ask how a person finds true financial freedom, and discover that it was never a number to reach but a trust to settle. We will ask where to start, and do the honest, practical work of facing reality and building a plan you can actually keep. We will ask what God is growing through your financial life, and why. And we will finish with the question that quietly changes everything, why giving changes the giver even more than it changes anyone else.

I want to be clear about what this is and what it is not. This is not a budgeting program, though there is real practical work ahead. It is a discipleship journey that happens to run straight through your checkbook. I have come to believe that money is one of the primary tools God uses to shape a human heart, not because accumulation is the point of life, but because what we do with our resources reveals, more honestly than almost anything else, what we actually trust. Jesus talked about money more than almost any other subject for exactly that reason. He understood what it does to us when we hold it wrong, and what it can do in us when we finally hold it rightly.

So I would ask two things of you as you begin. First, be honest. The exercises in these pages only work if you are willing to look clearly at where you actually are, without shame and without pretending. Second, take the next faithful step. Not the perfect step, not the whole staircase at once, just the next honest move in the direction you know is right. God has always worked with people who are willing to do that, and he will work with you.

Whether you are just beginning your financial life or have been at it for decades, whether the numbers are tight or comfortable, there is room for you here. Let us begin where it all begins, and trust the God who owns it all to meet you along the way.

Grateful to walk it with you,
Ron

**How to Use This Devotional**

This devotional is built to be walked one day at a time over about a month. The thirty readings are organized around six questions, moving across four weeks from the foundation of ownership all the way to the freedom of a generous life. You do not need to read ahead or rush. The journey is designed to build, each day resting on the ones before it, so the most fruitful way through is simply the steady one, a single day at a sitting.

Each day follows the same simple shape. It opens with a short line meant to capture the heart of the day, followed by two passages of Scripture, always from the NIV, that anchor the reading. Then comes a reflection, often a story from someone who has walked these principles out in real life, followed by a few short teaching sections that develop the day's idea. After the teaching you will find four things meant to move the truth from the page into your life. Think About It offers a couple of honest questions to sit with. The Exercise gives you one concrete thing to do, usually something you can complete in ten or fifteen minutes. The Declaration is a short sentence you can speak over yourself, a way of agreeing with what is true. Next Steps offers two options, so you can choose the one that fits your situation. And each day closes with a prayer you can pray as your own.

The real work of this devotional is not in the reading. It is in the Think About It questions and the Exercise, where honesty turns into change. I would gently encourage you to keep a pen and a notebook nearby, because writing your answers down, even briefly, has a way of making them real in a way that thinking them through never quite does. Do not skip the practical parts. They are where the transformation actually happens.

You can walk these thirty days on your own, and many people do. But if you have the chance to go through them alongside others, in a small group at your church or simply with a friend or your spouse, take it. Money is a subject most of us have learned to keep private, and there is something quietly freeing about doing this work in trusted company. If your group is using the companion small-group sessions, this devotional is designed to run right alongside them, deepening between meetings what the videos open up together.

A final word about grace. You will almost certainly miss a day somewhere along the way. When you do, do not start over and do not give up. Simply pick up where you left off. The goal was never a perfect thirty-day streak. The goal is a changed heart and a faithful life, and those are built the same way everything faithful is built, one honest step at a time.

Use the progress tracker on the next page to mark each day as you go, and let it be an encouragement rather than a scoreboard.

**The Declaration of Stewardship**

Before you begin the thirty days, it is worth settling the question underneath all the others. You might read this declaration slowly, and if you are willing, sign and date it as a way of marking where this journey begins. You can return to it whenever you need to remember what you have settled.

Father, I acknowledge that You own it all. The earth is Yours and everything in it, and everything I have called mine was always a gift from Your hand, entrusted to me for a season. I confess the weight I have carried as though the outcomes were mine to secure and mine to guarantee, and today I lay that weight down.

I take my place, not as an owner with rights, but as a steward with responsibilities. I receive what You have given as a trust. I will manage it with faithfulness and care, hold it with an open hand, and release the outcome back to You. Where I have kept money in a separate corner from my faith, I bring the two together and ask You to be Lord of all of it.

Teach me, over these thirty days and long after, to handle what You have entrusted in a way that honors You, and to become a person marked by contentment, strength, and direction. I am not the owner. I am the steward. And everything I have is Yours.

Signed: _______________________________  Date: _______________
