**You Were Never Meant to Walk This Alone**

If these thirty days have stirred something in you, the worst thing you could do now is try to carry it entirely on your own. Money is the subject most of us have been trained to keep private, and that privacy is exactly what allows fear, shame, and drift to do their quiet work. The people who actually change their financial lives, and keep them changed, are almost never the ones who did it in isolation. They are the ones who let a few trusted people walk the road with them.

There are three kinds of company worth seeking as you go forward, and you do not need all of them at once. The first is a person or two who will simply know the truth of where you are, a spouse, a close friend, a mentor, someone you can be honest with about the numbers and the struggles and the next steps. Honesty spoken out loud to another person has a weight that private resolve rarely does. The second is a community, ideally a small group at your church, where you can work through these principles alongside others who are asking the same questions. If your church offers the companion small-group study to this material, that is a natural place to begin. And the third, when your situation calls for it, is wise professional help.

On that last point, it is worth knowing that you do not have to choose between financial expertise and biblical wisdom. There are financial professionals who have been trained specifically to integrate the two, and the following page explains how to find one. Whatever combination of these you pursue, the principle is the same. God designed the life of faith to be lived in community, and your financial life is no exception. You were never meant to walk this alone, and you do not have to.
