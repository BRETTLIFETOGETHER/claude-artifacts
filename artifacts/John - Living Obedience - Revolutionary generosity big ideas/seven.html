02
Element one

The Book

40 Days of Irrational Obedience
$95,000

A forty-day book in the Purpose Driven shape rather than a chapter book. Four movements — Awakening · Surrender · Generosity · A Family Lifestyle — ten days each, with a rest day in every movement. Every day carries a Scripture, a story, an interview and one question. Five minutes a morning.

Specimen · Day Thirteen
Send Them Home

"You have too many men…" — Judges 7:2

Gideon has thirty-two thousand men and an enemy camped thick as locusts. God's first instruction is not a strategy. It is a reduction. By morning, ninety-nine percent of the army has walked away and he is told to attack. There is no version of that briefing that survives a board meeting.

God gives the reason plainly, and it is not tactical — Israel would boast against me, "My own strength has saved me." Gideon was not asked to surrender soldiers. He was asked to surrender the explanation.

Today. Name one advantage you are holding that would make the outcome explainable without God. What would it cost to send that one home?

What ships

·Forty daily readings, roughly 600 words each, publication-ready
·Four movement introductions and a ninety-day closing experience
·One narrative voice, written from Steve and Dale's own material
·Publishing recommendation — trade release versus controlled, and the derivative-rights position
03
Element two

The Curriculum

Six sessions · ninety minutes each
$50,000

Written so a first-time host can lead it without training. Because the teaching lives on film, the host's whole job is to press play, ask the question, wait, and finish on time. Preparation is fifteen minutes a week — which is what lets a church recruit thirty hosts instead of twelve.

Specimen · Session Two · Surrender

0:00 Opening question. What is the most valuable thing you have ever walked away from? Pairs, six minutes.

0:15 Scripture. Judges 7:1–8 read aloud, then verses 2 and 7 read again. Thirty seconds of silence before anyone speaks.

0:25 Film. Twelve minutes. The host says nothing before or after it.

0:40 Discussion. Gideon surrendered the explanation, not the battle — what explanation are you holding? Where has success in your life been perfectly attributable to you?

1:10 Challenge. Name the advantage out loud to one person. Same partner all six weeks.

1:22 Prayer. For courage rather than for outcomes.

What ships

·Participant guide and full leader script for all six sessions
·The verbatim lines that must not be improvised, and what to do when a room goes quiet
·Host training agenda and a six-week host email track
·Stands alone as a family study, or runs inside the campaign

Element four

The Interviews

Forty families · forty stories
$75,000

Dale's idea, and the most valuable single asset in the project. Forty households who actually did this, in their own words — the one thing a book cannot fake and a competitor cannot buy.

Specimen · the interview spine

What did God ask you to do that made no sense at the time?

Who told you not to? What did they say?

What did it cost — not in dollars, in the thing you don't usually say out loud?

What do your children know about why you did it?

If it had gone badly, would it still have been right?

One session, four assets

Each recorded conversation produces a story for the daily reader, a clip for the film series, a testimony for the campaign, and an archived transcript that becomes raw material for every volume that follows.

What ships

Sourcing and scheduling, the interviews themselves, transcription, story-shaping to publishable length, and written permissions and rights clearance across all forty households.

07
Element six

The Toolbox

The customisable platform
$85,000

The same forty days, assembled differently for each family. A household completes a ten-minute profile; the system reads seven dimensions and builds the journey that family actually needs rather than the one everybody gets. This is what turns a book into a relationship that lasts a decade.

Specimen · the profile and what it returns

Seven dimensions. Faith · Purpose · Family · Generosity · Legacy · Impact · Next Generation.

The return. Here is where your family is flourishing. Here is where you may be vulnerable. Here is where the generations do not agree. Here is the journey we would recommend next.

Then the toolbox assembles it — a 7, 21, 30 or 40-day journey, the family conversation guide, the giving statement, the liquidity worksheet, the twenty questions for one evening, and the prayer prompts. Same spine, different order, different depth.

Why it matters commercially

Completion is visible, so The Signatry can see which families are moving. And a family that receives something built for them comes back — which is the difference between a book and a platform.

One discipline

Never let the score become the goal. An assessment inside a book about surrender is one careless sentence away from turning obedience into a performance metric, and the design guards against it deliberately.

08
Element seven

The Collective

The Living Pledge, and the families who sign it
$45,000

The Signatry means the one who signs. Your vision is to write the last check — but the last check is only possible because somebody signed first, and this organisation has never asked anyone to sign anything.

Specimen · the pledge itself

We are not promising an amount. We are deciding an answer.

That what we hold is not ours. That we will give while we are living, so we can see where it goes. That our children will help decide. That we will name what is enough. And that when God asks, our answer is already yes.

Signed, dated, and witnessed by people we have named ourselves.

Not a giving commitment

And it must never be presented as one. That distinction is what protects it, and what makes it something a family will actually sign.

What ships

The pledge document, a six-session capstone, witness cards, an annual renewal, and the gathering format where families sign in the same room.
