## What this is for

Roughly nine-tenths of American wealth sits outside cash — in businesses, real estate, appreciated securities, equipment, land, and intellectual property. Roughly all of the giving comes out of the checking account.

That mismatch is not a spiritual problem. It is an information problem, and it silently caps the size of every gift you will ever make. This worksheet exists to surface what you actually own, when it might become liquid, and what could be decided before the window closes.

**This is not legal, tax, or financial advice.** It is a set of questions to bring to your own attorney and advisor. Nothing here should be acted on without them, and anything involving a pending transaction should go to counsel immediately — timing is often the whole issue.

## PART THREE — THE SEQUENCE QUESTION

There is one question underneath this entire worksheet.

> **Can this asset be given before it is sold, rather than after?**

Give-then-sell and sell-then-give produce materially different outcomes from the identical asset, and the difference routinely exceeds a third of the gift. A family who needed to sell two rental properties to net a gift and cover the tax found that giving one property before the sale produced more for the ministry than the two-property plan would have.

They did not become more generous that afternoon. They became better informed.

## PART FOUR — EIGHT QUESTIONS BEFORE A LETTER OF INTENT

If you are within five years of any possible sale, work these now. Not at closing. Not in diligence. Now.

**1.** What portion of this business do we intend to give, and have we said the number out loud to each other?

**2.** Has our attorney confirmed the latest point at which a gift of an interest can be made without jeopardizing the transaction or its treatment?

**3.** Who on our advisory team has actually done this before — not read about it, done it? If nobody has, who are we adding?

**4.** What does our family need from the proceeds, and is that number set deliberately or by default?

**5.** What do we want the first eighteen months after the sale to look like? Most owners plan the transaction in detail and the aftermath not at all.

**6.** What will we do with our time? The loss of purpose after an exit does more damage to a giver than any tax outcome.

**7.** Who is going to tell us no during this period, and have we asked them to?

**8.** If the sale does not happen, what of this plan should we do anyway?

## PART SIX — THE ADVISOR CONVERSATION

Bring three questions to the person who manages your money. Their answers will tell you a great deal.

**1.** *"If I wanted to give a portion of an appreciated asset rather than cash, what would that involve and when is the deadline?"*

**2.** *"Have you done this before, and can you describe how it went?"*

**3.** *"Do you know what I believe about money?"*

The third question is the real one. Most advisors have never been told, most have never asked, and most would serve you considerably better if they knew.

If your advisor cannot answer the first question, that is information. It does not mean they are a bad advisor. It means this particular conversation needs someone else in it.
