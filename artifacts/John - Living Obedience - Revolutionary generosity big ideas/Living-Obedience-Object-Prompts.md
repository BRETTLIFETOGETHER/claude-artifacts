# LIVING OBEDIENCE — OBJECT PROMPTS
### If you want photography, this is the road that isn't a cliché

---

## Why the last set failed

The wheat-field generation was good photography and a bad cover, and the proof was on your own screen: the identical image was simultaneously generating *The Harvest of Hope — A Story of Resilience.* Art that could carry any title carries none.

Figure-in-landscape-with-god-rays has been the default of Christian publishing since about 2004. It is not rescued by better weather, a smaller figure, or more drama. The genre itself is spent.

**The way out is an object, not a vista.** One thing, close, in real light, that means something specific. Serious business and ideas books have done this for decades, and it is the register your reader actually buys in.

---

## Shared style block — paste into every prompt

> Vertical 2:3 portrait book cover background artwork. Absolutely no text, no letters, no numbers, no symbols, no logos, no typography anywhere in the image.
>
> Fine-art still-life photograph, medium-format film look, shot on 100mm macro at f/4, single-source raking window light from the left, deep shadow, rich material texture, gentle film grain, muted palette of deep blue-black and warm brass. The object sits in the upper two-thirds; the lower third falls into near-black uncluttered shadow where a soft warm glow pools.

## Craft tail — paste at the end of every prompt

> Masterful, museum-quality, the work of a top still-life photographer; every surface texture visible; no studio gloss, no HDR, no props, nothing staged or decorative. Absolutely no text, no letters, no numbers, no symbols, no logos, no typography anywhere in the image.

## 01 · THE KEYS
**The strongest of these, and the one no other book in this category has.**

> A single worn brass key ring holding three keys, resting on a scratched dark walnut desk. A weathered hand has just let go and is withdrawing out of the top of the frame, still slightly open. The keys have caught the light and thrown a long shadow across the wood grain.

Handing over the keys is the universal picture of releasing something you built. For an owner, it isn't a metaphor — it's the actual object. Nothing religious in the frame, which is precisely why it will be believed.

---

## 02 · THE EMPTY CHAIR
**Quiet, and it will unsettle every founder who sees it.**

> An old wooden chair pushed back from the head of a long dark table in an empty room, the seat worn pale where someone sat for years. Late afternoon light comes low through an unseen window and lays a single warm band across the table and the empty seat.

You stepped out of the chair while you were still able to. That is Steve's actual story, and it is the whole book in one object.

---

## 03 · THE OPEN HAND
**The surrender image, done as a document rather than a pose.**

> A single weathered working hand, palm turned up and completely open, resting on a dark surface, photographed close from directly above. Every line, callus and scar is visible in the raking light. The hand is at rest, not reaching, not receiving.

Same idea as the arms-outstretched shot, but specific rather than symbolic. Nobody can tell whether this hand belongs to a farmer or a billionaire, which is the point.

---

## 04 · THE UNSIGNED PAGE
**Hold this one for The Living Pledge.**

> A single sheet of heavy cream cotton paper lying on a dark oak table beside an old fountain pen with the cap off. The page is entirely blank. Warm side light rakes across the tooth of the paper and the barrel of the pen. Deep shadow surrounds them.

---

## 05 · THE DOOR
**If you want a room rather than a thing.**

> A heavy old door standing half open in a dark interior, seen straight on. Warm daylight floods through the gap and falls in a hard-edged shape across a worn stone floor. Nothing else in the room. No figure.

---

## Generating well

**One object. Never two.** If the model adds a Bible, a candle, a cross or a coffee cup, regenerate with *"a single object alone on an empty surface, nothing else in the frame."*

**No people, except the withdrawing hand in 01.** A face or a figure drags it straight back toward the genre you just left.

**Kill the god-rays.** If a beam appears, add *"soft indirect window light, no visible light shafts."*

**Judge the bottom third first**, as before. That is where the title lives.

---

## My honest recommendation

Take the typographic route instead — cover **F** or **H** — and hold the object photography for the volumes underneath.

A book that looks like an ideas book gets picked up by a business owner. A book that looks like Christian inspiration gets picked up by someone who was already going to buy it. You are not trying to reach the second person; The Signatry already has him.

If you do want a photograph on the flagship, **The Keys** is the one. It says surrender without a single sunbeam, and it belongs to your reader's actual life.
