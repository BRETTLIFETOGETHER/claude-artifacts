# 40 DAYS OF GENEROSITY
## The Campaign Handbook

**For the pastor and the campaign coordinator · LifeTogether**
Status: developing, August 2026

# PART ONE — BEFORE YOU DECIDE

## What this campaign is

Six weeks. Three tracks. One book. A whole-church journey through what Scripture says about ownership, contentment, fear, sight, family, and joy — designed so a congregation of any size and any income can travel it together.

**The weekend** carries six messages. **The groups** meet weekly for six sessions. **The daily reader** runs all forty days. A church can run one track, two, or three. Run the weekend alone and the campaign still works, which is how most churches should start.

## The one thing you have to decide first

**Nobody is asked for anything.** No pledge cards. No commitment weekend. No capital appeal built into week six. No giving link on the closing slide.

That constraint is the product. It's why people talk honestly in groups, it's why they tell their friends, and it's the difference between formation and fundraising. Every church that runs this well protects it, and every church that runs it badly abandons it somewhere around week five.

Before you go further, get your executive pastor and your finance team to agree to it out loud. If they can't, don't run the campaign this season — run it in a season where you don't need anything, and everyone will be better served.

## When to run it

**Good.** January. Post-Easter. Early fall. Any season with nothing attached.

**Bad.** Alongside a budget appeal, a capital campaign, or at fiscal year end. Your congregation will read the six weeks as a setup, reasonably, because it usually is one.

**If you have no choice,** say so from the platform in week one, name exactly where the line is, and keep every ask entirely outside the groups. A congregation will forgive you for asking. It will not forgive you for asking while claiming you weren't.

## What it costs you

Six weekends of your teaching calendar. One staff member coordinating about four weeks out. A host recruitment push. No consultant, no travel, no on-site team.

# PART TWO — THE TWELVE-WEEK COUNTDOWN

## Twelve weeks out — decide

- Senior pastor commits to preaching weeks 1, 2, and 6 personally
- Executive team agrees to the no-ask covenant in writing
- Choose how many tracks: weekend only, weekend plus groups, or all three
- Set the launch date and put all six weekends on the teaching calendar
- Name one campaign coordinator with actual authority

## Ten weeks out — build the spine

- Pastor reads the full series outline and the daily reader
- Order materials with a 20 percent overage
- Identify six testimony candidates across the income range of the congregation
- Decide the children's track: family table version, kids ministry version, or both

## Eight weeks out — recruit hosts

- Set your host target: one host per eight to twelve adults you expect in groups
- Recruit from existing group leaders first, then from the congregation
- Announce the campaign from the platform for the first time
- Brief the six testimony candidates personally

## Six weeks out — train

- Host training: ninety minutes, once, with a second session offered
- Walk hosts through the covenant, session one, and what to do when it goes badly
- Open group sign-ups
- Promotional materials go up

## Four weeks out — fill the groups

- Second sign-up push from the platform
- Match unplaced people to groups personally, not by form
- Confirm testimony people and collect their three-minute drafts
- Distribute daily readers to everyone who signed up

## Two weeks out — final checks

- Every host has materials and has read session one
- Every testimony is briefed, timed, and free of dollar amounts
- Children's track leaders trained
- Weekend elements finalized for week one

## Launch weekend

- Week one message
- Covenant stated from the platform in the first ten minutes
- Daily readers available at every exit
- Groups begin that week or the next

## During

- Weekly host check-in by text — one question: *how did it go?*
- Watch week three closely; it's where the hardest material surfaces
- Do not add an ask. Someone will suggest it around week four.

## Two weeks after

- Debrief with hosts
- Ask three anonymous questions of participants
- Decide what the next step is for people who want one

# PART THREE — WHO DOES WHAT

**Senior pastor.** Preaches weeks 1, 2, and 6. States the covenant. Writes his own finish line before week two — a congregation can tell within ninety seconds whether the person preaching has done this.

**Campaign coordinator.** Owns the timeline, the materials, the sign-ups, and the host check-ins. This is roughly ten hours a week for four weeks and three hours a week during.

**Groups pastor or lay equivalent.** Host recruitment, training, and placement. This is where the campaign is won or lost — a church with forty hosts has a different campaign than a church with twelve.

**Children's leader.** Runs the family table cards and the four kids ministry sessions in parallel.

**Communications.** Six weeks of promotion, all of it pointing at the invitation rather than the outcome.

**Finance team.** Their job in this campaign is to do nothing, visibly. That is harder than it sounds and worth naming to them directly.

# PART FOUR — HOSTS

## What a host actually needs

Not training in content. The participant guide and leader script carry that. What a host needs is permission to be bad at it the first time and a clear picture of what the room should feel like.

## Host training agenda — ninety minutes

**[0:00–0:15] Why this campaign is different.** The covenant, said plainly. Every host must be able to state it in their own words before they leave.

**[0:15–0:35] Walk session one.** Not a summary — actually run the opening question with the hosts as the group.

**[0:35–0:55] What to do when it goes badly.** The silent group. The person who dominates. The couple who disagrees in front of everyone. The person who starts crying in week three.

**[0:55–1:15] What not to do.** Don't fix people. Don't answer your own questions. Don't chase completion — a group that got through two questions well did better than one that got through five.

**[1:15–1:30] Questions and prayer.**

## The three rules to repeat every week

1. Nobody is asked for anything, including in week six.
2. No numbers. People share decisions and reasoning, never amounts.
3. You may pass, on any question, without explanation.

# PART FIVE — TESTIMONIES

Six testimonies, three minutes each, one per week. This is the highest-leverage and most commonly botched element of the entire campaign.

**Recruit four weeks out.** An unbriefed testimony runs eleven minutes and mentions a dollar figure.

**Brief every single one personally.** Three minutes. One moment, not a life story. No amounts, ever. End on what changed in them rather than on what the gift accomplished.

**Vary them deliberately.** If all six come from the wealthiest households in your church, the campaign quietly teaches that generosity belongs to those people, and everyone else spends six weeks as an audience. Include at least two people who give out of very little. That single choice does more for the campaign than any other decision on this page.

**Week-by-week fit**

| Week | The kind of story that fits |
|---|---|
| 1 | Someone for whom the ownership question became real |
| 2 | Someone who set a limit on their own lifestyle |
| 3 | Someone who gave when it was genuinely frightening |
| 4 | Someone whose giving changed because of something they saw |
| 5 | A family, ideally two generations |
| 6 | Someone who's been at it for decades |

# PART SIX — WHAT TO MEASURE

**Count these.** Groups launched. Hosts recruited. Daily readers distributed. Attendance across the six weekends. Children's track participation. Testimonies delivered.

**Ask these, anonymously, two weeks after.** Did you set a finish line? Did you give something you'd never given before? Did your household have a conversation about money that you'd been avoiding?

**Do not report giving as a campaign result.** Giving frequently rises after this campaign, and the moment you claim it as the outcome you have retroactively made the whole thing an appeal. If your board asks, tell them the truth: we didn't run it for that, and we're not going to measure it that way.

# PART SEVEN — HOW IT FAILS

**The ask appears in week five.** Someone with good intentions suggests a small pledge card, just to capture momentum. This is the single most common failure and it undoes the entire six weeks in one Sunday.

**All six testimonies come from the same tax bracket.** The congregation quietly concludes the campaign wasn't for them and stops engaging in week three.

**Hosts were recruited but never trained.** Groups meet twice and dissolve.

**The pastor preaches it without having done it.** Week two is where this becomes obvious, and it cannot be recovered.

**It runs beside a budget appeal without acknowledgment.** The congregation isn't confused — they're clear, and what they're clear about is that they were handled.

**The church treats week six as the finish line.** It's the starting line. Have the next step ready by week four.

---

# PART EIGHT — WHAT COMES NEXT

Some percentage of your congregation will finish week six wanting more. Have these ready.

**For most people.** An ongoing group using the same material, or the 21-day journey as a personal reset.

**For families.** The family conversation guide, and an invitation to hold their first giving meeting.

**For business owners and households with assets.** A separate, hosted conversation — smaller, invitation-only, ask-free. This is where the legacy family edition lives, and it should never be announced from the platform.

**For your leaders.** Recruit next year's hosts from this year's groups, in week five, while it's still live.
