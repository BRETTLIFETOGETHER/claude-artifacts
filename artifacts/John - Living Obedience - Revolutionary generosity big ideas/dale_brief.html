Internal · prep call with Dale Armstrong

Before the Board

Thirty minutes. Three agreements. One idea worth more than the rest.

The goal of this call is not to preview the deck. It is to make sure that when you log off Monday, Dale is the one carrying the argument in the room. Get him to own three things and he will defend them after you're gone.

The three agreements

1
The title is Living Obedience — and Steve keeps his word.

Irrational stays, in the place where it works. It's chapter one and it's the talk Steve gives. It is not the spine.

2
This is a library, not a book — and the board should see that Monday.

He already said it: "present the book, then present what the entire vision looks like." Confirm he still wants that shape, and that ten minutes is the real number.

3
The Living Pledge gets introduced to him on this call, not to the board cold.

He has never heard of it. It is the largest idea in the proposal and it should reach the board through him, not from a stranger on a Zoom.

If he pushes back on the title

Don't argue taste. Argue extension — it's the argument that wins with an operator.

Say it roughly this way
"Run it out with me. Irrational Lifestyle? Irrational Legacy? Irrational Pledge? It dies at volume two. Now try Living Obedience, Lifestyle Obedience, Living Legacy, The Living Pledge. If the board is funding a library, the name has to carry six volumes — and only one of these does."

Then the second point, more quietly: irrational is the exact word a legacy family's attorney uses to talk them out of something. And Abraham wasn't being irrational — Hebrews says he reasoned that God could raise the dead. It isn't obedience against reason. It's obedience on information the spreadsheet doesn't carry.

Land it generously: Irrational Obedience is chapter one. Living Obedience is the spine. Steve keeps his oxymoron, in the place where oxymorons work.

The idea to give him

The Signatry means the one who signs

This is the sentence to say slowly, and then stop talking.

"Your vision is to write the last check. But the last check is only possible because somebody signed first. You're called The Signatry — and you've never asked anyone to sign anything."

The Living Pledge is not an idea borrowed from outside. It is their own name made literal. A written, dated, witnessed decision by a family that its answer to God is yes — before the question arrives. Their identity, their theology, and their product architecture converge on one page.

Where it goes: the pledge is stage four of the progression and the natural close of a legacy family experience. It is not a giving commitment and should never be presented as one — that distinction protects it and them.

Two editions, one house

Living Obedience — the consumer edition

Getting to yes. Works on anyone, at any income. This is the Purpose Driven analog and the volume play.

The legacy family edition

The pledge, the assets, the exit window, the generational conversation. Fewer families, deeper work, and where the advisor channel lives.

If a board member asks whether this is for everyone or for the wealthy, the answer is both, deliberately — and here's how they connect.

Two facts to get right before Monday

Terry Parker is NCF, not The Signatry. He co-founded the National Christian Foundation in 1982 with Larry Burkett and Ron Blue and drove the DAF model through the IRS. NCF is the multi-billion house. The Signatry is a different, younger organization — roughly half a billion in annual grants. Don't attribute NCF's scale to them in a room where seven people know the real number.

Don't explain donor-advised funds to them at all. These are the people who wrote the playbook, and ten minutes is far too short to describe their own business back to them with any precision. Speak to the heart, the book, and the system. Leave the tax to them.

What to ask him for

1
Confirm the shape and the clock.

Ten minutes, ending at eight. Book first, vision second, number third.

2
Ask what the chair actually needs to hear.

He knows the room. One sentence from him is worth an hour of guessing.

3
Ask whether Steve is in the room, and what he wants said about himself.

This should not read as a tribute. But the operating system he built is the reason only this house can publish it, and Dale should tell you how far to go.

Park until you're inside: the platform economics, the 100-tool library, the licensing model — and the observation that Rick Warren didn't know what a DAF was, and that this construct could run in churches worldwide. That last one is a Steve conversation, not a board conversation.
