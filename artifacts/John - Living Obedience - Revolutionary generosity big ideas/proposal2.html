Section one · the idea

What Living Obedience is

The definition
I am living. And I bend a knee to God the Father — so that everything I do from this point forward is His. Everything with my family. Everything with my work. Everything with my money. Not someday. Now.

That is the whole book. It is present tense, it is personal, and it is total. A person can only make it for himself — but nobody who makes it wants to stop there, which is why the progression that follows moves outward to the household, the business and the generations.

Why obedience and not generosity

Generosity is not the entry point. It is the outflow. A book about giving reaches people who already think of themselves as givers; a book about saying yes reaches everyone, and produces givers as a consequence. That inversion is the strategic decision underneath this whole proposal.

Why "living" and not "irrational"

Irrational is a superb chapter and a superb talk — the disciples leaving the nets makes no sense, and it should be told exactly that way. It is not a spine. Run it out: Irrational Lifestyle, Irrational Legacy, Irrational Pledge. It dies at volume two. Living extends across six.

The comparison we would make carefully
This is a Purpose Driven Life shaped opportunity — the same combination of one clear idea, a whole-life scope, and a pathway a person can actually walk.

Said carefully, because the claim is about the shape of the opportunity rather than a promise about sales. What that era proved is that a single decisive idea, supported by a system, reaches further than a better book with no system behind it.

Two editions, one house

Living Obedience is the consumer edition — getting to yes, and it works at any income. The legacy family edition carries the pledge, the assets, the exit window and the generational conversation.

Who it is for

Anyone, in the consumer edition. And specifically: business owners approaching a sale, families two years past one, and the advisors who sit with both.

Section two · why this house, and why now

You have already bought the attention

The Signatry has done something almost no ministry in America has done: it funded a consumer campaign at national scale and reached an audience most Christian organizations will never touch. Whatever anyone thinks of that, it proved the organization can operate at a scale of ambition far beyond a book.

But attention and formation are different products. A campaign gets a person to look up. It does not tell them what to do on Monday. Living Obedience is the pathway for the person who has already looked up — and it is the natural next act for a house that has spent heavily on the first half of that sentence.

Give · Grow · Grant
Every organization in this field can teach giving and granting. The Signatry is the only one that can write honestly about what happens to the money in between.

Twenty types of alternative investment in five years. General partner positions in operating businesses. Donor capital curated and grown rather than parked. That is a chapter no other publisher in this category can commission.

The commercial case

Reverse-engineering why a family chooses you

The Signatry benefits when a family gives here rather than somewhere else. That decision is rarely made on fees or features. It is made on trust, on shared conviction, and on whether the organization has been useful to the family before it ever asked for the account.

1
Acquisition.

A book and a campaign are the least threatening way to be useful to a family two years before they open a fund. Nobody is asked for anything, and the relationship starts anyway.

2
Retention.

A progression gives The Signatry a reason to be in a family's life for five years rather than at the moment of one transaction.

3
Deal flow.

The advisor edition reaches the professionals who sit with owners before a letter of intent — the exact window in which the largest gifts are either made possible or foreclosed.

Audience editions

Advisors · business owners · pastors · the next generation. Same spine, different stakes and instruments. A consumer's capacity is a skill and a spare room; an owner's is closely held stock and a letter of intent.

Why a progression rather than a sequel

One book reaches a reader once. Six volumes give an advisor something to bring back a second and third time — and the economics of volume two are entirely different from volume one.

Section five · the idea underneath all of it

The Signatry means
the one who signs

Your vision is to write the last check. But the last check is only possible because somebody signed first — and this organization has never asked anyone to sign anything.

A name written in the Lamb's Book of Life is the oldest picture of a decision recorded permanently. The Living Pledge is that picture applied to a life: a written, dated, witnessed decision by a person and a family that the answer is yes — before the question arrives, and before the amount is known.

It is not a giving commitment, and it must never be presented as one. That distinction is what protects it, and what makes it something a family will sign.

What it becomes

A volume in the progression. The close of every legacy family experience. A signing moment at a gathering. And, over time, a community of signers — which is the only asset in this proposal that appreciates rather than depreciates, and the one that carries this organization's own name.

Tier Three · The platform

Licensing · tools · enterprise
No fee

LifeTogether does not propose to charge The Signatry to build this. We propose to build and operate it at our own cost and risk, co-branded, on a revenue participation. If it works, both organizations earn. If it does not, the board has paid nothing for it.
