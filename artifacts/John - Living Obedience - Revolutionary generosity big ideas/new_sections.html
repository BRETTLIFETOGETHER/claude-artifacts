Section eight · the machine behind it

Why the second volume costs less than the first

LifeTogether does not build campaigns one at a time. It runs a catalogue and a production system — which is the reason a six-volume progression is a schedule rather than a gamble.

A master catalogue across twenty ministry categories, each title specified and ready to build.

20

From spiritual formation and generosity to marketplace, family and next generation.

What that changes for this project

The architecture is already solved — session shapes, daily-reading formats, leader scripts, campaign handbooks, promotion kits. What Living Obedience needs is its content, its stories and its voice. That is where the budget goes, rather than into inventing structure from nothing.

What it changes for The Signatry

You are not commissioning a book and hoping a system follows. You are putting your material into a system that already exists, and volumes two through four arrive on a schedule rather than as separate acts of faith.

The first volume buys the content. The system was already paid for.

Section ten · the schedule

What the first eighteen months look like

Days 1–30
Agreement and access

Scope confirmed, rights language settled, calendars opened. One named decision-maker inside the organisation.

Days 30–90
Interview capture

Recorded sessions with Steve and Dale, transcribed and archived. Outline delivered and approved. Story sourcing begins on the forty families.

Months 3–6
First draft, front half

Parts one and two written and reviewed. Curriculum architecture drafted alongside so the group edition never becomes an afterthought.

Months 6–9
Full manuscript

Complete draft delivered. Forty-day reader and six-session curriculum delivered in parallel. Publishing decision made — trade or controlled release.

Months 9–12
Edit, design, and the cover

Editorial pass, cover and interior design, endorsements sought. Publication-ready.

Months 12–18
Release and the first campaign

Launch, the first churchwide runs, and the scoped recommendation for Tier Two based on what the first year actually taught us.

What would slow it

Access, not writing. The single largest schedule risk is time with Steve and Dale, and the second is permissions on the family stories. Both are solvable in the first ninety days and expensive to solve later.

What we would report

A monthly note to one named person. Nothing published that either author has not read. No surprises in either direction.
