40 Days of Irrational Obedience  ·  Part Two: Surrender
Day Thirteen

Send Them Home

"You have too many men. Israel would boast against me, 'My own strength has saved me.'"
— Judges 7:2

Gideon has thirty-two thousand men and an enemy camped in the valley thick as locusts. He is outnumbered before a single sword is drawn, and God's first instruction is not a strategy. It is a reduction.

Twenty-two thousand go home before breakfast. Ten thousand remain, and God says it is still too many. By the time the sorting is finished Gideon has three hundred men — ninety-nine percent of his army has walked away — and he is told to attack.

There is no version of that briefing that survives a board meeting.

But God gives the reason plainly, and it is not tactical. Israel would boast against me. The reduction was never about the battle. It was about who would get the credit afterwards.

Which means the thing Gideon was asked to surrender was not soldiers. It was the explanation. He was being asked to win in a way that could never be attributed to him.

What this is

One of forty. Roughly six hundred words, five minutes, every morning for forty days — the same shape a church already knows from a Purpose Driven campaign.

Every day carries four things in this order: a Scripture, the story behind it, a short passage from one of the forty interviews, and one question a reader can actually answer before breakfast.

Why it matters

A chapter book is read once. A forty-day book is read together — which is what makes the campaign, the curriculum and the gathering possible at all.

Session Two  ·  Days 11–20

Surrender

Ninety minutes. Judges 7:1–8.
The big idea

Gideon was not asked to surrender soldiers. He was asked to surrender the explanation.

Say it this way
"Before we start — nobody in this room is going to be asked for anything. Not tonight, not in week six. If that changes how you listen, good. That's the point."

Opening question

What is the most valuable thing you have ever walked away from?

Pairs, six minutes. Then four people report what their partner said — not their own answer.

Discussion

1. What explanation are you holding on to?

2. Where has success in your life been perfectly attributable to you?

3. What have you called wisdom that might have been fear?

4. What would you have to send home?

If the room goes quiet

Count to fifteen before you speak. It will feel unbearable. Somewhere around eleven somebody starts talking, and it is usually the person who most needed to.

This week

Name the advantage out loud to one person. Same partner all six weeks — one text midweek, three words or fewer.

The Signatry

The Living Pledge

We are not promising an amount.
We are deciding an answer.

That what we hold is not ours, and never was.

That we will give while we are living, so that we can see where it goes and our children can see it with us.

That we will name what is enough, and say the number out loud to each other.

That the next generation will help decide, and will not be handed a result they had no part in.

And that when God asks, our answer is already yes — before we know the question, and before we know the cost.

Not a giving commitment

And it must never be presented as one. No amount, no percentage, no schedule. That distinction is what protects it — and what makes it something a family will actually sign.

Why it belongs here

The Signatry means the one who signs. The vision is to write the last check — but the last check is only possible because somebody signed first, and this house has never asked anyone to sign anything.

Where it happens

At the close of a Gathering, in a room with other families. Those who are ready sign. Those who aren't take it home. Nobody is told which they should be.
