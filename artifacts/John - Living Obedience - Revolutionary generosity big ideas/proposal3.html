01
Section one · the idea

What Living Obedience is

I am living. And I bend a knee to God the Father — so that everything I do from this point forward is His. Everything with my family. Everything with my work. Everything with my money. Not someday. Now.

That is the whole book. It is present tense, it is personal, and it is total. A person can only make it for himself — but nobody who makes it wants to stop there, which is why the progression that follows moves outward to the household, the business and the generations.

Why obedience and not generosity

Generosity is not the entry point. It is the outflow. A book about giving reaches people who already think of themselves as givers; a book about saying yes reaches everyone, and produces givers as a consequence.

Why "living" and not "irrational"

Irrational is a superb chapter and a superb talk. It is not a spine. Run it out — Irrational Lifestyle, Irrational Legacy, Irrational Pledge — and it dies at volume two. Living extends across six.

Section two · why this house, and why now

You have already bought the attention

The Signatry has done something almost no ministry in America has done: it funded a consumer campaign at national scale and reached an audience most Christian organisations will never touch.

But attention and formation are different products. A campaign gets a person to look up. It does not tell them what to do on Monday. Living Obedience is the pathway for the person who has already looked up — and it is the natural next act for a house that has spent heavily on the first half of that sentence.

Every organisation in this field can teach giving and granting. The Signatry is the only one that can write honestly about what happens to the money in between.

Twenty types of alternative investment in five years. General partner positions in operating businesses. Donor capital curated and grown rather than parked. That is a chapter no other publisher in this category can commission.

The commercial case — reverse-engineering why a family chooses you

1
Acquisition.

A book and a campaign are the least threatening way to be useful to a family two years before they open a fund. Nobody is asked for anything, and the relationship starts anyway.

2
Retention.

A progression gives The Signatry a reason to be in a family's life for five years rather than at the moment of one transaction.

3
Deal flow.

The advisor edition reaches the professionals who sit with owners before a letter of intent — the exact window in which the largest gifts are either made possible or foreclosed.

One note on sequencing. Tier One produces three assets a single book cannot use: an interview archive that is raw material for five more volumes, an assessment that generates data worth publishing annually, and a pledge that has nowhere to live inside one book.

Why a progression rather than a sequel

One book reaches a reader once. Four volumes give an advisor something to bring back a second and third time — and the economics of volume two are entirely different from volume one.

Section five · the idea underneath all of it

The Signatry means
the one who signs

Your vision is to write the last check. But the last check is only possible because somebody signed first — and this organisation has never asked anyone to sign anything.

A name written in the Lamb's Book of Life is the oldest picture of a decision recorded permanently. The Living Pledge is that picture applied to a life: a written, dated, witnessed decision by a person and a family that the answer is yes — before the question arrives, and before the amount is known.

It is not a giving commitment, and it must never be presented as one. That distinction is what protects it, and what makes it something a family will sign.

What it becomes

A volume in the progression. The close of every legacy family experience. A signing moment at a gathering. And, over time, a community of signers — the only asset in this proposal that appreciates rather than depreciates, and the one that carries this organisation's own name.

Tier Three · The platform

Licensing · tools · enterprise
No fee

LifeTogether does not propose to charge The Signatry to build this. We propose to build and operate it at our own cost and risk, co-branded, on a revenue participation. If it works, both organisations earn. If it does not, the board has paid nothing for it.

How participation would actually be written

A non-profit has no equity, so nothing here is structured as a percentage of the organisation. Participation means three ordinary, enforceable instruments: an author royalty on the book, a per-church licence on the campaign, and a revenue share on the platform — each arm's-length and priced at fair market value.
