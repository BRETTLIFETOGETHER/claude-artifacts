# 40 DAYS OF GENEROSITY
## Small Group Teaching Script

**For the host · LifeTogether**
Status: developing, August 2026

---

## How to use this script

Three kinds of text below, and the difference matters more than anything else in this document.

**SAY IT THIS WAY** — the exact words. Not because your phrasing is worse, but because these lines carry a promise the whole campaign rests on, and paraphrase erodes them. There are fewer than a dozen in six weeks.

**YOUR WORDS** — the point to make. Say it however you'd say it to a friend. If you read these aloud you will sound like a recording, and the room will match your energy.

**DO** — an action, not a sentence.

**HOST NOTE** — for you, never spoken.

**Mark your copy before the meeting.** Highlight only the SAY IT THIS WAY lines. Everything else you should be able to carry with a glance.

**The single most important skill is silence.** After a question, count to fifteen in your head before you say anything. It will feel unbearable. Somewhere around eleven, someone starts talking, and it's usually the person who most needed to.

# SESSION ONE · WHO OWNS IT?

**90 minutes.** The session that determines whether the other five work.

---

### 0:00 — Welcome and the covenant

**DO** — Have everyone seated with something to drink before you start. Do not begin standing.

**YOUR WORDS** — Welcome them. Thank them for coming. Say how long you'll go and that you'll finish on time. Then say you want to start with something before anything else.

**SAY IT THIS WAY**

> "Before we do anything, I want to tell you what this is and what it isn't.
>
> Over the next six weeks, nobody in this room is going to be asked for anything. There's no pledge card at the end of this. Nobody from the church will call you. I'm not going to ask, and I've been asked not to ask.
>
> If that changes how you listen — good. That's the point."

**HOST NOTE** — Do not soften this, do not joke through it, and do not add anything after it. Say it, pause for two full seconds, and move on. Half the people in your room have sat through something that promised this and didn't mean it. They're waiting to find out which kind this is.

**DO** — Read the five covenant lines from the participant guide, then say:

**SAY IT THIS WAY**

> "Two of those matter most. No numbers — we talk about decisions and reasoning, never amounts. And you can pass on any question, any week, and nobody will ask you why."

---

### 0:08 — Names

**YOUR WORDS** — Around the room: first name, and one sentence about who's at home. Nothing about work, nothing about what anyone does for a living.

**HOST NOTE** — This is deliberate. The moment occupations enter the room, people start ranking each other financially, and that's the exact dynamic this material has to survive.

---

### 0:15 — The opening question

**SAY IT THIS WAY**

> "Here's the first question. What's the most generous thing you have ever *received*? Not given — received."

**DO** — Pairs. Six minutes. Set a timer where they can see it.

**HOST NOTE** — Starting with receiving does two things. It disarms anyone who came in braced for an ask, and it puts the room in the posture the rest of the six weeks depends on. Do not skip it to save time.

---

### 0:22 — Report back

**YOUR WORDS** — Ask if three or four people would share what their partner said, not their own. Take the answers, thank each person, and don't comment on any of them.

**HOST NOTE** — You will want to draw the lesson out. Don't. Almost every answer will describe something small, personal, and connected to a relationship rather than a transaction. The room is teaching itself and will get there faster than you can lead them.

---

### 0:30 — The gap

**YOUR WORDS** — Make this point, in your language, in about four minutes:

Nearly everyone in this room would say God owns everything. And nearly everyone in this room, including you, manages money like an owner. That gap isn't hypocrisy. It's just that nobody ever asked us to make an actual decision about it — we inherited a belief and never converted it into anything.

**HOST NOTE** — Include yourself in the indictment out loud. A host who describes the problem as though he's already solved it loses the room in week one and never gets it back.

---

### 0:35 — The text

**DO** — Ask someone to read 1 Chronicles 29:11–14 aloud. Then ask a different person to read verses 13 and 14 again.

**YOUR WORDS** — Set it up before the reading, briefly:

David spent his whole life wanting to build the temple and got told he wouldn't be the one. So he did the next thing — gathered everything his son would need. Gold, silver, timber, and then his own personal treasure on top of it. The leaders followed him, the people gave willingly, and the whole nation celebrated. It's the largest offering in Israel's history.

Then he prays. And in front of everybody, at the highest point of the most generous moment of his life, he says this.

**DO** — The reading happens here.

**YOUR WORDS** — Afterward, one observation and then stop:

He doesn't thank them. At the peak of the most generous day of their lives, he tells them they never owned any of it.

---

### 0:44 — The turn

**YOUR WORDS** — Three or four minutes:

There are really only two ways to think about a gift. One is subtraction — this is mine, and some of it is leaving. The other is allocation — this belongs to someone else, and I'm deciding where it goes.

Every answer to a subtraction question is smaller than the last one, because subtraction hurts. I don't think anyone in history has ever given joyfully while doing subtraction.

Which means the question was never *how much should I give.* It's *what does the owner want done with this?*

**SAY IT THIS WAY**

> "That's the whole six weeks, honestly. Everything else is downstream of that one question."

---

### 0:50 — Testimony

**DO** — Your prepared person, three minutes, no dollar amounts.

**HOST NOTE** — Brief them the week before, personally. One moment, not a life story. If nobody is prepared, skip it entirely rather than asking someone cold — a cold testimony runs eleven minutes and mentions a number.

---

### 0:55 — Discussion

**SAY IT THIS WAY**

> "Four questions. We won't get through all of them, and that's fine."

**DO** — Ask question one. Then count to fifteen.

1. Where does your behavior contradict what you say you believe about ownership? Be specific — name a decision, not a category.
2. What did you learn about money in the house you grew up in that nobody ever said out loud?
3. If God owns it, what's your actual job description? One sentence.
4. What would change this week if you managed one account as a steward instead of an owner?

**HOST NOTE** — Question one is the session. If the room stays there for twenty minutes, you've had a better meeting than one that covered all four. Question two is the safety valve — go there if question one stalls, because everyone can answer it and it warms the room.

**HOST NOTE** — Expect defensiveness tonight. Someone will explain why their situation is different. Don't correct it. People who arrive guarded usually arrived that way because somebody has used this subject on them before.

---

### 1:15 — The practice

**DO** — Hand out the sentence cards.

**YOUR WORDS** — This week, fill in one sentence and sign it. *God owns ______, and my job is ______.* Put something specific in the first blank — an account, a house, a paycheck, a business. Then put it somewhere you'll see it for the next thirty-nine days.

**SAY IT THIS WAY**

> "Nobody collects these. I'm not going to check. It's between you and God, and I'll ask next week how it went, and you can say 'I didn't do it,' and that will be completely fine."

---

### 1:22 — Close

**YOUR WORDS** — Tell them what's next week in one sentence: if God owns it, the obvious next question is how much we're supposed to keep — and almost nobody asks it.

**DO** — Pray briefly. Thank God for something specific that the room didn't earn. Finish on time.

**HOST NOTE** — Do not add announcements after the prayer. The last thing said in the room should be the prayer, not logistics.

# THE FIXED LANGUAGE FOR SESSIONS TWO THROUGH SIX

Everything else you can carry from the leader guide. These are the moments that must not be improvised.

## SESSION TWO · ENOUGH

**Opening the session**

> "Quick check — did anyone write the sentence? No pressure either way, I just want to know."

**Before the finish-line exercise**

> "I want to name something before we do this. I'm not asking you to give more. I'm asking you to decide what's enough, and I genuinely don't know what you'll do with the answer. Those are different things."

**If someone says the number feels legalistic**

> "That's fair. Here's the difference as I understand it — a floor is a rule that tells you the minimum. A ceiling is a decision that ends the argument. One of those is a burden and the other one is freedom."

**HOST NOTE** — Someone in this session may get visibly uncomfortable. Fear surfaces here more than anywhere else in six weeks. Don't pursue it in the room. Note who it was, and text them tomorrow.

---

## SESSION THREE · OPEN HANDS

**Opening the teaching**

> "I want to say something that took me a long time to believe. Almost nobody in this room holds back because they love money. We hold back because we're afraid. And greed is something you repent of, but fear is something somebody has to walk you out of."

**Before the fear question**

> "This one's harder than it looks. I want the actual scenario — not 'the future,' not 'the economy.' The specific thing you rehearse at three in the morning. And you can pass."

**If the room goes somewhere heavy**

> "Thank you for saying that. I'm not going to try to fix it tonight, and I'd like to follow up with you this week if that's okay."

**HOST NOTE** — This is the most pastoral night of the six. Some of what surfaces won't be about money at all. Your job is to receive it, not solve it. Have your pastor's number in your phone.

---

## SESSION FOUR · WHAT DO YOU SEE?

**Setting up the teaching**

> "When God calls the prophets, he doesn't ask them what they're going to do. He asks what they see. Jeremiah gets shown a branch. Amos gets shown a plumb line. The assignment always comes after the sight."

**If someone says they aren't a visionary person**

> "Neither was the cloth merchant who funded the first English Bible. He had a warehouse and some ships. That's what God used. You're not required to become somebody else first."

**Closing the session**

> "Before you leave tonight, put the visit on your calendar. Not 'sometime.' A date. It's the only thing from this week that won't happen on its own."

---

## SESSION FIVE · TOGETHER

**Opening — say this before the teaching, not after**

> "One thing before we start. When I say family tonight, I mean whoever you share life with. If you're single, if your kids are grown, if your family is complicated or far away — this session is still for you. Somebody is watching how you handle money. That's all this needs."

**HOST NOTE** — People without children or nearby family will otherwise spend ninety minutes as spectators. Say it at the top, not as an afterthought at the end.

---

## SESSION SIX · THE RETURN

**The guardrail — do not skip and do not paraphrase**

> "I need to be careful here. God is generous, and the return is real. And none of that is a promise that giving makes you wealthy. Paul praises a church that gave beyond what they could afford, out of real poverty, and he never once says they got rich. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling you something."

**Before the closing exercise**

> "Last thing. I'm going to ask a few of you to read one sentence out loud — just the sentence, nothing else. Nobody responds, nobody comments, no applause. We just listen. Is that alright?"

**The very last words of six weeks**

> "Six weeks ago I told you nobody would ask you for anything. Nobody did. That was true then and it's true tonight. Let's pray."

**HOST NOTE** — Then pray and stop. No announcements, no next steps, no card, no link. Somebody in that room has been waiting six weeks to see whether you meant it. Let them find out that you did.

# DELIVERY NOTES

**Don't read this aloud.** Mark the SAY IT THIS WAY lines and carry the rest. A script read verbatim sounds like a script, and the room will go formal to match you.

**Sit down.** A host standing over a seated group has changed the meeting into a class.

**Answer nothing yourself.** If you answer your own question, you've taught the room that questions are rhetorical, and they'll stop working after week two.

**Let the silence be long.** Fifteen seconds. Count it.

**Never follow a hard answer with a solution.** "That sounds really difficult, thank you for telling us" is a complete response.

**Finish on time, every week.** People decide whether to come back based on whether you respected their evening.

**You will be bad at week one.** Every host is. The group forgives it entirely, and by week three you'll have stopped noticing the script.
