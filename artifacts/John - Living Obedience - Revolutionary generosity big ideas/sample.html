Sample · one day, written out

What a reader actually holds

Roughly six hundred words. Five minutes. Every morning for forty days.
Day Thirteen · Surrender

Send Them Home

"You have too many men. Israel would boast against me…" — Judges 7:2
The moment

Gideon has thirty-two thousand men and an enemy camped in the valley "thick as locusts." He is outnumbered before a single sword is drawn. And God's first instruction is not a strategy. It is a reduction.

Twenty-two thousand go home that morning. Ten thousand remain, and God says it is still too many. By the time the sorting is finished, Gideon has three hundred men — ninety-nine percent of his army has walked away — and he is told to attack.

There is no version of that briefing that survives a board meeting.

Why

God gives the reason plainly, and it is not tactical. Israel would boast against me, "My own strength has saved me." The reduction was never about the battle. It was about who would get the credit afterwards.

Which means the thing Gideon was asked to surrender was not soldiers. It was the explanation. He was being asked to win in a way that could never be attributed to him.

From a life · the daily interview

[One of the forty families, in their own words — the moment they gave up an advantage on purpose. Roughly one hundred and fifty words, drawn from the recorded interviews.]

Today

Name one advantage you are holding on to — a relationship, a reserve, a fallback, a reputation — that would make the outcome explainable without God.

What would it cost to send that one home?
Who gets the credit if this works the way you have planned it?

Sample · the small-group session

Six sessions, ninety minutes each

Written so a first-time host can lead it without training.

Session Two · Surrender. Covering days 11–20. Every session carries the same five parts, so a host learns the shape once and never has to learn it again.

0:00
Opening question

What is the most valuable thing you have ever walked away from? Pairs, six minutes, then four people report what their partner said.

0:15
Scripture, read aloud

Judges 7:1–8, read by one person. Then verses 2 and 7 read again by a second. Thirty seconds of silence before anyone speaks.

0:25
Video · twelve minutes

The teaching film for this movement. The host presses play and says nothing before or after it.

0:40
Discussion · four questions

1. Gideon was asked to surrender the explanation, not the battle. What explanation are you holding?
2. Where has success in your life been perfectly attributable to you?
3. What have you called wisdom that might have been fear?
4. What would you have to send home?

1:10
The challenge and the partner

This week, name the advantage out loud to one person. Same partner all six weeks. One text midweek, three words or fewer.

1:22
Prayer and take-home

Pray for courage rather than for outcomes. Days 21–30 for the week ahead.

What the host receives

A participant guide and a leader script for every session — including the exact words for the moments that must not be improvised, and what to do when the room goes quiet or goes somewhere heavy.

What makes it work

The teaching lives on the film, so the host is never teaching. Their whole job is to start on time, ask the question, wait, and finish on time. Preparation is fifteen minutes a week.

Sample · the same forty days, four ways

One spine, four editions

The Scriptures never change. The stories, the questions and the exercises change — because what a family is being asked to release is not the same thing in each room.

Consumer
The forty-day journey

Anyone, any income. The advantage being surrendered is a habit, a grudge, a Saturday, a spare room.

Church
The forty-day campaign

Weekend messages, six group sessions, the daily reader, a children's edition and a promotion kit. The format a church already knows how to run.

Legacy family
The forty days for a household of means

The advantage is closely held stock, a letter of intent, a trust, a name. Includes the family conversation guide and the pledge.

Advisors
The cohort edition

For the professional who sits with those families before the transaction — and has never been given permission to ask the question.

Forty days. Forty Scriptures. Forty families who actually did it.
05
Sample · what this is and isn't

This is a sample, not the book

What you have just read is four pages out of roughly two hundred. It exists to answer one question — what would we actually be commissioning — and to make the answer concrete rather than promised.

What is settled

The four movements. The daily shape: Scripture, story, interview, question. The six-session structure. The four editions. The forty-day arc from awakening to well done.

What is not

Which forty passages make the final cut. Which forty families tell their stories. The title of every day. Those are decisions to make with Steve in the room, not before it.

Subtitles under consideration

Finding the life you were meant to live on the other side of yes
Saying yes to God before the reasons arrive
What sixteen people in Scripture could see that everyone watching them couldn't
Build the boat. Send them home. Give the boy back.
The first ninety days produce the outline, the interview archive and the first movement written in full. Everything above becomes real, or gets replaced, in that window.
