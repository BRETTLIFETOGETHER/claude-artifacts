Category overview

Why this category matters

Hearing and obeying God is the heartbeat of discipleship — and the place most believers feel the most uncertain. They long to hear from God and quietly wonder why He seems silent, or they hear clearly and struggle to obey. Few topics are more central to spiritual maturity, and few are taught with practical, grace-filled clarity.

The Core Problem

Most believers want to follow God but sit between two gaps: a hearing gap, unsure how to discern His voice, and a doing gap, hearing and hesitating. The result is a faith that admires obedience more than it practices it. Leaders sense the need and lack a ready, biblically grounded pathway.

The Transformation

These campaigns move people from straining to hear toward a confident, responsive life — noticing His voice, discerning His leading, obeying the nudge, and answering with a wholehearted yes.

Lifestyle Obedience

Obedience as the pattern of an ordinary week.
02

Lifestyle Obedience moves obedience out of the crisis and into the calendar. Most of us are prepared for the dramatic yes and unprepared for the ordinary one — which is where nearly all of it actually happens.

The Big Idea
God asks for Tuesday far more often than He asks for the mountain. What you repeat is what you actually believe.

The Core Problem

We treat obedience as episodic — the altar moment, the big surrender, the hard call. So we brace for the rare test and drift through the ordinary week, never noticing that the shape of a life is built out of the parts nobody claps for.

The Transformation Promise

Participants stop waiting for significant moments and begin building repeatable ones — until obedience is no longer a decision they make but a shape their week already has.

Legacy Obedience

The obedience your children will inherit.
03

Legacy Obedience takes the private decision and makes it visible. Children inherit what they watched — not what they were told — and most families transmit far less than they intend.

The Big Idea
Obedience is caught across generations or it isn't caught at all. Your children will inherit what they saw you do, whether or not you meant them to.

The Core Problem

We obey privately and instruct publicly, then wonder why nothing carried. A family can hold real convictions for forty years and hand down nothing but assets, because the decisions were made where nobody could see them and the reasons were never told.

The Transformation Promise

Participants learn to make obedience visible and repeatable — building the objects, practices and stories that give the next generation something to ask about, and an answer when they do.

Living Obedience for Advisors

The counsel that shapes a family's whole financial life.
04

The marketplace edition. Written for the Christian advisor, attorney or CPA who sits across from families at the moments that decide everything — and who has usually never been given permission to raise the question that matters most.

The Big Idea
The most consequential conversation an advisor will ever have with a client is not about returns. It is about who owns it — and most advisors have never been told they're allowed to ask.

The Core Problem

Advisors are trained to optimize and taught to stay out of a client's convictions. So the question goes unasked, and a family makes the largest decisions of its life with excellent technical counsel and nobody to ask what any of it is for.

The Transformation Promise

Participants learn to raise the ownership question well — without proselytizing and without losing the client. And they settle their own answer first, because a family can tell inside two minutes whether the person across the table has done this.

The Living Pledge

Settle the answer before the question arrives.
05

The capstone of the progression. Not a giving commitment and not a campaign card — a written, dated, witnessed decision about how a person intends to answer God, made in advance of knowing what will be asked.

The Big Idea
People who decide in the moment mostly decide no. A pledge is not a burden you take on — it is the argument you never have to have again.

The Core Problem

Most of us leave the answer open, believing that keeps us free. It doesn't. An undecided person deliberates every request on its merits, and deliberation under pressure reliably produces the safe answer.

The Transformation Promise

Participants settle the answer in advance and put it in writing — reducing every future decision from whether to how much and when — and name the people permitted to hold them to it.
