01
Section one · the idea

What Irrational Obedience is

I am living. And I bend a knee to God the Father — so that everything I do from this point forward is His. Everything with my family. Everything with my work. Everything with my money. Not someday. Now.

Every ask God makes in Scripture fails the reasonable test at the moment it is made. Build a boat with no rain. Send thirty-one thousand seven hundred soldiers home. Give back the son you waited a century for. Cook your last meal for a stranger. Irrational is the observer's word, never the obeyer's — and the book's whole job is to show what the person obeying could see that the person watching could not.

Why obedience and not generosity

Generosity is the outflow, not the entry point. A book about giving reaches people who already think of themselves as givers. A book about saying yes reaches everyone — and produces givers as a consequence.

Why irrational

Irrational generosity would concede that the giving makes no sense. Irrational obedience describes what the disciples actually did. Leaving the nets mid-workday is irrational by every worldly measure, and Scripture tells it approvingly.

The shape

A forty-day book in the Purpose Driven shape rather than a chapter book — four movements, Awakening · Surrender · Generosity · A Family Lifestyle, ten days each. Every day carries a Scripture, a story, an interview and one question. Five minutes a morning, for forty mornings.

Day Thirteen · Surrender

Send Them Home

"You have too many men. Israel would boast against me…" — Judges 7:2

Gideon has thirty-two thousand men and an enemy camped in the valley thick as locusts. He is outnumbered before a single sword is drawn. And God's first instruction is not a strategy. It is a reduction.

Twenty-two thousand go home that morning. Ten thousand remain, and God says it is still too many. By the time the sorting is finished Gideon has three hundred men — ninety-nine percent of his army has walked away — and he is told to attack. There is no version of that briefing that survives a board meeting.

God gives the reason plainly, and it is not tactical. Israel would boast against me, "My own strength has saved me." The reduction was never about the battle. It was about who would get the credit afterwards. Which means the thing Gideon surrendered was not soldiers. It was the explanation.

Element eight · the gathering

The room where it is signed

A pledge nobody gathers around is a document. A pledge signed in a room is a movement.
The Living Pledge Gathering
Twenty-four hours · twelve to eighteen families · no ask, stated on arrival
EVEDinner, and one story — a family two years further down the road than everyone else in the room.
9:00What did God ask you that made no sense? — each household, six minutes, no interruptions.
10:30Twenty questions, one evening — households separate. Parents in one room, the next generation in another.
13:00Back together — what surprised you about what they said?
15:00Writing — each family drafts its own pledge. Nobody reads anyone else's.
17:00The signing — those who are ready. Witnesses named aloud. Nobody is asked to.
YEARThe return — the same families, twelve months on, reporting what actually changed.
Specimen programme · the format, the room, and the annual return

The discipline that makes it work is the same one Generous Giving proved over twenty-five years: a room where nobody is asked for anything produces honesty that no solicitation ever has. Say it on arrival and hold it to the last minute.
