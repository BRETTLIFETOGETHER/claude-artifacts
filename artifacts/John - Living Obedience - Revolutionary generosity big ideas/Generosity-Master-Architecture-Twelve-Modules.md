# THE GENEROSITY MASTER ARCHITECTURE
### Twelve modules. Four editions. One content library.

**LifeTogether · Status: developing, August 2026**

---

## THE PRINCIPLE

Do not build four curricula. Build one library and four wrappers.

Twelve modules carry the whole biblical and practical tradition. Every edition — the legacy family edition, the consumer edition, the 40-day campaign, the 30-day small group experience — draws from the same twelve. What changes between editions is not the theology. It is the **stakes and the instruments**.

A consumer's capacity module is about time, skill, a spare room, and a used car. A legacy family's capacity module is about a closely held business and a letter of intent. Identical doctrine, different arithmetic. Get that right and one revision improves every product you sell.

**Source discipline.** This synthesizes Scripture and the public teaching of the movement — Ron Blue, Randy Alcorn, Rick Warren, David Green and Bill High, the National Christian Foundation, Generous Giving's documented convictions, Gospel Patrons, Daryl Heald, Todd Harper, April Chapman, The Signatry. It reproduces no organization's proprietary curriculum. Anything drawn directly from Generous Giving's materials requires their written permission.

# THE TWELVE MODULES

---

## 1 · OWNERSHIP — Whose is it?

**Core idea.** Stewardship begins with a transfer of title, not a change of feeling. Almost every Christian affirms God's ownership and manages money as an owner anyway.

**Primary Scripture.** Psalm 24:1 · 1 Chronicles 29:11–14 · Deuteronomy 8:17–18
**Supporting.** Haggai 2:8 · 1 Corinthians 4:7 · Job 41:11 · Leviticus 25:23

*David's prayer in 1 Chronicles is the strongest ownership text in Scripture and the least preached. Everything we gave you was already yours.*

**The tradition.** Ron Blue's life work sits here. So does Alan Barnhart's first settled principle and Stanley Tam's legal transfer of his company in 1936.

**Exercise.** Write and sign one sentence: *God owns ______, and my job is ______.* Date it. Review annually.

**Legacy family variant.** The sentence becomes a family ownership statement, read at the annual meeting, and the documents get audited against it — trusts, entities, the will. Do the paperwork and the conviction actually match?

**Consumer variant.** One account, one month. Manage a single checking account as a steward reporting to an owner and notice what changes.

---

## 2 · IDENTITY — Steward is a name, not a task

**Core idea.** Behavior follows identity. A person who thinks of himself as an owner who gives will always be doing subtraction. A person who thinks of himself as a steward is doing allocation.

**Primary Scripture.** Luke 12:42–48 · Matthew 25:14–30 · 1 Peter 4:10
**Supporting.** Genesis 1:28 · Genesis 2:15 · 1 Corinthians 6:19–20

**The tradition.** The parable of the talents is judged on what the money *did*, not on what was received — the earliest argument that inputs are the wrong metric.

**Exercise.** Write your job description as a steward the way you'd write a role you were hiring for. Responsibilities, authority, reporting.

**Legacy family variant.** Extend it to succession: stewardship passes from one steward to the next; ownership can only be divided among heirs. That distinction solves the fairness problem, the fragmentation problem, and the fourth-generation problem at once.

**Consumer variant.** Name the three things you steward that aren't money — a skill, a room, an hour, a relationship.

---

## 3 · THE WARNING — What wealth does

**Core idea.** Scripture's warnings to the wealthy are not decorative, and the people who took them literally have the best stories.

**Primary Scripture.** Matthew 19:16–26 · Luke 12:13–21 · 1 Timothy 6:9–10 · Mark 4:19
**Supporting.** Proverbs 11:28 · Ecclesiastes 5:10–15 · James 5:1–6 · Revelation 3:17

*Alan Barnhart was twenty-four when he read the camel and the needle, and spent two years working through hundreds of passages before he changed anything.*

**The tradition.** Randy Alcorn's argument that giving is the antidote to materialism's grip belongs here, alongside Ron Sider's older provocation.

**Exercise.** The two-year study, compressed: read one money passage a day for the length of the campaign before making a single decision.

**Legacy family variant.** Name specifically what wealth has already cost your family — a relationship, a child's motivation, a decade of anxiety. This is the hardest and most necessary conversation in the legacy edition.

**Consumer variant.** Identify the purchase you were sure would satisfy you and didn't. Where did the feeling go?

---

## 4 · ENOUGH — The finish line

**Core idea.** The tithe is a floor. Nobody ever handed you a ceiling. The keep question — how much do we hold back — reframes everything downstream and ends the annual argument.

**Primary Scripture.** Proverbs 30:8–9 · 1 Timothy 6:6–8 · Philippians 4:11–13 · Exodus 16:16–20
**Supporting.** Hebrews 13:5 · Luke 3:11 · 2 Corinthians 8:13–15 · Matthew 6:11

*The manna could not be stored. God built a lesson about enough into the food itself.*

**The tradition.** The single most predictive practice in the field. Barnhart's income cap set in year one; April Chapman's inversion from how-much-to-give to how-much-to-keep; Ron Blue's contentment teaching.

**Exercise.** Name the annual number at which your household has enough. Name the review date. Tell two people. If you can't write the number, write the obstacle — that answer is worth more.

**Legacy family variant.** Two numbers, not one: the lifestyle cap and the amount that transfers to each child. Decide what is helpful rather than what is possible, and say it aloud while everyone is alive to discuss it.

**Consumer variant.** Cap the lifestyle and let the giving float — fix consumption at today's level so the next raise becomes a giving decision automatically.

---

## 5 · FEAR — The actual barrier

**Core idea.** Almost nobody withholds because they love money. They withhold because they are afraid. Greed you repent of; fear you get walked out of.

**Primary Scripture.** Matthew 6:25–34 · 1 Kings 17:7–16 · Hebrews 13:5–6 · Philippians 4:6–7
**Supporting.** Psalm 37:25 · Proverbs 3:5–6 · Matthew 14:22–33 · Deuteronomy 31:6

*The widow at Zarephath is asked for her last meal during a famine with her son beside her. Read it as a fear story rather than a giving story and it changes shape.*

**The tradition.** LeTourneau pledging from underneath a six-figure loss. Müller's ledgers kept precisely so the claim could be tested.

**Exercise.** Name the fear specifically — not "the future," the actual scenario you rehearse at 3 a.m. Then list three times God provided in ways you have half forgotten.

**Legacy family variant.** The fear is rarely running out. It is a child ruined, a name diminished, a business failing after the sale. Name the real one.

**Consumer variant.** Give something this week you will actually notice, and write down what you felt on the way to doing it.

---

## 6 · TREASURE AND HEART — Which follows which

**Core idea.** Giving is not the report card of a changed heart. It is the mechanism of one. Your heart follows your treasure, in that order.

**Primary Scripture.** Matthew 6:19–21 · Matthew 6:24 · Luke 12:33–34 · Luke 16:1–13
**Supporting.** Colossians 3:1–2 · 1 John 2:15–17 · Matthew 13:44–46

**The tradition.** Alcorn's central contribution, and the reason generosity belongs in spiritual formation rather than in the finance committee.

**Exercise.** Give deliberately toward something you do *not* yet care about, and watch what happens to your affection over ninety days.

**Legacy family variant.** Audit where the family's treasure actually is — time, attention, and calendar as well as capital. The heart followed something. Find out what.

**Consumer variant.** Track where your money went last month without judgment, then read Matthew 6:21 and ask what it says about you.

---

## 7 · SIGHT — What do you see?

**Core idea.** God's question to the prophets is not *what will you do* but *what do you see.* Sight precedes assignment. A person who cannot name anything specific gives generically, to whoever asks best.

**Primary Scripture.** Jeremiah 1:11–13 · Amos 7:7–8 · Luke 10:30–37 · Nehemiah 2:11–18
**Supporting.** Zechariah 4:1–6 · Habakkuk 2:2–3 · Isaiah 6:8 · Esther 4:14

**The tradition.** The Gospel Patrons line — Monmouth saw an English Bible before one existed; Lady Huntingdon saw a drawing room full of aristocrats who would never stand in a field.

**Exercise.** The sight inventory: three things seen with your own eyes in five years you could not stop thinking about. Then the access-expertise-grief inventory — what has God already put you next to?

**Legacy family variant.** Concentrate. Pick one problem, learn it properly, and define what finished looks like there. Concentration produces expertise; expertise produces better giving.

**Consumer variant.** Go and look at one thing you already support. One visit, scheduled before the study ends.

---

## 8 · CAPACITY — What you actually have

**Core idea.** Roughly nine-tenths of wealth sits outside cash and roughly all giving comes out of the checking account. The bottleneck is competence, not character — people are more willing than they are equipped.

**Primary Scripture.** Deuteronomy 26:1–11 · Proverbs 3:9–10 · 2 Corinthians 8:12 · Mark 12:41–44
**Supporting.** Exodus 35:20–29 · 1 Chronicles 29:1–9 · John 6:8–13 · Acts 4:32–37

*Firstfruits are the crop itself, not currency converted from it. And the boy brought loaves, not coins.*

**The tradition.** NCF built asset-based giving into an institution. Barnhart and Green gave companies. Monmouth gave a shipping network. Huntingdon gave a room.

**Exercise.** The asset inventory. List everything you own that is not cash. Circle what has appreciated. That circled list is your real capacity.

**Legacy family variant.** Business interests, real estate, appreciated securities, grain, royalties. The sequence question governs everything: can it be given *before* it is sold? Pair with the eight questions before a letter of intent.

**Consumer variant.** Same exercise, wider definition. A skill, a truck, a spare room, a professional network, a Saturday. Most people badly underestimate this list.

---

## 9 · TIMING — Give while you live

**Core idea.** Capital deployed at fifty-five works thirty years longer than capital deployed at eighty-five. A gift at death is a transaction; a gift during life is a partnership. And an estate plan teaches your children nothing.

**Primary Scripture.** Proverbs 3:27–28 · Luke 12:16–21 · Ecclesiastes 11:1–6 · James 4:13–17
**Supporting.** Psalm 90:12 · 2 Corinthians 9:5 · Galatians 6:10

**The tradition.** David Green moving ownership during his lifetime. The Signatry's argument that velocity matters more than balance — measure how fast the money leaves, not how much is held.

**Exercise.** The speed audit. Time your last significant gift from conviction to settlement, in days. Write down what slowed it.

**Legacy family variant.** Model one planned bequest as though it were given this year and simply look at the number. Then build the infrastructure that makes a yes take days rather than months.

**Consumer variant.** Do the thing you have been meaning to do for a year. This week.

---

## 10 · FAMILY — Transmission

**Core idea.** Children inherit what they helped decide. Instruction transmits almost nothing; participation transmits nearly everything. A family with one generous parent and four silent heirs produces litigation, not legacy.

**Primary Scripture.** Deuteronomy 6:4–9 · Psalm 78:4–7 · Joshua 4:20–24 · 2 Timothy 1:5
**Supporting.** Proverbs 13:22 · Proverbs 20:21 · Luke 15:11–32 · Deuteronomy 4:9

*Joshua stacked twelve stones so that when children asked what they meant, someone would have to tell the story. Most families have no stones.*

**The tradition.** Bill High's legacy work; Green's mission trust the family cannot reach; four-generation families who did it on purpose.

**Exercise.** Build a stone — an object, a date, an annual meal that provokes the question. Then hold the family meeting with real dollars the next generation allocates and defends.

**Legacy family variant.** Equal isn't always fair. The great wealth transfer is a discipleship event currently handled by attorneys. Governance, family purpose statement, and a next-generation allocation with real authority.

**Consumer variant.** Household is whoever you share life with. Let one person outside yourself help decide one gift this month.

---

## 11 · COMMUNITY — The room and the table

**Core idea.** Generosity is caught, not argued. Trust is built where nobody is selling, and generous families do better work in each other's company than they do alone.

**Primary Scripture.** 2 Corinthians 8:1–7 · Acts 2:44–47 · Acts 4:32–35 · Hebrews 10:24–25
**Supporting.** Proverbs 27:17 · Ecclesiastes 4:9–12 · Philippians 1:3–7 · 3 John 5–8

*Paul uses one church's generosity to provoke another's — the earliest recorded use of contagion, and he does it without asking.*

**The tradition.** Generous Giving's ask-free retreat, privately funded since 2000 precisely so it never has to ask. The Gathering's convening model. The church's opportunity and its usual failure.

**Exercise.** Sit in one room this year where nobody wants anything from you. Then tell one person the reasoning behind a gift — the decision, never the amount.

**Legacy family variant.** Build or join a table of six to twelve peers that meets on a calendar rather than on impulse. Loneliness, not stinginess, is the more common condition.

**Consumer variant.** Give with two other people toward one thing, and decide it together.

---

## 12 · JOY AND THE FINISH — What it's all for

**Core idea.** The giver is the primary beneficiary, and the joy is real rather than compensatory. And the largest available vision is a finishable one — almost everyone offers perpetual need; almost nobody offers an ending.

**Primary Scripture.** Acts 20:35 · 2 Corinthians 9:6–15 · Matthew 24:14 · Revelation 7:9
**Supporting.** Proverbs 11:24–25 · Luke 6:38 · 1 Timothy 6:17–19 · 2 Timothy 4:7 · Matthew 13:1–23

*The parable of the sower: the seed is identical every time and the soil varies. The multiple depends on where you sow — which means the question was never how much, but where.*

**The guardrail, stated in every edition.** The return is real and it is not a promise of wealth. The Macedonians gave beyond their ability out of severe poverty and Paul never reports that they got rich. Hold the joy and the cost together or you preach something cruel.

**The tradition.** Todd Harper's two decades on the joy thesis. Buford's hundredfold, held alongside the loss of his son. The Signatry's vision of the last check to the last missionary.

**Exercise.** Write the end-state sentence. One sentence, no qualifiers: *When our work is done, ______ will be true.*

**Legacy family variant.** Work backward from the end state to this year's number. Then measure the household's outflow rate and publish it to your own family once a year.

**Consumer variant.** Give one gift this year that nobody will ever attribute to you.

# THE FOUR EDITIONS

## EDITION ONE — THE LEGACY FAMILY EDITION

**Who.** Households with significant assets, their adult children, and where invited, their advisors. Eight to sixteen people. Never treated as funding sources.

**Format.** An overnight or two-day hosted experience, then four quarterly follow-ups over the year. The follow-ups are what distinguish it — a single retreat produces feeling; a year produces structure.

**Module weighting.** All twelve, with the retreat carrying 1, 4, 5, 7, 12 — ownership, enough, fear, sight, the finish. The quarterly sessions carry 8, 9, 10, 11 — capacity, timing, family, community — because those require documents, advisors, and time between meetings.

**What only this edition includes.** The asset inventory worked with real numbers. The pre-exit checklist. Equal-isn't-fair and the inheritance conversation. Family governance and the next-generation allocation. Advisor integration.

**The rule.** No solicitation, ever, by anyone, including the host organization. These families are handled constantly and will detect an ask through a wall.

**Instruments.** Kingdom Giving Statement · Legacy & Liquidity Worksheet · Family Giving Conversation Guide · the end-state sentence.

---

## EDITION TWO — THE CONSUMER EDITION

**Who.** Anyone. A trade book plus a devotional reader, sold in the ordinary market.

**Format.** Twelve chapters, one per module, roughly 4,000 words each. Story-led, mechanism-light.

**The translation rule.** Every module stays; every instrument scales down. Capacity becomes time, skill, and possessions rather than closely held stock. Timing becomes *do it this week* rather than *before the letter of intent*. Family becomes household. The theology does not move an inch.

**What this edition must do that the legacy edition need not.** Prove in the first fifty pages that this is not a book for wealthy people. Lead with the widow's two coins and the Macedonians giving out of poverty. If the first three stories are business owners, the ordinary reader closes it and is right to.

**What to cut.** Asset-giving mechanics, trust structures, exit planning, advisor integration. All of it belongs in an appendix or not at all.

---

## EDITION THREE — THE 40-DAY CAMPAIGN

**Who.** A whole congregation. Three tracks — weekend, groups, daily reader.

**Structure.** Twelve modules across six weeks. Roughly three days per module, plus four rest days.

| Days | Modules | Week theme |
|---|---|---|
| 1–7 | 1, 2 | Who owns it |
| 8–14 | 3, 4 | Enough |
| 15–21 | 5, 6 | Open hands |
| 22–28 | 7, 8 | What do you see |
| 29–35 | 9, 10 | Together |
| 36–40 | 11, 12 | The return |

**Daily reading format.** Story or testimony · short teaching · exercise or action step · next-step challenge · three reflection questions.

**Non-negotiables.** No pledge card in week six. Testimonies drawn from across the income range of the congregation, not from its wealthiest households. Run in a season with nothing attached to it.

---

## EDITION FOUR — THE 30-DAY SMALL GROUP EXPERIENCE

**Who.** A group of eight to sixteen, running four to five weeks. Also the compressed edition for churches that cannot give six weeks of the calendar.

**Structure.** Thirty readings, twelve modules, four or five sessions.

| Days | Modules | Session |
|---|---|---|
| 1–6 | 1, 2, 3 | Whose is it? |
| 7–13 | 4, 5 | Enough, and what I'm afraid of |
| 14–20 | 6, 7 | Treasure, and what I see |
| 21–26 | 8, 9 | What I have, and when |
| 27–30 | 10, 11, 12 | Together, and what it's for |

**What compression costs.** Modules 3, 6, and 11 get folded rather than featured. That is an acceptable loss — the warning, the treasure-and-heart argument, and community are the three that survive being carried inside another session. Never compress 4, 5, or 12; enough, fear, and joy are load-bearing.

# WHAT ACTUALLY CHANGES BETWEEN EDITIONS

| | Legacy family | Consumer | 40-day | 30-day |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Theology** | identical | identical | identical | identical |
| **Modules** | all 12, weighted to 8–11 | all 12, even | all 12, paired | 12 folded into 5 |
| **Capacity means** | business interests, real estate | time, skill, possessions | both, taught side by side | both |
| **Timing means** | before the letter of intent | this week | this season | this month |
| **Family means** | governance and succession | household | household | household |
| **Advisors** | in the room | absent | absent | absent |
| **Lead story** | an owner | a person of ordinary means | a person of ordinary means | either |
| **The ask** | never | never | never | never |

That final row is the only one that must never vary. It is the discipline the entire tradition rests on, and it is the first thing that will be quietly abandoned under commercial pressure.

# OPEN ITEMS

**Permissions.** Anything drawn from Generous Giving's materials, the Barnhart video, or Green's book requires written permission. The module architecture and Scripture index above are original and clear.

**The story bank.** Every module needs three stories at three income levels. That inventory does not yet exist and is the single largest remaining build.

**Sequencing.** The consumer edition and the 40-day campaign should launch within one season of each other; the legacy edition can precede both, since it needs no publisher.
