40 Days ofGenerosity

Six weeks in which a church talks honestly about money — and asks for none of it.

A churchwide campaign from LifeTogether  ·  Weekend · Groups · Daily Reader

The decision

Your congregation braces when you say the word.

Most churches only talk about money when they need some. Over twenty years that trains a congregation to hear every mention of generosity as the opening of an appeal — and they are not wrong to.

Which leaves a pastor with a real problem. The families in your church are navigating the largest transfer of wealth in history, business owners are reaching exit age all at once, and almost none of them has ever been discipled on the subject by anyone. The people best positioned to help them have every reason to be suspected of self-interest.

This campaign exists to break that pattern in one congregation at a time. It disciples people out of fear rather than into the offering.

The one rule
Nobody is asked for anything.

No pledge cards. No commitment weekend. No capital appeal built into week six. No giving link in the newsletter footer. That constraint is not politeness — it is the reason people tell the truth in a living room, and it is the whole product.

The arc

Six weeks, six questions

The order is not interchangeable. Each week rests on the one before it — fear cannot be preached to a room that has not settled ownership.

01

Who Owns It?

Whose is this, actually?

David gathers the largest offering in Israel's history and tells the room they never owned any of it. Subtraction becomes allocation.

02

Enough

How much do we keep?

The tithe is a floor. Nobody was ever handed a ceiling — and the ceiling is what ends the argument.

03

Open Hands

What am I actually afraid of?

Almost nobody withholds out of greed. Greed is repented of; fear has to be walked out of.

04

What Do You See?

What has God put in front of me?

God asks the prophets what they see, not what they will do. Sight comes before assignment, every time.

05

Together

Who decides, and who comes after?

Joshua stacked twelve stones so children would have to ask. Most families have nothing that provokes the question.

06

The Return

What is any of this for?

Joy, held honestly alongside cost — and a vision with an ending rather than a growth curve.

The design

Three tracks. Run one, two, or all three.

The Weekend

Six messages with texts, illustrations, transitions and the trap in each one named. Run this alone and the campaign still works — which is how most churches should start.

The Groups

Six sessions, ninety minutes, video-led. Because the teaching lives on screen, a host's whole job is to press play, ask a question, wait, and finish on time. Preparation is fifteen minutes a week.

The Daily Reader

Forty readings. A story, a Scripture, an exercise, a next step and three questions each morning. Someone who never joins a group still moves with the church.

Almost everyone will offer you a need. Almost no one will offer you a finish.

Counsel we'd rather give you now

Don't run this at fiscal year end.

Run it in a season when the church needs nothing and your congregation will believe you. Run it beside a budget appeal and they will read six weeks as a setup — reasonably, because it usually is one.

Giving frequently rises after this campaign. That is a side effect, and the moment it becomes the goal the campaign stops working. If your church must run it near a funding season, say so from the platform in week one and keep every ask entirely outside the groups. A congregation will forgive you for asking. It will not forgive you for asking while claiming you weren't.

What it costs you

Six weekends of the teaching calendar. One staff member coordinating for about four weeks. A host recruitment push. No consultant, no travel, no on-site team.
