# 40 DAYS OF GENEROSITY
# PART TWO · THE SMALL GROUP GUIDE

**Six sessions · 90 minutes each**
LifeTogether · Status: developing, August 2026

---

## What changed by adding video

The teaching now lives on screen, which means the host is no longer a teacher. That is a bigger deal than it sounds. A church that needed twelve people capable of teaching can now recruit thirty people capable of pressing play and asking a question — and group capacity, not interest, is what limits every campaign of this kind.

**So the host's whole job is four things:** start on time, ask the question, wait, and finish on time.

## The covenant — read aloud in Session One, posted every week after

**Nobody is asked for anything.** No pledge, no card, no follow-up call — not in week one, not in week six.
**No numbers.** Decisions and reasoning, never amounts.
**No comparison.** The widow's two coins outweighed everything else in the treasury.
**What is said here stays here.**
**You may pass** on any question, any week, without explanation.

# SESSION ONE · WHO OWNS IT?

**Big idea.** Generosity doesn't begin when you decide to release something. It begins when you realize it was never yours.

### The video — 13 minutes
Teaching pastor on camera. Opens with the gap between what we say about ownership and how we behave, told against himself. Walks David's offering in 1 Chronicles 29 and the prayer at the peak of it. Lands the distinction between subtraction and allocation. **Closing line:** *The question was never how much should I give. It's what does the owner want done with this.*

### Opening prompt
What's the most generous thing you have ever **received**? Not given — received.

*Pairs, six minutes, then three or four report what their partner said.*

### Group Scripture reading
**1 Chronicles 29:11–14.** One person reads it through. A second person reads verses 13 and 14 again.
*Then sit with it for thirty seconds before anyone speaks.*

### Video discussion questions
1. He doesn't thank the givers — he tells them they never owned it. What would it have felt like to be standing there?
2. Where does your behavior contradict what you say you believe about ownership? Name a decision, not a category.
3. What did you learn about money in the house you grew up in that nobody ever said out loud?
4. If God owns it, what's your actual job description? One sentence.

### Challenge and accountability
**This week:** Write and sign one sentence — *God owns ______, and my job is ______.* Something specific in the first blank: an account, a house, a paycheck.

**Accountability:** Pair up now and exchange numbers. One text midweek, three words or fewer. *Did you write it?*

*Nobody collects these. The host asks next week and "I didn't do it" is a complete and acceptable answer.*

### Prayer and takeaway
**Pray:** Each person thanks God aloud for one specific thing they did not earn.
**Take home:** The sentence card, and Days 1–6 of the daily reader.

# SESSION TWO · ENOUGH

**Big idea.** The tithe is a floor. Nobody ever handed you a ceiling — and the ceiling is what sets you free.

### The video — 14 minutes
Teaching pastor. Opens on the number we each thought would be enough at twenty. Works Agur's prayer and the manna that couldn't be stored. Introduces the finish line as a practice, told through Alan Barnhart capping his income in year one. **Closing line:** *I'm not asking you to give more. I'm asking you to decide what's enough, and I don't know what you'll do with the answer.*

### Opening prompt
When you were twenty, what number did you think would be enough? Did you get there — and what happened when you did?

### Group Scripture reading
**Proverbs 30:7–9** and **Exodus 16:16–20.** Different readers.
*Ask the room: why do you think God made the extra manna spoil?*

### Video discussion questions
1. A floor tells you the minimum; a ceiling ends the argument. Which have you been operating with?
2. What's the honest reason a number is hard to write?
3. Who decided what "enough" looks like for you — and were they trustworthy?
4. What has increased in your life in the last ten years, and did your giving increase with it?

### Challenge and accountability
**This week:** Write the annual number at which your household has enough. If you can't write it, write down what makes it hard — that answer is worth more.

**Accountability:** Tell your partner *that* you wrote it. Never the figure.

### Prayer and takeaway
**Pray:** Agur's prayer, out loud, together. Both halves.
**Take home:** Days 7–13.

*Host note: someone may go quiet tonight. Fear surfaces here more than anywhere. Don't pursue it in the room — text them tomorrow.*

# SESSION THREE · OPEN HANDS

**Big idea.** The enemy is fear, not greed. Greed you repent of. Fear somebody has to walk you out of.

### The video — 12 minutes
Teaching pastor, gentler tone than the other five. Names fear as the actual barrier. Tells the widow at Zarephath as a fear story rather than a giving story. Ends on what Hebrews actually promises — presence, not provision. **Closing line:** *The comfort is a person, not a balance.*

### Opening prompt
What's the thing you'd find hardest to lose? Be honest — it's often not money.

### Group Scripture reading
**1 Kings 17:7–16,** read aloud in full by one person. It's short and it lands better read than summarized.
Then **Hebrews 13:5–6.**

### Video discussion questions
1. From outside, what she did looks reckless. From inside it's trust. What's the difference?
2. Name your fear specifically — not "the future." The actual scenario you rehearse at three in the morning.
3. When has God provided in a way you didn't expect and have half forgotten?
4. What would you do differently if you were certain you'd be cared for?

### Challenge and accountability
**This week:** Give something away that you'll actually notice. Not a check — a thing, an afternoon, or an introduction that costs you something.

**Accountability:** Text your partner what you felt on the way to doing it. Not what you gave.

### Prayer and takeaway
**Pray:** In pairs. Each person names their fear aloud to God in their own words, and the partner prays one sentence over them.
**Take home:** Days 14–20.

*Host note: this is the pastoral week. Some of what surfaces won't be about money. Receive it, thank them, don't solve it, follow up privately.*

# SESSION FOUR · WHAT DO YOU SEE?

**Big idea.** Sight comes before assignment. You fund what you can picture.

### The video — 13 minutes
Teaching pastor, plus 90 seconds of footage from one place the church actually supports. Works God's question to the prophets — what do you see, not what will you do. Tells Humphrey Monmouth, the cloth merchant who funded Tyndale and went to the Tower for it. **Closing line:** *You're not required to become somebody else first. He had a warehouse and some ships.*

### Opening prompt
What have you seen in the last five years that you couldn't stop thinking about? Not a cause — something you saw.

### Group Scripture reading
**Jeremiah 1:11–13,** then **Nehemiah 2:11–18.**
*Note aloud: before Nehemiah said a word to anyone, he rode out at night and looked at the walls himself.*

### Video discussion questions
1. Monmouth had a warehouse and ships, and that's what God used. What do you already have?
2. Where has your giving gone to something you've never actually seen?
3. What has God put you close to — a place, a skill, a loss?
4. What would you have to be willing to be interrupted by?

### Challenge and accountability
**This week:** Go and look at one thing. A ministry, a neighborhood, a person's work.

**Accountability:** Put the date on your calendar before you leave this room tonight, and tell your partner what it is.

### Prayer and takeaway
**Pray:** Ask God to show each person one specific thing this week.
**Take home:** Days 21–27.

# SESSION FIVE · TOGETHER

**Big idea.** Children inherit what they helped decide. Instruction transmits almost nothing.

### The video — 13 minutes
Teaching pastor, plus a two-generation family from the congregation on camera. Works Joshua 4 and the twelve stones — stacked so children would have to ask. Makes the case that participation transmits what instruction never does. **Closing line:** *Most families have no stones. Nothing to ask about.*

### Opening prompt
Who taught you to give — and how did they teach you? Almost nobody will name a sermon.

### Group Scripture reading
**Joshua 4:20–24** and **Deuteronomy 6:4–9.**

### Video discussion questions
1. What are your family's stones? What object or practice makes someone ask?
2. What do the people closest to you know about *why* you give?
3. Who's missing from your generosity — someone who should be deciding with you and isn't?
4. What would it take to make one gift together this year, decided together?

### Challenge and accountability
**This week:** Have one conversation with a spouse, child, friend, or roommate about a gift you'll make together. Give them a real vote.

**Accountability:** Report one sentence next week — who you talked to, and what surprised you.

### Prayer and takeaway
**Pray:** For the people who come after us.
**Take home:** Days 28–34.

*Host note: say at the top, not the end, that "family" means whoever you share life with. Otherwise the single people in your room spend ninety minutes as spectators.*

# SESSION SIX · THE RETURN

**Big idea.** The giver is the primary beneficiary — and there's an ending worth aiming at.

### The video — 15 minutes
Teaching pastor. Joy first: the heart follows the treasure rather than leading it. Then the guardrail, stated plainly — the return is real and it is not a promise of wealth, and the Macedonians gave out of poverty and Paul never says they got rich. Closes on the finish: a great multitude from every nation, and somebody writes the last check. **Closing line:** *Almost everyone will offer you a need. Almost nobody will offer you a finish.*

### Opening prompt
What's actually changed in you over six weeks? Anything, however small.

### Group Scripture reading
**Acts 20:35,** **2 Corinthians 8:1–5,** and **Revelation 7:9.** Three readers.

### Video discussion questions
1. Why does the guardrail matter — what goes wrong when someone promises return without cost?
2. Where have you seen real joy in a generous person, and what did it look like?
3. What would be true if your part of the work were finished?
4. What's the one thing you're going to do, and when?

### Challenge and accountability
**This week and after:** Bring out the sentence you wrote in Session One. Read it. Change what needs changing. Then write one more — *When our work is done, ______ will be true.*

**Accountability:** Keep your partner. Agree on one check-in date ninety days from tonight. Put it in your phones before you leave.

### Prayer and takeaway
**The reading:** If the room is willing, invite three or four people to read **only** their end-state sentence aloud. No discussion, no response, no applause. Say that before you begin.
**Pray:** For courage after the group ends.
**Take home:** Days 35–40.

**And then nothing.** No announcement, no next steps, no card, no link. Somebody in that room has waited six weeks to see whether the covenant held. Pray, and let them go.

# HOST PREPARATION — fifteen minutes a week

Watch the video. Read the four discussion questions. Highlight the one you'd start with if the room is quiet. That's the whole preparation.

**Three things to remember every week.** Ask the question and count to fifteen before you speak. Never answer your own question. Finish on time.

**You'll be bad at Session One.** Every host is. The group won't notice, and by Session Three you'll have stopped thinking about it.
