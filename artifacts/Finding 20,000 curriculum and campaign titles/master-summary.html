III. Decisions Made

The positioning, in order of importance.

  1. They have proven sermons. We have proven strategy.
    Brett’s line, verbatim, and the only competitive claim that requires insulting nobody.
    • Their sermons are well written and biblically sound — concede it immediately
    • A high rating means another pastor liked reading it, not that it produced a next step
    • One is concerned with the reference. The other is concerned with the result.
  2. You come for a sermon. You leave with a service.
    Service Builder exists because we cannot compete on sermons and should stop trying.
    • The other thirty-five minutes is the product: worship, order, slides, experience, response, next step
    • Nothing new was built — nine scattered features were named as one
  3. The overview sermon is not week one.
    Most campaign kits ship week one as the free sample, so the previewing pastor reads an unfinished thought.
    • Week one assumes five more messages and ends incomplete on purpose
    • The overview compresses the whole argument and ends complete
    • This is the fix for the free-tier problem
  4. Illustrations: prompt, mine, create, coach.
    “Excavation” retired at Brett’s instruction. Mining his own archive is the method nobody else can copy.
    • Nobody else asks for the sermon archive, so nobody else can mine it
    • Plus the Story Finder, which generates illustrations from the congregation
  5. Six days, not seven.
    Sunday is the sermon. Monday to Saturday belong to it.
    • Recorded on or before Sunday, one sitting, from the pastor
    • Every day ends with a question that goes to a named human
    • The forward-looking question gathers illustrations for sermons not yet preached
  6. The buyer is the bivocational pastor.
    If it works for him it works for everyone above him. Build for the large church and it works for nobody else.
  7. Naming: two names, two jobs.
    The Sermon Library is the free front door and wins the search. The Pastor’s Library is the paid, comprehensive one.
    • Sermon to Strategy™ is the strongest asset to trademark — a method name is ownable, a descriptive one is not
    • 40 Day Campaigns is the campaign product, not the platform
The sermon is the least valuable thing you sell and the most expensive thing to produce. Everything built around it is where the defensibility lives.

Three numbers, then the library ladder.

Reasoned from the market shape and the verified competitor position. None of it validated.

  1. All-access is 3.4× one library, deliberately
    A church wanting four does the math itself, which makes all-access the default rather than the upsell.
  2. The Pastor’s Library prices separately, as a project
    $8–15K foundation, $25–45K legacy, $50–100K+ full archive. It is labour; mixing labour into a subscription breaks both.
  3. Founders year: one dollar per adult attender
    Cap the cohort at 100 churches, print the renewal price on the same page, floor $100 and cap $2,500, and ask for a testimonial rather than a discount.
  4. Nine-month scenarios
    Careful $45K · Base $139K · Strong $332K. Modelling, not forecasts.
Before publishing a numberTest $1,990 and the $290 floor on ten pastors you know. The structure will survive contact with the market. The dollars probably will not. Not financial advice — run the assumptions past someone qualified.
V. Still Missing

Seven gaps, ranked by what they cost.

  1. Nothing produces an actual file
    Every platform describes deliverables. None generates a PDF, a Word document, a deck or a print-ready journal a church can use on Sunday. This is the single largest gap between prototype and product.
  2. Eight built against 21,534 titles
    Every founder conversation lands here. The 120 flagship kits recommendation is still unruled.
  3. No shared profile across the nine
    A pastor fills in the profile in the sermon library and the staff platform knows nothing about it. One profile, written once, read by everything — that is the whole Pastor’s Library thesis and it is not wired.
  4. Kids, student and family editions
    Ranked the most defensible asset three times. Still zero. A household hearing one idea in four rooms is the thing nobody else can ship.
  5. Nothing for the person in the pew
    Nine platforms, all staff-facing. The only member-facing product is a devotional. There is no congregant experience at all.
  6. No onboarding — the first thirty days after yes
    A church subscribes and then what? No sequence, no setup call, no first-campaign plan. This is where subscription churn is decided.
  7. Video, Spanish, and the measurement dashboard
    Video conceded and still zero. The campaign platform runs three languages; nothing else does. And we tell churches to measure without giving them anything to measure with.
