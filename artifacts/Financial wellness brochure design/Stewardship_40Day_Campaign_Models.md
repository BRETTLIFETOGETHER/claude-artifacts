# The Stewardship Collection — 40-Day Campaign Models
### Ten Complete Churchwide Campaigns · 40-Day Devotional + 6-Session Small-Group Curriculum
**Lifetogether · Brett Eastman, Founder · brett@lifetogether.com**

This document expands all ten campaigns in The Stewardship Collection from the 30-day / four-session
brochure format into complete **40-day, six-session** campaign models. Each campaign is built to function
as a stand-alone churchwide series and as a foundation that can later be developed into a full devotional
workbook, a small-group leader guide, and a complete campaign kit.

---

## How the 40-Day Model Works

Every campaign follows the same proven architecture, so a church can run any of the ten — or all ten over
a multi-year calendar — with a single, familiar rhythm.

**The 40-Day Journey.** Forty consecutive daily devotionals carry each participant through a personal
transformation. The forty days are organized into six weekly movements that mirror the six small-group
sessions. Days 1–7, 8–14, 15–21, 22–28, and 29–35 form five seven-day weeks; Days 36–40 form a shorter
sending week that lands the campaign on a clear next step. Each devotional below is given as a title and a
one-sentence summary — the seed from which a full 400–600 word daily reading, Scripture, reflection
prompt, and prayer can be written.

**The Six-Session Curriculum.** Groups meet once per week for six weeks. Each session gathers the week the
group has just lived through the devotionals, then moves the group from insight to obedience. Every session
provides a theme, a goal, a Scripture focus, an opening question, four discussion questions, and a weekly
application step or group exercise.

**The Campaign Arc.** Across six weeks every campaign moves the same direction: from **seeing** (a new way
of understanding) to **surrender** (a heart decision) to **sending** (a sustained practice). Week 1 names
the problem and reframes it. Weeks 2–5 build the new convictions and practices. Week 6 commissions people
into a lasting change and a concrete next step.

**Recommended Cadence.** Launch on a Sunday with a vision message; begin Day 1 the following Monday; hold
Session 1 at the end of Week 1; conclude with a celebration Sunday after Day 40. Best results come when the
weekend message, the daily devotional, and the small-group session all carry the same weekly theme.

# CAMPAIGN 1 — Life Stewardship

**Subtitle:** A 40-Day Journey to Managing All of Life Well
**Big Idea:** Everything you have — your time, talent, treasure, trust, and testimony — is on loan from God.
When you stop clutching life as an owner and start managing it as a steward, the pressure of ownership
gives way to the peace of trusteeship, and every part of life becomes an act of worship.
**Target Audience:** All church and workplace; the ideal foundational or kickoff series for a stewardship
emphasis, a new ministry year, or a mixed-maturity congregation.
**Transformation Goal:** Move participants from a money-only, ownership mindset to a whole-life trusteeship
identity, so they can name and faithfully manage all five things God has entrusted to them.
**Key Scriptures:** Psalm 24:1; 1 Corinthians 4:2; 1 Peter 4:10; Luke 12:48; Luke 16:10–11; Matthew 6:21;
Matthew 5:16; Romans 14:12; Colossians 3:23–24.

### The Campaign Arc (Week 1 → Week 6)
The series opens by answering the one question underneath everything — *owner or manager?* — and lets that
reframe land before building anything on it (Week 1). The middle four weeks then walk the congregation
through the five trusts in turn: time (Week 2), talent (Week 3), treasure (Week 4), and trust and testimony
together (Week 5). The journey concludes by lifting everyone's eyes to the day of accounting and the joy of
hearing "well done," then sends each person out with one integrated, surrendered life and a single forward
commitment (Week 6). The arc moves from *reframe* to *inventory* to *account*.

### Small-Group Curriculum — Six Sessions

**Session 1 — Owner or Manager?**
- **Theme:** The single reframe the whole campaign is built on.
- **Goal:** Help each person consciously trade the identity of owner for the identity of steward.
- **Scripture Focus:** Psalm 24:1; 1 Corinthians 4:2; Luke 12:48.
- **Opening Question:** When you hear the word *stewardship*, what is the first thing you assume it's about — and where did that assumption come from?
- **Discussion Questions:**
  1. What in your life do you most instinctively call "mine," and how would it change things to call it "managed"?
  2. Psalm 24:1 says the earth and everything in it belongs to God. Where is that easy to believe, and where is it hard?
  3. Paul says stewards are required to be faithful, not successful. How does that lower some pressures and raise others?
  4. Which of the five trusts — time, talent, treasure, trust, testimony — do you sense you steward best right now, and which one have you barely thought about?
- **Application Step / Group Exercise:** Each person writes a one-line "title transfer" — a sentence handing one specific thing back to God as its true owner — and reads it to the group.

**Session 2 — Stewarding Your Time**
- **Theme:** Time is the one trust you cannot earn back.
- **Goal:** Move the group from a vague sense of busyness to an honest audit and one concrete realignment.
- **Scripture Focus:** Psalm 90:12; Ephesians 5:15–16.
- **Opening Question:** If a stranger studied your last seven days, what would they conclude you value most?
- **Discussion Questions:**
  1. Where does your time actually leak — and is that leak a decision or a default?
  2. What does "number our days" do to the way you think about an ordinary Tuesday?
  3. Name one thing that is genuinely important but consistently gets your leftovers. Why?
  4. What would "making the most of every opportunity" look like in your specific season right now?
- **Application Step / Group Exercise:** Each person identifies one recurring time-leak to cut and one important relationship or practice to give that time to this week; partners check in midweek.

**Session 3 — Stewarding Your Talent**
- **Theme:** Your gifts were given to serve, not to store.
- **Goal:** Help each person name their gifts honestly and find one place to deploy them.
- **Scripture Focus:** 1 Peter 4:10; Romans 12:6; Matthew 25:24–27.
- **Opening Question:** What is something you do so easily that you're surprised when others find it hard?
- **Discussion Questions:**
  1. Peter says each of us has received a gift to serve others. What gift have you been given — and are you using it?
  2. The servant in Matthew 25 buried his talent out of fear. Where does fear keep your gifts hidden?
  3. What's the difference between developing a gift for God and performing a gift for approval?
  4. Where in this church, your workplace, or your family is your specific gift exactly what's needed?
- **Application Step / Group Exercise:** Each person names one concrete way to put a gift to work in the next two weeks; the group prays over each commitment.

**Session 4 — Stewarding Your Treasure**
- **Theme:** Money is a map to the heart and a test of faithfulness.
- **Goal:** Bring earning, giving, and spending under God's ownership without shame or pressure.
- **Scripture Focus:** Matthew 6:21; Luke 16:10–11.
- **Opening Question:** What money lesson did you absorb growing up — spoken or unspoken — that still shapes you?
- **Discussion Questions:**
  1. If your spending is a map of your heart, what does the map currently show?
  2. Jesus links faithfulness with little money to being trusted with true riches. Why do you think He connects the two?
  3. Where do you feel most in control financially, and where most anxious — and what does the pattern reveal?
  4. What would change if you genuinely managed your money as God's rather than your own?
- **Application Step / Group Exercise:** Each person chooses one small, specific, measurable financial change for the next 30 days and tells a partner why it matters.
*(Sensitivity note for leaders: hold this session with grace; money carries shame for many. Keep the focus on the heart and on God's ownership, not on dollar amounts or comparison.)*

**Session 5 — Stewarding Trust & Testimony**
- **Theme:** Relationships, influence, and your story are trusts too.
- **Goal:** Expand stewardship beyond resources to people, reach, and witness.
- **Scripture Focus:** Luke 12:48; Matthew 5:16.
- **Opening Question:** Who has been entrusted to your care in this season — and how are you doing with that trust?
- **Discussion Questions:**
  1. "Much given, much required." What have you been given that you sometimes treat as a perk rather than a trust?
  2. Whatever reach or influence you have — at home, at work, online — who is it actually for?
  3. Matthew 5:16 says your life is always telling a story about God. What story is it telling this week?
  4. What part of your testimony have you kept quiet that someone may need to hear?
- **Application Step / Group Exercise:** Each person identifies one relationship to invest in and one specific way to let their light shine this week; the group commits to ask about it next time.

**Session 6 — Giving an Account**
- **Theme:** Living now in light of the day we give an account — and the joy of "well done."
- **Goal:** Integrate all five trusts and send each person out with one durable commitment.
- **Scripture Focus:** Romans 14:12; Colossians 3:23–24; Matthew 25:21.
- **Opening Question:** Over these six weeks, which trust has God most clearly put His finger on for you?
- **Discussion Questions:**
  1. How does the reality of one day "giving an account" change the way you want to live now — without fear, but with purpose?
  2. Colossians 3:23 says ordinary work becomes worship when done for the Lord. Which ordinary task could you start offering to Him?
  3. Looking across the five trusts, where have you grown most in this campaign — and where do you still feel the gap?
  4. What does "well done, good and faithful servant" stir in you, and what would it take to aim your life at those words?
- **Application Step / Group Exercise:** Each person names one specific stewardship commitment to carry past the campaign and the person in the group who will ask them about it in 30 and 90 days.

### 40-Day Devotional Outline

**Week 1 — Owner or Manager? (Days 1–7)**
- Day 1 — The Question Under Every Question — Before you can manage anything well, you have to answer who owns it; today you decide.
- Day 2 — The Weight of "Mine" — Why ownership quietly exhausts us, and how trusteeship lifts the load.
- Day 3 — The Earth Is the Lord's — Psalm 24:1 is the title deed: everything, everywhere, already belongs to God.
- Day 4 — On Loan — Reframing every possession, hour, and relationship as something entrusted, not owned.
- Day 5 — The Manager's Job Description — What God actually asks of a steward is faithfulness, not ownership.
- Day 6 — The Freedom of Not Owning — The unexpected relief that comes when you hand the title back to God.
- Day 7 — Naming What You Hold — A personal inventory of the five trusts God has placed in your hands.

**Week 2 — Stewarding Your Time (Days 8–14)**
- Day 8 — The Only Nonrenewable Resource — Time cannot be earned back, so stewardship begins by taking it seriously.
- Day 9 — Number Your Days — The wisdom Psalm 90 promises to those who face their own finitude.
- Day 10 — Where the Hours Go — An honest, unflinching audit of how your week is actually spent.
- Day 11 — Redeeming the Time — Making the most of every opportunity in a chronically distracted age.
- Day 12 — The Tyranny of the Urgent — Reclaiming time from what shouts to give it to what matters.
- Day 13 — Margin Is Stewardship — Why unhurried space is not laziness but faithful management of a limited trust.
- Day 14 — A Calendar Surrendered — Handing God your schedule as a genuine act of worship.

**Week 3 — Stewarding Your Talent (Days 15–21)**
- Day 15 — Gifted on Purpose — You were handed abilities specifically meant to serve others.
- Day 16 — The Gifts You Overlook — Naming the talents that come so naturally you forget they are gifts.
- Day 17 — Buried or Invested — The fear that hides our gifts, and the call to put them to work.
- Day 18 — Use It or Lose It — Stewardship of talent means deploying it, not hoarding or comparing it.
- Day 19 — The Body Needs Your Part — Your gift is a missing piece the community is waiting on.
- Day 20 — Developing the Deposit — Growing a gift is itself an act of faithful stewardship.
- Day 21 — Offered Back — Returning your talents to God as instruments of service, not trophies of self.

**Week 4 — Stewarding Your Treasure (Days 22–28)**
- Day 22 — Where Your Treasure Is — Your money is a map, and today you read where your heart actually lives.
- Day 23 — Faithful with Little — Jesus makes small-money faithfulness the training ground for true riches.
- Day 24 — Five Things You Can Do with Money — A steward's simple framework: earn, save, give, invest, owe.
- Day 25 — The First Portion — Honoring God off the top as a weekly declaration of who owns it all.
- Day 26 — Naming Enough — Deciding what "enough" is, so treasure serves you instead of ruling you.
- Day 27 — Generosity as Worship — Giving is the clearest evidence that money is not your master.
- Day 28 — A Budget Surrendered — Bringing your actual financial plan under God's actual ownership.

**Week 5 — Stewarding Trust & Testimony (Days 29–35)**
- Day 29 — Much Given, Much Required — Every privilege you hold is a trust, not a perk.
- Day 30 — The People in Your Care — Stewarding the relationships God has specifically entrusted to you.
- Day 31 — The Trust of Influence — Whatever reach you have was lent to you for the good of others.
- Day 32 — Your Life Is a Testimony — You are always, today, telling a story about God to someone.
- Day 33 — Stewarding Your Story — The God-given testimony you were never meant to keep to yourself.
- Day 34 — Reputation as a Resource — Guarding the trust other people have placed in you.
- Day 35 — Entrusted with the Gospel — The greatest trust of all, and what a faithful steward does with it.

**Week 6 — Giving an Account (Days 36–40)**
- Day 36 — The Day of Accounting — Living now, without fear but with purpose, in light of the report we will give.
- Day 37 — Well Done — The words every steward longs to hear, and how a life gets aimed at them.
- Day 38 — Faithful in Everything — How the most ordinary task becomes worship when done for the Owner.
- Day 39 — One Integrated Life — All five trusts, no longer compartments, managed for the One who gave them.
- Day 40 — Naming What's Next — Choosing the single stewardship commitment you will carry into the rest of your life.

### Recommended Next Steps After the Campaign
- **Make it a rhythm:** invite participants to keep a quarterly "five-trusts review" — a simple self-audit of time, talent, treasure, trust, and testimony.
- **Pathway to the next campaign:** Life Stewardship is the on-ramp; point groups toward *Entrusted* (Campaign 2) to deepen the identity, or *Living Open-Handed* (Campaign 10) to grow in generosity.
- **Serve as a group:** channel the talent-stewardship momentum into a shared serving commitment in the church or community.
- **Generosity step:** offer a clear, no-pressure next step for first-time or growing givers tied to Week 4.
- **Leader multiplication:** identify two or three participants to be trained as the next round of group leaders.
