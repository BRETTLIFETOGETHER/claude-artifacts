Fifty guided journeys across ten categories — ten days, twenty-one days, or a full month.

The same ten felt-need categories that organize the tool library organize this one. Three Lengths, Three Different Products

Ten days is a taste.
Thirty is a season.

10 Days

Entry · One finished piece

One felt need, one arc, one painting. Short enough that a woman who has never finished a devotional will finish this. Each day carries Scripture, a short devotional from John, reflection questions, a creative exercise, a journal prompt, and a prayer. She ends with a piece, a title for it, and a word for the season.

$19–$39 · 30 journeys in this library

21 Days

Habit · Three movements

Long enough for a practice to take hold. Three weeks in three movements — name it, work it, carry it — building toward one larger work or a set of three companion pieces. This is the natural length for a women's ministry group, a cohort, or a January launch, and it is where the daily video from Kim and John earns its keep.

$39–$79 · 14 journeys in this library

30 Days

Season · A body of work

A month is not a longer journal; it is a different product. Five movements, a real body of work at the end, and enough time for something to actually change. Sold as a cohort, licensed to churches for a season, and the only format that ends with something worth hanging in a show — or handing to a family as an heirloom.

$79–$149, or church license · 6 journeys

The honest math: 10 categories × 5 journeys = 50. Thirty are 10-day, fourteen are 21-day, six are 30-day. Four categories carry no 30-day journey on purpose — nobody in acute burnout, and no couple, is signing up for a month.

What To Build First

Write two.
Then let sales decide
the third.

Fifty journeys is a decade of writing, and it should be read as a map rather than a plan. What makes it valuable now is not the volume — it is that any conversation with a church, a partner, or a guest can end with a specific title rather than a vague promise. That is worth having on paper long before most of it is written.

Two journeys carry the first year: Paint Your Prayer and Created Free, both already named in earlier work. Write those completely, with the daily video, and sell them hard. The third should be chosen by what actually sells — and my expectation is that it will be My Word for This Season, because January is the one month of the year when people go looking for exactly that, and it is the shortest path from a free download to a paid experience.

One structural note worth deciding early: every 21-day and 30-day journey should be built from an existing 10-day rather than written from scratch. Same spine, deeper work, more movements. Otherwise this library becomes fifty unrelated writing projects instead of ten families with three depths each.

One Architecture, End To End

Need. Journey.
Experience. Piece.

She names what she's carrying. A journey meets her there for ten days, three weeks, or a month. Somewhere in it she decides she wants to do this in a real studio with real people. She books. And often, at the end, she asks whether you would paint something for her family.

Ten categories run the entire length of that path — the assessment, the tool library, the journey library, the experiences, and the commissions all use the same ten. Nothing has to be translated between stages, nothing gets orphaned, and a single conversation can move from a free download to a five-figure heirloom without ever changing vocabulary.
