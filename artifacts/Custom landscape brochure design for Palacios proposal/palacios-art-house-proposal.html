The Idea

Nobody is paying you
to paint.

They are paying for the drive out through the vineyards, the smell of oil and turpentine in a real studio, a table set with care, a host who remembers their name, and a question nobody has asked them in years. They are paying for two hours where their phone stays in their bag. They are paying to take home something they made with their own hands that means something — and to be able to point at it on the wall five years from now and tell the story.

That distinction changes everything about how this is priced, described, marketed, and sold. A painting class competes with every other painting class and loses on price. A private faith-and-art experience in wine country competes with nothing, because there isn't another one.

So the business is not art lessons. It is a small number of beautiful, deeply personal experiences, priced for what they actually deliver, with original artwork and commissions earning on the back end and digital resources carrying the story further than the two of you can drive.

Don't call it

Painting class · Craft night · Paint-and-sip · Art lessons · Christian art shop

Do call it

Private faith-and-art experience · Boutique creative retreat · Wine-country art and reflection · Live worship art · Legacy commission

The Unfair Advantage

This only works
because it's the two of you.

Kim

An original painter with a warm, expressive presence and real hospitality instinct. She paints Christian and nature-inspired work, she paints live during worship, and she can put a brush in the hand of a woman who swears she isn't creative and have her laughing within ten minutes. The painting is the craft. The permission she gives people is the product.

John

Thirty years a pastor, and a designer and illustrator besides. He can take a color, a symbol, a psalm, and a woman's hard year and tie them together in four minutes in a way she'll remember for a decade. He is the reason this is formation and not a craft table — and his thirty years of relationships are the distribution channel most new businesses spend a fortune trying to buy.

Together

An artist and a teacher, a host and a guide, married, on their own beautiful property, in a town people already drive to on purpose. Very few people can do the art. Fewer can do the theology. Almost nobody does both under one roof with a table set and a view out the window. That combination is the whole moat.

The rarest thing here isn't the painting. It's the pairing.

The Place

McMinnville is already
a destination. You don't
have to create demand.

Oregon wine country runs on a very specific kind of traveler: women on a road trip, couples on an anniversary, sisters at a milestone birthday, mothers and daughters, small groups of friends who booked the boutique hotel months ago. They arrive with money set aside, two or three days to fill, and a quiet worry that the whole weekend will end up being four tasting rooms in a row.

They are looking for the one thing they'll talk about on the drive home. Right now, in this town, nobody is selling it to them. That is the opening — and it closes the moment somebody else notices.

Start at home. Your property, your studio, your table, your light. More control, more margin, more intimacy, better atmosphere, no venue rules, and it feels premium because it is personal. The wineries, hotels, and Airbnb hosts don't need to host you. They need to send people to you.

Where The Real Money Is

Experiences fill the calendar.
Commissions build the business.

A twelve-guest event is a wonderful day and roughly two thousand dollars. A single family legacy commission is five to twenty-five thousand, takes place largely in the studio, and comes from a relationship that already exists. The experiences are not the destination — they are the introduction. Every retreat guest, every live art weekend, every donor family gathering is a conversation that can end with an original.

So build the commission conversation into the experience itself. A closing question — is there a place, a season, a person, or a promise you'd want painted? — costs nothing and changes the economics entirely.

This is also where the two of you are most differentiated. Anyone can sell a print. Almost no one can sit with a family, listen for the story underneath the story, and give it back to them in oil.

How People Find You

Borrow the audiences
that already trust someone.

You do not have an audience yet, and you should not spend a year building one from scratch. Other people already have the exact women you want: the boutique hotel with the concierge, the Airbnb host answering "what should we do Saturday?", the winery whose guests are looking for a second activity, the women's ministry director planning next spring's retreat, the counselor with a client in a hard season.

Give each of them one beautiful PDF and a simple promise: we host private art-and-reflection experiences for groups and visitors who want something more meaningful than another tasting room. Ten good referral partners are worth more than ten thousand impressions.

And warm always beats cold. Past art buyers, people who came to Kim's shows, thirty years of church relationships, friends of friends — that list is long and it is already warm. It just needs someone to work it.

Lindsey — Experience Concierge

Warm calls, referral asks, inquiry follow-up, scheduling, confirmations, partner outreach, testimonial collection. Her first sixty days: 100 warm contacts, 20 referral conversations, 10 serious inquiries, 3 paid bookings, 5 partner relationships.
