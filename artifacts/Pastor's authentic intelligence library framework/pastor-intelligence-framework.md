# Pastor Intelligence
### Not Artificial. Actual.

**Artificial Intelligence starts with a prompt.**
**Pastor Intelligence starts with a pastor.**

---

## The Core Conviction

AI can imitate a pastor's cadence. Given enough transcripts, it can mimic vocabulary, sentence rhythm, even favorite phrases. But imitation is not intelligence — it's a costume.

Real ministry content carries something imitation can't manufacture: an actual pastor, an actual conviction, and an actual body of teaching that has already borne fruit in a real congregation. Our commitment is to lean into AI-enabled ministry content aggressively — but responsibly. Innovation without integrity is a shortcut. Integrity without innovation is a missed opportunity. We want both.

That means every campaign, every curriculum, every class starts with **Pastor Intelligence** — the purpose, passion, and proven wisdom that make one pastor's ministry unmistakably theirs — and only then uses AI to organize, extend, and multiply it.

**Not artificial content. Actual pastoral wisdom, multiplied.**

Depending on the room, you need different amounts of detail. All three below describe the same underlying idea — pick the one that fits the conversation:

## Quick Pitch: The 5 PI Pillars

For a 30-second version, skip the alliteration entirely and use the process in five moves:

1. **Pastoral Intelligence** — your voice, theology, burden, and life message.
2. **Personal Intuition** — your sense of your people, timing, tone, and needs.
3. **Proven Instruction** — the teaching that has already borne fruit.
4. **Practical Implementation** — the next steps, curriculum, and campaigns that help people live it.
5. **Program Integration** — the launch plan that puts it into the life of the church.

**One-liner:** *Pastor Intelligence turns your proven pastoral wisdom into practical ministry resources your church can actually use.*

## Framework Two: The 12P Lens
### For Real Discovery

This is the working framework for an actual discovery session. It sits between two things that aren't themselves one of the 12:

- **Beneath it:** the *Preaching Library* — the raw material. Every one of the 12P categories gets discovered by mining what the pastor has already taught, not by inventing new answers in the room.
- **Above it:** *Possibilities* — the output. Once the 12 are understood, the question becomes what they can become: books, devotionals, campaigns, curriculum, leader training, family editions.

**The 12:**

1. **Purpose** — why the church exists.
2. **Passion** — what burden keeps returning.
3. **Personal Vision** — the future this pastor sees before others see it.
4. **Philosophy** — how this church believes ministry and discipleship work.
5. **Paradigm** — the lens through which this pastor sees church, people, and mission.
6. **Point of View** — what keeps this pastor's message from sounding generic.
7. **Pathway** — how people take next steps, from attending to leading and going.
8. **People** — who this church is called to reach and disciple.
9. **Place** — the local geography, culture, and community this church is planted in.
10. **Pain** — the real needs people are actually carrying.
11. **Priorities** — what this pastor chooses when everything matters.
12. **Passages** — the Scriptures that anchor the message.

*Core line: AI knows audiences and topics in general. Pastor Intelligence knows this pastor, this church, and these people.*

## The Full Reference List
### For Deep Research Only — Not a Pitch Tool

Across both discovery passes, these are every distinct facet that's come up. Most discovery work only needs the 12P above — but if a project calls for a specific angle, these extras are available:

- **Personal Values** — the stated convictions a pastor protects and repeats (distinct from Priorities, which is what actually gets chosen under pressure).
- **Personality** — the pastor's natural communication style: teacher, shepherd, storyteller, strategist, prophet, coach.
- **Practices** — the specific behaviors the church wants to normalize (attend, join a group, give, invite, serve).
- **Patterns** — what has repeatedly worked: recurring themes, strongest campaigns, most-repeated phrases.
- **Proof** — the testimonies and stories that show the mission is actually working.

Use these when a project specifically calls for a voice guide (Personality), a discipleship-culture initiative (Practices), or a case-study/testimony library (Patterns, Proof) — not as a default addition to the 12P.

## Framework Three: The 7 A's
### How Pastor Intelligence Gets Built

The Lens tells you *what* to capture. The 7 A's govern *how* it gets turned into content responsibly.

**1. Authentic — It Starts With the Pastor's Voice**
Not a persona. Not a style transfer. The raw material is the pastor's own words — preached, taught, written, recorded. AI's job is to organize that voice, never invent it.

**2. Actual — A Real Library, Not a Simulated One**
Every insight traces back to something the pastor and the church actually taught. If it didn't happen in real ministry, it doesn't belong in the library.

**3. Aggregated — Collected and Curated**
Years of sermons, series, classes, devotionals, and talks gathered into one organized library — instead of scattered across drives, transcripts, and memory.

**4. Aligned — Connected Across the Whole Church**
One library, many uses: campaigns, curriculum, classes, and the broader content engine all draw from the same source instead of every team rebuilding from scratch.

**5. Analyzed — Reviewed by the Pastor and Team**
Before anything becomes a product, the pastor and senior team assess what's ready, what needs refining, and where it belongs. Human judgment stays in the loop at the point that matters most.

**6. Applied — Integrated Into the Calendar, AI as Advisor Not Author**
Pastor Intelligence recommendations feed the planning calendar as *suggestions*. AI proposes a next series, a curriculum angle, a campaign theme — the team is never obligated to take it. AI recommends; it never decides.

**7. Approved — Confirmed by the Pastor and Senior Team**
Nothing ships without sign-off from the people accountable for the pulpit and the church's witness.

*Core line: The 7 A's are the guardrails that let us move fast without moving carelessly.*

## Discovery Questions

Organized by category. Ask these directly and record answers close to verbatim — the pastor's own phrasing is usually more valuable than any summary of it.

**Purpose**
- Why does your church exist?
- What would be missing in your community if your church did not exist?
- What mission language do your people already know?

**Passion**
- What topics do you keep returning to?
- What burden has God placed on your life?
- What message do people most often thank you for?

**Personal Vision**
- What future do you see for your church that your people may not fully see yet?
- What do you believe God is calling your church to become in the next 3–5 years?
- What would be different if your church fully lived its mission?

**Philosophy**
- How do you believe people grow spiritually?
- What role do groups play? What role does Sunday play? What role do families play?
- What ministry methods have you moved away from, and why?

**Paradigm**
- Do you see your church primarily as a family, movement, hospital, training center, mission base, school, or spiritual home?
- What metaphor best describes your church?

**Point of View**
- What do you see differently than other pastors?
- What phrases, frameworks, or metaphors do you use repeatedly?
- What makes your teaching sound unmistakably like you?

**Pathway**
- What is the clear next step for a first-time guest?
- What is the pathway from attender to member? From member to disciple? From disciple to leader?

**People**
- Who are your people, and who are you called to reach?
- What are they carrying? What do they need most right now?

**Place**
- Where has God planted this church — geographically and culturally?
- What does your community need that a church in a different context wouldn't?
- How does your local culture shape the way you teach and apply Scripture?

**Pain**
- What are people in your church actually struggling with right now?
- What need shows up again and again in counseling conversations, prayer requests, or care meetings?
- What pain would you be doing people a disservice to ignore in this next series?

**Priorities**
- What do you say yes to most often? What do you say no to most often?
- What gets funded first, and what gets staff attention first?
- What tradeoffs reveal your real priorities?

**Passages**
- What Scriptures keep shaping this message?
- What passage best captures your burden right now?
- What Scripture do you want people to walk away memorizing?

**Preaching Library** *(the source)*
- What are your strongest sermon series, and what made them land?
- What themes keep showing up across the years, unprompted?
- What sermons should not be allowed to disappear into the archive?

**Possibilities** *(the output)*
- What sermon series could become a devotional?
- What message could become a 7-Day Experience or a 40-Day Campaign?
- What teaching could become leader training?
- What message should outlive this season?
