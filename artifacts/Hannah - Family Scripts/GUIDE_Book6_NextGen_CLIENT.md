# A WELCOME FROM TOM AND BRETT

This is the last book in the series and it is the one everything else has been aimed at.

Because the future of your family is not in your documents. It is in the people who will be holding them, and whether anybody prepared them.

**Here is what we most want to say before you begin.**

Tom has a conviction about this generation that is not the common view.

He has no doubt the next generation will have a greater impact than the one before it. People talk about the wonderful baby boomers, and that is because they have not seen the next one coming along. The children those boomers raised.

In many cases these young people care a great deal more about people than their parents did. Their parents are more transactional. The children are more relational.

That is not what you usually hear, and after thirty years of sitting with families across three and four generations, it is what Tom has actually observed.

**And one more thing.**

**Most families never do this.**

Not the money part. Most families handle the money. What they do not do is prepare anybody, because preparation has no deadline, produces no visible result, and nobody sends a reminder.

So if you are opening this and feeling behind, you are not.

You do not have to do it perfectly. You do not have to do it with everyone. And whatever your children's ages are, it is not too late, because the thing being transmitted right now is still moving.

It appears throughout this book, and in most families reading it the children are grown.

That is not carelessness. The relationship does not stop being what it is. But one of the things this book will keep asking is whether you are still treating an adult like one.

# From Heirs to Stewards

## From Receiving to Being Responsible

**HOST NOTE.** If a member of the next generation is in the room, do not look at them while reading the section about readiness. This session is about the older generation's decisions, not the younger one's performance.

## INTRODUCTION

Here is the question parents bring to this book, and most of them have never said it out loud.

**Are they ready?**

And underneath it, a harder one. If they are not, whose job was that.

**KEY VERSE**

"It is required of stewards that one be found trustworthy."

**1 CORINTHIANS 4:2**

An heir receives. A steward is responsible.

Those are two different words for two different relationships to the same thing, and Scripture is not neutral between them. It does not call us heirs. It calls us stewards.

Now think about what most families actually prepare their children to be.

We prepare them to receive. Enormous effort goes into engineering the arrival. And then we hand it to a person who has been positioned their whole life as the endpoint of the transfer rather than the next one responsible for it.

That is not their failure. It is what they were trained for.

## THE ONE QUESTION

>

## OPENING STORY

**It's the Father Who Isn't Ready**

Tom worked with a family where the son had been in the business for years. He was capable. He had proven himself by every measure anyone could name.

And the father kept saying, not yet. He is not ready.

The son was close to walking away. Not in anger. He had simply concluded it was never going to happen and that he should build something of his own before it was too late.

When it finally moved, what became clear was this. It was not that the boy was not ready.

It was that the father was not ready.

He was not selfish. He was frightened, and he had good reasons, and none of them had anything to do with his son's competence. He was frightened of what he would be afterward.

Nobody says that out loud. So the sentence that comes out is *he is not ready yet*, and that sentence can be said indefinitely and always sounds responsible.

## THE FRAMEWORK

### Heir and Steward

- **An heir is defined by what arrives.** The word describes a position in a sequence. Nothing is required of you to hold the title. It is passive by definition.

- **A steward is defined by what is entrusted.** Somebody handed you something that belongs to someone else and expects an account of what you did with it.

- **Responsibility can be given early. Receiving cannot.** You cannot give somebody their inheritance at twenty-two to see how it goes. You can give them responsibility at twenty-two, in a size that fits, and watch.

- **And trustworthiness develops through being trusted,** which means the first extension of trust always precedes the evidence for it. That first extension is yours to make and nobody else can make it.

**Brett's phrase is the one to hold. Not adult children. Growing adults, with the emphasis on growing.**

## WHERE YOU ARE STARTING

**If your children are young.** You have the easiest version. Responsibility given at eight is a completely different experience than responsibility given at thirty-eight, and the habits set now are the ones that scale.

**If your children are grown and capable.** Your risk is not their readiness. It is that the role assignment made twenty years ago has never been revisited, and they can tell.

**If you are genuinely worried about somebody.** That worry is information rather than a verdict. Name what specifically would have to be true for the answer to become yes. If you cannot name three things, the obstacle is somewhere other than their readiness.

## THE TOOL

### The Permission Conversation

The conversation most families have never had, and it goes in one direction people do not expect.

You are not asking permission from them. You are giving it.

Sit down with one person in the next generation and say three things.

**Here is something I would like you to take responsibility for.** Specific, real, with a consequence if it goes badly.

**Here is what I will not do.** Name what you are not going to step in and fix. This is the part that makes it real, and it is the part families skip.

**And here is what I would need to know.** Not oversight. What would tell you it is going well or badly, agreed in advance so it is not experienced as checking up.

Then do the hardest part, which is not intervening the first time it wobbles.

# Passing On Faith and Values

## What Does Not Transfer Automatically

**HOST NOTE.** If some people in the room hold a different faith or none, say at the start that they are welcome and that the session's core question works for anybody: what does your family believe, and has anyone said it out loud.

## INTRODUCTION

Here is the question in this session.

**Will they keep any of this?**

Not the money. The rest of it. The faith, the values, the way of seeing things that took you a lifetime to arrive at.

**KEY VERSE**

"These words shall be on your heart. You shall teach them diligently to your sons and shall talk of them when you sit in your house and when you walk by the way."

**DEUTERONOMY 6:6-7**

Tom says legacy coaching is very simple. You need to love Jesus. You need to teach your kids to love Jesus. And hope they teach their kids to love Jesus.

That is it. And if that happens, they will probably be good stewards, because they will know God's principles and seek to live by them.

Here is what makes this more than a pious sentiment. Money is the only part of this that transfers without any effort on your part. Everything else requires you. Values do not transfer through a will. Faith has no custodian.

Notice the mechanism in the verse. When you sit in your house and when you walk by the way. That is not a curriculum. It is ordinary life with something running through it.

## THE ONE QUESTION

> **What do your children believe you believe, and where did they get that?**
>
> Answer that one and the session worked.

## OPENING STORY

**The Worldview Class**

Tom took a worldview class alongside one of his sons-in-law.

Everybody in the room had the right answers. The material was good, the group was serious, and the doctrine was solid.

And the practical part was missing.

What struck Tom was how easy it is to know this in your head. The question is whether we are living it out. He has read a great many books about raising children and has not found one that talks about the importance of teaching children to serve other people.

He thinks it is critical.

When his children were growing up, they simply took them along. You are going to serve some people, you take your kids. They are playing while you are working, but they know what you are there for.

Nobody sat anybody down. They just went.

## THE FRAMEWORK

### What Does Not Transfer Automatically

Four things, and families do the first well and the rest inconsistently.

- **Modeling.** The most important and the one that happens whether you intend it. Your children see the version of you that nobody else sees, and that version is doing the teaching.

- **Telling.** Modeling alone is incomplete. They see what you do and do not automatically know why, so they copy the behavior and miss the reason. A behavior without a reason does not survive a hard year.

- **Serving.** Almost nothing written about raising children covers this. You take them with you. They are playing while you are working, and they know what you are there for.

- **Stories.** Not lessons. Decisions that shaped your life, told plainly. That is where the transfer actually begins.

**Modeling without telling produces children who inherit a pattern without the conviction underneath it.**

## WHERE YOU ARE STARTING

**If your family has been faithful and never explicit.** Twenty years of church attendance and nobody has ever said, in their own words, at their own table, why any of it matters to them personally. That is the most common gap in this session and it takes one conversation.

**If your children have drifted.** Naming that is more useful than working around it. And the third legacy is still transmitting, which means what you live this year still reaches them, whether or not they are currently listening.

**If your household is divided on this.** Do not perform agreement you do not have. Children detect it immediately and it undermines whatever is genuine.

## THE TOOL

### The Faith Story

One story. Told out loud, to your family, once.

Not a lesson and not a testimony in the formal sense. A decision that shaped your faith, with the actual circumstances in it.

Three things make it work.

**It has a specific moment.** Not a season. A day, a conversation, a decision you can locate.

**It includes what it cost.** Stories where everything worked out and nothing was risked do not transfer. The cost is what makes it credible.

**And it names what you were afraid of.** That is the part that makes a young person able to imagine themselves in it.

Then ask somebody older than you for theirs. Most people have never been asked and would give almost anything to be.

# SESSION 3

# Building Trust Across Generations

## Why Assumptions Fill the Silence

**HOST NOTE.** This is the session where somebody may say something that has been sitting for years. Make sure they finish the sentence. Do not fix it in the room and do not let anyone else fix it either.

## INTRODUCTION

Here is the question in this one, and parents ask it more often than they admit.

**Why don't they tell me things?**

Not what are they hiding. Why has the flow of information thinned, and when did that start.

**KEY VERSE**

"With all humility and gentleness, with patience, showing tolerance for one another in love."

**EPHESIANS 4:2**

The reason legacy does not move from one generation to the next is a lack of communication and trust. Sixty percent of the time.

And the two are connected. The only way you build trust is by communicating. Trust is not a feeling that develops on its own between people who love each other. It is a conclusion people draw from repeated evidence, and the evidence is being told things.

When communication is missing, something takes its place. A friend of Tom's used to call them vain imaginations. People fill the gaps with their own interpretation, and the interpretation hardens into a fact.

## THE ONE QUESTION

> **Is there anyone in your family you have something unresolved with?**
>
> Answer that one and the session worked.

## OPENING STORY

**I Didn't Know You Really Cared About My Opinion**

A family Tom worked with had a member who had been away for years. He had drifted, there was history, and he came for one hour as a favor.

He stayed two days.

At the end of it he said something Tom has never forgotten. He said, I did not know you really cared about my opinion.

Nobody had ever asked him. Not once. And the absence of asking had been read, correctly, as an absence of interest, so he had removed himself, and the family had interpreted the removal as disinterest on his part.

Both were wrong. Neither would have found out without somebody asking a question.

## THE FRAMEWORK

### How Trust Is Built and Rebuilt

- **Trust is not a status. It is a running total.** Each small thing handled well adds to it. Each small thing handled badly subtracts. And it accumulates in both directions between every pair of people in a family, continuously.

- **Which means it can be built deliberately,** and the mechanism is small and boring. Give somebody something to handle. Let them handle it. Notice that they did. Families skip the third step, which is the one that registers.

- **And it can be rebuilt after it falls.** Trust gets built, it falls down, and it has to be rebuilt. Over and over again. Families that do not understand this treat a breach as permanent.

- **Rebuilding is not a conversation.** It is a series of small things over a period of time, and the conversation may not be necessary at all.

**Somebody has to extend the first trust before the evidence exists. That extension is what a parent does here.**

## WHERE YOU ARE STARTING

**If your family communicates well.** Your work is the quiet person rather than the flow. In most families somebody has less information than the others and has never mentioned it.

**If somebody has gone quiet.** Something cost them once and they concluded honesty was not worth it. Reopening it takes repeated evidence rather than one conversation.

**If there is a real breach.** Do not try to fix it in a session. Find out first whether you are the only one who sees it, and whether the other person would say the same thing about what happened.

## THE TOOL

### Is There Anyone?

One question, and it does more than anything else on this list.

**Is there anyone in your family that you have an issue with?**

Tom asks it early because he wants to know before he ends up sitting across from that person. Brett tried it in his own family and somebody looked at him and said, yeah. My mom.

And then you are in it, which is exactly the point.

Three rules for asking it.

**Ask it of one person at a time,** never the room.

**Do not defend anybody,** including yourself, and especially not the person named.

**And do not try to resolve it in that conversation.** Asking does not resolve anything. It makes the thing sayable, which is the necessary first step and is frequently mistaken for the whole job.

If you ask and then defend, you have made it worse than not asking. The person has taken a risk and learned the risk does not pay.

## THE FRAMEWORK

### Stewardship Is Management

- **The definition first.** Stewardship is managing everything you have been entrusted with. Money, time, relationships, health, influence. Giving is one thing a steward does.

- **The three jars.** Give, save, spend. Pay God first, pay yourself second, live on the rest. Simple enough for a child and it is the order rather than the percentages that does the work.

- **The five uses.** There are only five things you can do with money. Live on it, give it away, pay taxes, repay debt, save and invest. Every dollar goes into exactly one, which means increasing any one requires decreasing another.

- **And co-ownership.** Not doing everything, which produces dependence. Not withholding everything, which is neither loving nor necessary. They carry something real and so do you.

**The more you do for them, the more you take away from them. What you are removing is not comfort. It is competence, and competence only develops through the friction you are relieving.**

## THE TOOL

### The Three Jars, at Any Age

The instrument is simple and it scales from a child's allowance to a family's year.

**Give.** A portion off the top, decided before anything else.

**Save.** The next portion, also decided in advance.

**Spend.** Whatever remains.

The order is the whole point. If giving comes last, it comes from what is left, and what is left absorbs every other decision. In a good year it grows. In a tight year it disappears. Over time the pattern teaches that giving is optional in a way lifestyle is not.

For a family with grown children, run the five uses instead. At the beginning of the year decide roughly what proportion goes to each. Then look at it together in six months.

Somebody will say, well, I missed that one, didn't I. And that is fine. There is no shame in it. The point is to know.
