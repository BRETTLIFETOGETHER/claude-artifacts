# Day 6

## Put Them All on the Table

### Let's see how they fit together

**"Plans fail when there is no counsel, but with many advisers they succeed."** PROVERBS 15:22

Last session was about knowing what you have. This one asks a harder question. Does any of it work together?

The instruction I give is simple and families find it surprisingly difficult. Put them all on the table. Let us see how they fit together.

Not one account at a time. All of it, at once, in front of the same set of eyes.

Here is why that is difficult. Families do not build in one sitting. They build over decades, and each decision was made in its own moment for its own reason, usually with a different person advising and a different set of circumstances in view.

The retirement account was set up at a job you left in 2004. The policy was bought when the children were small. The property came through an inheritance and nobody has thought about it since. The account at the second institution exists because of something that happened during a transition and it was never consolidated.

Every one of those decisions was reasonable. Not one of them was made with the others in view, because the others did not all exist yet.

So what you end up with is not a plan. It is a sediment layer of good decisions, and nobody has ever looked at the whole column.

I have sat with families where three different money managers were running three portions of the money and all three were using essentially the same strategy. They could have had one person put a third in each place and gotten the identical result for a fraction of the complexity and cost.

Nobody did anything wrong. Nobody was even inefficient in isolation. It was only visible from above.

The proverb says plans fail without counsel and succeed with many advisers. The word many is doing something specific. It is not saying get one very good adviser. It is describing a picture assembled from multiple vantage points.

But there is an unstated requirement in that verse. The advisers have to be looking at the same thing.

Many advisers each seeing one piece is not counsel. It is fragmentation with a professional finish.

So the assignment for this session begins with a table and everything on it.

**4.** What would be visible from above that is invisible from inside?

# Day 7

## All the Money Managers Use the Same Strategy

### They could have had just one person

**"See to it that no one takes you captive through philosophy and empty deception."** COLOSSIANS 2:8

Here is the specific version of fragmentation that surprises families most.

You hire three different money managers, thinking you have diversified. And all three of them are using the same strategy.

You have not diversified. You have paid three times for one approach and added two sets of statements to your life.

Now I want to be careful, because the mistake underneath this is not stupidity and it is not greed on anybody's part. It is a reasonable instinct applied without a view of the whole.

The instinct is to not put everything with one person. That is sensible. Families have been burned by concentration, and spreading things out feels like prudence.

But spreading across people is not the same as spreading across strategies, and nobody in the arrangement is positioned to notice. Each manager sees their own portion and is doing a competent job with it. None of them sees the other two.

So the family experiences the appearance of diversification and holds the reality of concentration, and the only person who could see it is the family, and the family has never put it on one table.

There is a second version worth naming. Sometimes the three managers are doing genuinely different things, and the different things are working against each other. One portion is growing aggressively. One is generating income. One is minimizing taxes. Each objective is legitimate and the three of them together produce a result nobody designed.

Paul warns about being taken captive through philosophy and empty deception. He is writing about something larger than money, and the mechanism transfers. A framework can be perfectly coherent inside itself and still lead you somewhere you did not intend, and it happens most easily when nobody is examining the framework itself.

The examination is the work. Not distrust of the people helping you. Just the plain question of whether the pieces, together, are doing what you actually want.

Most families have never asked it because they assumed somebody else had.

### Reflect

**1.** How many people manage portions of what your family has?

**2.** Do any of them know about the others?

**3.** Are you diversified across strategies or only across people?

**4.** What is each portion actually trying to do?

# Day 8

## A Life Insurance Policy Doesn't Go Through a Will

### You don't see that until it's too late

**"A prudent person sees evil and hides himself, but the naive proceed, and pay the penalty."** PROVERBS 27:12

Here is a fact that surprises more families than almost anything else in this book.

A life insurance policy does not go through a will. Neither does a retirement account.

Those assets go wherever the beneficiary designation says they go, and the beneficiary designation is a form somebody filled out at some point and probably has not looked at since.

Which means a family can have a carefully drafted, expensive, thoroughly considered will that governs a much smaller portion of their estate than they believe.

I want to be plain about the size of this. In a lot of families, the assets that pass outside the will are the majority of what will actually transfer. Retirement accounts, policies, anything held jointly, anything with a named beneficiary. The will handles what is left after all of that moves on its own.

And people do not see it until it is too late, because nothing in ordinary life prompts a review. Nobody sends a notice saying your family has changed, please come back in.

So the form filled out in 1997 is still governing, and it names somebody appropriate to 1997.

I have seen an ex-spouse on a form. I have seen a parent who died years ago. I have seen children listed in a way that made sense before one of them developed a situation that made an outright transfer genuinely unwise.

None of that is negligence. It is what happens when a document that never expires meets a family that keeps changing.

The proverb says the prudent person sees it coming and takes cover while the naive walk on and pay. The distinction is not intelligence. It is whether somebody looked.

Here is what makes this the most fixable problem in the entire series. Checking every beneficiary designation you have takes about one afternoon. It costs nothing. It requires no professional and no decision, only a review.

And I have watched that one afternoon change what happens to an entire family.

### Reflect

**1.** When did you last look at a beneficiary designation?

**2.** Who is named, and would you name them today?

**3.** What portion of what you have passes outside your will?

**4.** What would happen if the forms were followed exactly as written?

# Day 9

## Why Is It There?

### A purpose within the larger picture

**"For which one of you, when he wants to build a tower, does not first sit down and calculate the cost, to see if he has enough to complete it?"** LUKE 14:28

Once everything is on the table, there is a question worth asking of every single item.

Why is it there?

Not what is it worth. Not how is it performing. Why does this exist, and what job is it doing inside the larger picture?

Most families have never asked that about most of what they hold, and the answers, when they come, fall into a few categories.

Some things are there for a reason that still applies. Good. That is the whole point and there is nothing to do.

Some things are there for a reason that expired. The policy protects against something that is no longer a risk. The account was for a goal that has been reached or abandoned. The property served a purpose in a season that has ended.

Some things are there because somebody sold them, and the sale was more convincing than the need.

And some things are there for no discoverable reason at all. They came from somewhere, they have been maintained, and nobody could tell you what job they are doing.

I am not suggesting you liquidate anything. The point of the question is not efficiency. It is that a family who can say why each piece exists has moved from holding a collection to running a system, and only one of those can be handed to somebody else.

Think about what happens when a family cannot answer it. Somebody eventually inherits a set of holdings with no explanation attached, and their only options are to keep everything indefinitely or to make changes without knowing what they are undoing.

Both of those are bad, and both are avoidable by a family that wrote down why.

Jesus describes a man who wants to build a tower and sits down first to calculate whether he has enough to finish it. It is usually read as a warning about ambition, and there is a quieter thing in it. He is checking whether the resources he has actually serve the thing he intends.

That is the question. Not do I have enough. Does what I have serve what I am building.

### Reflect

**2.** Which of those reasons still applies?

**3.** What is there because somebody sold it?

**4.** What could you not explain to somebody who inherited it?

# Day 10

## Who Owns What

### Different assets pass in different ways

**"By wisdom a house is built, and by understanding it is established."** PROVERBS 24:3

This is the last day of the second letter, and it is the one that carries the most weight.

Different assets pass in different ways. And which way an asset passes depends almost entirely on who owns it.

Something held individually moves one way. Something held jointly moves another. Something in a trust moves a third way. Something inside a business entity moves a fourth. Something with a beneficiary named moves before any of those and does not wait for a will.

Families assume that ownership is a technicality. It is not a technicality. It is the single largest determinant of what actually happens.

I have sat with parents who were completely clear about their intentions and completely wrong about the outcome, because the ownership structure was going to override what they had described to me twenty minutes earlier. Not through anybody's error. The structure simply predates the intention and nobody reconciled the two.

So here is the exercise, and it is two facts per item.

Who owns it. And where does it go.

That is all. Not the value, not the performance, not the strategy. Two facts, written down, for every significant thing your family holds.

Most families cannot complete that table on the first attempt, and the incomplete rows are the most useful output of this entire session.

Now, a word to whoever is doing this alone. You may look at that table and conclude you need professional help, and for some of it you will. But do not let that stop you from filling in what you can. There is a difference between needing help to act and needing help to understand, and the professions have not always been careful about the distinction.

You are allowed to understand your own situation. You are responsible for it, and nobody else can carry that.

The proverb says a house is built by wisdom and established by understanding. Building and establishing are two different verbs, and most families have done the first.

That is the letter A. Next is the M, and it is the one Tom cares about most.

## It Will Not Happen

### If it's not written down and codified

**"Record the vision and inscribe it on tablets, so that the one who reads it may run."** HABAKKUK 2:2

This is the letter Tom is most passionate about, and it comes down to one sentence.

If it is not written down and codified, it will not happen.

Not it might not happen. It will not.

That is a harder claim than families expect and it holds up. Whatever you have decided, whatever you have explained to your spouse, whatever everybody in your family understands to be your wish, has exactly no force unless it exists in a document.

I have watched families discover this. The intention was clear. Everyone knew it. Several people could describe it. And when the moment came, none of that mattered, because the thing that governs is the paper.

There is a particular version that hurts. A parent tells one child something in a conversation, meaning it entirely, and never records it. That child arrives at the moment expecting what they were told, and the document says something else, and now the family has a conflict that is not about money. It is about whether somebody was lied to, and nobody was, and the misunderstanding is unfixable because the only person who could resolve it is gone.

So the instruction is unglamorous. Write it down. Then have it drafted properly so it holds.

Now, some people do not have documents at all, and I want to say something to them without any scolding attached.

My son is a lawyer. It took me three years of badgering him before he made his will, and he had three children.

Knowing better has almost nothing to do with it. The obstacle is not information and it is not usually money. It is that the task has no deadline, produces no visible benefit, and requires an afternoon of thinking about your own death.

That is a genuinely unappealing set of properties, and it explains the delay far better than negligence does.

Habakkuk is told to record the vision and inscribe it, and the reason given is that the one who reads it may run. Somebody else, later, acting without you present.

That is what a document is for. Not preservation. Enabling somebody to move when you cannot.

# Day 12

## That Might Be Fifty Percent Correct

### The three myths of estate planning

**"Teach us to number our days, that we may present to You a heart of wisdom."** PSALM 90:12

There are three myths I hear constantly, and I want to name all three.

**The first myth is, I am never going to die.**

Nobody says that out loud. Everybody plans as though it were true, which is the same thing operationally. The plan assumes a runway that has not been measured.

**The second myth is, I have the highest priced attorneys in town, so my estate must be in good shape.**

I tell people that might be fifty percent correct. You may well have the highest priced attorneys in town. That is no guarantee at all that your estate is in good shape.

A document is a snapshot of what you told somebody at a moment. If your situation changed and you did not go back, the quality of the drafting is irrelevant. The best attorney in the state cannot draft around information you did not give them in 2011.

**The third myth is, it is my kids' money.**

And I say, God did not give it to your kids. He gave it to you.

That one lands hardest, and it deserves a careful reading, because it is not an argument for keeping everything. It is an argument about responsibility.

If it was given to you, then you are the one accountable for what happens to it, including whether the people receiving it are prepared. You cannot outsource that to the next generation by treating the transfer as a foregone conclusion.

Families that hold the third myth tend to make one particular mistake. They treat the plan as an administrative matter about how to move something that already belongs to somebody else, rather than as a stewardship question about what will actually serve the people they love.

Those two framings produce different plans.

The psalm asks God to teach us to number our days so that we may present a heart of wisdom. It is the answer to the first myth, and notice the outcome. Not urgency. Wisdom.

Numbering the days is not morbid. It is the precondition for planning that is connected to reality.

# Day 13

## God Didn't Give It to Your Kids

### Make sure they're prepared

**"It is required of stewards that one be found trustworthy."** 1 CORINTHIANS 4:2

I want to spend a whole day on the third myth, because it does more damage than the other two combined.

God did not give it to your kids. He gave it to you.

The reason this matters is not about entitlement. It is about who is responsible for preparation.

If it is already theirs and you are simply holding it temporarily, then your job is administrative. Move it efficiently, minimize the friction, and the outcome is their business.

If it was given to you, then you are accountable for the transfer itself, which means you are accountable for whether the people receiving it are ready.

Those are radically different jobs, and only one of them involves any work with people.

So if you are going to pass something on to your children, you need to make sure they are prepared to handle it. That is your responsibility, not theirs. They cannot prepare themselves for something they have not been told about, and they cannot practice with something they have never been trusted with.

Now, families sometimes hear this as a reason to withhold, and that is not what it means either. Preparation is not a test somebody has to pass before they qualify. It is something you build in them, deliberately, over years.

And it looks like ordinary things. Giving somebody a real responsibility with a real consequence. Letting them make a decision and handle the result. Telling them what exists before it arrives. Answering questions honestly rather than deflecting until later.

None of that requires a large amount of money. It requires the willingness to let somebody handle something before you are certain they will handle it well.

Paul says it is required of stewards that one be found trustworthy. The word required is worth noticing. Not preferred, not ideal. Required.

And trustworthiness is a thing that develops through being trusted, which is a slightly uncomfortable loop, because it means the first trust has to be extended before the trustworthiness has been demonstrated.

That first extension is your job. Nobody else can do it.

# Day 14

## Who's Running It When You're Not There

### Somebody has to be in charge

**"Select out of all the people able men who fear God, men of truth, those who hate dishonest gain, and you shall place these over them."** EXODUS 18:21

There is a question inside the documents session that is not really about documents.

If you were in a car wreck tomorrow and in the hospital for a month, who is running the business?

Or if there is no business, who is making the decisions? Who signs? Who do people call on day three?

Somebody has to be in charge. And if it is not clear, the disruption starts immediately and it compounds.

I want to distinguish this from succession, because families collapse the two and then only solve the further one.

Succession is who leads it next, eventually, permanently. It is philosophical, emotional, and years away.

Continuity is thirty days from tomorrow. It is administrative, unemotional, and applies immediately.

Almost every family has thought about the first and written down nothing about the second, which is precisely backwards in terms of probability.

I watched a man go into the hospital for a month with nothing arranged. Nothing had needed to be arranged before. And his vice president was calling around trying to find out who actually had the authority to decide something, and the honest answer was nobody, and a month of paralysis followed that nobody had planned for and everybody paid for.

There is a second layer here that applies to families without businesses too. In many households, one person handles everything financial. If that person is unavailable, the other one is not merely inconvenienced. They are starting from zero, under stress, with people around them offering advice.

Jethro tells Moses to select able people and place them over the work, and it comes at the end of a passage where Moses is doing everything himself and being told plainly that he will wear out.

The advice is not about delegation as a management technique. It is about a system that survives one person's limits.

Your family is a system. Right now it may have exactly one point of failure.

### Reflect

**2.** Do they know?

**3.** What would they need that they do not have?

# Day 15

## They Want Cash

### The government doesn't want your business

**"Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's."** MARK 12:17

This is the last day of the M, and it is the practical consequence that families most often miss.

If there is an estate tax obligation, the government does not want your business. It does not want your land or your building or a share of your company.

They want cash.

That single fact reshapes a lot of planning, and here is why it matters even to families who assume it does not apply to them.

If a significant portion of what your family holds is illiquid, and an obligation comes due in cash, the family has three options. Pay it from reserves, if the reserves exist. Borrow against the asset. Or sell something.

The third option is the one families end up in, and they end up there under a deadline, which is the worst possible condition for selling anything.

I have watched families sell a business their grandfather built, at a discount, on a clock, to satisfy an obligation that was entirely foreseeable and had simply never been planned for.

Nothing about that was a surprise to anyone who looked. It was only a surprise to the family.

There is a version of this that applies to families well below any estate tax threshold, and I do not want anyone to skip the day. The principle is liquidity, not taxation.

Ask what obligations will arrive, when, and whether cash exists to meet them. Funeral costs. A period where income stops and expenses do not. A property that has to be maintained while a decision is being made. Legal and administrative costs that arrive immediately.

A family with everything in illiquid form is a family that will be making decisions under pressure at the worst moment.

Jesus says render to Caesar what is Caesar's and to God what is God's. It is a famously economical answer to a trap, and inside it is an assumption that both obligations are real and both will come due.

There is no version of stewardship in which the obligation to Caesar simply does not apply.

So the question is not whether. It is whether you will meet it from a plan or from a forced sale.
