# USING THIS GUIDE

## This works alone, with your spouse, or with your whole family

You can do every part of this by yourself. Nothing here requires anyone else to participate, and if you are the only person in your family who has picked this up, you are not doing it wrong. Somebody in every family goes first.

But it is better together, and it is meant to end up there. Each session has questions you can discuss out loud and exercises you do privately. When you see the fork, take whichever side fits the room you are in.

**With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write it down.**

## What you are agreeing to, and what you are not

This material asks you to think about your life, your marriage, your money, and your children, sometimes inside the same question. That is deliberate, because those things do not sort neatly in real life either.

You are never required to say a number out loud. You are never required to share what you wrote in a private exercise. Nobody has to reveal anything to anyone in order to participate, and no one should be reading over your shoulder.

Some of what comes up will be tender. Go at the pace that is right for the people in the room.

## What you need each week

**The session video.** About twenty minutes. Watch it before or during the session, not after.

**This guide.** Bring something to write with. There is real writing space and you will use it.

**The companion book.** *Family Legacy by Design.* Each session assigns five short readings, one per day, for the week following. The Reading Plan in the appendix lays out all thirty.

## How much time this takes

The video runs about twenty minutes. Working through a session takes about an hour if you are moving, longer if the conversation opens up, which it often does. The private exercise adds twenty to thirty minutes and is best done in one sitting rather than in pieces.

The weekly action at the end of each session is one thing. It is meant to be done, not planned.

## Where this sits in the series

This is the second book in the Family Foundation Series and it is the one most families should start with. *Family Legacy by Design* comes before it and lays the foundation. What follows is *Getting on the Same Page as a Family*, then *Conversations That Matter*, then *Family Meetings That Bring You Together*, then *Raising the Next Generation*.

Clarity comes first for a reason. You cannot align a family around something that has never been made clear, and you cannot have a real conversation about something nobody has defined.

## If you are in the next generation

You may be holding this because your parents handed it to you, or because you picked it up yourself. Either way, this is written for you too, not only about you.

Each session has a section addressed to you directly. The questions are the same questions, asked thirty years earlier, which is an advantage rather than a disadvantage.

# SESSION 1

# The Five Areas of Your Legacy

## Bringing Focus to What Matters Most

## INTRODUCTION

**KEY VERSE**

"A plan in the heart of a man is like deep water, but a man of understanding draws it out."

**PROVERBS 20:5**

Most families are not confused about their money. They are confused about what the money is for. There may be advisors, documents, and accounts that would hold up under any scrutiny, and then someone asks what the family is actually trying to pass on and the room goes quiet in a way the balance sheet does not explain.

That silence is not a failure. It is almost always the first honest moment a family has had on this subject, because nobody ever asked. Tom's conviction after thirty years is that almost everything that matters to a family is already there. It has simply never been drawn out. This session opens the five areas of legacy, and they exist for one reason. Legacy is too large a word to act on. Five specific areas give a family somewhere to stand.

Every family already has a legacy. The only question is whether anyone chose it.

## OPENING STORY

**Five Answers in One Room**

They had done well. Not spectacularly, but well enough that the questions in front of them were real ones. Two grown children, a son-in-law, a house that had been paid off for years, and a business that had grown steadily for two decades.

Tom asked them what mattered most to this family.

There were five people in the room and he got five different answers. Not contradictory ones. Not angry ones. Just five people who had lived in the same house, eaten the same meals, and gone to the same church for thirty years, and who had never once been asked to say out loud what they were building together.

The father assumed everyone already knew. The mother had an answer she had never said because nobody asked. One of the children thought the answer was obvious and was surprised to hear it was not. The son-in-law had a view he had been careful never to offer.

Nothing was wrong with that family. That is the part worth sitting with. They loved each other, they were together often, and by any measure they were doing better than most. And they were about to make a series of significant decisions off five different pictures of what they were doing and why.

## GET STARTED

Take a few minutes before you begin. With your spouse or family, take these together. On your own, take them with a pen.

A word about how this works. You will be asked to think about your own life and your family, sometimes inside the same question. That is deliberate. You are never required to say a number out loud, and you are never required to share what you wrote in a private exercise. Nothing here asks you to trade information for participation, and nobody should be reading over your shoulder.

**1.** What is one thing your family is known for that nobody ever decided on? *With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write it down.*

**2.** If someone asked each person in your family what matters most here, how similar do you think the answers would be? *With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write it down.*

## BEFORE YOU BRING THE FAMILY IN

This is about the two of you, not about your children.

Tom says it in almost every family he works with. You start with Mom and Dad. Not because the children matter less, but because a family cannot get aligned around something the two people at the center have never said to each other.

And frequently one spouse does not have the full picture. Not because anything is hidden. Because that was the arrangement, and it worked, and nobody revisited it.

So before the family conversation, there is a conversation between the two of you. If you are working through this alone, or if there is no spouse, this section becomes the moment you decide who the first person is that you will say any of it to.

- We say what we actually think, not what will keep the peace this evening.
- Neither of us has to have an answer ready. Not knowing is an acceptable answer.
- What is said here stays here until we both agree it goes further.
- We are not solving anything tonight. We are finding out where we actually are.
- We give each other room to be unfinished. Clarity rarely arrives in one pass.
- One real step beats a good intention.

## LEARN

**Watch the Session 1 teaching video.**

The teaching in this series is a conversation rather than a lecture, so rather than filling in blanks, you will capture what you heard and put it against your own family. Each prompt below names what Tom and Brett covered. Your job is the second half.

**Tom opens with the five areas of legacy: personal, family, financial, business, and charitable. His reason is that legacy on its own is too broad a term and people never focus it.**

Which of the five is clearest in your family right now, and which two have never been discussed?

**Brett names three words that run through the whole series: clarity, alignment, and communication. They go in that order for a reason.**

Which of the three is thinnest in your family, and what has that cost you so far?

**Tom draws the line between a legacy by default, which is what a family drifts into over time, and a legacy by design, which is the one they chose.**

Name one place a legacy by default is forming in your family right now and nobody has said so out loud.

**Tom says there is usually a gap between where a family says it wants to be and where it actually is, and that the gap is not a failure. It is information.**

Where is the gap in your family, and how long has it been there?

**Tom and Brett say the two things they want for every family are family harmony and the wise transfer of both wealth and wisdom.**

If you had to choose which of those two your family is currently better at, which is it, and what does that tell you?

## REFLECT

*With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write your answers.* There are more questions here than you need. Take the ones that catch.

**1.** Tom says most of what matters to a family is already there and nobody has asked for it. Where has that been true in your family?

**2.** Clarity, alignment, communication. Which of the three is thinnest for you, and what has it cost?

**3.** Where is a legacy by default forming in your family right now?

**4.** You can be completely clear about what you want and still have never said it to anyone. Where is that true for you?

**5.** What keeps you from asking the deeper question in your own family? Time, discomfort, history, fear of the answer, or something else?

**6.** Tom talks about hearing what sits behind what someone says. When did you last hear something a family member did not say out loud?

**7.** Answer this one about someone else in your family rather than about yourself. What do you think matters most to them, and how confident are you that you are right?

## THE FRAMEWORK

### The Five Areas of Legacy

Tom's framework exists because legacy as a word is too broad to act on. Five areas give a family somewhere specific to put the conversation.

- **Personal Legacy**, which includes spiritual legacy. What you want to be remembered for, and what your walk with God has actually looked like.

- **Family Legacy**. Your children, your grandchildren, your parents if they are still living. What your family legacy has been, and what you want it to be from here.

- **Financial Legacy**. The transfer piece. What amount of inheritance would be a blessing to the people who receive it rather than a curse.

- **Work and Business Legacy**. If you own a business, succession and continuity. If you do not, what you have built through your work and what happens to the influence you carry.

- **Charitable Legacy**. What you want to do for the kingdom of God, both now while you are living and at the end of your life.

**Which of the five is clearest to you? Start there. Then name the one you avoid most consistently, and be honest about whether your family avoids it or you do.**

## THE TOOL

### The Family Legacy by Design Assessment

Every session introduces one of Tom's tools. This one comes first because it produces the map everything else runs on.

The Assessment walks all five areas and asks two things in each. How clear are you, and how clear is the rest of your family. It takes about fifteen minutes.

The value is not in the score. It is in the distance between your answers and the answers of the person sitting next to you. Complete it yourself this week. Then ask one other person in your family to complete it separately, without seeing yours, and compare.

Do not compare in order to be right. Compare in order to find out where the conversation needs to start.

## FOR THE NEXT GENERATION

You are on the receiving end of a legacy right now, and most of it has nothing to do with what you will inherit. It is in how your family handles disappointment. What they do when they are wrong. What they laugh at. What they never talk about.

You did not choose any of that. You are already carrying it.

Which means the five areas are not a set of questions for later. They are the same five questions your parents are working through, asked thirty years earlier, and you have thirty more years to do something with the answers.

Take the framework and run it on yourself. Personal, family, financial, work, charitable. Not what your family is clear on. What you are.

Then ask one question you have been holding.
