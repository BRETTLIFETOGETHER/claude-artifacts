**A formatting inconsistency that will bite at layout.** Overview and Clarity use `# **DAY 1**` and `# **Day 1**`. Meetings uses `## **Day 1**`. Section headers are all-caps in Overview and title case everywhere else. Anyone building a template from one book will have to rebuild it for the next.

**Day 20 of the Overview ends mid-book** with no closing and no handoff to Day 21.

# PART 2 — WHAT THE FORMAT DOES WELL

This is a better-designed product than the participant guide, and the reason is worth naming.

**The load is right.** Four Reflect questions, one Practice, one Question to Carry, four Bucket lines. About ten asks, spread across a day rather than crammed into an evening. The curriculum asks thirty-one in a single sitting. The devotional asks ten across twenty-four hours. One of those two designs matches how people actually live.

**Every title is verbatim Tom.** Deep Waters. The Golden Goose. Shirt Sleeves to Shirt Sleeves. The Fifth Child. The Final Exam. Thirty days of headlines and not one of them was written by a copywriter. That is the strongest asset in the entire series and it transfers to the family edition untouched.

**Prayer is a direction, not a written prayer.** *Ask for honesty about the distance between the legacy you intend and the one you are currently living.* That respects the reader and it avoids the formulaic prayer prompt the series bans everywhere else.

**The Bucket is genuinely good.** Private, four lines, nobody reads it. It is the only place in either product where a person can be completely honest, and it is well named.

**Scripture is sourced rather than selected.** The Day 1 build notes confirm Proverbs 13:22 is quoted by Tom on video. That is the right standard.

# PART 3 — WHAT BREAKS IN A FAMILY EDITION

**One Question to Carry is entirely advisor-facing.** *Take this to a client this week.* Who I will ask, when, what came back.

This is the section that needs the most work and it is also the biggest opportunity. An advisor has to pick a client. A family member already knows exactly who they need to ask and has been avoiding it. The same structure aimed at a family is stronger than it is aimed at an advisor.

**Reflect is written to a book of business.** *Take one client. What is the distance between his intended legacy and his lived one.* Every one of the four questions on Day 1 assumes a practice.

**Practice uses advisor instruments.** Day 1's table has a column for "one client family" beside "my own family." Day 5 asks the reader to list the five questions they ask most often in client meetings and mark each as clarity or information. Neither has a family equivalent.

**The body voice addresses a professional.** *Now put that against your book of business.* *That is either a gap in the market or a gap in your service.* *I have never met an advisor who found that comfortable.*

**And one structural thing.** The coaching journey is a companion to the coaching series. The family journey should be able to stand alone, because for a lot of families it will be the first thing they touch and possibly the only thing. It has to work without the video and without the guide.

# PART 4 — WHAT THE FAMILY EDITION NEEDS

**Same shape.** Thirty days, six weeks, five days a week, matched to the six sessions. That is what makes it double as the curriculum's reading plan rather than competing with it.

**Same six sections,** with two renamed.

| Coaching | Family |
| --- | --- |
| Reflect | Reflect |
| Practice | Practice |
| **One Question to Carry** | **One Question to Ask** |
| The Bucket | The Bucket |
| Prayer | Prayer |

*One Question to Ask* keeps the three-line structure. Who I will ask. When. What came back, in their words rather than my summary.

**Same titles.** All thirty carry across verbatim, because they are Tom's own words and they work on any reader.

**Different body.** First person Tom, addressed to a family member rather than an advisor. The teaching does not change. The second half of each paragraph does.

**One addition.** A short line at the end of each week naming what the next five days are about, so a family that falls behind can see where they are rather than quietly giving up.

---

# PART 5 — WHY THIS SHOULD BE BUILT BEFORE THE CURRICULUM

Three reasons, and the third is the real one.

**It is unblocked.** The participant guide is waiting on the Family Legacy by Design Assessment, the Family Health Survey, permissions on three opening stories, and the statistics attribution. The devotional needs none of them.

**It fixes what the audit found.** The guide's central problem is that it asks a family for thirty-one questions in an evening. The devotional asks for ten across a day. It is already designed correctly for the reader the guide was overshooting.

**And it is the on-ramp.** A family that will not sit everyone down will still read a page. The devotional sells the curriculum rather than competing with it, and the same thirty days become the guide's reading plan when they go deeper.

Six of these is 180 days of daily material off assets already owned.

# PART 6 — WHAT TO FIX ON THE COACHING SIDE

Separate from the family build, and worth doing before either ships.

**Write Overview Days 21 to 30.** The book stops mid-stride.

**Write Alignment and Next Generation.** Sixty days, two entire titles.

**Bring Communication up to density.** It is thirty percent under the others.

**Standardize the heading levels and capitalization** across the four existing books before anyone builds a layout template.
