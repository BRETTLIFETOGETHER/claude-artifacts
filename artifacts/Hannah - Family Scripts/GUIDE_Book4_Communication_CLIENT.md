# A WELCOME FROM TOM AND BRETT

Families do not talk about what matters.

That is the observation underneath this whole book, and people hear it as an accusation when it is really a description.

The families we are describing are not estranged. Many of them see each other constantly, show up for every birthday, and would tell you without hesitation that they are close. And they are close.

What they do not do is talk about the things that actually determine what happens to them.

Not the money, though that is one. What each person expects. What somebody has been carrying alone for fifteen years. What one child assumed about a decision made in 2009. Whether the plan everybody is nodding at is one anybody actually agreed to.

Those conversations do not get had. And the reason is almost never that people do not care.

**Here is what makes waiting expensive.**

The conversation you are not having is not neutral ground. Silence is not a pause. Everyone in your family is drawing conclusions in the absence of information, and those conclusions harden. So when the conversation finally does happen, and it will, it is no longer one conversation. It is one conversation plus a decade of accumulated interpretation.

The topic does not get harder. The topic collects things.

**And one more thing before you begin.**

**Most families never do this.**

Not because they do not love each other. Because there is no natural moment when it comes up, and because the anticipated version of the conversation is always worse than the real one.

If you are opening this and feeling behind, you are not. You are ahead of nearly everyone, by the width of one decision.

You do not have to do it perfectly. You do not have to do it with everyone. And you do not have to finish it today.

# USING THIS GUIDE

## Where this book sits

If you have been through *Family Meetings*, this is going to sound close and it is not the same thing.

A family meeting is an event. It is scheduled, it has a purpose, somebody leads it.

This is everything else. This is the kitchen. This is the car. This is the phone call. This is the ten minutes after everybody else has gone to bed.

Most of what actually gets communicated in a family never happens in a meeting.

## This works alone, with your spouse, or with your whole family

You can do every part of this by yourself.

**With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write it down.**

## Who leads this

Somebody has to press play and read the questions out loud. That person is a **host**, not a teacher.

The host does not need to know more than anyone else. Start the video. Read the question. Then be quiet long enough for somebody to answer it.

It should probably not be the person who usually decides things in your family, and it should rotate if you can.

**Three rules for whoever has the chair.** Read the question and then stop talking. Answer last, not first. And you are not responsible for fixing anything that comes up.

## One warning about this particular book

More than any other in this series, this one is going to produce a conversation you were not planning to have.

That is the point, and it is also why this guide is worth reading a day ahead rather than in the moment before you speak to somebody.

If something surfaces in a session and it is bigger than the session can hold, stop. Name it, agree when you will come back to it, and do not try to finish it that evening. Half of what makes a hard conversation go well is knowing you can pause it.

## If your family does not look like the one in this book

**If there is no spouse.** Every session has a section built around a husband and wife. Read it as being about the first person who will know.

**If a child is estranged.** This book will feel pointed at you, and Session 5 in particular. Do the work anyway. Being able to write down what you would say, and to whom, is worth something on the day the door opens.

**If your family is blended.** The expectations questions in Session 5 are genuinely harder in your situation, because there are more sets of them and they were formed in different households. Give that session more time than the others.

**If your resources are modest.** Almost every example here involves money in some form, because that is where families most often get stuck. But every barrier, every listening skill, and every hard conversation in these six sessions exists at any income.

**The companion book.** *Family Legacy by Design.* Each session assigns five short readings.

**And one honest note about the questions.** There are more in each session than you will answer. Each session marks **one question** as the one that matters.

# SESSION 1

# Why Families Don't Talk About What Matters

## The Conversations That Never Happen

**HOST NOTE.** This session asks people to name something they have been avoiding. Do not ask anybody to read theirs out loud, and say so before the question comes up.

## INTRODUCTION

Here is the question people bring to this book.

**Why can't we talk about this?**

Not why won't they. Why can't we. Because most people have tried, in some form, and something stopped it, and they could not name what.

**KEY VERSE**

"Therefore, laying aside falsehood, speak truth each one of you with his neighbor."

**EPHESIANS 4:25**

Families do not talk about what matters, and there are a whole bunch of reasons behind it.

It is not that they do not care. It is not that they are unwilling. It is that they do not know how to enter the conversation without creating tension, or saying the wrong thing, or opening something they are not sure how to handle.

So instead of stepping into it, they step around it. Over time, that becomes the pattern.

Notice who Paul says to speak truth to. Not strangers. Not enemies. Neighbors. The people you see all the time, who are the hardest to be plain with, because you have so much more to lose and so many more chances to do it later.

## THE ONE QUESTION

> **What is the conversation your family has not had, and who is it really with?**
>
> Answer that one and the session worked. Everything else is optional.

## OPENING STORY

**Eleven Years**

A father Tom worked with was completely clear. He knew exactly what he wanted for his family and could have said it in one sentence.

He had never told his children any of it.

Not out of secrecy. He was waiting for the right moment, and the right moment had not come in eleven years. There was always a wedding, or a hard season, or a good Christmas nobody wanted to spoil.

Meanwhile his children had built their own picture from the silence, and it was not the one he would have drawn. One of them had concluded something about her standing in the family that was simply not true and that nothing had ever contradicted.

He was not avoiding the conversation. He was waiting for a version of it he could predict, and that version does not exist.

## THE FRAMEWORK

### The Four Barriers

Almost every unhad conversation in a family runs into one of four things.

- **Discomfort.** The subject touches money, expectations, roles, or history, and nobody knows how to raise it without creating tension.

- **Fear.** Of conflict, of being misunderstood, of saying something that cannot be taken back. So people stay quiet to keep things comfortable, and it works, right up until it does not.

- **Assumption.** People believe they already understand each other, so the conversation feels unnecessary. Assumptions are rarely as accurate as people think.

- **Timing.** There will be a better moment later. Later is the most reasonable-sounding barrier and the most expensive one.

**Which of the four is yours? Not your family's. Yours.**

## WHERE YOU ARE STARTING

**If your family has never talked about any of this.** You are the majority and you are not behind. Do not open with the biggest thing. Ask one person one real question and listen without correcting them.

**If you tried once and it went badly.** One bad conversation is evidence about that conversation, not about your family. Something specific went wrong: wrong room, wrong person, wrong moment, or an answer somebody was not ready to hear. Name which one before you try again.

**If you are the only one who wants this.** That is common and it is not disqualifying. Everything in this book works for one person, and a person who has changed how they listen changes the conversations they are in, whether or not anyone else read anything.

## THE TOOL

### The Barriers Check

For the conversation you named, work out which barrier is actually stopping you.

Ask the four questions in order and stop at the first honest yes.

- **Is it discomfort?** Would I know how to raise it if I were confident it would go well?
- **Is it fear?** Am I protecting the relationship, or protecting myself?
- **Is it assumption?** Do I believe I already know what they would say, and have I checked?
- **Is it timing?** Could I name what specifically I am waiting for?

That last one has a test attached. If you can name what you are waiting for, you are exercising judgment. If you cannot, or if the answer is some version of a better moment, you are postponing.

Different barriers have different remedies, and families waste years applying the wrong one.

# SESSION 2

# Understanding, Not Agreement

## The Goal Is Not to Win

**HOST NOTE.** Somebody will treat this session as a technique for getting their way more gently. If that shows up, the correction is in the framework itself. Understanding is not a softer route to agreement.

## INTRODUCTION

Here is the question in this one, and most people have never separated the two things in it.

**Do I have to win this?**

Because that is what a hard family conversation feels like. Somebody is going to have to move, and it is probably going to have to be you, and so you arrive braced.

**KEY VERSE**

"He who gives an answer before he hears, it is folly and shame to him."

**PROVERBS 18:13**

Agreement is transactional. It is a yes or a no, and it closes something.

Understanding is relational. And here is the part people miss. Understanding is what actually leads to planning and problem solving.

You cannot solve anything with somebody you do not understand. You can force a decision. You cannot solve.

Change the target and the whole room changes. If the goal is understanding, nobody has to concede anything today, which means nobody has to defend anything today, which means people can actually say what they think.

## THE ONE QUESTION

> **Could you state the other person's position well enough that they would recognize it?**
>
> Answer that one and the session worked.

## OPENING STORY

**The Unanimous Yes**

A family Tom worked with had a decision to make, and the parents genuinely wanted their children's input.

So they presented the situation and asked what everybody thought.

And they got agreement. From all four children, immediately, unanimously.

The parents took it as a good sign. Tom took it as a warning, because unanimous and immediate is almost never input. It is people being agreeable.

He asked a different question in the next meeting. Not whether anybody agreed. He went around the room and asked each person to say what would concern them about it.

That question is much harder to answer politely, and it produced three genuine objections nobody had voiced, one of which changed the decision.

The family had not been withholding. They had been asked a question that only had one socially available answer.

## GET STARTED

**1.** What is the disagreement your family keeps having? *With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write it down.*

## BEFORE YOU BRING THE FAMILY IN

A short reminder. Couples develop an agreed public position and one of them delivers it.

That is not deception. It is partnership. But the agreed version is a compromise, and the views underneath it are usually more interesting.

Find out what your spouse actually thinks, separately from what the two of you say.

"The goal is not to get agreement. The goal is to get understanding."

**TOM CONWAY**

## THE FRAMEWORK

### Understanding, Not Agreement

- **Agreement is transactional.** A yes or a no. It closes something and it can be extracted from people who do not mean it.

- **Understanding is relational.** It is what leads to planning and problem solving, because you cannot solve anything with somebody you do not understand.

- **Understanding comes first.** People need to feel heard before they become open, and openness is the precondition for alignment rather than the result of it.

- **The way to understanding is empathetic listening.** Not what did they say. Why did they say it.

**Take your family's recurring disagreement. Write the other person's position as they would write it. Then show it to them and ask whether you got it right.**

## WHERE YOU ARE STARTING

**If your family argues openly.** You have more material to work with than families who do not. Your work is changing the target, not the volume. The same conversation aimed at understanding produces different results with the same people.

**If your family avoids disagreement entirely.** Your risk is the unanimous yes. Compliance looks exactly like agreement from the outside and it is not the same thing, and the difference only shows up later when nobody follows through.

**If one person always concedes.** That person has learned that the conversation costs more than the point, and they are usually right about the cost. Nothing changes until they have evidence that a different answer is survivable.

## THE TOOL

### The Second Question

The first answer is almost always the prepared one. The real answer comes on the second or third pass, and only if somebody stays in the room long enough to ask again.

Three moves, and none of them are clever.

**Tell me more about that.** Four words. It is not a question, which is why it works. It does not demand anything specific, so the person continues wherever they were actually going.

**Why is that important to you?** This one goes down a layer rather than sideways.

**And say nothing.** After somebody finishes, wait. Most people, given four or five seconds, will add something, and the addition is usually the real thing.

There is one warning. If somebody senses you are running a technique on them, it closes them down faster than not asking at all. The difference between drawing something out and extracting it is entirely in whether you actually want to know.

# SESSION 3

# Learning to Listen

## What Happens When Someone Finally Feels Heard

**HOST NOTE.** This is the session to model rather than teach. Ask one question and then genuinely wait, in front of everyone, for longer than is comfortable. That demonstration does more than the content.

## INTRODUCTION

Here is the question underneath this session, and it is asked by more people in a family than you would guess.

**Does anyone actually hear me?**

Not do they love me. Do they hear what I say, and does it go anywhere.

**KEY VERSE**

"Everyone must be quick to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger."

**JAMES 1:19**

Listening is one of those things everybody assumes they already do well, and almost nobody does.

The difference is not attention. Plenty of people listen with total attention and are still not listening well, because attention can be entirely evaluative.

When somebody finishes speaking, what is the first thing in your mind? If it is an assessment, agreement or disagreement, that is evaluative listening. If it is closer to *so that is what that has been like*, that is something else.

Both are legitimate and only one of them makes a person feel heard.

## THE ONE QUESTION

> **Who in your family has stopped saying certain things, and when did that start?**
>
> Answer that one and the session worked.

## OPENING STORY

**She Never Told Them**

Tom worked with a family where the parents believed their adult daughter was disengaging. Pulling back, less involved, possibly upset about something.

The daughter believed her parents did not listen to her and had not for years.

Neither had said a word. Both were behaving on the basis of a conclusion, and each new piece of evidence got sorted into the frame that already existed, so the frames strengthened rather than corrected.

When they finally talked, it took about twenty minutes to dissolve something that had been running for the better part of a decade.

She had never told them, because saying it out loud felt like an accusation. And they had never asked, because asking felt like admitting something.

## THE FRAMEWORK

### Creating the Environment

The words matter far less than whether the person believes it is safe to say something true.

- **Safety comes before content.** If people do not feel safe they will respond without opening up, and the difference is invisible from your side.

- **Framing sets the temperature.** If a conversation feels like pressure or evaluation, people hold back. If it feels like an opportunity to be heard, they engage.

- **Silence is where the answer is.** Ask a question and then do not fill the space. Most of us end the pause in about two seconds, and whatever was rising does not come back.

- **Temperament changes everything.** Some people are quick to speak, some need time. If you do not account for that, you hear from the same voices every time and miss the others completely.

**Ask one question this week and count to six before saying anything. What comes after five seconds is different from what comes in the first two.**

## WHERE YOU ARE STARTING

**If your family talks over each other.** The problem is not volume, it is that a room without deliberate space distributes itself by temperament. Your work is asking one person, by name, and then holding the silence.

**If your family is quiet and polite.** Politeness can be a form of not saying anything. The tell is not tension, it is a slight carefulness, and subjects that never come up.

**If somebody has already stopped talking to you.** That did not happen suddenly. Something cost them once, and they concluded honesty was not worth it. Reopening it takes evidence rather than a conversation, and evidence takes repetition.

## THE TOOL

### The Opening Line

Most families do not have a listening problem. They have a starting problem.

Write the sentence you will actually use, in your own words, for the conversation you named in Session 1.

Three things a good opening line does.

**It removes the alarm.** *Nothing is wrong* is not optional. Without it, people assume a diagnosis.

**It names the subject.** Not vaguely. If people do not know what it is about, they will imagine something worse.

**It asks rather than announces.** *Could we* rather than *we need to.*

Write it out. Say it out loud once before you use it. Most people discover the version in their head is more accusatory than they intended.

### Direct, Truthful, Respectful

## THE TOOL

### Triangulation, and How to Beat It

Here is what triangulation is. You have something with person A. Instead of going to person A, you go to person B and talk about person A.

Over time it hardens. Person B now has an opinion about person A formed entirely from one side, and person A has no idea it exists and no way to answer it.

**Two moves, and neither is complicated.**

If you are carrying something, take it to the person it belongs to. Not perfectly. Just take it there.

That question, asked kindly, ends more dysfunction in families than almost anything else available.

## THE FRAMEWORK

### Clarity and Culture

- **One conversation creates clarity.** After a good conversation everybody knows something they did not know before. That is real and it is perishable.

- **Repetition removes the significance.** The first time is an event, and events make people careful. By the fourth or fifth time it is just a thing this family does, and people say more because less is riding on it.

**It cannot be answered with fine.** *How are you* fails this. *What is the hardest thing about this month* does not.

**It is the same every time,** so the person can anticipate it and think in advance.

**It goes both directions.** If you ask it, you answer it.

Write yours, and then use it every time for a year. You will be surprised how much accumulates.
