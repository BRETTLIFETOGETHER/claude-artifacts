# FAMILY FOUNDATION SERIES — CURRICULUM DESIGN

How the family participant guides differ from the coaching guides, and what tools belong in them.

Built against the coaching curriculum, which carries all six guides, thirty-six sessions, and thirty-five named tools.

---

# PART 1 — WHAT THE COACHING GUIDE DOES

Seventeen sections per session, in fixed order.

Session Divider · Introduction with key verse · Opening Story · Get Started · Building a Healthy Culture · Learn · Reflect · The Framework · Scenarios · The Tool · Exercise · Circles of Influence · Next Steps · Practice Conversation · Going Deeper · This Week's Readings · Final Thoughts

Plus the appendix: Scenario Responses, the Readiness Assessment, the Coaching Plan, and a thirty-day Reading Plan against the companion book.

**The design principle underneath it is solo-first.** Every element works for one advisor alone, with the pair and team forks added on top. That is why the convention reads "Team or pair: discuss. Solo: write it down."

---

# PART 2 — WHAT CARRIES, WHAT CHANGES, WHAT GOES

## Thirteen of seventeen carry with minor rewording

Session Divider. Introduction and key verse. Opening Story. Get Started. Learn. Reflect. The Framework. Scenarios. Next Steps. Going Deeper. This Week's Readings. Final Thoughts.

The changes are mostly one word at a time. **Learn** currently asks the advisor to apply each point to their book of business; the family version applies it to their own family. **Reflect** ends with a seventh question about you rather than a client; in the family guide every question is already about you, so the seventh becomes the one about someone else in your family. **This Week's Readings** swaps the companion book from *Family Legacy Coaching by Design* to the **Family Book**, which already has twelve chapters that map cleanly across the six titles.

## One section has no family equivalent and must be replaced

**Building a Healthy Culture** examines the advisor's own team and practice. There is no family version of a practice.

Replace it with **Before You Bring the Family In** — the husband and wife conversation that has to happen first. Tom says repeatedly that you start with Mom and Dad, and that the wife frequently does not know what they have. In the coaching guide that is an observation. In the family guide it becomes a step, sitting in exactly the slot where the advisor was asked to look at their own house before looking at anyone else's. Same function, different subject.

## Two sections need reaiming rather than replacing

**Circles of Influence** currently offers two colleagues to invite. In the family guide it becomes two family members, and it matters far more, because the whole point is that this does not stay with one person.

**Practice Conversation** currently sends four questions to a colleague, partner, mentor, or client family. In the family guide it goes to one person in your own family, and it becomes the bridge between private work and family work.

## The Exercise needs restructuring

Coaching runs Part One on a client, Part Two deeper, Part Three on you, then a five-item self-check.

The family version has no client, so the three parts become **Part One: You. Part Two: Your Marriage. Part Three: Your Family.** Same widening motion, same self-check at the end. That sequence also encodes the order Tom actually teaches: get clear yourself, get clear with your spouse, then bring the family in.

## Two things the family guide needs that the coaching guide does not

**A next-generation track.** The coaching guide talks about the next generation for six books and never once to them. A G2 or G3 reader will hold the family guide. Every session needs something addressed to them, and in Book 6 they are a plausible primary reader rather than an over-the-shoulder one.

**A together element.** This is the largest structural difference. The coaching guide is solo-first because an advisor works alone. The family guide has to be solo-first **and** together-capable, because a family that only ever does this privately has missed the point. Recommend one clearly marked **Together** block per session: one thing to do with at least one other person in the family before the next session. That is what the weekly action in the scripts already does, and the guide should carry it.

## Six are advisor instruments with no family use

**The Four Essential Skills**, **The Three Moves**, **The Next Generation Interview** as an advisor-administered instrument, **The Family Meeting Binder and Commitment** as a practice artifact, the **Legacy Coach Readiness Assessment**, and the **Family Legacy Coaching Plan**.

Replace them with:

| Slot | Family tool |
| --- | --- |
| Overview 2 | **The Family Purpose Question.** Ask each adult what they believe the family is for. Collect answers, do not correct them. |
| Overview 3 | **What Would Be Said.** Write the two or three sentences you would want said about your life, then show one person. |
| Overview 4 | **The Wealth and Wisdom Inventory.** Two columns. What is being passed on in each, right now, whether you meant it or not. |
| Overview 5 | **The Seven Points Check.** Self-scored against the Workbook Legend's seven common points of failure and six points of success. |
| Overview 6 | **The Starting Point.** Which of the five books your family begins with, and the date. |
| Communication 6 | **The Standing Question.** One question you ask every time you talk, for a year. |

# PART 4 — THREE PROBLEMS TO FIX BEFORE THE FAMILY BUILD

**The Family Values Exercise appears four times** in the coaching curriculum: Clarity Session 3, Communication Session 5, Meetings Session 5, and Next Generation Session 2. A family working through all six books would meet the same exercise four times. Recommend it lives in **Meetings Session 5**, where it is a half-day gathering with everyone in the room, and the other three sessions reference it rather than repeat it.

**The Five Uses of Money appears twice**, in Alignment Session 6 and Next Generation Session 4. Both uses are legitimate but different: Alignment is the family allocating for the year, Next Gen is teaching a young person the framework. Keep both, but name them differently so it reads as deliberate.

**The Goals Conversation appears three times** in variant forms across Overview and Clarity. Same fix: one canonical version, referenced elsewhere.

# PART 5 — THE APPENDIX

Coaching carries Scenario Responses, the Readiness Assessment, the Coaching Plan, and a thirty-day Reading Plan.

Family should carry:

**Case Study Responses.** Same function. Twelve per book, matching the two per session.

**The Family Readiness Assessment.** Replaces the Legacy Coach Readiness Assessment. Self-scored, family-facing.

**The Family Legacy Plan.** Replaces the Coaching Plan. What this family decided, in one place, with dates.

**Reading Plan.** Thirty readings against the **Family Book** rather than the coaching book. Its twelve chapters map across the six titles without any new writing.

**When to Bring Someone In.** New. A short honest page on the moments a family should get professional help, drawn from the line already closing every Alignment and Meetings session. This is the natural handoff to the coaching side and it belongs in the family guide, not hidden from it.

# PART 6 — WHAT THIS MEANS FOR SCHEDULE

The six scripts are built and they slot into the **Learn** section of each session. Everything else in the seventeen-section architecture is guide material and does not exist yet.

At the coaching guide's density, roughly 3,400 words per session excluding appendix, a family guide runs about 20,000 words per book and 120,000 across the series.

The order I would build in: **Clarity first**, since it has the richest source and is the front door. Then Next Generation, then Meetings, then Alignment, then Overview, then Communication last, since Communication still has no filmed source.
