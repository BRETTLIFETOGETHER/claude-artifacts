## THE STRUCTURAL DIFFERENCE

These two books do not run parallel and should not.

**The coaching Overview previews the other five titles by teaching a compressed version of each.** Sessions 3, 4, 5, and 6 are miniatures of Clarity, Alignment, Communication, and Next Generation. That works for an advisor who needs a map before they start.

**The family Overview builds a foundation first.** What legacy is, what a family is for, what you are already passing on, and the two things every family transfers whether they mean to or not. It previews the system once, in Session 6, and it previews what was actually written rather than teaching it in advance.

Built last on purpose. Session 6 can now describe five books that exist.

# 🎬 SESSION 1 — WHAT IS A LEGACY, REALLY?

Understanding What You Are Already Passing On

*Source: Coaching Overview script S1; Workbook Legend S1; Family Book ch. 1*

**BRETT: CENTER**

Well, welcome to Family Legacy by Design.

My name is Brett, and I am here with Tom Conway, who has come alongside more families in this space than probably anyone over the last thirty years. Tom, I thank you for your life, your leadership, and your legacy, and I am glad to be starting this with you.

This is the front door. Everything else in this series comes after this book, and this book has one job. To make sure we all mean the same thing by the word legacy before we spend any time on it.

And a word before we start. Some of what comes up here is going to be personal. You may be watching alone, with your spouse, or with your whole family in the room. Go at the pace that is right for the people you are with. Nobody has to say everything out loud today.

**BRETT: TURN**

So Tom, when a family sits down and starts talking about legacy, what are they usually thinking?

**TOM: CENTER**

When most people hear that word, they think about something that happens at the end of life. What they leave behind.

And that is part of it. It is a very small part of it.

Legacy is not something that happens when you die. It is something that is being formed right now, every day, whether you are thinking about it or not.

It is something you received from the people who came before you. And it is something you are transmitting to the people who come after you.

So the question changes. It is not just, what am I going to leave. It becomes, what am I building into the lives of the people around me right now?

**BRETT: CENTER**

Tom, before we go further, I want you to walk people through something you do early with every family. The reality check.

**TOM: CENTER**

It is five sentences, and they are all obvious, and almost nobody has said them out loud.

**I will die.**

**I will take nothing with me.**

**I will probably die at a time I did not anticipate.**

**Someone else will get my things.**

And the last one is the one that matters. **I can only decide, before I die, who gets my things after I die.**

That is it. That is the whole window. And most people spend the entire window meaning to get to it.

**BRETT: CENTER**

That last one lands hard, Tom, because it is not morbid. It is just arithmetic.

**BRETT: TURN**

So let's say the other part plainly. Is this about money?

**TOM: CENTER**

Legacy is not about money.

And I want to be careful, because money matters and this whole series deals with it. But the most significant legacy anybody leaves is not about money at all.

It is about **values.** It is about **character.** And it is about **memories.**

`[TOM: a family you have sat with where what the next generation actually carried forward had nothing to do with what was in the estate.]`

**BRETT: CENTER**

And Tom, you break it into three, which I think is the most useful frame in this entire book.

**TOM: CENTER**

There are three kinds of legacy.

There is the legacy you have **received.** You did not choose it. Some of it is wonderful and some of it is hard, and both of them are shaping you.

There is the legacy you will **pass on.** That is the one everybody thinks about, and it is mostly out ahead of you.

And there is the legacy you are **living out today.**

That third one is the most important. Because it is the one you are shaping right now, through how you live, how you decide, and how you treat people. And it is the one your family is actually watching.

**BRETT: CENTER**

So it is not something you define later. It is something you are already living into.

> **Scripture.** Ecclesiastes 2:18-19. Solomon says he hated all the fruit of his labor, because he had to leave it to the one who would come after him, and who knows whether that person will be wise or a fool. That is the anxiety underneath every family in this series, written three thousand years ago.

**BRETT: CENTER**

Tom, let me give you a scenario.

**SCENARIO ONE.** A couple who have done very well. Solid plan, everything structured and organized. From the outside you would say they have done this right. And there has never been a conversation in that family about what any of it is for.

**BRETT: TURN**

**TOM: CENTER**

The financial side is strong, and the relational and personal side has been quietly drifting for years.

And what happens is that when the next generation steps in, they receive something they do not understand. They have the thing without the meaning of the thing.

So the question is not whether they will get it. They will. The question is whether anybody told them what it was for.

**BRETT: CENTER**

Let me give you another version of this.

**SCENARIO TWO.** A family with real connection. They spend time together. They talk. There is genuine care. And nobody has ever used the word legacy in that house.

**BRETT: TURN**

**TOM: CENTER**

That family is much further along than they think.

Because over time, something has taken shape. There is a shared understanding of what matters. Not perfectly. Clearly enough that people are moving in the same direction.

So when decisions come, whether about money or opportunity or responsibility, they are not starting from scratch. They are building on something that was already formed.

They just did not have a name for it.

**BRETT: CENTER**

To the next generation watching this, one thing to sit with.

You are on the receiving end of a legacy right now, and most of it has nothing to do with what you will inherit. It is in how your family handles disappointment. What they do when they are wrong. What they laugh at.

You did not choose any of that. You are already carrying it. And you are the first person who gets to decide which parts of it you pass on.

**BRETT: CENTER**

**THIS WEEK.** Name one thing you received from the generation before you that had nothing to do with money.

Then name one thing you would rather not pass on.

**BRETT: TURN**

Tom, bring us home.

**TOM: CENTER**

What families pass on is not decided at the end. It is built day by day, over time.

So the question worth asking is simple. If the people closest to you described what is being passed on right now, would it feel intentional? Or would it feel more like default?

**BRETT: CENTER**

Next session, why your family was built to last longer than you.

We will see you there.

# 🎬 SESSION 2 — THE FAMILY GOD DESIGNED

Why Your Family Was Built to Last Longer Than You

*Source: Workbook Legend S1 and S11; Coaching Overview script S2; Family Book ch. 2*

**BRETT: CENTER**

Welcome back to Family Legacy by Design. My name is Brett, this is Tom, and we are grateful you are back with us.

Last session we said legacy is three things. What you received, what you will pass on, and what you are living right now. And we said the third one matters most.

This session goes underneath that and asks a question most families have never been asked.

What is a family actually for?

Because you have an answer, whether you have ever said it or not. And that answer is quietly running your family right now.

**BRETT: TURN**

So Tom, lay out the two views.

**TOM: CENTER**

There is a modern view and there is a biblical view, and most of us are living the modern one without ever having chosen it.

The modern view is that we raise our children to **independence.** We raise them up, and we kick them out.

And there is a place for that. You do not want them living in the basement at thirty-five. But if independence is the goal, then the finish line is the day they no longer need you. And once you cross that line, what exactly is left?

**BRETT: CENTER**

And the biblical view.

**TOM: CENTER**

**Interdependence,** not independence.

Multiple generations loving and serving one another. Staying connected. Continuing to influence each other, and living with a shared sense of purpose over time.

That is a completely different picture. It is not a handoff. It is something that continues.

**BRETT: CENTER**

And Tom, that reframes what success even means.

If independence is the goal, success is: they are on their own. If interdependence is the goal, success looks like ongoing connection and influence over time.

It is not just about launching the next generation. It is about staying engaged with them and continuing to build something together.

**BRETT: TURN**

Tom, where does this come from in Scripture?

**TOM: CENTER**

It goes all the way back to the covenant.

When God makes His promise to Abraham in Genesis 17, He does not stop at Abraham. He says, I will establish my covenant between me and you and your offspring after you, throughout their generations.

**Throughout their generations.** God's design for family was never one generation. It was always meant to extend beyond that. To children, to grandchildren, and beyond.

So what you do today does not just affect today. It carries.

> **Scripture.** Genesis 17:7. *To be God to you and to your descendants after you.* The promise has a horizon built into it, and the horizon is longer than a lifetime.

**BRETT: CENTER**

And Tom, that stretches how you think about time.

Because now you are not asking what the right decision is this year. You are asking what kind of family this is shaping over the next generation or two.

**TOM: CENTER**

That is right. And most families have never had that horizon put in front of them.

They are making good decisions for a five-year window in something that runs for a hundred years.

**BRETT: CENTER**

Tom, let me give you a scenario.

**SCENARIO ONE.** A father who did the modern version well. He raised capable children. They are independent, successful, and none of them need anything from him.

And he is not sure what his role is anymore.

**BRETT: TURN**

**TOM: CENTER**

`[TOM: a father in that exact position, and what he found when he stopped trying to be needed and started being involved.]`

Because he did not fail. He succeeded at the wrong finish line.

His role now is not provider and it is not decision maker. It is something older than either of those. He is the person who holds the family's memory, and who can say things nobody else in that family has standing to say.

That is not a lesser role. In most cultures for most of history, that was the most important one.

**BRETT: CENTER**

Let me give you another version of this.

**SCENARIO TWO.** A family that has drifted geographically. Three states, different lives, everybody doing fine. And the assumption is that this is just what happens.

**BRETT: TURN**

**TOM: CENTER**

It is what happens if you let it. It is not what has to happen.

And I would ask them a question. Is your family a group of people who used to live together? Or is it something that still has a purpose?

Because those are two different things, and every family is one or the other, and almost nobody has decided which.

**BRETT: CENTER**

To the next generation, this session is the one where you have more say than you think.

Whether your family is interdependent in twenty years is not going to be decided by your parents. It will be decided by whether you and your siblings and the people you marry choose to stay in each other's lives when nothing requires it.

Your parents can build the table. They cannot make you come to it.

**BRETT: CENTER**

**THIS WEEK.** Ask each adult in your family what they believe the purpose of your family is.

Do not correct any of the answers. Just collect them.

**BRETT: TURN**

Tom, bring us home.

**TOM: CENTER**

Legacy is not something that gets handed off. It is something that continues.

And a family that understands that operates differently, because it is not counting down to a transfer. It is building something it expects to still be there.

**BRETT: CENTER**

Next session, the legacy you are living right now, and what your family sees when you are not explaining yourself.

We will see you there.

# 🎬 SESSION 3 — THE LEGACY YOU ARE LIVING RIGHT NOW

What Your Family Sees When You Are Not Explaining Yourself

*Source: Coaching Overview script S1; Workbook Legend S1; Family Book ch. 3*

**BRETT: CENTER**

Welcome back to Family Legacy by Design. My name is Brett, I am with Tom, and we are glad you have joined us again.

We have said there are three kinds of legacy. Received, passed on, and lived out today. And we said the third one is the most important.

So this session is about that one. The one happening right now, in your house, this week.

**BRETT: TURN**

Tom, why is that one the most important?

**TOM: CENTER**

Because it is the only one you are actually in control of.

You cannot change what you received. You cannot fully control what happens after you are gone. But the one you are living right now, that is entirely yours.

And here is the line I keep coming back to. People do not replicate what you say. They replicate what you live.

You can say a lot of things. What people actually see, that is what sticks.

**BRETT: CENTER**

And Tom, they are always watching.

**TOM: CENTER**

They are. Your kids are watching. Your grandkids are watching. The people you work with are watching.

And whether you realize it or not, you are teaching all the time. You are teaching by how you live, how you respond, and what you prioritize.

Your life becomes the message.

**BRETT: CENTER**

And Tom, here is what makes that uncomfortable, and I think we should just say it.

They see the whole thing. At church you have got your church face on. At home you have got your real face out. And they live in your house.

**TOM: CENTER**

They get all of it. The good, and the bad, and the parts you would not want on video.

But that is not a reason to despair. It is a reason to be honest. Because a family that watches you handle something badly and then watches you make it right has learned more than a family that only ever saw the polished version.

**BRETT: CENTER**

Tom, I want to put a hard thing on the table, because this is where this session earns its keep.

There is usually a gap between what a family says matters and how that family actually spends its time and its money.

We will say family is the most important thing. Then look at where last year went.

**TOM: CENTER**

And that is not hypocrisy. That is being human.

The gap is almost never a character problem. It is a clarity problem. Nobody decided, so the calendar decided. And the calendar is not evil. It is just loud.

`[TOM: a family that looked at the gap between what they said mattered and where the time actually went, and what they did about it.]`

**BRETT: CENTER**

And Tom, the thing you have to be careful about here is judgment.

**TOM: CENTER**

You do. Including with yourself.

The point of naming the gap is not to feel bad about it. It is so you can do something about it. No shame. Just information.

> **Scripture.** 1 Corinthians 11:1. *Be imitators of me, just as I also am of Christ.* Paul is not being arrogant. He is acknowledging that people will imitate somebody, and he would rather it be pointed somewhere.

**BRETT: CENTER**

Tom, let me give you a scenario.

**SCENARIO ONE.** A father who has told his children a hundred times that generosity matters. And they have never once seen him give anything, because he has always given quietly and privately, on purpose, for good reasons.

**BRETT: TURN**

**TOM: CENTER**

He has the right instinct and the wrong application.

Because there is a difference between not announcing your giving to the world and not showing it to your children. Your children are not the world. They are the people you are training.

So let them see it. Let them be in the room when the decision gets made. Let them help decide. That is not showing off. That is the transfer.

**BRETT: CENTER**

Let me give you another version of this.

**SCENARIO TWO.** A family where the parents genuinely do live what they say. It is consistent. And if you asked the children what the family values, they would give you a vague answer, because it has never been named out loud.

**BRETT: TURN**

**TOM: CENTER**

`[TOM: a family where the modeling was strong and the naming was missing, and what changed when somebody finally said it.]`

Because modeling by itself is powerful and it is incomplete. Your kids see what you do. They do not automatically know why.

So they may copy the behavior and miss the reason. And a behavior without a reason does not survive a hard year.

You have to do both. Live it, and then say why.

**BRETT: CENTER**

To the next generation, here is the piece of this that belongs to you.

You are going to spend a fair amount of your adult life sorting out which parts of what you saw at home you actually want to keep.

That is not disloyalty. That is what the third legacy means. Your parents did it with what they received, whether they told you about it or not.

And the parts you keep on purpose will hold much better than the parts you keep by default.

**BRETT: CENTER**

**THIS WEEK.** Ask someone who lives with you what they would say your family values, based only on what they can see.

Then compare that to what you would have said.

**BRETT: TURN**

Tom, bring us home.

**TOM: CENTER**

You are not going to model this perfectly. Nobody does.

But your family does not need a perfect example. They need a real one that is pointed in the right direction, and that admits it when it is not.

**BRETT: CENTER**

Next session, the two things every family passes on whether they mean to or not.

We will see you there.

# 🎬 SESSION 4 — WEALTH AND WISDOM

Why One Without the Other Does Not Work

*Source: Workbook Legend S1; Coaching Overview script S1; Family Book ch. 4*

**BRETT: CENTER**

Welcome back to Family Legacy by Design. My name is Brett and this is Tom, and we are honored you are here.

We have talked about what legacy is, what a family is for, and the legacy you are living right now.

This session names the two things every family transfers. And I mean every family, at every level, whether they plan it or not.

**BRETT: TURN**

Tom, give them the two.

**TOM: CENTER**

Every family passes on two things. **Wealth,** and **wisdom.**

And the question is not whether that is happening. It is happening right now. The question is how.

Because if wealth is transferred without wisdom, it can create more problems than it solves.

**BRETT: CENTER**

And Tom, most systems are built to move one of those.

**TOM: CENTER**

That is the gap. There are entire industries built to move assets. There is almost nothing built to prepare people.

So you end up with a very sophisticated mechanism for transferring the first thing, and no mechanism at all for the second one. And then everybody is surprised at the outcome.

**BRETT: CENTER**

Tom, and this is where the word stewardship comes in, and you have strong feelings about that word.

**TOM: CENTER**

I do. Because stewardship means **management.** It does not mean giving.

The church has confused that, because we have stewardship campaigns and stewardship Sundays, and people hear the word and think it means the offering.

Stewardship is managing everything you have been entrusted with. Your money, your time, your relationships. All of it.

And underneath that is a conviction. God owns it all. We manage it. That is the whole arrangement.

**BRETT: CENTER**

And Tom, if that is true, then the question changes.

**TOM: CENTER**

It does. It is not what do I want to do with my money. It is what does the owner want done with it.

And that is a very different conversation, and honestly a freer one. Because you are not carrying it alone anymore.

> **Scripture.** Proverbs 4:7. *Acquire wisdom; and with all your acquiring, get understanding.* With all your acquiring. It assumes you are going to acquire. It just tells you what to get alongside it.

**BRETT: CENTER**

Tom, let me put the question on the table that this whole series comes back to.

**TOM: CENTER**

What amount of inheritance are you going to leave your children that will be a **blessing and not a curse?**

Because more is not automatically better. If somebody is not prepared, more creates problems instead of solving them.

`[TOM: a family where the wealth arrived and the wisdom had not, and what it cost. Relational shape. No figures.]`

**BRETT: CENTER**

Tom, let me give you a scenario.

**SCENARIO ONE.** A couple who have thought very carefully about the wealth side. Documents, structures, timing, all of it considered. And they have never once thought about what their children would need to know in order to handle it well.

**BRETT: TURN**

**TOM: CENTER**

They have done half the work extremely well.

And I would ask them one question. If this arrived tomorrow, what would each of your children do with it in the first year?

And most parents, if they are honest, can answer that immediately. They know. They have just never said it out loud, because saying it out loud feels like a judgment on their child.

It is not a judgment. It is a starting point. Because now you know exactly what preparing them means.

**BRETT: CENTER**

Let me give you another version of this.

**SCENARIO TWO.** A family with very little to transfer financially. Modest resources, no business, nothing complicated. And they have concluded that none of this applies to them.

**BRETT: TURN**

**TOM: CENTER**

They have it exactly backward.

Because the wisdom side is the half that transfers regardless of the amount. And it is the half that determines whether whatever does transfer helps or hurts.

A family with very little that transfers wisdom well will do better across three generations than a family with a great deal that transfers none.

And I have watched both.

**BRETT: CENTER**

To the next generation, one thing.

If something is coming to you, the useful question is not how much. It is what would I need to know in order to handle this well, and who could teach me.

Ask that question out loud, to your parents, before anything arrives. It will change how they think about the whole thing, and it will change how they think about you.

**BRETT: CENTER**

**THIS WEEK.** For each person who will receive something from you, write one sentence about what you hope it does in their life.

Not the amount. What it is for.

**BRETT: TURN**

Tom, bring us home.

**TOM: CENTER**

You are going to pass on both. That part is settled.

The only thing you get to decide is whether the wisdom arrives with the wealth, or somewhere behind it, or not at all.

**BRETT: CENTER**

Next session, how families drift when nobody meant to.

We will see you there.

# 🎬 SESSION 5 — WHY FAMILIES DRIFT

Legacy by Default and How It Happens

*Source: Workbook Legend S1; Coaching Overview script S2*

**BRETT: CENTER**

Welcome back to Family Legacy by Design. My name is Brett, I am with Tom, and we are grateful you are back.

This session is the honest one.

Because nobody plans to drift. No family sits down and decides to let this go badly. And it goes badly often enough that there is a whole body of work about why.

So we want to put the patterns in front of you. Not to frighten anybody. Because a pattern you can see is a pattern you can interrupt.

**BRETT: TURN**

So Tom, when families do not make it across generations, what actually goes wrong?

**TOM: CENTER**

It comes down to four things, and the order surprises people.

The biggest by far is a lack of **communication and trust.**

The second is **unprepared heirs.** The next generation receiving something they were never made ready for.

Third is a lack of **clarity** about what the family is trying to do together, and where each person fits in that.

And fourth, and this is the surprising one, is **the financial plan itself.** Taxes, investments, structures. That accounts for the smallest share of it by a wide margin.

**BRETT: CENTER**

Which is remarkable, Tom, because that is where nearly all of the attention goes.

**TOM: CENTER**

It is where all the attention goes because it is the part you can hire somebody to fix.

The other three you cannot outsource. They are yours.

> **Attribution note.** These proportions come from research on generational wealth transfer and should be cited when this is filmed. See the build notes.

**BRETT: CENTER**

Tom, and underneath those four, there is a pattern of specific things that show up. Give them the list.

**TOM: CENTER**

When families struggle, you tend to see these seven.

**No foundation of faith.**
**Lack of parental or grandparental involvement.**
**Lack of clarity about the family legacy.**
**Lack of communication among the generations.**
**Lack of intentionality.** Usually because everybody is too busy.
**Lack of preparation of the next generation.**
**Lack of generosity, or of any real spirit of stewardship.**

**BRETT: CENTER**

And Tom, not one of those is dramatic.

That is what makes it hard to catch. It is never one big failure. It is a pattern of small gaps that never get addressed, and over time the gaps start setting the direction.

**BRETT: TURN**

So give them the other side. What do you see in the families that do this well?

**TOM: CENTER**

Six things, and they line up almost exactly against the seven.

**A foundation of faith.**
**Shared values.**
**Intentional modeling** by the parents and the grandparents.
**Regular, open, honest communication.**
**Intentional preparation of the next generation.**
**A commitment to stewardship and generosity.**

And what stands out is that every one of those is developed. None of them just happen. They are modeled and reinforced and repeated over time.

> **Scripture.** Amos 3:3. *Do two people walk together unless they have agreed to do so?* Drift is not a decision. It is what happens in the absence of one.

**BRETT: CENTER**

Tom, let me give you a scenario.

**SCENARIO ONE.** A family where nothing is wrong. No conflict, no crisis, everybody is fine. And if you laid the seven against them honestly, four of them would be true.

**BRETT: TURN**

**TOM: CENTER**

That is the most common family in this whole series.

And I would tell them the good news, which is that four of seven at year ten is a very different situation than four of seven at year thirty. You have time and nothing has broken yet.

But do not wait for something to break in order to start. That is what most families do, and by then you are doing repair work instead of building work.

`[TOM: a family that started before anything was wrong, and what that made possible later.]`

**BRETT: CENTER**

Let me give you another version of this.

**SCENARIO TWO.** A family where something has already broken. There is real distance. Somebody is not speaking to somebody. And they are hearing all of this thinking it is too late for them.

**BRETT: TURN**

**TOM: CENTER**

It is not too late.

I have watched families come back from things I did not think they would come back from. And it almost always started the same way, which is that one person decided to stop waiting for the other one to move first.

You do not have to fix everything. You have to be the one who goes first. And that is available to anybody watching this, today.

**BRETT: CENTER**

To the next generation, here is what I would say about this session.

Look at the seven again and notice how many of them are about somebody not doing something. Not involving. Not clarifying. Not communicating. Not preparing.

Almost none of them are about a wrong action. They are about absence.

Which means the correction is almost always small. It is showing up, asking, and saying the thing. And you can do all three of those without anybody's permission.

**BRETT: CENTER**

**THIS WEEK.** Look at the seven points of failure honestly and name the one that is most true of your family right now.

Just name it. Do not fix it yet.

**BRETT: TURN**

Tom, bring us home.

**TOM: CENTER**

Every family is going to have a legacy. That part is not optional.

The only question is whether it is one somebody chose, or one that just happened while everybody was busy.

By design, or by default.

**BRETT: CENTER**

Next session is our last, and it is where the whole system comes together.

We will see you there.

# 🎬 SESSION 6 — THE PATH AHEAD

Clarity, Alignment, and the Conversations That Carry Them

*Source: Workbook Legend S1; Coaching Overview script S1 and S6*
