# Day 16

## The Twin W's

### Every family passes on both

**"Acquire wisdom; and with all your acquiring, get understanding."** PROVERBS 4:7

Every family passes on two things. Wealth and wisdom.

We call them the twin W's, and the reason for the pairing is that they always travel together, whether or not anybody arranged it.

The question is never whether both are being transferred. It is what condition each of them is in when it arrives.

Here is the asymmetry that causes most of the trouble.

Wealth transfers on its own. It has custodians, statements, documents, and a legal system built to move it. You could do nothing and it would still arrive, at a date determined by events rather than by you.

Wisdom has no such machinery. There is no institution responsible for it. Nobody sends a reminder. It moves only if a person deliberately moves it, over years, and it moves through relationship rather than through paperwork.

Which means the two are on completely different timelines and only one of them is on a schedule.

And here is what follows. If a family does nothing in particular, wealth arrives fully and wisdom arrives partially or not at all, and the family will experience that as a surprise despite it being entirely predictable.

Now, I want to define wisdom here, because it is a word that can float free.

I do not primarily mean spiritual insight, though that is part of it. I mean the accumulated practical judgment that produced whatever your family has. How to make a decision under uncertainty. What a dollar costs to earn. How to tell whether somebody is trustworthy. When to hold and when to move. What matters and what only looks like it matters.

That is a real body of knowledge and it exists almost entirely in your head.

Proverbs says acquire wisdom, and with all your acquiring, get understanding. The construction is interesting. It assumes acquiring is happening. It is not counseling against it.

It says that alongside whatever else you are gathering, gather this.

Most families are excellent at the first half of that sentence and have never consciously attempted the second.

# Day 17

## Wealth Without Wisdom

### It creates more problems than it solves

**"An inheritance gained hurriedly at the beginning will not be blessed in the end."** PROVERBS 20:21

If wealth transfers automatically and wisdom does not, then the default outcome for a family that does nothing in particular is wealth without wisdom.

And wealth transferred without wisdom creates more problems than it solves.

I want to be precise about why, because the usual explanation is moral and I do not think that is the mechanism.

The usual explanation is that money corrupts people. I have not found that to be true in the way it is usually meant. The people I have watched handle a transfer badly were not corrupted by it. They were unequipped for it.

Here is the difference. A corrupted person becomes worse. An unequipped person becomes overwhelmed, and overwhelmed people make a predictable series of decisions.

They defer to whoever seems confident. They avoid the parts they do not understand, which is most of it. They spend on things that produce a feeling of control. And they say yes to people they should say no to, because saying no requires a framework they were never given.

None of that is a character failure. It is what anyone does when handed something they have no basis for handling.

And the family watching concludes that the money ruined them, which produces the wrong response next time, which is to hold on longer and tell them less.

Proverbs says an inheritance gained hurriedly will not be blessed in the end. The operative word is hurriedly, and it describes a pace rather than an amount.

Hurriedly is what happens when everything arrives at once because nothing arrived before.

Which suggests the remedy, and it is available to any family at any size.

Slow it down. Let something small arrive early. Let them handle it and see what happens. Let something slightly larger arrive after that.

You are not testing them. You are building the framework that makes the eventual arrival survivable, and the framework can only be built by experience.

A person who has managed something at twenty-five is not the same person at fifty-five as one who has not, regardless of what they were told.

# Day 18

## God Owns It All

### We manage it

**"The earth is the Lord's, and all it contains, the world, and those who live in it."** PSALM 24:1

Underneath the twin W's is a conviction, and if a family does not hold it, everything else in this series is technique.

God owns it all. We manage it.

That is the whole arrangement, and it changes the question entirely.

If you own it, the question is what do I want to do with my money. That is a legitimate question and it has no external reference. Your preferences are the standard, and reasonable people can reach any conclusion.

If you manage it, the question becomes what does the owner want done with it. That is a different question and it has a reference point outside yourself.

I want to be careful here, because this can be preached in a way that produces guilt and extracts money, and that is not what I mean.

It is actually a freer position, not a more constrained one.

Here is why. If you own it, you carry the entire weight of every decision. Every allocation is yours to justify, every mistake is yours alone, and the accumulated responsibility is genuinely heavy. I have met people crushed by it who could not have told you what was crushing them.

If you manage it, the weight is different. You are accountable, which is real, and you are not ultimately responsible for outcomes in the same way. A manager who acts faithfully with what was entrusted has done the job, whether or not the results were spectacular.

That is a lighter position and most people who hold it will tell you so.

And it clarifies the family conversation enormously. If it is ours, then who gets what is a negotiation among people with competing claims. If it is His, then the conversation is about what would be a faithful arrangement, which is a question a family can actually answer together.

The psalm says the earth is the Lord's and everything in it, and then adds a phrase people skip. And those who live in it.

Not only the assets. The people.

Which includes the children you are trying to decide about, who were also given rather than produced.

# Day 19

## What Would It Do in Their Life

### Not how much. What for.

**"A good person leaves an inheritance to his children's children."** PROVERBS 13:22

Here is the question that changes an inheritance conversation, and most families have never asked it.

Not how much. What for.

What do you want this to accomplish in the life of each person who receives it?

I ask that early and the pause is always long, because families arrive having thought carefully about amounts and structures and timing and never once about purpose.

And without purpose, there is no basis at all for deciding the amount. If you do not know what you hope it will do in somebody's life, you have no way to evaluate whether more or less would serve them better.

So the number gets decided by default, which usually means as much as possible, structured as efficiently as possible.

Now here is where it gets uncomfortable, and useful.

Ask the question person by person rather than in general. What would this do in her life. What would this do in his.

The answers are different, and the differences are the whole point.

For one child it might fund something they are already building, and it would accelerate a direction that is clearly right. For another it might remove a pressure that has been producing growth. For a third it might arrive at exactly the wrong moment in a difficult stretch.

Same amount. Three completely different effects, and only one of them is the one you imagined when you were thinking about the total.

The proverb says a good person leaves an inheritance to his children's children. Two generations, which means whatever you leave has to survive a handoff performed by somebody else.

That reframes the purpose question. It is not only what would this do in your child's life. It is what would this do in the life of somebody you may never meet, handled by somebody you are currently deciding about.

Which brings you back to preparation, every time, from whatever direction you approach it.

The amount is downstream. The purpose is the decision.

### Reflect

# Day 20

## Prepared to Handle It

### That's your responsibility, not theirs

**"It is required of stewards that one be found trustworthy."** 1 CORINTHIANS 4:2

This is the last day of the session and it puts the responsibility where it belongs.

If you are going to pass something on to your children, you need to make sure they are prepared to handle it.

That is your responsibility. Not theirs.

I want to defend that claim, because it can sound like an unfair burden.

A person cannot prepare for something they have not been told about. They cannot practice with something they have never been trusted with. And they cannot develop judgment about a situation they have never been placed in.

All three of those require somebody else to act first, and the somebody else is you.

Which means that the common framing, that the next generation needs to step up and get ready, is backwards. They cannot get ready alone. Readiness is something you build with somebody.

Here is what preparation actually consists of, and none of it requires a large sum.

Telling them what exists, in stages, appropriate to age. Giving somebody a real responsibility with a real consequence. Letting a decision be made and letting the result stand. Answering questions honestly rather than deflecting until later. Explaining reasoning rather than announcing conclusions.

That is the whole curriculum and it takes years.

Now the thing that makes families hesitate, which is worth naming. Preparation requires letting somebody handle something before you are confident they will handle it well.

There is no way around that. Trustworthiness develops through being trusted, which means the first extension of trust always precedes the evidence for it.

Paul says it is required of stewards that one be found trustworthy. Required, not preferred.

And found, which implies a moment of examination that arrives whether or not anybody prepared for it.

Your children will be examined. Not by you, and possibly not while you are here.

The only variable you control is whether anything was built beforehand.

## Sixty Percent

### It is not the plan

**"Two are better than one, because they have a good return for their labor."** ECCLESIASTES 4:9

When families do not make it across generations, the reasons are known and they are not the ones people expect.

The largest share by a wide margin is communication and trust. Roughly sixty percent.

Next is unprepared heirs, at about a quarter.

Then a lack of clarity about what the family is trying to do together, at around ten percent.

And last, at under five percent, the financial plan itself. Taxes, investments, structures.

That ordering is the single most useful piece of information in this book, and it is almost exactly inverted from where families put their attention.

I want to sit on the last item, because it is where nearly all professional effort goes.

The financial plan accounts for the smallest slice. Not zero, and it matters, and I have spent a career on it. But it is the part you can hire somebody to handle, which is precisely why it receives the attention. It has a solution, the solution can be purchased, and purchasing it produces the feeling that the matter has been addressed.

The other three cannot be outsourced. There is nobody to hire for communication and trust. Nobody can prepare your heirs for you. And no professional can tell your family what it is for.

Those are yours, and they are the ninety-five percent.

Now, I do not offer these numbers to alarm anyone. I offer them because they redirect effort.

A family that spends one more evening on the estate plan is optimizing a slice that was already mostly fine. A family that spends the same evening telling their children something true is working on the largest share.

Ecclesiastes says two are better than one because of the return on their labor. It is a plain statement about the productivity of relationship, and it sits in a book largely concerned with the futility of effort.

Even there, relationship is described as producing a return.

That is the finding. Relationship is not the soft part of this work. It is where the arithmetic actually is.

# Day 22

## Seven Points of Failure

### None of them is dramatic

**"By wisdom a house is built, and by understanding it is established."** PROVERBS 24:3

When families struggle across generations, the same seven things show up.

No foundation of faith.

Lack of parental or grandparental involvement.

Lack of clarity about the family legacy.

Lack of communication among the generations.

Lack of intentionality, which is almost always because everybody is too busy.

Lack of preparation of the next generation.

And lack of generosity, or of any real spirit of stewardship.

I want you to notice what is not on that list.

There is no scandal. No betrayal. No bad actor. No single catastrophic decision.

Every one of the seven is an absence. Something that did not happen, over a long period, without anyone noticing that it was not happening.

That is what makes drift so difficult to catch. A family looking for problems will not find these, because they are not problems. They are gaps, and gaps do not announce themselves.

And each one, individually, is unremarkable. A busy year with no intentionality is not a crisis. A decade of them is a different family.

Here is the practical value of the list.

It gives you something to check against, and checking is the only way an absence becomes visible. Without a list, a family assesses itself by asking whether anything is wrong, and the answer is usually no, and the assessment ends there.

With a list, you can find four things that are simply missing.

Now the good news, and it is substantial.

Four of seven at year ten is a completely different situation from four of seven at year thirty. Absences can be filled. Nothing on that list requires undoing anything. Every item is available to start this year.

Proverbs says a house is built by wisdom and established by understanding. Two verbs.

Building is the visible part and most families have done it. Establishing is what makes it hold, and it is the part that consists largely of things that do not look like activity.

# Day 23

## Six Points of Success

### Every one of them is developed

**"Let us consider how to encourage one another in love and good deeds."** HEBREWS 10:24

The families that make it across generations show six things, and they line up almost exactly against the seven.

A foundation of faith.

Shared values.

Intentional modeling by the parents and the grandparents.

Regular, open, honest communication.

Intentional preparation of the next generation.

And a commitment to stewardship and generosity.

Read those and notice what is common to all six.

Not one of them happens by itself.

Every single one is developed, modeled, reinforced, and repeated over time. There is no version of this list that a family stumbles into.

That is the finding I would want to leave with a family reading this session. The families that hold together are not luckier, and they are not made of better material. They did specific things, repeatedly, and the things are not secret.

I also want to point out what is not on the list.

There is nothing about the amount of money. Nothing about the structure. Nothing about the quality of professional advice, which I say as somebody who provides it.

None of those distinguish the families that hold from the families that do not.

Now the word intentional appears twice in six items, and I do not think that is accidental.

Intentional modeling. Intentional preparation.

Both of those are things families do accidentally all the time. You model whether you mean to. You prepare somebody by default whether you mean to.

The word intentional is what separates the two lists. The same activities occur in both kinds of family. In one they are chosen.

Hebrews says consider how to encourage one another. Consider is the operative verb. Give thought to it, plan it, work out how.

Encouragement is being described as a thing requiring forethought, which is a strange way to describe something we usually treat as spontaneous warmth.

That is the difference between the two lists, in one word.

# Day 24

## Nobody Plans to Drift

### It is not a decision

**"Therefore we must pay much closer attention to what we have heard, so that we do not drift away from it."** HEBREWS 2:1

Nobody plans to drift.

No family sits down and decides that the next twenty years will produce something nobody chose. That is not how it happens and it is worth understanding how it does.

Drift is not a decision. It is the absence of one, extended over time.

Here is the actual sequence, and every step is reasonable.

Something needs attention and it is not urgent, so it waits. Something else becomes urgent and takes the time that would have gone to the first thing. A year passes with nothing decided about the first thing, and its status quietly changes from pending to settled without anyone settling it.

Meanwhile the family changes. Children grow, marry, move. Circumstances shift. And the arrangements made for a family that no longer exists remain in force, because arrangements do not expire.

At no point did anybody choose anything. And yet after twenty years the family is somewhere specific, with specific arrangements, producing specific outcomes.

That is a legacy by default. Not neglect. Accumulation.

Now here is what makes it hard to interrupt.

Drift produces no signal. There is no moment where a family notices it is drifting, because drifting feels exactly like ordinary life. The signal only arrives when something forces a comparison, and by then a great deal has accumulated.

Which is why families need a scheduled comparison. Something that forces the question annually, whether or not anything appears to require it.

Hebrews says pay much closer attention so that you do not drift away. The image is nautical. A boat that is not steered does not stay where it was. It goes wherever the current goes, and the movement is imperceptible from on board.

Notice the remedy offered. Not correction. Attention.

You do not fix drift by steering harder once. You prevent it by looking, regularly, at where you actually are compared to where you meant to be.

That is an annual conversation and it does not need to be long.

# Day 25

## It Assembled Itself

### Something will happen. The question is whether you chose it.

**"The plans of the diligent lead certainly to advantage, but everyone who is hasty comes certainly to poverty."** PROVERBS 21:5

This is the last day of the session, and here is the plain version.

A legacy by default is not the absence of a plan. It is a plan that assembled itself.

Something will happen when you die. That part is settled. Documents exist or they do not, and either way a process runs and outcomes occur.

The question is not whether. It is whether anybody chose it.

I want to name the two ways a family ends up here, because they are different and they need different responses.

The first is the family with nothing in place. No documents, or documents from twenty years ago, and a general intention to sort it out. That family will have their outcomes determined by the state they happen to live in, and I guarantee they would not have chosen it.

The second is more common among families who read books like this. Everything is technically in place. The documents exist and are current and were drafted well.

And nobody has decided what any of it is for.

That family has a plan for the mechanics and a default for the meaning, and the meaning is the part that determines whether the mechanics help anybody.

Both are legacies by default. One is obvious and the other is invisible, and the second is more dangerous because it looks handled.

The proverb sets diligence against haste, and the word that matters is certainly. Both roads have certain outcomes. Neither is a coin flip.

I take that to mean the following. Doing nothing is not neutral, and it is not a delay. It is a decision with a known result, and the result is that somebody else decides.

Sometimes that somebody is a state. Sometimes it is whichever of your children is most comfortable being forceful in a difficult week.

You still get to choose. That is the entire point of the book in your hands, and of the five that follow it.

But the window in which choosing is available is not permanent, and it is shorter than most people assume.

# Day 26

## The Order Matters

### You cannot align around something never made clear

**"Let all things be done properly and in an orderly way."** 1 CORINTHIANS 14:40

Three words run through everything that follows this book.

Clarity. Alignment. Communication.

And they go in that order for a reason that is not stylistic.

Clarity is being clear on what actually matters. Alignment is getting your life, your decisions, and your documents to reflect it. Communication is telling the people it affects.

Here is why the order is not optional.

You cannot align around something that has never been made clear. You will align around something, because alignment is not avoidable, and what you will align around is whatever is loudest. Usually a deadline, a tax consequence, or the opinion of whoever pushes hardest.

And you cannot communicate something nobody has defined. You will communicate something, because families always do. It will be a version assembled from fragments, and it will not be yours.

So a family that starts in the wrong place does not fail to make progress. It makes progress toward something nobody selected.

I see the inverted order constantly and it looks like this.

A family starts with communication, because a conversation is urgent. So they gather everyone and talk, and the conversation has no anchor, and it produces a lot of feeling and no direction.

Or a family starts with alignment, because a professional raised something, so documents get drafted against preferences that were never examined. The paperwork is excellent and it encodes a set of assumptions nobody has tested.

Both families worked hard. Neither started at the source.

Paul says let all things be done properly and in an orderly way. It is said about worship in a chaotic church and it is a claim about sequence rather than about tidiness.

Order is not decoration. Some things depend on others and cannot be done first.

That is the map for the five books ahead. Clarity, then alignment, then the conversations that carry both.

You are not required to do them in order. Most families should.

**1.** Which of the three has your family attempted first?

# Day 27

## Family Harmony and Wise Transfer

### The two things we want for every family

**"How good and how pleasant it is for brothers to live together in unity!"** PSALM 133:1

Everything in these six books serves two outcomes.

Family harmony. And the wise transfer of both wealth and wisdom.

That is all. Every question, every framework, every uncomfortable conversation is aimed at one or the other.

I want to say a word about the order, because most people assume the second is the objective and the first is a pleasant side effect.

It runs the other way.

If a family transfers everything perfectly and the relationships do not survive, nobody would call that success. I have watched families execute a technically flawless transition and end with siblings who do not speak.

The plan worked. The family did not.

And it is not a trade-off. Harmony is not something you spend in order to buy a better structure. In practice the families with the most harmony transfer most successfully, because trust makes preparation possible, and preparation determines whether the transfer holds.

One is the soil the other grows in.

Now, harmony gets misread, so let me be precise.

It does not mean agreement. It does not mean the absence of conflict, and a family with no conflict at all is usually a family where somebody has stopped speaking rather than a family at peace.

Harmony in the musical sense is different notes held together. That is closer. Different people, genuinely different, holding.

And wise transfer is not the same as efficient transfer. Efficient means the maximum arrives with the minimum lost along the way. Wise means it arrives in a form and at a time that serves the person receiving it, which is frequently not the maximum and frequently not the soonest.

Efficiency is a technical question with a professional answer. Wisdom is a family question with no professional answer at all.

The psalm calls unity good and pleasant and treats it as worth beholding. Not easy. Not automatic.

Something a family achieves rather than something a family has.

**2.** Is your family peaceful or is somebody quiet?

# Day 28

## Start With the Two of You

### Before you bring anybody else in

**"Two are better than one. And if one can overpower him who is alone, two can resist him."** ECCLESIASTES 4:9,12

Here is the practical first step, and it is smaller than families expect.

Start with the two of you.

Before you bring the children in, before any meeting, before any professional, the two people at the center have to be able to say what they want to each other.

Because if you cannot say it to each other, you certainly will not be able to say it to your family.

I put this near the end because it is the thing most likely to actually happen after somebody closes this book. A family meeting requires coordination. An evening does not.

And in a great many marriages, this conversation has never occurred. Not because of secrecy. Because there was never an occasion, and the years supplied plenty of decisions and no reason to examine the assumptions underneath them.

What I have watched happen in that evening is worth describing.

Two people who have been married for decades discover that they are not in the same place about something significant. Usually not opposed. Adjacent, in a way neither had noticed, because they had each been operating from a slightly different picture.

That discovery is not a crisis. It is the most valuable thing available to that family, and it can only happen between two people in a room.

Now, for readers without a spouse, and there will be many. Widowed, divorced, single, or married to somebody who will not engage.

The principle holds and the person changes. There is somebody who will eventually have to understand what you decided. A sibling, an adult child, a friend who will be executor. Start with them.

Somebody has to be the first person who knows.

Ecclesiastes says two are better than one, and then adds that two can resist what would overpower one alone.

The context is not marriage specifically. It is about the difference between carrying something alone and carrying it with somebody, and the claim is that the second is not merely more pleasant.

It is more durable.

A great many people reading this have been carrying the whole picture alone for a very long time and have not noticed the weight, because they have never put it down.

# Day 30

## Be the One Who Is Ready

### Somebody in every family goes first

**"But as for me and my house, we will serve the Lord."** JOSHUA 24:15

This is the last day of the first book, and I want to end with the person most likely to be reading it.

Somebody who cares about this more than the rest of their family does.

That is not a criticism of anyone. In almost every family I have worked with, one person is further along on this than the others, and they are frequently uncertain whether they have any standing to raise it.

You do.

Everything in this book works for one person. You can get clear alone. You can look at what you have alone. You can examine what you are passing on alone. And a person who has done that changes the room they walk into, whether or not anybody asked them to.

Do not wait for your family to be ready. Be the one who is ready.

And I want to say something specific to whoever is not the oldest person in their family.

Somebody in every family goes first, and there is no rule that it has to be the patriarch or the matriarch. I have watched this start with a daughter. With a son-in-law. With somebody who simply got tired of everyone guessing.

If it is you, you are not overstepping. Asking your family to talk about where it is going is not a claim to authority. It is an offer.

Now the honest part. You may be the one who keeps going first, more than once, without being thanked, and possibly without anybody else ever taking a turn.

That is not failure. That is what it usually looks like, and it is still worth doing.

Joshua says as for me and my house, we will serve the Lord. It is said at the end of a long life to a nation being asked to choose, and it is notable for its grammar.

He does not say I hope. He does not say we should. He does not wait to see what anybody else decides.

He declares it about his household, as the person responsible for it, out loud, in front of witnesses, and then he lets everyone else answer for themselves.

That is what going first looks like.

So decide about your house. Then say it out loud to one person.
