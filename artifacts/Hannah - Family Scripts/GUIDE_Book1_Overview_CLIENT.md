# A WELCOME FROM TOM AND BRETT

This is the front door.

Everything else in the Family Foundation Series comes after this book, and this book has one job. To make sure everyone in your family means the same thing by the word legacy before anybody spends time on it.

Between the two of us we have spent a long time in rooms where families talk about what happens next. Tom has sat across the table from them for about thirty years. Brett has spent most of his career building material that helps people have conversations they would not otherwise have.

For a long stretch, Tom thought he understood the job. Help people get clear on what they want to do with what they have been entrusted with. Make sure the documents match. Communicate it to the people who will inherit it.

Then he started noticing that families with what looked like airtight plans were coming apart anyway.

Eventually we had to admit the problem was also with the way all of us had been trained. The profession gets very good at the money and leaves the people mostly alone.

We know a family whose relationships have nearly been destroyed by the very thing they spent forty years building. When we asked whether they would be better off without any of it, they said they would not go that far. And then they said something we have not been able to put down.

*We wish we had known more of the kinds of things you are talking about.*

That is why this exists.

**And here is what we most want you to hear before you begin.**

**Most families never do this.**

Not because they do not love each other. Because nobody ever asked them to sit down and think about it, and there is no natural moment when it comes up on its own.

So if you are opening this and feeling behind, you are not. You are ahead of nearly everyone, by the width of one decision.

You do not have to do it perfectly. You do not have to do it with everyone. And you do not have to finish it today.

# USING THIS GUIDE

## What this book is and is not

This book will not tell you what to put in your will, how to structure anything, or what to say to your children.

Those come later, and they come in the five books that follow. This one is about the question underneath all of them, which is what you are actually trying to pass on.

If you have already started somewhere else in the series, you have not done anything wrong. Most families should begin with **Family Clarity**. This one is for the family that wants to know what they are getting into first, or for the person who needs to be able to explain to somebody else why any of it matters.

## This works alone, with your spouse, or with your whole family

You can do every part of this by yourself.

**With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write it down.**

## Who leads this

Somebody has to press play and read the questions out loud. That person is a **host**, not a teacher.

Start the video. Read the question. Then be quiet long enough for somebody to answer it.

It should probably not be the person who usually decides things in your family, and it should rotate if you can manage it.

**Three rules for whoever has the chair.** Read the question and then stop talking. Answer last, not first. And you are not responsible for fixing anything that comes up.

## If your family does not look like the one in this book

**If there is no spouse.** Every session has a section built around a husband and wife. Read it as being about the first person who will know. Somebody eventually has to understand what you decided.

**If your family is blended.** Session 2, on what a family is for, matters more in your situation than in almost any other, because the answer was never inherited and has to be built.

**If your resources are modest.** Almost every example in this material involves families with a great deal. Session 4 is the one where that will feel most true, and it is also the session where the amount matters least. Wisdom transfers at any scale, and it is the half that determines what the other half does.

# What Is a Legacy?

## Understanding What You Are Already Passing On

**HOST NOTE.** People will assume this session is abstract and will disengage. Say at the start that it ends with five sentences that are entirely concrete, and that the whole session is aimed at them.

## INTRODUCTION

Here is the question most people bring to this book.

**What am I actually leaving behind?**

And underneath it, usually unsaid, a second one. Whether it will be enough, and whether it will be the right kind of thing.

**KEY VERSE**

"So I hated all the fruit of my labor for which I had labored under the sun, for I must leave it to the man who will come after me. And who knows whether he will be a wise man or a fool?"

**ECCLESIASTES 2:18-19**

When most people hear the word legacy, they think about something that happens at the end of life.

That is part of it. It is a very small part of it.

Legacy is not just what you leave behind. It is what you pass on every day. Something you received from the people who came before you, and something you are transmitting to the people who come after you.

So the question changes. Not just what am I going to leave. What am I building into the lives of the people around me right now.

Solomon's anxiety in that verse is under nearly every family we have sat with. And notice what he never considers. He never asks whether the person could be prepared.

## THE ONE QUESTION

> **If the people closest to you described what is being passed on right now, would it feel intentional, or would it feel like default?**
>
> Answer that one and the session worked. Everything else is optional.

## OPENING STORY

**More Than What You Leave**

He had built a substantial company and handed it to his son. By any measure a planner would use, the file was closed. The business had grown. Succession was done, and done well.

Then, in conversation, he described a set of projects he had taken on in some struggling neighborhoods near where the company was headquartered. Places that stood to gain far more from his presence and his leadership than from a check.

He said the company was no longer his day job.

This was his day job. This was his passion.

There is nothing in a standard planning process that would ever have surfaced it. Everything in the process is built to capture what you have. Nothing in it asks what you are for.

He had spent decades building what the world calls a legacy, and then went looking, on his own, for the one he actually wanted.

## THE FRAMEWORK

### The Three Kinds of Legacy

- **The one you received.** You did not choose it. Some of it is wonderful, some is hard, and both are shaping you. You can examine it and decide what to carry. You cannot change that it happened.

- **The one you will pass on.** The one everybody thinks about. It is planned, arranged, and none of it has moved yet.

- **The one you are living out today.** This is the most important, and the reason is mechanical rather than moral. It is the only one currently transmitting.

The received one has already happened. The one you will leave has not begun. The one being lived is moving, today, into the people around you, whether or not you have thought about it.

**Which means the correction available to you is real rather than theoretical, and it starts this week rather than at the end.**

## WHERE YOU ARE STARTING

**If you have never thought about any of this.** Start with the reality check. Five sentences, all obvious, almost never said out loud. Say them and see what happens.

**If you have thought about it constantly and done nothing.** You are not procrastinating in the ordinary sense. The task has no deadline and produces no visible result. Name a date rather than an intention.

**If you are late to this.** Grown children, a good deal already behind you. The third legacy is still transmitting, which means what is available to you is not a theory. An adult child hearing a parent say I got that wrong and I have been thinking about it receives something they will remember far longer than the original failure.

# The Family God Designed

## Why Your Family Was Built to Last Longer Than You

**HOST NOTE.** This session names something most people have never questioned. Expect somebody to say the question is strange. That reaction is the finding.

## INTRODUCTION

Here is a question almost nobody in a family has been asked.

**What is my family even for now?**

It is the question that arrives after the children are grown. The organizing purpose was raising them, and it worked, and now what.

**KEY VERSE**

"I will establish My covenant between Me and you and your descendants after you throughout their generations."

**GENESIS 17:7**

There is a modern view of family and an older one, and most of us are living the first without ever having chosen it.

The modern view is that we raise our children to independence. We raise them up and we kick them out. There is a place for that and you do not want them living in the basement at thirty-five.

But if independence is the goal, then the finish line is the day they no longer need you. And once you cross that line, what exactly is left?

The older view is interdependence. Multiple generations loving and serving one another, staying connected, continuing to influence each other over time.

Notice the horizon in the covenant. Throughout their generations. Longer than a lifetime.

## THE ONE QUESTION

> **Is your family a group of people who used to live together, or something that still has a purpose?**
>
> Answer that one and the session worked.

## OPENING STORY

**The Wrong Finish Line**

Tom worked with a father who had done the modern version well.

He raised capable children. They were independent, successful, and none of them needed anything from him. By every measure available he had succeeded.

And he was not sure what his role was anymore.

He had not failed. He had succeeded at the wrong finish line.

What Tom pointed out was that his role had changed rather than ended. He was no longer provider and no longer decision maker. He had become the person who holds the family's memory, and who can say things nobody else in that family has standing to say.

That is not a lesser role. In most cultures for most of history it was the most important one.

**TOM CONWAY**

## THE FRAMEWORK

### Independence and Interdependence

- **Independence is not wrong.** You want capable people who could run their own lives. The interdependence version still requires that work.

- **But independence as the goal has a finish line,** and the finish line arrives around the time your children turn thirty, and nothing in the modern view describes what comes after it.

- **Interdependence is not dependence.** A family where an adult cannot function without their parents is not interdependent, it is arrested.

- **And it is not enmeshment.** It does not mean everyone is in everyone's business, or that decisions require consensus, or that adult children owe an accounting.

It means people who could function separately choose not to entirely.

**That is a disposition rather than an arrangement, which means it can be true of a family in three time zones.**

## WHERE YOU ARE STARTING

**If your children are still at home.** You have the easiest version of this, because the practices you set now become normal rather than being introduced later. Decide what your family is for while everyone is still in one house.

**If your children are grown and doing well.** This is the most common place to be reading this, and the risk is not conflict. It is a quiet thinning where nothing is wrong and nothing is being built.

**If your family has scattered.** Geography is not the obstacle people think it is. Interdependence is a disposition, and disposition travels.

## THE TOOL

### The Family Purpose Question

Ask each adult in your family what they believe the purpose of your family is, now that everyone is grown.

Ask separately. Do not correct any answer. Write them all down.

Three things you will usually find.

The answers differ more than anybody guessed. At least one person will find the question strange, which is itself the finding, because it means they have been operating from the modern view without knowing there was another one.

And one person will have been waiting to be asked.

Then, once you have collected them, ask a second question. Not what is our family for, but what would we lose if we stopped being a family in any active sense.

That second question produces different answers than the first, and the two together are what a family actually has to work with.

# SESSION 3

# The Legacy You Are Living Right Now

## What Your Family Sees When You Are Not Explaining Yourself

**HOST NOTE.** This session can produce real discomfort, and the correction is at the end of it rather than the beginning. If somebody sinks, tell them Session 3 ends with no shame in it, and mean it.

## INTRODUCTION

Here is the question in this one, and parents carry it whether or not they say it.

**What are they learning from me?**

Not what have I taught them. What are they picking up, from watching, that I never intended to teach.

**KEY VERSE**

"Be imitators of me, just as I also am of Christ."

**1 CORINTHIANS 11:1**

There are two legacies running in every family and only one of them has started.

The intended legacy is the one you have in mind. It is careful, it reflects your best thinking, and it is aimed at a moment in the future.

The lived legacy is what people are actually observing. It has been running the whole time, without review.

People do not replicate what you say. They replicate what you live.

Paul is not being arrogant in that verse. He is acknowledging something about how people learn, which is that they copy somebody, and the only question is who.

## THE ONE QUESTION

> **What is the distance between your intended legacy and your lived one?**
>
> Answer that one and the session worked.

## OPENING STORY

**He Gave Quietly for Thirty Years**

A man Tom worked with intended to pass on generosity.

He had arranged significant charitable giving at the end of his life, structured carefully, and he meant every bit of it. And he had given all his life, quietly and privately, on principle, because that is what he believed Scripture asked of him.

His children had never seen him give anything.

So what was he passing on? Not generosity. Discretion, maybe. Privacy. Possibly the impression that this family keeps money to itself.

Nothing about his intention was wrong. It simply never got transmitted, because transmission requires witnesses.

The fix was not to change the intention. It was to let his children be in the room when a decision got made, and to say why, out loud, once.

## THE FRAMEWORK

### What They Replicate

- **The lived legacy is the only one transmitting.** The intended one has not begun. Whatever is true in your house this year is moving, this year.

- **The small consistent things transmit most.** Not the large failures, which are visible and usually get addressed. How you talk about people who are not in the room. What you say when you are tired. Whether you follow through on something minor.

- **The repair teaches more than the performance.** A family that watches a person fail and then make it right has learned something a polished version could not teach.

- **And modeling without naming is incomplete.** Your children see what you do. They do not automatically know why. A behavior without a reason does not survive a hard year.

**Live it, and then say why. Most families do one of those two.**

## WHERE YOU ARE STARTING

**If you have been intentional and never said so.** You are further along than you think. What is missing is the naming, which takes one sentence and turns a pattern into something a person can choose rather than just inherit.

**If you have found a gap you did not expect.** That is not hypocrisy. Nobody decided that work would take the place family was supposed to have. It happened one reasonable yes at a time.

**If you are reading this late, with grown children and a good deal behind you.** The material can read as a list of things you did not do. The third legacy is still transmitting, and an adult child hearing a parent say I got that wrong receives something they will remember far longer than the original failure.

## THE TOOL

### Three Sources of Evidence

You do not find the gap by reflection. Reflection tells you what you value, and you already know that.

You find it by looking at evidence.

**Last year's calendar.** Where the time actually went, not where you meant it to go.

**Where the money actually went.** Not the budget. The statements.

**KEY VERSE**

"Acquire wisdom; and with all your acquiring, get understanding."

Every family passes on two things. Wealth and wisdom.

# Why Families Drift

## Legacy by Default and How It Happens

**HOST NOTE.** The seven points will land on somebody. Emphasize that four of seven at year ten is a completely different situation from four of seven at year thirty, and that every item on the list is an absence rather than a failure.

## INTRODUCTION

Here is the question in this session.

**How did we end up here?**

Because nothing dramatic happened. There was no crisis, no betrayal, no single bad decision. And yet the family is somewhere specific that nobody chose.

**KEY VERSE**

"Do two people walk together unless they have agreed to do so?"

**AMOS 3:3**

Nobody plans to drift.

Drift is not a decision. It is the absence of one, extended over time. Something needs attention and it is not urgent, so it waits. Something else becomes urgent. A year passes, and the status of the first thing quietly changes from pending to settled without anyone settling it.

Meanwhile the family changes and the arrangements do not, because arrangements do not expire.

At no point did anybody choose anything. And after twenty years the family is somewhere specific.

That is a legacy by default. Not neglect. Accumulation.

## THE ONE QUESTION

>

Tom sat with a family where nothing was wrong.

Absences can be filled. Nothing on that list requires undoing anything.
