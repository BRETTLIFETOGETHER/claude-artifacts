**The lighter cut.** For advisors, FAMILY is a collection checklist: gather it, review it, find the gaps, produce the report. For families it is a conversation checklist: do we both know, do the kids know, and what happens if. Same six letters. Different unit of work.

**What came out.** Form numbers. Irrevocable life insurance trust mechanics. Cost basis and step-up. Discounts for non-controlling interests. Estate tax percentages. Anything that requires a professional to act on. **What stayed.** Every relational consequence.

**Where advisors get named.** Each session ends by identifying the moment a family should bring in help. Honest, useful to the family, and it feeds the coaching side without turning the title into a sales pitch.

# 🎬 SESSION 2 — HOW IT ALL FITS TOGETHER

A is for Assets Under Management

*Source: filmed Alignment S2*

**BRETT: CENTER**

Welcome back to Family Legacy by Design. My name is Brett, and this is Tom, and we are grateful you are back.

Because most families do not build everything at once. It develops over time. Different decisions, different seasons, different people advising along the way. And each piece can make sense on its own, and when you step back it still feels like a collection of separate systems that are not connected to each other.

**BRETT: TURN**

So Tom, when you are trying to get all the cards on the table, what are you really helping a family understand?

**TOM: CENTER**

You are helping them understand how everything works together as a system.

Most families have assets spread among different places. Sometimes different people managing them. Different strategies, different structures. Each piece may make sense on its own. But they have never stepped back to look at how it all fits.

Sometimes people hire three different money managers and all three use the same strategy. So really they could have had one person put a third of the money in each place and gotten the same thing.

The goal is to move from a collection of parts to a coordinated whole.

**BRETT: CENTER**

And Tom, here is what I find striking. On the surface everything can look fine. Accounts performing, decisions made with good intent, reports arriving. And underneath there is fragmentation. Pieces that do not connect. Decisions made in isolation.

**TOM: CENTER**

That is right. Let me give you a simple example.

You have got one part of your assets trying to grow aggressively. Another part trying to generate income for the family. And a third part trying to minimize taxes.

Each one of those makes complete sense by itself. But if they are not coordinated, you have three strategies pulling in three directions, and nobody is asking whether they actually work together.

**BRETT: CENTER**

Tom, where does the clarity start?

**TOM: CENTER**

It starts with ownership. Who owns what?

Because assets might be held individually. They could be held jointly. They could be in a trust. They could be inside an entity. And if that is not clearly understood, you cannot see how anything fits.

And here is where it gets real. You may have a parent who assumes everything passes a certain way. But some of it is in a trust. Some of it has a beneficiary named. Some of it sits inside a business. And when you actually map it out, it does not go the way they thought.

That is not a technical problem. That is a clarity problem.

**BRETT: CENTER**

And that raises the question underneath this whole session. It is not just what you have. It is how it moves.

**TOM: CENTER**

Exactly. Once ownership is clear, the next question is how those assets actually move from one generation to the next, or from husband to wife.

Because different things pass in different ways. Some go through a will. Some go through a trust. Some go by a beneficiary designation.

And a lot of people think everything passes through their will. Many of their assets do not go through the will at all. A life insurance policy does not. A retirement account does not. Those go wherever the beneficiary form says they go, and that form might have been filled out twenty years ago.

So the result is not what anybody expected. And you do not find that out until it is too late.

**BRETT: CENTER**

Tom, let me give you a scenario.

**SCENARIO ONE.** A family with several different people helping them. One handles the investments. Another handles insurance. Someone else did the estate work. Every one of them is good at their job and doing it well. Nobody has ever looked at how all of it fits together.

**BRETT: TURN**

**TOM: CENTER**

There is fragmentation going on.

Each piece is being managed independently, and there is no coordination across the whole. So decisions get made in isolation instead of in alignment.

`[TOM: the family who bought a policy to solve one problem and created another because of where they put it. Keep the lesson. Lose the mechanics and the figure.]`

And the solution is not to replace what is there. It is to bring it together and put things in the right place.

**BRETT: CENTER**

Let me give you another version of this.

**SCENARIO TWO.** A father who has told his children, everything is set, it is all handled, you do not need to worry about it. And he believes that completely. And he has never checked a beneficiary form since the year his second child was born.

**BRETT: TURN**

**TOM: CENTER**

Yeah. And he is not being careless. He is being human.

Those forms get filled out at a moment in time, and life keeps moving, and nobody sends you a reminder. But those forms override everything else. Whatever the form says is what happens, no matter what the will says and no matter what he told his family.

It takes about an afternoon to check them all. And I have seen that afternoon change what happens to an entire family.

**BRETT: CENTER**

# 🎬 SESSION 3 — THE DOCUMENTS THAT SPEAK FOR YOU

# 🎬 SESSION 4 — WHAT YOU ARE PROTECTING
