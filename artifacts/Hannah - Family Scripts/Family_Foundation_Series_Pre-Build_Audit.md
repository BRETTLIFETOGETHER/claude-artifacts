# FAMILY FOUNDATION SERIES

## Pre-Build Audit and Build Plan

Six titles, six sessions each, thirty-six video scripts. Brett and Tom, two-chair
format. Derived from the coaching series: four filmed titles, three scripts, the
Family Book, the Training Manual, and the Workbook Legend.

---

# PART 1 — THE STRUCTURAL PROBLEM

## The coaching series is written in third person

Every framework in the coaching material assumes a neutral outsider. Not as tone,
as mechanism. Tom says it plainly on camera:

- The facilitator exists so Dad is one voice among ten, not the person running
  the room. Mom and Dad get two votes out of ten.
- The advisor advocates for the kids because "the kids would never say that to
  their parents, but you can say it. And you don't have a dog in the fight."
- The advisor asks the question nobody in the family will ask. "You intimidate
  your kids." The wife confirms it. A family member cannot deliver that line.
- Tom interviews the next generation separately, then reports back to Mom and Dad.

Remove that person and roughly a third of the coaching content has no mechanism.
This is the design problem. Vocabulary is the easy part.

## What the family edition has to answer that the coaching edition never asks

**Who asks the hard question when there is no coach in the room?**

Every Family title needs a working answer. Options, in order of how much source
supports them:

1. **The question does the work.** Tom's own teaching is that the genius is in the
   question asking. A written prompt can carry a question a family member cannot
   say in their own voice. "The book asked us this" is cover.
2. **Rotate the chair.** Filmed Meetings Session 5: a family passed facilitation
   around, matriarch then a son-in-law then a daughter. Source exists.
3. **Split the room.** Filmed Meetings Session 5: separate gatherings for the men,
   the women, and the married-ins. Source exists.
4. **Bring someone in.** Keeps the advisor pathway open and feeds the coaching
   business. Should appear, but not as the default or the family edition becomes
   a lead magnet.

# PART 2 — WHAT MUST BE ADDED

These have no equivalent in the coaching source. This is net new writing.

## 1. A seat for the next generation as readers

The coaching series talks about the next generation for twenty-four thousand
words and never once to them. The family edition will be read by them. Every
session needs something addressed to a G2 or G3 reader, not only to the
patriarch and matriarch.

This is the single largest addition and the clearest differentiator.

## 2. Spouse before family

Tom repeats that you start with Mom and Dad, and that the wife frequently does
not know what they have. In the coaching edition that is an advisor observation.
In the family edition it has to become a step: the husband and wife conversation
that happens before anything goes to the whole family.

Source is strong. Filmed Alignment Session 1 has the wife who "doesn't have a
clue," and Tom's line that this alone is like marital counseling. Filmed Meetings
Session 3 has the whole engage-both-spouses teaching.

## 3. Ground rules and safety, earlier and broader

The coaching edition puts ground rules inside the Meetings title because that is
where a professional needs them. A family opening these topics carries history
into the first sentence. Safety language has to appear in Session 1 of every
title, not once in Book 5.

Includes an explicit "if this gets hard" path. The coaching edition never needs
one because the advisor is the containment.

## 4. Scale neutrality, deliberately built

Tom's clients are ultra high net worth and the improv defaults to large numbers.
Brett names the wider audience on camera in Alignment Session 6: "mass affluent."
Tom supports it in Clarity Session 4: "even $100,000, $500,000 can be a huge
amount of money to a normal family."

Every principle in the family edition has to work at both ends. Requires a stated
rule, not case-by-case judgment. See Part 4.

## 5. A family action between sessions

The coaching edition ends each session with the advisor going to do this with
clients. The family edition needs the family to do something before the next one.

Source is abundant but scattered across the coaching material as advisor tools:
the prayer request list passed around the family, the six questions before a
son-in-law proposes, the values exercise, the zero-to-ten six areas sheet, the
top three prayer requests reported back a year later, the charity-as-another-child
question. Each needs converting from advisor instrument to family assignment.

## 6. Direct spiritual practice

Tom's spiritual material is strong but framed advisor-to-client: "I ask them how
many times a week do you spend time with God." The family edition can speak
directly. The Family Book is explicitly faith-based, so this is consistent with
the published line.

## 7. A defined replacement for the commercial case

The coaching series is held together by the advisor retention argument, threaded
through every session. Remove it and the spine goes with it.

The replacement is already in the source and currently underused because the
commercial argument is carrying the weight: **legacy by design versus legacy by
default.** In the family edition that line has to do the whole job, which means
it needs to appear with specific stakes in every session, tied to that session's
content, not as a slogan in Session 1.

# PART 4 — THE STORY LAYER

## Tom's improvised stories split cleanly in two

This is the most useful finding for the rewrite, because one half travels
unchanged and the other half does not.

**Relational stories. No dollar figures. Move over intact.**

- "You intimidate your kids." / "I do not." / Wife: "You absolutely do."
- The daughter who assumed her parents were not listening, and never said so.
- The son whose college food money went to phone calls to a girl in Croatia,
  whom he married.
- Tom's father matching every saved dollar. The Renault at fifteen, $342, cash.
- The family member who had been away for years, came for an hour, stayed two
  days, and said "I didn't know you really cared about my opinion."
- The son who stood up mid-meeting and said "we are a family first."
- The gun room, and Brett's uncle's Browning.
- The son who liked fishing while his father kept taking him to baseball.
- The granddaughter who went to school to write songs.
- Pamela.
- The executor buying the widow a Lexus while her son argued for a Ford.
- "Sue, can we go out and have coffee?" / "Is this a meeting?"

**Financial stories. Numbers do the work. Do not travel as written.**

- $45M estate, $500K per child raised to $1M.
- $50M estate, charity as the fifth child, eighteen months to decide.
- $25M policy owned by the company, moved out.
- $60M policy no longer needed.
- $100 billion private equity firm.
- Billion and a half company, 8,500 employees, 25,000 dependents.
- $40M reveal to the family, and the son-in-law's reaction.
- The $800,000 gift the CPA missed.
- Ten to thirty to fifty percent of AGI.

**The rule.** Relational stories carry across untouched. Financial stories keep
the shape and lose the figure, or get replaced from the family-scale end of the
source. Tom already models the smaller register on camera when he wants to. Use
those instances.

**A consequence worth naming.** In the coaching edition the big numbers are
credentialing. They prove Tom has been in rooms the viewer has not. In the family
edition the same numbers are exclusionary. A family at $2 million hears that this
is not for them. Same content, opposite effect.

# PART 5 — FAMILY ALIGNMENT, THE LIGHTER CUT

## Keep the acrostic. Keep six sessions. Change the unit of work.

FAMILY is memorable, it is filmed, and it is in the Training Manual and the
Family Book. Do not break it to save length.

For advisors, FAMILY is a **collection** checklist: gather it, review it, find
the gaps, produce the report.

For families, FAMILY is a **conversation** checklist: do we both know, do the
kids know, and what happens if.

| Letter | Coaching unit of work | Family unit of work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **F** Financial Statement | Ownership, fair market value, cost basis, debt terms | What do we own, what do we owe, and does my spouse know |
| **A** Assets Under Management | Coordinate managers, tax-paying vs retirement, strategy overlap | Is everything pulling the same direction, and who is watching the whole |
| **M** Mandatory Documents | Wills, trusts, POAs, ILITs, shareholder and buy-sell agreements | Do our documents say what we would say, and when did we last look |
| **I** Insurance Coverage | Insured, owner, beneficiary, inside or outside the estate | What are we actually protecting, and does it still match our life |
| **L** Liabilities and Taxes | Six return types, K-1s, gift and estate filings | What is attached to what we have built, and who will carry it |
| **Y** Yearly Cash Flow | Sources and five uses, projection, reserves | Where is our money actually going, and does it match what we say matters |

**What comes out of the family cut**

Form numbers (706, 709, 1041). ILIT mechanics. Cost basis and step-up. Discounts
for non-controlling interests. The $100,000 QCD threshold. Cousin consortium
governance. Anything requiring a professional to act on.

**What stays**

Every relational consequence. The wife who did not know. The documents that said
something the client had never read. The uncle named executor who died three
years ago. The business with no continuity plan and the owner in the hospital
for a month. Values versus valuables. There is no such thing as an independent
financial decision.

**Where advisors get named.** Each session ends by identifying the moment a
family should bring in help. This is honest, it serves the family, and it feeds
the coaching side without turning the title into a sales pitch.

## The Communication gap

Four of six Family titles sit on twenty thousand or more words of Tom on camera.
Communication sits on one unfilmed script of seven thousand, which still contains
the two lines flagged in draft as written to sound like Tom rather than sourced
from him.

It is also, for a family audience, arguably the most important title in the
series. Families do not fail on documents. They fail on conversations. Tom says
so in every title.

**Three ways to close it, in order of preference**

1. **Film Coaching Communication first**, then build the Family edition from the
   transcript like the other four. Adds schedule, produces the same source
   density as everything else.
2. **Mine the other four titles.** There is substantial communication teaching
   scattered across them: Clarity Session 2 on open-ended questions and listening
   for understanding, Meetings Session 3 in full, Next Gen Session 3 on trust and
   vain imaginations, Alignment throughout. Enough to build from, but it means
   Family Communication is assembled rather than derived, and it will overlap
   with the other titles unless the boundaries are drawn first.
3. **Build from the script as-is.** Fastest, thinnest, and inherits the fidelity
   problem.

Recommendation: option 1 if the schedule allows, option 2 if not.

## Two smaller gaps

**Family Overview** depends on a script that has never been performed. The Family
Book's first four chapters are better source than the script and should lead.

**Appendices B, C, D, E** are referenced in the Workbook Legend but not in hand.
Needed if the Family Alignment title carries any checklist at all, even a
simplified one.

# PART 7 — HOW THE SCRIPTS GET BUILT

## Per title, three batches

**Batch 0 — Pre-build.** Source report for that title. Session lock with titles
and subtitles. Framework assignments. Story assignments, naming which of Tom's
filmed stories lands in which session and which need a family-scale replacement.
Scripture assignments with references and translation. Open items flagged. No
copy written until this is approved.

**Batch 1 — Sessions 1 to 3.** Full scripts. Brett open per the six-move pattern,
camera blocking, both speakers written, two-scenario device, story cues bracketed
where Tom improvises, session close and handoff to the next.

**Batch 2 — Sessions 4 to 6.** Same, plus the series close in Session 6.

Then assembly into a single prompter-ready file, plus a separate build continuity
report. Nothing ships with QA notes or verification maps inside the script body.

## Structural rules carried over from the coaching series

- Brett's six-move open on every session. Series welcome, name the pair,
  gratitude, locate the session, address the audience, hand to Tom with an open
  question.
- Two scenarios near the end of each session, introduced by Brett with "let me
  give you another version of this."
- Camera blocking on every block. CENTER and TURN.
- Both speakers fully written.
- One closing question per session for the family to sit with.
- Preview of the next session before the close.
- No em-dashes. No fill-in-the-blank lines in the script body.

## New rules for the family edition

- **A next-generation address in every session.** Not a mention. Something
  written to them.
- **A family action before the next session.** One thing, specific, doable in a
  week.
- **Scale-neutral framing.** Any figure that appears has to work at both ends, or
  the story loses the figure.
- **Safety language in Session 1 of every title,** not only in Meetings.
- **A bracketed story cue where Tom improvises,** so the slot is reserved rather
  than hoped for. Meetings proved the delta is three to one and that all of it is
  evidence.
- **NASB throughout,** per the published Family Book, pending confirmation.

## Recommended series order

1. **Clarity.** Richest source, cleanest spine, strongest title pair, natural
   front door for a family.
2. **Next Generation.** Strong source, highest emotional pull, and it is what
   families say keeps them up at night.
3. **Meetings.** Only title with script and film both. Fastest build.
4. **Alignment.** Hardest transformation. Do it once the family voice is
   established across three titles.
5. **Overview.** Better built after the other five exist, since an overview
   should preview what has actually been written.
6. **Communication.** Last, and only after the source question in Part 6 is
   resolved.

Note the tension: the Overview is Book 1 commercially but the weakest source and
the best candidate to build late. Worth deciding deliberately rather than by
default.
