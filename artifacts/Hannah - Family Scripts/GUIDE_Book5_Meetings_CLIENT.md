# A WELCOME FROM TOM AND BRETT

Tom says something to families all the time, and it lands differently depending on how old they are.

There is going to be a family meeting. It is just a matter of whether you are going to be there or not.

That is not a threat and it is not a sales line. It is a description of what actually happens.

Somewhere in the future of your family there is a room. Your children are in it. Possibly their spouses. There are documents on the table, or a house to deal with, or a decision that cannot wait. And they are talking about what you wanted.

That meeting is going to happen. The only variable is whether you were present for it, or whether it happened without you and everyone in the room had to reconstruct your intentions from fragments.

**Now, a word about the phrase itself.**

If *family meeting* already sounds heavy to you, you are not alone. For a lot of families it sounds like something serious is about to be announced, and that association alone is why most families never have one.

Nothing in these six sessions requires a boardroom or an agenda with roman numerals. A family meeting is a time when a family comes together on purpose to talk about something that matters.

**And one more thing before you begin.**

**Most families never do this.**

Not because they do not care. Because there is no natural occasion for it. Birthdays are celebratory. Holidays are crowded. A random Tuesday feels arbitrary and slightly alarming, because if somebody calls a meeting on a Tuesday, everybody assumes there is bad news.

So if you are opening this and feeling behind, you are not.

You do not have to do it perfectly. You do not have to do it with everyone. And if something is worth doing, it is worth doing poorly, because the second time will be better and the third better than that.

## Who leads this

Somebody has to press play and read the questions out loud. That person is a **host**, not a teacher.

There is an irony here worth naming, because this book is about who leads a family gathering, and working through it requires somebody to lead a family gathering.

Take that as practice. Whoever hosts these six sessions is doing the thing Session 3 describes, and doing it before they have read Session 3.

It should probably not be the person who usually decides things in your family, and it should rotate if you can manage it.

**Three rules for whoever has the chair.** Read the question and then stop talking. Answer last, not first. And you are not responsible for fixing anything that comes up.

## If your family does not look like the one in this book

**If there is no spouse.** Every session has a section built around a husband and wife. Read it as being about whoever you would plan a gathering with.

**If your family is blended.** Your question about who belongs in the room is genuinely more complicated. Session 2 is where that gets decided, and the answer is usually more people rather than fewer.

**If your family is scattered.** Geography is the most common obstacle and Session 6 is written for you specifically. You are not going to get everyone in a room this year. Start smaller.

# Why Have a Family Meeting

## There Is Going to Be a Family Meeting

**HOST NOTE.** Somebody in the room will find the phrase family meeting alarming. Say out loud at the start that nothing here requires anything formal, and that the first one can be thirty minutes at the end of a dinner you were already having.

## INTRODUCTION

Here is the question people bring to this book.

**Do we really need to do this?**

Because the family talks. There are calls and texts and holidays. Everybody gets along. And adding a meeting to a family that seems fine sounds like manufacturing a problem.

**KEY VERSE**

"Commit your works to the Lord and your plans will be established."

**PROVERBS 16:3**

Most families talk on a regular basis, and those conversations stay at a surface level. What is missing is the ones further out. Where things are going, what we are building together, what the future is going to look like.

A family meeting is simply a time when a family comes together intentionally to talk about things that matter to them. It is not complicated and it is not overly formal.

What it really is, is creating space. Space that does not happen on its own.

Notice the order in the verse. Commit first. The establishing is His part. A lot of families try to establish first, building the structure and hoping clarity fills it.

## THE ONE QUESTION

> **Is there a date on a calendar?**
>
> Answer that one and the session worked. Everything else is optional.

## OPENING STORY

**They Kick the Can Down the Road**

Families think about having a meeting and everybody starts to get a little nervous, because they begin anticipating what might happen.

They know the personalities. They know the dynamics. They are not sure how it is going to go.

So instead of stepping into it, they kick the can down the road. Not because it is unimportant. Because it feels unclear.

Tom has sat in a great many of these now, and the anticipated disaster almost never arrives. What arrives instead is usually awkwardness for about ten minutes, and then somebody says something honest, and the room changes.

The meeting that gets cancelled is almost never the meeting that would have happened.

## THE FRAMEWORK

### Purpose Before Structure

- **Purpose first.** Are you trying to communicate something more clearly, solve a problem, teach something, or build understanding? If that is not clear, the meeting will drift.

- **Then an agenda.** Not formality for its own sake. It tells people what you are trying to accomplish, how the conversation will move, and roughly how long it will take.

- **Then ground rules, and only a few.** Listen to understand. Give people the opportunity to speak at any time. Keep it respectful. When people know the expectations, they are far more comfortable participating.

- **And everyone who should be there.** Including the spouses. If they are not there, your son or daughter is going home and telling them what happened, and it will not be the same as what was said.

**Write your purpose in one sentence, starting with the words we want. Everything else in this book serves that sentence.**

## WHERE YOU ARE STARTING

**If your family has never done this.** You are the majority. Do not open with the biggest subject. The first one can be thirty minutes at the end of a meal that was already happening, about something almost pleasant.

**If you tried once and it did not go well.** Something specific went wrong: no purpose, wrong people, wrong subject for a first attempt, or somebody reacted and the room closed. Name which before you try again.

**If your family already gathers but never talks about anything.** You do not need a new occasion. You need thirty minutes inside the one you already have.

## THE TOOL

### The Initial Family Meeting Agenda

Four lines. That is the whole thing.

**Why we are getting together.** One sentence, in plain language, sent ahead so nobody arrives imagining a diagnosis.

**What we will talk about.** Two items at most. Not four.

**How long it will take.** Say it and then honor it. Ending early is a gift.

**What we are not doing.** This one is unusual and it does more than the other three. Naming what is *not* on the agenda removes most of the anxiety people bring.

Send it ahead. Two things happen when you do. The nervous ones stop imagining disasters because they can see the actual subject. And the thoughtful ones arrive having actually thought.

# Different Ways to Gather

## Matching the Meeting to What You Need

**HOST NOTE.** The section on personal objects will surprise people and may produce a real conversation on the spot. That is fine. Let it run.

## INTRODUCTION

Here is the question in this one.

**What are we even supposed to talk about?**

That is the honest obstacle for most families. Not resistance. A blank page.

**KEY VERSE**

"There is an appointed time for everything, and there is a time for every matter under heaven."

**ECCLESIASTES 3:1**

One of the most common problems is using the same kind of meeting for everything.

Once a family finally has a gathering, the tendency is to cover everything in one sitting. Finances, family dynamics, future plans, unresolved tensions, succession. All of it. And when that happens, the meeting feels full. It does not feel productive.

Not every conversation is the same, and matching the format to the purpose is most of what separates a good gathering from an exhausting one.

## THE ONE QUESTION

> **Which of the four kinds of meeting is your first one, and does everyone know?**
>
> Answer that one and the session worked.

## OPENING STORY

**The Gun Room**

Tom had a client with a gun room. Thirty-five guns.

He told him he needed to decide which rifle went to which grandchild before they fought about it after he was gone.

Because here is the thing. Most family fights are not over the money. They are over the china. They are over the jewelry. They are over the guns, especially with the men. The cars.

Brett has his own version. He had an uncle who took him hunting as a boy, and that uncle had a Browning twelve-gauge, and Brett would have loved to have it. He never had the heart to ask. His uncle passed, the gun went to his aunt, and he still feels awkward about it.

It was never about the gun.

Money is fungible. A dollar is identical to another dollar. A grandfather's watch cannot be divided, and the person it did not go to has received information about their place in the family whether or not any was intended.

## THE FRAMEWORK

### Four Kinds of Meeting

- **Learning.** You are teaching people how something works. Maybe how a trust functions in your family. The goal is understanding, because at some point they are going to be responsible for it.

- **Alignment.** People are sharing perspectives. You want everybody's input so there is ownership in whatever comes next. Nothing is decided.

- **Decision.** We have a lake house and we are thinking about selling it. What do you think? Something gets settled.

- **Long-term planning.** Here is where we are, here is what we are considering, and we want everybody to speak into it.

These require different preparation, different length, and different expectations, and families run all four the same way.

**Name which one your next gathering is. Out loud, at the start, to everyone. That single sentence does more than any amount of preparation.**

## WHERE YOU ARE STARTING

**If you have never had any of the four.** Start with a learning meeting or an alignment meeting. Neither requires anybody to agree to anything, which makes the first one much easier to hold.

**If your family only ever has decision meetings.** That is the most common pattern and it teaches people that gathering means something is being asked of them. One alignment meeting, with nothing to decide, changes what the invitation means.

**If your family gathers but avoids the substance.** Pick one of the four topic categories and put it on the agenda by name. Vagueness is what allows a gathering to stay pleasant and empty.

# SESSION 3

# Who Leads and How

## Creating a Room Where People Will Speak

**HOST NOTE.** This session names something uncomfortable about whoever usually leads. If that is the person hosting, they should read it aloud themselves rather than having somebody else say it.

## INTRODUCTION

Here is the question in this one, and it is the honest problem at the center of this whole book.

**Who is supposed to run this?**

Because everything about leading a good gathering assumes somebody in the room can hold it neutrally. And in a family, nobody is neutral. Everybody has a stake and a history and a position.

**KEY VERSE**

"For God has not given us a spirit of timidity, but of power and love and discipline."

**2 TIMOTHY 1:7**

A lot of times when the patriarch leads it, the kids are thinking, Dad is going to tell us what he wants us to hear. We need to be careful. We cannot say what we really think.

That is not a criticism of any father. It is a description of what happens in a room where one person has thirty years more experience and the final say.

The good news is that there are four ways families solve this themselves, without anyone from the outside, and they are all available to you.

## THE ONE QUESTION

> **What would change if the person who usually decides spoke last?**
>
> Answer that one and the session worked.

## OPENING STORY

**You Intimidate Your Kids**

Tom interviewed a man's four adult children separately, which is something he does.

Then he sat with the father and his wife.

He said, you intimidate your kids.

The man said, I do not.

His wife said, you absolutely do.

Here was a man who was totally unaware. Not a tyrant. Not harsh. By every account a good father who loved his children and had provided for them well. And all four of them, independently, had described walking into a room with him and becoming careful.

He had no idea, and the reason he had no idea is that nobody had ever told him, and the reason nobody had ever told him is exactly the thing they were describing.

## THE FRAMEWORK

### Who Holds the Room

In the coaching version of this material there is an advisor who has no stake in the outcome. Most families do not have that, and here are the four ways families solve it themselves.

- **The question does the work.** If it is written down in front of everybody, nobody had to be the person who raised it. The page asked it, not your brother. That is real cover and it is frequently enough.

- **Rotate the chair.** Tom watched a family pass facilitation around. The matriarch took one part, then a son-in-law, then one of the daughters. Engagement went up markedly because it stopped being one person's meeting.

- **Split the room.** Brett worked with a large family and gathered all the men, then all the women, then the seven who had married in. That last conversation surfaced things the parents needed to hear that would never have come out in the full room.

- **Bring somebody in.** Always available, and it should not be the default, or the family never develops the capacity.

**None of these requires the person with the most authority to become neutral, which is not possible. They arrange the room so neutrality is not required.**

## WHERE YOU ARE STARTING

**If one person has always led.** The move is not to remove them. It is to have them speak last, once, and see what changes. That costs nothing and it is the highest-return experiment in this book.

**If nobody leads and it wanders.** Somebody does need to take responsibility for guiding it. That is not controlling. It is creating direction without taking over.

**If one person's presence shuts the room down.** That is worth naming privately rather than in the room, and it may be the case for bringing somebody in for the first few gatherings. Rotate the chair after trust has been built, not before.

## THE TOOL

### The Follow-On Questions

The first answer is almost always the practiced one. These are what get you past it, and none of them require authority to use.

**Tell me more about that.** Four words, used after somebody has already finished.

**Why is that important to you?** Goes down a layer rather than sideways.

**We have not heard from you yet.** Said to one person, by name.

**What did you think about what he just said?** Asked of a spouse, in the room. It can get sparky and what comes out is almost always what the room needed.

And the one that is not a question. **Silence.** Ask something real and then wait longer than is comfortable. The pause is where the thinking happens, and most of us end it in two seconds.

### Rhythm, Not Events
