# A WELCOME FROM TOM AND BRETT

Between the two of us we have spent a long time in rooms where families talk about what happens next. Tom has sat across the table from them for about thirty years. Brett has spent most of his career building material that helps people have conversations they would not otherwise have.

For a long stretch, Tom thought he understood the job. Help people get clear on what they want to do with what they have been entrusted with. Make sure the documents match. Communicate it to the people who will inherit it.

Then he started noticing that families with what looked like airtight plans were coming apart anyway.

Siblings stopped speaking. A business was sold within months of a transition everybody had called a success. Children received far more than they had any framework for holding, and instead of freedom it produced conflict, or distance, or a low-grade resentment nobody named and nobody could shake.

For a while the assumption was that the problem was with those families. Eventually we had to admit it was also with the way all of us had been trained. The profession gets very good at the money and leaves the people mostly alone.

We know a family whose relationships have nearly been destroyed by the very thing they spent forty years building. There is a lawsuit with one of their children. There are grandchildren they do not see. When we asked whether they would be better off without any of it, they said they would not go that far. And then they said something we have not been able to put down.

*We wish we had known more of the kinds of things you are talking about.*

That is why this exists.

**Now here is the thing we most want you to hear before you begin.**

**Most families never do this.**

Not because they do not love each other. Because nobody ever asked them to sit down and think about it, and there is no natural moment when it comes up on its own.

So if you are opening this and feeling behind, you are not. You are ahead of nearly everyone, by the width of one decision.

A few other things.

**You do not have to do this perfectly.** If something is worth doing, it is worth doing poorly, because the second time will be better and the third better than that.

**You do not have to do this with everyone.** If you are the only person in your family holding this book, you are not doing it wrong. Somebody in every family goes first.

**And you do not have to finish it today.** Six sessions, one conversation at a time.

We are asking.

**TOM CONWAY AND BRETT EASTMAN**

# USING THIS GUIDE

## This works alone, with your spouse, or with your whole family

You can do every part of this by yourself. Nothing here requires anyone else to participate.

But it is better together, and it is meant to end up there. When you see the fork, take whichever side fits the room you are in.

**With your spouse or family: discuss. On your own: write it down.**

## Who leads this

Somebody has to press play and read the questions out loud. That person is a **host**, not a teacher.

The host does not need to know more than anyone else and is not responsible for anyone's answers. The job is three things. Start the video. Read the question. Then be quiet long enough for somebody to answer it.

**Two things about who the host should be.**

It should probably not be the person who usually decides things in your family. Tom's reason is worth hearing plainly. When the father runs the meeting, the children are already thinking that he is going to tell them what he wants them to hear, so they listen politely and say very little.

And it should rotate. A different person each session if you can manage it.

**Three rules for whoever has the chair.** Read the question and then stop talking. Answer last, not first. And you are not responsible for fixing anything that comes up.

## If your family does not look like the one in this book

This material assumes a household that many families do not have. Here is what to do if yours is different.

**If there is no spouse.** Widowed, divorced, single, or married to somebody who will not engage. Every session has a section called Before You Bring the Family In, built around a husband and wife. Read it as being about the first person who will know. Somebody eventually has to understand what you decided. A sibling, an adult child, a friend who will be executor. Start with them, and the section works unchanged.

**If a child is estranged.** Some of these questions assume you can reach everyone. If you cannot, do not skip the session. Do the work about the people you can reach, and when a question asks about your whole family, answer it about them anyway. The absence is information, and being able to name what you would say if you could say it is worth something on the day you can.

**If your family is blended.** Remarriage, stepchildren, children from two marriages. Your inheritance questions are genuinely harder than the ones described here, not easier, and you should not let the simplicity of the examples suggest otherwise. Where this guide says children, read the specific people in your situation and let the difference stand rather than smoothing it. Session 4 in particular will need more time than the others.

**If your resources are modest.** Almost every story in this material involves families with a great deal. That is who Tom has spent thirty years with, and it can make this feel like it is for somebody else. It is not. A hundred thousand dollars can be an enormous amount to a family, and every single question in these six sessions works at any scale. The only thing that changes is the number, and the number is the least important variable in this book.

## What you are agreeing to, and what you are not

This material asks you to think about your life, your marriage, your money, and your children, sometimes inside the same question.

You are never required to say a number out loud. You are never required to share what you wrote in a private exercise. Nobody has to reveal anything to anyone in order to participate.

Some of what comes up will be tender. Go at the pace that is right for the people in the room.

## What you need each week

**The session video.** About twenty minutes.

**This guide.** Bring something to write with.

**The companion book.** *Family Legacy by Design.* Each session assigns five short readings.

## How much time this takes

The video runs about twenty minutes. Working through a session takes about an hour. The private exercise adds twenty minutes.

**And one honest note about the questions.** There are more in each session than you will answer, and that is intentional rather than an oversight. Each session marks **one question** as the one that matters. If you answer only that one, the session worked.

## Where this sits in the series

This is the second book in the **Family Foundation Series Curriculum**, and it is the one most families should start with. **Family Legacy Overview** comes before it. What follows is **Family Alignment**, then **Family Communication**, then **Family Meetings**, then **Next Generation**.

Each session has a section addressed to you directly. The questions are the same questions, asked thirty years earlier, which is an advantage rather than a disadvantage.

# The Five Areas of Your Legacy

## Bringing Focus to What Matters Most

**HOST NOTE.** People will rate themselves high in whichever of the five areas they are most comfortable in. That is not the interesting number. Ask about the low one. And if someone rates everything a four, they have not started yet.

## INTRODUCTION

Most people who open a book like this are carrying one question, and it is usually not the one on the cover.

**Will my kids be okay?**

That is it. That is what is actually underneath the estate planning and the trusts and the conversations about fairness. Not what will I leave. Will the people I love be all right, and did I do the things that would make that more likely.

Nobody says it that way, because it sounds soft next to the technical questions. But it is the real one, and everything in these six sessions is aimed at it.

**KEY VERSE**

"A plan in the heart of a man is like deep water, but a man of understanding draws it out."

**PROVERBS 20:5**

Here is what makes that question hard to answer. Legacy is too large a word to act on. So families think about it in general, feel the weight of it, and put it down again.

Five specific areas give you somewhere to stand.

Tom's conviction after thirty years is that almost everything that matters to your family is already there. It has simply never been drawn out. Every family already has a legacy. The only question is whether anyone chose it.

## THE ONE QUESTION

> **If someone asked the five people closest to you what matters most in this family, how similar would the answers be?**
>
> Answer that one and the session worked. Everything else is optional.

## OPENING STORY

**Five Answers in One Room**

They had done well. Not spectacularly, but well enough that the questions in front of them were real ones. Two grown children, a son-in-law, a house that had been paid off for years, and a business that had grown steadily for two decades.

Tom asked them what mattered most to this family.

There were five people in the room and he got five different answers. Not contradictory ones. Not angry ones. Just five people who had lived in the same house, eaten the same meals, and gone to the same church for thirty years, and who had never once been asked to say out loud what they were building together.

The father assumed everyone already knew. The mother had an answer she had never said because nobody asked. One of the children thought the answer was obvious and was surprised to hear it was not. The son-in-law had a view he had been careful never to offer.

Nothing was wrong with that family. That is the part worth sitting with. And they were about to make a series of significant decisions off five different pictures of what they were doing and why.

## BEFORE YOU BRING THE FAMILY IN

This is about the two of you, not about your children.

Tom says it in almost every family he works with. You start with Mom and Dad. Not because the children matter less, but because a family cannot get aligned around something the two people at the center have never said to each other.

And frequently one spouse does not have the full picture. Not because anything is hidden. Because that was the arrangement, and it worked, and nobody revisited it.

If you are working through this alone, this section is the moment you decide who the first person is that you will say any of it to.

- We say what we actually think, not what will keep the peace this evening.
- Neither of us has to have an answer ready.
- What is said here stays here until we both agree it goes further.
- We are not solving anything tonight.
- One real step beats a good intention.

## THE FRAMEWORK

### The Five Areas of Legacy

Tom's framework exists because legacy as a word is too broad to act on.

- **Personal Legacy**, which includes spiritual legacy. What you want to be remembered for.

- **Family Legacy**. Your children, your grandchildren, your parents if they are still living.

- **Financial Legacy**. What amount of inheritance would be a blessing rather than a curse.

- **Work and Business Legacy**. If you own a business, succession and continuity. If you do not, what you have built through your work.

- **Charitable Legacy**. What you want to do for the kingdom of God, now and at the end of your life.

**Which of the five is clearest to you? Start there. Then name the one you avoid most consistently, and be honest about whether your family avoids it or you do.**

## WHERE YOU ARE STARTING

**If your family has never talked about any of this.** You are the majority, and you are not behind. Do not open with the biggest question. Ask one person what they think this family cares about, and listen without correcting them.

**If you tried once and it went badly.** One bad conversation is evidence about that conversation, not about your family. Something specific went wrong: wrong room, wrong person, wrong moment, or an answer someone was not ready to hear. Name which one it was before you try again.

**If you are doing this alone.** Everything here works for one person, and a person who has gotten clear changes the room they walk into whether or not anyone asked them to. Do not wait for your family to be ready. Be the one who is ready.

## THE TOOL

### The Family Legacy by Design Assessment

The Assessment walks all five areas and asks two things in each. How clear are you, and how clear is the rest of your family. It takes about fifteen minutes.

The value is not in the score. It is in the distance between your answers and the answers of the person sitting next to you.

Complete it yourself this week. Then ask one other person in your family to complete it separately, without seeing yours, and compare.

Do not compare in order to be right. Compare in order to find out where the conversation needs to start.

# Your Personal Legacy

## What Do You Want to Be Remembered For

**HOST NOTE.** This is the most private session in the book. Nobody should be pressed to read what they wrote in the exercise, and you should say so out loud before you begin.

## INTRODUCTION

Here is the question underneath this session, and almost nobody says it out loud.

**Did I do enough?**

Not enough money. Enough of the thing you were actually here for. Most people carry a low-grade uncertainty about whether their life added up to what they intended, and it gets louder rather than quieter with age.

**KEY VERSE**

"For we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand."

**EPHESIANS 2:10**

It surprises people that a series about family legacy starts with one person. But a family cannot get clear together if the people in it have never gotten clear themselves.

Notice what the verse says. Prepared beforehand. Which means the thing you are trying to get clear about was already decided about you. You are finding it, not inventing it.

Which changes the question from *did I do enough* to *have I found what I was for.* Those feel similar and they are not.

## THE ONE QUESTION

> **What is your prepared answer to what matters most, and what is underneath it?**
>
> Answer that one and the session worked.

## OPENING STORY

**The Four-Second Answer**

He was in his late fifties and had been successful at nearly everything he had put his hand to. Tom asked him what he wanted to be remembered for.

He answered in about four seconds. It was a good answer. Solid, decent, the kind of thing you would be glad to hear a man say. And it was completely prepared. He had said it before, probably many times, and had never once gone underneath it.

Tom said, that is good, tell me more. And then he waited.

What came out on the third pass was something much more specific and much more personal, and by his own admission he had never said it to anyone. Not his wife. Not his children. It had been sitting there for thirty years, fully formed, and nobody had asked a second time.

## THE FRAMEWORK

### The Personal Legacy Questions

Tom does not run a personality assessment. He asks questions and then he asks again.

- **What has shaped you?** Some are wonderful and some are hard, and both count.

- **What are you good at?** Not what you are known for. What you are actually good at.

- **What do you love?** The thing that gives you energy rather than the thing that earns you approval.

- **What has God entrusted to you?** Not what you own. What you have been made responsible for.

- **What do you want said?** The two or three sentences you would want spoken about your life.

**Take the one you answered fastest. That is probably the one you have prepared. Go underneath it.**

## WHERE YOU ARE STARTING

**If you have not thought about this in years.** That is normal and it is not a character problem. Most people last considered these questions in their twenties and have been busy since. Start with what has shaped you and let the rest wait.

**If you have thought about it constantly and never said it.** You are further along than you think and further behind than you feel. The work for you is not more reflection. It is one sentence, spoken to one person.

**If you are not sure you are allowed a legacy of your own.** Especially if you have spent decades pouring into everyone else. You were given something individually, not only as a spouse or a parent, and your family is better when you know what it is.

## THE TOOL

### The Goals Conversation, Personal Legacy

Set aside forty-five minutes. Not fifteen. Answer these in writing, in order, and do not move to the next one until the previous one has more than one sentence under it.

- What has shaped my life most, for better and for worse?
- What am I actually good at?
- What do I love doing, regardless of whether I am good at it?
- What has God entrusted to me that I am responsible for?
- What would I want said about me by the people who know me best?
- What would have to change in the next year for that to become more true?

Then do the part most people skip. Read your answers to one person.

**Part Three. The eulogy.** Write the two or three sentences you would want spoken about your life. Not your accomplishments. What kind of person you were, and what you gave yourself to.

Then answer this. What in your current calendar and current spending would have to change for those sentences to be true?

# SESSION 3

# Your Family Legacy

## Defining What Your Family Stands For

**HOST NOTE.** This is the session where somebody may say something that has been sitting for years. If that happens, your job is to make sure they finish the sentence. Do not fix it in the room and do not let anyone else fix it either.

## INTRODUCTION

Here is what families actually want to know when they get to this session.

**Is it too late for us?**

For the distance that opened up. For the thing that happened. For the child who is further away than they used to be, and for the years that went by while everyone was busy.

**KEY VERSE**

"Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brothers to dwell together in unity!"

**PSALM 133:1**

Notice what the verse does not say. It does not say easy.

The answer to the question is almost always no, it is not too late, and the reason is that most families are further along than they think. They have simply never talked about it, and never talking about it feels identical from the inside to not having anything.

Tom wants two things for every family. Family harmony, and the wise transfer of both wealth and wisdom. This session is about the first one, and about the word that makes it possible. Shared. Not agreed. Shared.

## THE ONE QUESTION

> **If you asked everyone in your family what this family stands for, how many different answers would you get?**

## WHAT CHANGED IN THIS VERSION

Not for print. A record of the seven changes applied.

**1. Questions cut roughly in half.** Get Started went from two to one. Learn from five prompts to three. Reflect from seven to four. Going Deeper from three passages to one. Family Conversation from four to two. Final Thoughts from two to one.

**2. The One Question.** Every session now marks a single question in a box. If a family answers only that, the session worked. Everything else becomes optional by contrast rather than by omission.

**3. Each session opens with the family's question.** Will my kids be okay. Did I do enough. Is it too late for us. What happens the day I am gone. What happens to what I built. What was it all for. The framework is reached as the answer rather than stated as the premise.

**4. Four missing paths written.** A new front-matter section covers no spouse, an estranged child, a blended family, and modest resources. Each is a direct paragraph rather than an apologetic aside.

**5. Most families never do this** now appears in the welcome, in bold, before the reader begins.

**6. A progress page.** Six lines at the back, one per session, plus the conversation.

**7. The conversation is named and threaded.** Session 1's Get Started asks the family to write down the one conversation they have been avoiding. Every session closes by returning to it, and Session 6 asks whether it happened and what the first sentence will be.
