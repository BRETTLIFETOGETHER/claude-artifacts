# Day 16

## You Have It in the Wrong Place

### We need to put it in the right place

**"If anyone does not provide for his own, and especially for those of his household, he has denied the faith."** 1 TIMOTHY 5:8

A lot of people tell me they have life insurance and therefore they are in great shape.

And I say, you are not necessarily in great shape. You have insurance. You may have it in the wrong place. Put it in the right place and then it is great that you have it.

The distinction is not a technicality and it costs families real money.

Here is the version I see most. A policy is bought inside a company, because that is where the money was and it seemed simplest. Then at the moment it pays, it increases the value of the business, which increases the estate, which increases the obligation. The thing purchased to relieve pressure has added to it.

Nobody did anything wrong. The policy is fine. It is in the wrong place.

There is another version where a policy is owned by the person it insures, which sounds obviously correct and frequently is not, because it pulls the proceeds into the estate rather than keeping them outside of it.

I am not going to walk through mechanics here, and you should not act on any of this from a devotional. What I want you to leave with is the question, because the question is what most families have never asked.

Not do we have coverage. Is it in the right place.

And I want to say something to families who feel out of their depth reading this. You are not being asked to become an expert. You are being asked to notice that a decision was made, possibly years ago, possibly by default, and that nobody has revisited it.

Noticing is the whole job. The fixing requires somebody else and that is fine.

Paul writes that a person who does not provide for their own household has denied the faith, which is startlingly strong language. He is not talking about insurance products. He is talking about the obligation underneath them.

Providing is not only about income while you are alive. It includes what remains in place when you are not, and whether it works the way you assumed.

So the question for today is short. Where is it, and is that the right place?

# Day 17

## They'll Sell You Insurance Whether You Need It or Not

### You may or may not need it

**"The naive believes everything, but the sensible person considers his steps."** PROVERBS 14:15

I am going to say something that sounds cynical and is not meant that way.

Some people are insurance salesmen. They will sell you insurance whether you need it or not. They will tell you that you need it, and you may or you may not.

I say this as somebody who has recommended a great deal of insurance to a great many families, and who thinks it solves problems nothing else solves.

The issue is not the product. The issue is that the question is almost never asked from the family's side.

The usual sequence is that somebody presents a need, the need sounds real, and the family buys a solution to it. What almost never happens is a family sitting down independently and asking what they are actually protecting and from what.

That order matters enormously, because when the need is defined by the person selling the solution, the need and the solution will always match.

So define it yourself first. What are you protecting? Not which policies do we hold. What would be lost, and who would lose it.

For most families the honest list is short. A way of life for a spouse. A period of stability while people figure things out. A business that would otherwise have to be sold quickly. Sometimes a specific obligation that will come due.

Write that list before anybody shows you anything, and then evaluate what you already own against it.

You may find you are underinsured against the thing that would actually hurt and overinsured against something that stopped being a risk fifteen years ago.

The proverb says the naive believe everything and the sensible consider their steps. It is not counseling suspicion of people. It is counseling a pause between hearing something and acting on it.

That pause is where you ask what you are protecting.

And here is the freeing part. Once you know what you are protecting, most of these conversations get much shorter, because you can evaluate a proposal against your own list rather than against somebody else's presentation.

### Reflect

**1.** What are you actually protecting? Say it in one sentence.

**2.** Who would lose what, and when?

**3.** What are you insured against that is no longer a risk?

**4.** What is unprotected that would genuinely hurt?

# Day 18

## To Protect the Family Lifestyle

### He doesn't need protection at his net worth

**"A good person leaves an inheritance to his children's children, and the wealth of the sinner is stored up for the righteous."** PROVERBS 13:22

Insurance has a purpose and the purpose changes over a life. Families rarely notice the change.

Early on, the purpose is obvious. A young family loses an income and everything falls apart. Coverage is protecting a lifestyle that depends entirely on somebody continuing to earn.

Later, for some families, that stops being true. The assets themselves can sustain the household. The income is no longer the load-bearing wall.

At that point the policy that made complete sense at thirty-five is protecting against something that can no longer happen, and it is still being paid for every year.

I had a client with substantial life insurance who had decided to leave his entire estate to charity. He did not need the coverage at all. His family was provided for, the estate had no liquidity problem, and the policy was solving a problem he no longer had.

What I liked about how he handled it was that he looked. He examined the thing rather than continuing it by inertia, and he made a decision.

That is all this day asks. Look at it.

Now, the reverse also happens and it is more common. A family's situation grows and the coverage does not. Income doubles, obligations expand, a business appears, more people become dependent, and the policy amount is still calibrated to a life the family stopped living a decade ago.

Both errors come from the same source, which is that nothing prompts a review. There is no annual notice asking whether your circumstances still match your coverage.

So the family has to prompt it themselves, and almost none do.

The proverb about leaving an inheritance to children's children is usually read as being about accumulation. Read it again with today in mind. It describes something that has to survive two handoffs, which means it has to be protected across a span longer than one person's working life.

Protection is not a young family's concern that gets outgrown. It changes shape and it does not go away.

The question is whether the shape you have matches the shape you need now.

### Reflect

**1.** What was your coverage originally protecting?

**2.** Is that still the risk?

**3.** Has your situation grown while the coverage stayed the same?

**4.** When did anyone last look?

# Day 19

## Hoping That We Don't Screw This Up

### It's not about these fourteen people sitting in the room

**"So then, while we have opportunity, let us do good to all people, and especially to those who are of the household of the faith."** GALATIANS 6:10

I sat in a meeting with a family across three generations. The grandfather was ninety-two. His son was sixty-five. His grandson was thirty-five.

And the grandfather said something I have never forgotten.

He said what we are doing here is not about these fourteen people sitting in this room. He said we have eighty-five hundred employees. And if each one of them has two people in their family, that is twenty-five thousand people hoping we do not screw this up.

I want to sit on that, because it is the widest view of protection anyone has given me.

He was not thinking about the family's wealth. He was not thinking about his own legacy in the way that word usually gets used. He was thinking about people he had never met who were depending on decisions being made in that room.

Now, your family probably does not employ eighty-five hundred people. The principle scales down without losing anything.

Who depends on what you have built? Not who inherits it. Who depends on it.

Employees, if you have them. But also a household that would be affected. A ministry that counts on your giving. A parent whose care is supported. A grandchild whose education was quietly promised. A person who works for you in some small capacity and whose life would change.

Most families have never made that list, and it is longer than they expect.

The reason this belongs in the insurance session is that protection is usually calculated around a nuclear family and the actual circle of dependence is wider.

Galatians says while we have opportunity, let us do good to all, and especially to the household of faith. There are two rings in that sentence. The wider ring and the nearer one. Both are named, and the wider one is named first.

The grandfather in that room had both rings in view at ninety-two, and it was visibly what made him careful rather than what made him anxious.

That is the disposition worth having. Not the weight of it. The clarity of it.

## What Are You Protecting

### Not a product conversation, a protection conversation

**"The prudent sees the evil and hides himself, but the naive proceed, and pay the penalty."** PROVERBS 22:3

This is the last day of the I, and it comes back to where the session started.

This is not a product conversation. It is a protection conversation.

Everything else in this session has been circling that. Where is it held. Do you need it. Has it changed. Who depends on it. Every one of those questions is downstream of a single one.

What are you protecting?

Answer that in your own words, before anybody shows you anything, and most of the rest resolves itself.

Here is the shape of a good answer, and it is not a number.

I am protecting my wife's ability to stay in this house and not have to make decisions in the first year. I am protecting a business so it does not have to be sold in ninety days. I am protecting a grandchild whose situation is not going to change. I am protecting the fact that my children will not have to choose between caring for their mother and their own families.

That is a protection conversation. It has people in it.

Compare that to how these decisions usually get made, which is a coverage amount attached to a rule of thumb.

Now, the honest caveat for this session. Some of this genuinely requires help, and I have said that repeatedly rather than pretending otherwise. Ownership structures, tax treatment, and what belongs inside or outside an estate are not things to work out from a devotional.

But defining what you are protecting is not technical. That part is yours and nobody can do it for you, and it is the part that determines whether the technical work is aimed at the right target.

The proverb says the prudent see it coming and take cover. It appears twice in Proverbs, almost word for word, which in that book is a way of underlining something.

Taking cover is not fear. It is the ordinary work of looking ahead at a small cost so somebody else does not pay a large one later.

That is the letter I. Next is the L, and it is the other side of everything you have looked at so far.

# Day 21

## The Most Revealing Document You Can Find

### All your income goes into your tax return

**"Let us examine and search out our ways."** LAMENTATIONS 3:40

An income tax return is the most revealing document you can find.

All your income flows into it. Even something nobody mentioned to you shows up there, because it has to.

I say that to advisors and they nod. I say it to families and they are surprised, because most people file the return and never look at it as anything except a compliance exercise.

Look at it as a picture instead.

You can see where the income actually comes from. Earned income. Passive income. Business income. Investment income. What is producing and what is not. What exists that you had half forgotten. What somebody set up years ago that is still quietly running.

That is a portrait of your financial life, assembled by legal requirement, updated annually, and sitting in a drawer.

There is a second thing the return will tell you, and it is the one that matters for this session. It shows what is attached to everything.

Families look at what they have built. Almost nobody looks at what is attached to it. Debt, obligations, tax exposure, future liabilities that will arrive when something is sold or transferred.

Those things are always there, quietly shaping what is actually available and what decisions can really be made. And they do not appear on the statements a family looks at monthly.

Now here is the practical instruction, and it is a good evening rather than a project.

Sit down with last year's return, with your spouse, and read it together. Not to check anybody's work. To understand where the money comes from and what is attached.

You do not need to be able to prepare a return to read one. Ask the person who prepares yours to walk you through it once. Most will, and most are surprised to be asked.

Lamentations says let us examine and search out our ways. It is written from the lowest point in the book, and the instruction is not to fix anything yet.

It is to look. That is what a return is for, on a day like today.

# Day 22

## Shackled Because of Their Debts

### Not worth what they think they're worth

**"The rich rules over the poor, and the borrower becomes the lender's slave."** PROVERBS 22:7

Debt is really important, and a lot of people are shackled because of their debts.

They owe money to somebody, and until that comes down, they really are not worth what they think they are worth.

I want to take that in two directions, because the first one is arithmetic and the second one is the part that actually matters.

The arithmetic is simple. Net worth is what you own minus what you owe, and families consistently carry the first number in their heads and not the second. So the figure a person quotes to themselves is usually the gross one, and the decisions they make are calibrated to a number that is not real.

The second direction is the one the proverb is actually about. The borrower becomes the lender's slave.

That is not a statement about arithmetic. It is a statement about freedom.

Debt constrains what you can do, and specifically it constrains what you can do quickly. A family carrying significant obligations cannot easily change direction, cannot easily be generous on short notice, cannot easily take a season with less income, and cannot easily say no to something they would rather not do.

None of that shows up on a balance sheet as a problem. It shows up in a life as a narrowing of options that people usually attribute to circumstance.

I want to be fair. Not all debt is foolish and some is genuinely productive. A mortgage on a home you can afford is a different thing from a line of credit funding a lifestyle. Borrowing to build something with a return is a different thing from borrowing to preserve an appearance.

The question is not whether you carry debt. It is whether you know exactly what you carry, on what terms, coming due when.

That last part is where families are most vague. Not the amount. The timing. Whether something is spread over thirty years or arriving all at once changes everything about what it means, and a good number of families have never assembled that picture.

So the assignment is to write it down. What you owe, to whom, at what rate, due when.

Freedom is a real category in Scripture and it is used about money more often than people expect.

**2.** What are the terms, and when does each come due?

# Day 23

## It Says It's Worth a Million

### If they sell it, it's not worth a million

**"For which one of you, when he wants to build a tower, does not first calculate the cost?"** LUKE 14:28

Here is one of the more useful sentences in this whole letter.

It says it is worth a million dollars. If they sell it, it is not worth a million dollars.

Families carry values in their heads that are gross rather than net, and the difference is frequently large.

An asset appreciates. The statement shows the appreciated value. The family thinks about that number, plans around that number, and mentions that number when describing where they stand.

Then the asset is sold and a meaningful portion goes to tax, and the family discovers that the number they had been carrying was never the number available to them.

That is not a trick and nobody misled anybody. It is simply that the reported value and the usable value are different figures, and only one of them was ever displayed.

I want to draw out the practical consequence, because it is not merely a matter of feeling disappointed.

Families make real decisions off the gross number. They decide what to leave to whom. They decide how much they can afford to give. They decide whether a business needs to be sold to cover something. All of those decisions are correct only if the number is correct.

And the gap is largest exactly where families are least likely to look, which is on assets that have been held a long time and appreciated a great deal. The most valuable thing you own is frequently the one where the difference between gross and net is widest.

There is a second version that applies to businesses. A valuation is a number produced under a set of assumptions, and the price a buyer will actually pay under time pressure is a different number entirely.

Jesus describes a man calculating whether he has enough to complete a tower. He is not calculating what he owns. He is calculating what is available for the purpose.

That is the distinction. Not what does it say. What is actually usable.

So take the two or three largest things you hold and ask, honestly, what would be left if this were converted.

It is an uncomfortable exercise and it changes plans.

# Day 24

## The Worst Asset to Give to Charity Is Cash

### You've got highly appreciated stocks

**"Honor the Lord from your wealth and from the first of all your produce."** PROVERBS 3:9

I tell generous families something that surprises them.

The worst asset to give to charity is cash.

Not because cash is bad. Because if you hold something that has appreciated substantially, giving that instead accomplishes the same thing for the charity and does something quite different for you, and then you can take the cash you would have given and buy back the same position.

I am not going to walk through the mechanics, and you should not act on it from here. What I want you to leave with is the awareness that it exists.

Because here is what actually happens in a lot of generous families. They give, faithfully, from checking, every year, in the least efficient way available to them, and nobody has ever mentioned that there is another way.

That is not the family's failure. Nobody told them.

And the amount at stake is not trivial. For a family that gives significantly and holds appreciated assets, the difference over a decade can fund another whole area of giving.

I want to name the objection I hear, because it is a good one. Some families feel that optimizing a gift is somehow in tension with the spirit of giving. That thinking about tax treatment corrupts the act.

I would push back gently. Stewardship includes the mechanism. If the same sacrifice on your part can produce more for the work you care about, choosing the less effective route is not more spiritual. It is just less effective.

The proverb says honor the Lord from your wealth and from the first of your produce. The word first is the instruction. Not from what is left over after everything else has been decided.

Notice it also says from your wealth, not from your income. Those are different sources, and most families give exclusively from the second one because that is where the checking account is.

Giving from wealth rather than from income is a different conversation and most families have never had it.

So today is not asking you to give more. It is asking whether the giving you already do is arranged the way you would arrange it if somebody had explained the options.

**"Then render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's."** MATTHEW 22:21

This is the last day of the L, and I want to close it somewhere other than technique.

Jesus is handed a trap. The question is designed so that either answer damages him, and he asks for a coin, and then he says render to Caesar what is Caesar's and to God what is God's.

I raise that because a great deal of financial conversation in Christian families carries an undertone that taxes are essentially theft and that minimizing them is a moral good in itself.

Ron Blue used to say something that stopped me the first time I heard it. He said he loves paying taxes.

That is not a question about a percentage. It is a question about whether the second obligation in that verse has ever been treated as an obligation at all.

## No Such Thing as an Independent Financial Decision

### There's only five things you can do with your money

**"For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also."** MATTHEW 6:21

There is no such thing as an independent financial decision.

Every one is connected to every other one, because there are only five things you can do with money and every dollar goes into one of them.

You can live on it. You can give it away. You can pay taxes with it. You can repay debt. You can save and invest it.

That is the complete list. There is no sixth.

Which means that when you decide to do more of one, you have decided to do less of something else, whether or not you noticed making that second decision.

When you buy a five-dollar coffee, you could have done something else with it. That is not an argument against coffee. It is a description of how a closed system works.

Families find this either liberating or uncomfortable depending on where they are.

It is liberating because it makes the whole thing tractable. Five categories is a manageable number. You can allocate five things at the start of a year and look at them again in six months, and that is the entire discipline.

It is uncomfortable because it removes the possibility of adding without subtracting. A family that wants to give more and does not want to spend less has not made a plan, they have made a wish.

Here is how I actually run this with families. At the beginning of the year, take the five and decide roughly what proportion goes to each. Then six months later, look at it together.

Usually somebody says, well, I missed that one, did I not.

And that is fine. There is no shame in it. I am not trying to catch anybody. I want people to know, because a family that knows can decide, and a family that does not know is being decided for.

Jesus says where your treasure is, your heart will be also. Read the direction of that sentence carefully. It is not saying your money follows your heart.

It says your heart follows your money.

Which means the allocation is not a report on what you love. It is one of the things forming what you love.

# Day 27

## Are They Living Within Their Means

### The first question I usually ask the next generation

**"I have learned to be content in whatever circumstances I am."** PHILIPPIANS 4:11

When I sit down with somebody in the next generation, this is usually the first question I ask.

Are you living within your means?

Not what will you inherit. Not what are your goals. Are you currently running your own life on what you currently have?

The reason I start there is that it is the only reliable predictor I have found.

Whatever system a person is using now, with what they have now, is the system they will use later with more. Habits do not upgrade when the numbers do. They scale. Somebody who spends slightly more than they earn at fifty thousand will spend slightly more than they earn at five hundred thousand, and the arithmetic gets worse rather than better because the slightly more is a larger number.

Families find this uncomfortable to look at, especially in a child they are worried about, because it feels like judging.

I would frame it differently. You are not judging anybody. You are gathering the single most useful piece of information available about readiness, and the person themselves usually knows the answer.

There is a second reason I ask it, and it applies to the older generation as much as the younger.

Living within your means is not about income level. I have met families with very little who were entirely within their means and families with a great deal who were not. The second group is not obvious from outside, which is exactly what makes it dangerous.

If a household's expenses have expanded to consume everything, then every decision that family makes is constrained, regardless of the size of the numbers involved. They cannot easily be generous. They cannot easily absorb a bad year. They cannot easily say no.

Paul says he has learned to be content in whatever circumstances. The word learned is the load-bearing one. It was not a temperament he was born with. It was acquired, presumably slowly and with some difficulty.

Contentment is a skill, and it is the skill underneath living within your means, and it can be taught to a young person by somebody willing to model it.

Which raises the harder question. Is it currently being modeled in your family?

# Day 28

## Money Changes People

### They don't become better, they become worse

**"For the love of money is a root of all sorts of evil."** 1 TIMOTHY 6:10

I am going to say something blunt that I have watched enough times to believe.

When there is a bunch of money involved, people become different. And they do not become better. They become worse.

I want to be precise about who I mean, because this is not a statement about greedy people.

I mean ordinary, decent people, in families that love each other, at the moment when a significant amount of money enters the picture. Something happens. Positions harden. Old grievances that had gone quiet reappear with new evidence attached. People who had never thought about fairness start keeping score.

None of them planned it. Most would be ashamed to see a transcript.

And I do not think the money creates the material. I think it removes the reason to keep it buried. Whatever was already there, unaddressed, becomes suddenly worth raising, because now there is something attached to it.

Which is why this belongs in a book about alignment rather than a book about character.

A family that has done the work in these six letters has removed most of the fuel. Everyone knows what exists. Everyone knows what was decided and why. The documents say what the family would say. Nobody is discovering anything at the worst possible moment.

That family can still have conflict. But the conflict is about the thing itself rather than about a decade of accumulated interpretation arriving at once.

Paul says the love of money is a root of all sorts of evil. Note what he does not say. He does not say money is a root of evil. He says the love of it is.

And love is not something people admit to having. Nobody in your family would describe themselves as loving money. It shows up not as affection but as fear of not getting a fair share, which feels like justice rather than love and behaves identically.

So the question for a family is not whether anyone loves money. Everyone will say no and mean it.

It is whether the conditions exist for that fear to have something to work with.

# Day 29

## You Need to Not Judge People

### The thing you have to be careful about is judgment

**"Do not judge, so that you will not be judged."** MATTHEW 7:1

There is a warning I give to anybody doing this work, and it applies just as much to a parent looking at a child as to an advisor looking at a client.

The thing you have to be careful about is judgment.

If a man flies around in a private plane, you need to not judge him. Everybody in the room is thinking, well, he could take that money and give it to the kingdom. Well, he could. But he does not. That is not what he has chosen.

And you are responsible for the stewardship of your own assets, not his.

God is not going to ask you what somebody else did with their money. He is going to ask you what you did with what He gave you.

When you actually get that perspective, you stop being judgmental toward anybody, and something else happens too. You become useful to people, because they can tell.

I put this near the end of the Alignment book on purpose, because these six letters produce an enormous amount of information about other people. You will look at somebody's debt. You will look at what somebody spends. You will find out what somebody has not done and should have.

That information can be used two ways and only one of them helps.

The families that handle this badly turn the exercise into an audit. Somebody becomes the person who noticed, and being noticed in that way closes people down permanently. You will get one honest disclosure and then never another.

The families that handle it well treat every piece of information as something they were trusted with rather than something they discovered.

There is a particular version of this between generations. A parent looks at an adult child's finances and cannot help but evaluate. The evaluation is usually accurate and almost always counterproductive, because the child was not asking for an assessment and now knows they will be assessed.

Jesus says do not judge so that you will not be judged. It is one of the most quoted verses and one of the least applied inside families, where we tend to assume that closeness grants standing.

It does not. Closeness grants access, which is a different thing and a more fragile one.

## Where Your Treasure Is

### What am I doing in my life that's taking faith?

**"For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also."** MATTHEW 6:21

This is the last day, and it is where the six letters land.

Where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.

I have quoted that verse already in these thirty days and I am ending on it deliberately, because the direction of it is the whole point of this book and it is the opposite of what most people assume.

We think the sentence means that a person's spending reveals what they love. That is true and it is the smaller reading.

The larger reading is that the spending is forming the loving. Where you put your treasure is where your heart goes, which means the allocation is not a report card. It is one of the instruments by which a person is shaped.

That changes what these six letters have been for.

F was what you own and what you owe. A was whether any of it works together. M was whether the documents say what you would say. I was what you are protecting. L was what is attached to it all. And Y was where the money actually goes.

Every one of those looks like an administrative exercise. None of them is.

They are six ways of finding out where your treasure actually is, as opposed to where you have assumed it was, and therefore where your heart has been going without your having chosen the direction.

A family that has done this work knows something most families never find out. Not what they have. What they have been becoming.

There is a question worth ending on, and it is a harder one than anything else in this book.

What am I doing in my life right now that is taking faith?

Not what have I arranged. Not what have I protected or organized or optimized. What am I doing that would not work unless God showed up?

Because a family can complete all six letters, get every document right, close every gap, and end up with a perfectly aligned life that requires nothing of God at all.

That would be a shame, and it is a real risk of doing this work well.

So finish the alignment. And then ask what it freed you to do.
