# HOW TO USE THIS

Thirty readings. Five a week for six weeks, with two days off. Ten minutes each.

**This is the most practical book in the series and the one people most want to skip.** It is about documents, accounts, coverage, debt, and where the money actually goes. If that sounds like the part you were hoping somebody else would handle, you are the reader we wrote it for.

**One thing before you start.** Everything in these six weeks is a conversation before it is a document. If you get the paperwork organized and skip the conversation, you have done the easy half and left the part that actually determines whether your family holds together.

**This works completely on its own.** No videos required, no guide required, nobody else in your family required.

**It also runs alongside the guide.** If your family is working through *Getting on the Same Page as a Family*, these thirty days are the reading plan. Week One goes with Session 1, and so on.

**Each day has six parts.** The reading. Reflect. Practice. One Question to Ask. The Bucket, which is private and which nobody reads. And Prayer, which is a direction rather than a written prayer.

**A word about numbers.** Nothing in these thirty days requires you to say a figure out loud to anyone. The Bucket is where the real numbers go, and the Bucket is yours.

# Day 1

## They Don't Know What They Have

### Most families are working from pieces

**"For which one of you, when he wants to build a tower, does not first sit down and calculate the cost?"** LUKE 14:28

A lot of times, families do not know what they have.

I want to say that plainly on the first day, because most people assume it is not true of them, and then we sit down and go through it, and it turns out to be true of nearly everyone.

They do not know what it is worth. They do not know who owns it. And that last one matters more than people expect, because whether something is held by the husband, or the wife, or jointly, or by an entity, changes entirely how it moves when it moves.

Here is what I usually find. There is not an absence of information. There is an abundance of it, in pieces.

There is a retirement statement that arrives quarterly and gets filed. There is a policy somebody bought in 1998 for a reason that made sense in 1998. There is a property, or a share of a property, that came through a parent. There is a business, and the business has a value that has never been established by anybody. There are accounts at two institutions because of something that happened during a job change eleven years ago.

Every one of those pieces is known by somebody. Not one person knows all of them at once.

So the family has access to information and no picture. And you cannot make a decision from information. You make decisions from a picture.

Notice what the verse actually describes. A man wants to build a tower, and the first thing he does is sit down. Not gather advisors. Not draw plans. He sits down and calculates, because he is trying to find out whether what he has will reach where he is going.

That is the entire exercise of this week, and it comes before every technical question in the other five.

I will tell you the objection I hear most, because you may be having it right now. *We are not complicated enough for this.* Or the other version, *my advisor handles all that.*

On the first one, complication is not the issue. A family with one house, one retirement account, and one policy has exactly the same problem as a family with twelve entities. Neither one has looked at it together.

On the second one, I would ask a gentle question. If something happened to you this week, could your spouse call your advisor and ask an intelligent question? Not because the advisor is untrustworthy. Because there is a difference between somebody managing something and somebody understanding something, and only one of those transfers.

There is a reason this is the first letter of the acrostic. Everything in the other five weeks depends on knowing what is in front of you.

You are not being asked to fix anything today. You are being asked to sit down and find out.

# Day 2

## What You Own Minus What You Owe

### That is the whole definition

**"Know well the condition of your flocks, and pay attention to your herds."** PROVERBS 27:23

A financial statement is what you own minus what you owe. That is the difference. That is your net worth.

I lead with the definition because the phrase intimidates people who have no reason to be intimidated. A financial statement is not an instrument that requires a professional. It is two lists and some subtraction.

What you own goes on one side. The house. The accounts. The retirement. The business, if there is one. The policy that has cash value. The property that came from your mother.

What you owe goes on the other. The mortgage. The line of credit. Anything owed on the business. The obligation nobody counts because it is owed to a family member.

The number at the bottom is not a grade. This is where people get stuck, so let me be direct about it. That number does not measure how well you have lived, how hard you have worked, or whether God is pleased with you. It measures one thing, at one moment, and its only purpose is to tell you where you are standing.

The proverb is instructive here, and the wording repays attention. *Know well the condition of your flocks.* Not the value. The condition.

Those are different things. Value is what something would fetch. Condition is how it is actually doing, whether it is healthy, whether it will still be there in a decade. A shepherd who only knows the market price of sheep does not know his flock.

Most families track value, sporadically, and almost none track condition. They know roughly what the account said last quarter. They do not know whether the whole arrangement is sound.

And here is a thing worth knowing about your own number. It is probably not what you think.

People who are doing well usually guess low, because they are counting the accounts and forgetting the business or the property. People who are anxious usually guess high on the debt side, because the thing that keeps them awake is more vivid than the thing that does not.

Either way, the guess is a feeling. The statement is a fact. And a family cannot align around a feeling.

One more thing before you go. You are going to be tempted to make this exact. Do not. Approximate is fine for now. The purpose today is not precision, it is possession of the picture.

Get it roughly right, in one place, on one page. Precision can come later and will come easier once the shape is visible.

# Day 3

## I Can't Believe We Have All This

### The conversation most couples have never had

**"The heart of her husband trusts in her, and he will have no lack of gain."** PROVERBS 31:11

A lot of times the wife does not have a clue.

I do not say that unkindly and it is not a criticism of anybody. She knows he goes to work every day. She knows there is a business. She has no idea what it is worth, because that was the arrangement, and the arrangement worked, and nobody ever revisited it.

Then we put the statement in front of both of them and she looks at it and says something like, my goodness, I cannot believe we have all this.

And just that alone is like marital counseling.

I have watched that moment many times and it never gets less interesting, because of what is actually happening in it. It is not the number. It is that for the first time in a long marriage, two people are looking at the same information at the same time and making the same observations about it.

Up to that point, one person has been holding the whole thing. And here is what nobody says out loud about that arrangement. It is lonely for the one holding it.

Men in particular will tell me, once we are alone, that they have carried it by themselves for thirty years and it never occurred to them that they could put it down. Not because their wife could not handle it. Because they had gotten into a pattern in year three and had never gotten out.

The proverb says the heart of her husband trusts in her. Notice which direction that runs. It is not about her trusting him with the money. It is about him trusting her with the truth of it.

I want to speak to whichever side of this you are on.

If you are the one holding it, the reason to share it is not fairness. It is that one day, and neither of us knows the day, she is going to have to understand all of this at once, while grieving, with people around her offering advice. That is the worst possible moment to learn it for the first time, and you can spare her that on a Tuesday evening this month.

If you are the one who has not been shown it, you are allowed to ask. Not as an accusation. As a request. *I would like to understand what we have, so I am not learning it in a hard week.* Almost no husband refuses that when it is asked that way.

And if there is no spouse, this day still applies. Somebody is going to have to understand it. Decide who, and start.

### Reflect

**2.** If that person were unavailable for a month, what would happen?

**3.** If you are the one holding it, what has that cost you that you have not named?

**4.** If you are not, what has kept you from asking?

# Day 4

## A Big Disconnect

### Between where you are and where you said you wanted to be

**"Consider your ways!"** HAGGAI 1:5

Once a family can see where they are, we hold it up against what they told us they wanted. And a lot of the time we have to say, there is a big disconnect here.

That sentence lands hard the first time somebody hears it about their own family, so let me say immediately what I mean by it and what I do not.

A gap is not a failure. It is information.

Most families are surprised to find gaps, and the surprise is the point. They were not ignoring anything. They did not know the gaps were there, because nobody had ever put the two pictures side by side. What you said you wanted, and what your life currently reflects.

And a gap is not always something missing from the structure. Sometimes everything is technically in place and the gap is in preparation. The documents are fine and the person who will receive what they describe has never been told anything and has no idea what is coming.

I remember telling a man what would actually happen the day he died. His son would receive a check, that week, for an amount his son had never handled in his life.

He said, that would be horrible.

He was right, and nothing in his plan was defective. Every document said what he wanted it to say. The gap was between what the paper would do and what he actually hoped would happen to his son.

That is the kind of thing that only shows up when you put the two side by side.

Haggai says it in two words, and they are two of the sharpest words in Scripture. *Consider your ways.* The people had built houses while the temple sat unfinished, and the prophet does not accuse them of anything. He tells them to look.

Looking is uncomfortable in a specific way. It is not the discovery of a disaster. It is the discovery that a hundred reasonable decisions, made over twenty years, have added up to something nobody would have chosen.

That is what a legacy by default actually is, seen up close.

So today is not about fixing anything. Today is about being willing to hold the two pictures next to each other and say out loud where they do not match.

You will not close every gap. Some of them will take years. But you cannot close a gap you have not named, and naming it costs nothing except the willingness to look.

# Day 5

## You Can't Change Where You Are

### If you don't know where you are

**"The wisdom of the sensible is to understand his way."** PROVERBS 14:8

There is a simple reason we start here rather than somewhere more interesting.

You cannot change where you are if you do not know where you are.

That sounds obvious written down. It is not obvious in practice, because almost every family I have worked with wanted to start somewhere else. They wanted to talk about the trust. Or the business sale. Or what to do about a particular child. Those are real questions and they are all downstream, and answering a downstream question from an unknown position produces a confident decision that turns out to be built on nothing.

I have seen families spend serious money restructuring something that did not need restructuring, because nobody had established the starting point.

So here is what this first week has actually been.

Day one, most families do not know what they have. Day two, the definition, which is two lists and subtraction. Day three, the conversation between two people who have never looked at the same page at the same time. Day four, the distance between what you said you wanted and what is actually in place.

None of that required a professional. None of it cost anything. And a family that does those four things has done more than most families ever do.

The proverb puts it this way. The wisdom of the sensible is to understand his way. Not to change his way. To understand it.

Understanding comes first and it is treated in Scripture as wisdom in its own right, not as a preliminary step to something more important.

Now, I want to be honest about what comes next, because the other five weeks get more technical than this one. Assets and how they fit together. Documents. Insurance. Debt and taxes. Cash flow. Some of it will be outside what you can handle alone, and I will tell you plainly when that point arrives.

But none of it is beyond your understanding. There is a difference between needing help to act and needing help to understand, and the professions have not always been careful about that distinction.

You are allowed to understand your own situation. In fact you are responsible for it, and nobody else can carry that for you.

That is the letter F. What you own and what you owe.

Next week is the A, and it asks a harder question. Not what do you have, but does any of it work together.
