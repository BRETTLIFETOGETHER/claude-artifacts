Part One

The Gap Between
Heard and Lived.

Two failures, opposite in direction, identical in outcome. One produces a congregation that admires the Bible. The other produces a congregation that feels inspired on Sunday and unchanged by Wednesday. Both leave the sermon where it started.

Failure One · Timeless Without Timely
Faithful and Forgettable
Abstract
Academic
Disconnected
Impractical
Forgettable
Inspirational without application
Biblical information without formation
The text was handled well. Nothing happened Monday. The congregation learned something true and did nothing with it, and no one noticed because attendance held.
Failure Two · Timely Without Timeless
Relevant and Rootless
Trendy
Superficial
Merely motivational
Culturally driven
Emotionally manipulative
Detached from Scripture
Focused only on self-improvement
People felt something. It was not the weight of God's Word. The room responded to the delivery rather than the text, and the effect expired by Tuesday.

The timeless gives the sermon authority. The timely gives it relevance. The tools give it clarity. The next step gives it direction. The pathway gives it continuity. The mission gives it multiplication.

Remove any one and the sermon stops short of transformation

Part Two

Two Kinds of
Sermon Intelligence.

A pastor is running two systems at once. One is enduring and portable — it would preach in any century. The other is local and perishable — it only works in this room, this season, with these people. A catalytic sermon requires both, and most tools supply only the first.

Part Three · The Spine

The Six Movements.

This is the arc every catalytic sermon travels. Not six parts of a sermon — six stages of a person's journey from hearing to being changed to changing someone else. Click any movement to open it.

Part Four

The Twelve-Step
Sermon Builder.

The six movements describe what happens to a person. These twelve steps describe what a pastor does at the desk on Tuesday. The order matters — begin with the end in mind, and the sermon builds backward from the transformation you intend.

Part Five

The Eleven Tools.

Each tool is a bridge between timeless truth and timely formation. They are not add-ons to the sermon. They are the machinery that carries the sermon out of the room. Click any to open it.

Part Six

The Transformation
Timeline.

Transformation is not an event on Sunday. It is a sequence with a known shape, known failure points, and a known duration. This is what has to happen and when — and where most churches lose people.

Inspiration lasts seventy-two hours. Everything past Wednesday runs on architecture, not on how good Sunday felt.

Wednesday is the failure point. Design for it or lose the week.

Part Seven

The System
That Delivers It.

A plan without machinery is a philosophy. Every movement in this document has a working tool behind it. Here is what exists and which stage it serves.

Part Eight

How You Know
It Worked.

Attendance measures the building. Response cards measure the moment. Neither measures transformation. Six numbers, one per movement — and only the last one matters at ninety days.

The final question is not only how will this sermon change me. It is how will the change in me help someone else encounter Jesus.

Movement six is the only one that compounds
