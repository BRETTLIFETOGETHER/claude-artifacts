Sunday · Purpose Driven Life · Purpose Three

You Were Created to
Become Like Christ.

The full message — audio, outline, and manuscript. Listen on the commute, scan the outline in two minutes, or read every word.

14:22 / 38:40
📋 The Full Outline
I
God's Goal Is Not Your Comfort
Every believer assumes God wants them happy. Scripture says he wants them holy. The gap between those two assumptions explains most spiritual disappointment.
Romans 8:28-29 · Hebrews 12:10-11
II
Growth Is Not Automatic
Time in church does not equal transformation. Attendance is not formation. The person who has been here fifteen years and is not measurably more like Jesus has been attending, not growing.
Ephesians 4:22-24 · 2 Peter 3:18
III
God Uses Circumstances to Form Character
The situation you have been begging God to remove may be the classroom he enrolled you in. Not every hardship is discipline, but no hardship is wasted.
James 1:2-4 · Romans 5:3-5
IV
Christlikeness Takes Intentional Commitment
You will not drift into Christ's character. Nobody accidentally becomes patient. Formation requires a decision, a practice, and a community.
1 Timothy 4:7-8 · Philippians 2:12-13

The One Thing
God cares more about who you are becoming than what you are going through.
📝 Full Manuscript

Let me start with a question that might sting a little.

If I asked the people closest to you — your spouse, your kids, the person in the cubicle next to you — whether you are more like Jesus than you were a year ago, what would they say?

Not whether you know more. Not whether you attend more. Whether you are more like him. More patient. Less defensive. Quicker to forgive. Slower to need the last word.

Because here is what I have come to believe after decades of watching people in this room: God's ultimate goal for your life on earth is not comfort. It is character.

Romans 8:29 says it plainly — God knew what he was doing from the very beginning. He decided from the outset to shape the lives of those who love him along the same lines as the life of his Son.

Now here is the second thing, and this one is harder.

Spiritual growth is not automatic. It does not happen by osmosis. You do not become like Christ by sitting in a room where his name is spoken.

I have watched people attend this church for twenty years and remain exactly who they were on the day they walked in. Not because they were bad people. Because they were attending, not growing. Those are different verbs.

Which brings me to the third thing, and this is where it gets personal.

You have been praying about a situation. I know you have. Everyone in this room has one. And you have been asking God to change it, remove it, fix it.

What if he has been trying to use it?

Not causing it. Using it. There is a difference and it matters enormously. But James says count it all joy when you meet trials, because the testing of your faith produces steadfastness — and steadfastness is a character word.

So what do you do with that?

You stop asking get me out of this and you start asking what are you forming in me through this.

That is not resignation. That is not pretending it does not hurt. It is a different question — and different questions get different answers.

Here is the last thing I want to leave you with.

Philippians 1:6. He who began a good work in you will bring it to completion.

He began it. You did not. He will complete it. You cannot. And in between the beginning and the completion is a long, slow, unglamorous middle — and that middle is where you live.

God is more patient with your progress than you are.

So before you leave today, do one thing.

Name the character trait. Just one. The one you already know. Write it on the card in your seat, put it somewhere you will see it Monday morning, and then this week, text one person who knows you well and ask them: What is one area of my character you have watched God working on?

And then — and this is the hard part — do not defend yourself.

God cares more about who you are becoming than what you are going through.
