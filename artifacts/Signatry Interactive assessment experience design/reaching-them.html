For Dale Armstrong · The Signatry

Reaching the families nobody has figured out how to reach.

The Signatry exists so that Christian families and the businesses behind them can give with wisdom and joy rather than confusion. The people you most need to reach — the owner approaching an exit, the RIA with three hundred relationships, the family already carrying more than it can steward alone — are exactly the people advertising reaches worst.

That is not a budget problem. Capacity buys privacy. A family with real means is not persuaded by a claim; they move when a peer speaks or when the professional they already pay hands them something. Every dollar spent buying their attention competes with a referral that costs nothing and carries more weight. So the work is not finding a channel nobody has bought. It is building assets the people who already hold that trust want to pass along — and spending on media only to make those assets findable.

Seven ways to build it

1
Make the advisor the channel, not the audience.A large RIA has hundreds of relationships and one recurring problem: how to open a conversation beyond the portfolio. Give the firm the experience under its own name, co-branded, at no development cost. You do not buy the audience — the advisor lends it, and gets something to be generous with.
2
Let the families be the advertising.A family of means will not be moved by an institution describing generosity. They lean forward when a family they recognise describes what it cost them. Filmed testimony is the only creative that survives contact with this audience. Release privately to advisors first, publicly second.
3
Go where advisors already gather.Kingdom Advisors, the custodial conferences, the RIA succession circuit. The instrument is not a booth — it is Steve on a platform and an assessment people leave holding. Ten rooms a year outperforms ten million impressions.
4
Catch the owner at the liquidity moment.A business owner becomes a legacy family at exit — surrounded by a banker, an attorney and a CPA, none of whom has a framework for what the money is for. Exit-planning networks are an underworked door, and the giving mechanics are genuinely useful before the transaction closes.
5
Use the assessment as the front door.A diagnostic is the one thing a high-capacity person will voluntarily open, because it is about them and costs ten minutes. It is already built. Results stay with the family, never routed anywhere — which is exactly why it earns the next conversation.
6
Replace the event with the peer-hosted table.Not a Signatry dinner with a Signatry speaker. A family hosting six families in their home around a shared text. It scales slowly and converts at a rate no event matches, because the host has staked their own credibility on it.
7
Borrow platforms that already own the trust.Pulpits of churches with high-capacity congregations, the handful of podcasts owners actually listen to, and a small number of institutional partners with real lists. Host-read placement on the right three shows will outrun any programmatic spend.

Where paid media earns its keep

Narrow trade press where advisors read, podcast host-reads, and search on the terms a family types at three in the morning after a diagnosis or a sale. Nothing broad, nothing programmatic, no display. The measure is not cost per click — it is whether an advisory firm asked for the licensed edition afterward.

Phase one, by spring

The forty days delivered. The assessment live under its own address. Ten advisory firms running it co-branded. That is a narrow enough target to hit and a real enough proof to build on. The next action is smaller still: name the ten firms, the way Steve is naming the forty families.

The honest version of “best in class” is this: stop trying to buy attention from people who have learned to ignore it, and start making things their advisors, their peers and their pastors want to hand them. That is slower to start and almost impossible to compete with once it is running.
