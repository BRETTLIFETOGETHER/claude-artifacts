Abraham: Go Before You Know

“Go from your country, your people and your father’s household.”Genesis 12:1, NIV
The Story

He was seventy-five. He had a house, a name, a settled life and a father buried in the ground behind him. And the instruction that arrived contained no destination — no map, no distance, no description of the land. Only the word go, and a promise attached to a place God declined to name.

We read it knowing how it turns out, which flattens the thing. Read it the way he received it: a man well past the age when people start over, told to leave every source of security he has for a location to be disclosed later. Any reasonable advisor would have told him to wait. He went.

Steve’s Teaching

Obedience usually begins before God reveals the destination. That is not a flaw in how God leads; it is the mechanism. Had Abraham been given the map, the journey would have been a relocation. Without it, it was faith.

Most of us are not waiting on God for permission. We are waiting for detail — for the outcome to become predictable enough that yes stops feeling like a risk. But detail is exactly what God withholds, and on purpose: a yes that requires certainty first is not a yes at all. It is a transaction with conditions.

Irrational Obedience Today

A business owner sensed, three years before any offer existed, that he was to give away a portion of his company — not sell it and give the proceeds, but give the equity, before there was a buyer or a number. His attorney asked what the tax analysis showed. He had not run one, because he was afraid it would talk him out of it.

Think About It

What have you been calling discernment that is actually a wait for more information?

Talk About It

What is the most recent thing our household did that could not be fully explained to someone outside it?

Live It

Write down one thing you believe God has already asked of you. Do not solve it today — put it where you can see it.

Pray

Father, I have been waiting for details You never promised. Make me willing to move on what I already know.
