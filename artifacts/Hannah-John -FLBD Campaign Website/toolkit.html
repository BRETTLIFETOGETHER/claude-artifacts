The Family Legacy Toolkit

The Instruments Of An
Intentional Family.

Ten tools for the work between the readings — values, mission, letters, heirs, meetings, and giving. Six are alive on this page: fill them at the table, and they save quietly in this browser and print clean enough to frame. The other four wait as designed kits.

Instrument 01 · From Session Two Of The Flagship

The Family Values Worksheet.

Choose the five values your family wants to be known for — then make each one visible. A value that never shows up in behavior is a slogan; this page asks the four questions that turn words into practice.

Saved in this browser ✓
Instrument 02 · From Session Four Of The Flagship

The Spiritual Legacy Statement.

Five guided blanks become one statement your family can read aloud, sign, and revisit every year. Rough is fine — a drafted sentence at the table beats a perfect one that never gets written.

Instrument 03 · From Days Seventeen & Twenty

The Legacy Letter Composer.

What is spoken can be treasured; what is written can be inherited. Six prompts walk you through the letter — begin with the first line and let the rest take the time it takes. You do not need to say everything. Begin with what matters most.

Instrument 04 · From Session Three Of The Flagship

The Heir Readiness Worksheet.

For each member of the next generation, prayerfully consider eight dimensions of readiness. There are no scores on this page, on purpose. This is not a judgment exercise. It is a preparation exercise.

Saved in this browser ✓
Instrument 05 · Built On The Six-Session Frame

The Family Meeting Agenda.

Pick the movement your next gathering should live in, and the agenda composes itself — an opening question, a Scripture for the table, the conversation, one step, and a blessing to close. Question sets are composed in the framework's voice; shuffle until one fits your family.

Saved in this browser ✓
Instrument 06 · From Day Nineteen & The Generosity Domain

The Family Giving Plan.

Generosity taught is easily forgotten; generosity practiced together becomes part of a family's name. Name the purpose, choose the priorities, decide how the youngest participates, and set the rhythm.
