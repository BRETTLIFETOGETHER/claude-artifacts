How To Open It, From Either Side.

The same conversation started two ways — by the advisor who wants to raise it, or by the family member who wants their advisor to.

For The Advisor · Not Shown To Families Introducing This To A Client Family

Four moves, in order. Say them in your own words — change anything that does not sound like you.

  1. “Before we talk about the plan, I want to ask about the family.” Signal the shift before making it.
  2. “Most families I sit with have a strong plan and a quiet table. I don’t want that for you.” Name the gap without naming their gap.
  3. “There’s a short assessment — ten minutes, the two of you, no numbers.” Make the first step small and specific.
  4. “Then I’d like to give your family a thirty-day journey. On me.” Gift it. Do not sell it.
Drafts, not scripts. The point is the order, not the wording.
For The Family Asking Your Advisor Or Pastor For This

If your family wants to start and your advisor has not raised it, this is how to raise it yourself.

  1. “I read something that named what I’ve been worried about.” Start with the worry, not the product.
  2. “It’s not about the plan. It’s about whether our kids are ready for it.” Move the subject to the people.
  3. “There’s a ten-minute assessment. Would you walk through it with us?” Give them a role they can say yes to.
  4. “If this isn’t your lane, is there someone you’d point us to?” Leave a door open either way.
Bring one printed reading to the meeting. It does more than a description.
