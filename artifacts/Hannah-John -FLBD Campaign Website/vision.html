The Vision · Why Campaigns, Why Now

The Room Just Got Bigger.

Everything Tom Conway does happens in a room — a living room, a conference room, a Sunday-school room — with families leaning in while a shepherd asks the questions no one else will. The Family Legacy Collection changes exactly one thing about that picture. The size of the room.

Chapter One

The Family Journey Isn't A New Idea.
It's The Oldest One.

"And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up."

Deuteronomy 6:7 · KJV

Sitting. Walking. Lying down. Rising. God's original design for passing faith between generations was never an annual event — it was a daily rhythm, woven through ordinary mornings and ordinary tables. A forty-day family journey doesn't invent anything. It hands a household back the cadence Deuteronomy assumed it would have.

Chapter Two

A Shepherd's Reach Has Grown Before.

Four rooms, one unbroken line — each larger than the last, none replacing the one before it.

The Tent · Deuteronomy 6

Faith Was A Daily Household Rhythm

Before there were classrooms or curricula, there was a family, a doorframe, and a command to talk of these things morning and night. The table was the first sanctuary.

The Letters · The First Century

Words Went Where Feet Could Not

Paul planted churches face to face — then kept shepherding them from cities away, in his own voice, through pages read aloud in rooms he would never enter. Most of what remains of his ministry is exactly that: the letter, still arriving.

The Campaign · A Generation Ago

Forty Aligned Days Moved Whole Congregations

Everyone reading the same short chapter each morning, every group asking the same questions that week, every sermon landing on soil the readings had already turned. Tens of thousands of churches walked that pattern — and the Lifetogether team has spent the decades since refining the craft behind it.

The Table · Now

The Same Craft, Set Where Tom's Ministry Has Always Pointed

Not the sanctuary this time. The kitchen table. The Collection takes the proven architecture of the churchwide campaign and aims it at the smallest, oldest congregation there is: a family.

Chapter Five

A workshop is an event. A journey is a habit two people can keep together from two different cities — a grandmother in one state and a sixteen-year-old in another, reading the same page before breakfast, carrying the same question into their Sunday call. Four children grow up. Eight grandchildren scatter the way blessings do. Some of the most important tables are the ones you can't drive to.

Where It All Ends Up

"There will be a family meeting."

Tom has told families the rest for years: the only question is whether you'll be present for it. Every journey in the Collection is, at bottom, forty days of rehearsal — so that when the family meeting comes, it finds a family already practiced at sitting down together, already fluent in the conversation, already there.
