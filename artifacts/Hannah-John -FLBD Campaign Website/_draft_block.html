Instrument 07 · The Conversation Draft

Not A Script. A Draft.

A script assumes there is a correct performance and you are failing it. A draft assumes the opposite: here is a place to start, in your own voice, that you will change. Every conversation instrument in the library follows this shape.

The Shape
  1. Before you say anythingWhat is actually at stake, and what he is likely bracing for.
  2. The openingAbout a hundred words you could say. Written to be edited, not read aloud as written.
  3. What to changeThe lines to cut, and the one place to put your own story instead of ours.
  4. If he says thisThe four responses that actually come back, and the next sentence for each.
  5. How to end wellThe same close, whichever way it went.
  6. If it goes badlyWhat to do the next morning.

We are not writing this for you. We are giving you a draft of what you could say, and what to do depending on what comes back.

A Mother, Her Son, And The Thing She Has Not Said

For a matriarch speaking to an adult son about his family or the business

Before you say anything

You are not trying to correct him. You are trying to hand him something while you still can. He will assume this is criticism, because that is what most conversations about how he is living have turned into. The first twenty seconds decide which conversation this becomes.

One thing to settle before you begin: you are not asking him to change anything today. If you need a decision out of him tonight, this will fail.

The opening — a draft

Michael, I want to say something I have been carrying for a while, and I would rather say it badly than not say it at all.

I am seventy-eight. I do not know how many good years are left, and that has made me braver than I used to be.

I watch you work the way your father worked. I was proud of him and I am proud of you. But I also remember what it cost the rest of us, and I do not think you can see that yet from where you are standing. Your children are learning what matters from your calendar, not from what you tell them.

I am not asking you to change anything tonight. I am asking you to let me say it, and then to tell me if I have it wrong.

What to change

Cut the line about his father if that comparison would land as an accusation rather than a kindness. Put your own sentence where ours is thin: one specific thing you have watched, said plainly and without a lesson attached. Specific is generous. General sounds like a verdict.

Keep the last line exactly as it is. Ending on tell me if I have it wrong is what makes this a conversation instead of a speech.

If he says this
“You have no idea what it takes to run this.”

He is defending, which means he heard it. Do not defend back and do not withdraw the point. Try: “You are right that I do not. Tell me what it takes.” Then listen for eight or ten minutes without steering. Most of what you wanted to say gets said by him.

He goes quiet.

Quiet is usually agreement he is not ready to speak. Do not fill it and do not repeat yourself. Try: “You do not have to answer. I just did not want to leave it unsaid.” Then let the room be quiet with him.

“You are right. I need to do better.”

The most dangerous answer, because it ends the conversation and changes nothing. Try: “I do not want you to feel bad. I want one thing to be different by Christmas. What would you pick?” A named, small, dated thing is worth more than agreement.

“You and Dad were not perfect either.”

True, and saying so out loud takes the weapon out of his hand. Try: “No, we were not. That is exactly how I know.” Do not defend the past. Your own regret is the most credible thing you have.

How to end well

However it went, close the same way and do not add to it: “Thank you for letting me say that. I love you and I am not worried about you. I am just not willing to run out of time.”

If it goes badly

Some of these end with a door closing. Do not chase it that night and do not apologize for saying it. Write four sentences the next morning: that you love him, that you would say it again, that you are not asking for a response, and that you will be at the same table on Sunday. Then be at the table on Sunday.
