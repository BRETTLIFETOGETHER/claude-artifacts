A Shepherd Among Advisors.

The Collection carries one man's questions. It exists because of the rooms he's already filled — and the tables he can't reach.

Tom Conway
CPA · Kingdom Advisor
Founder, Legacy by Design
"Wisdom as well as wealth" — the six words under everything he builds.

Tom Conway has spent his career in the rooms where families make their biggest decisions — Ernst & Young, Cru, Ronald Blue, Perimeter Church, Generous Giving, the National Christian Foundation, Kingdom Advisors, the Haggai Institute. A CPA by training and a shepherd by temperament, he kept noticing the same thing in every one of those rooms: families were planning their estates and losing their children. The documents were immaculate. The dinner tables were silent.

So he built Legacy by Design around a different premise — that the transfer of wisdom must precede the transfer of wealth, that clarity, alignment, and communication are estate-planning disciplines, and that the five areas of a family's legacy deserve the same rigor as its balance sheet. He contributed to unHERITAGE and unPREPARED; in 2026, Family Legacy by Design and Family Legacy Coaching by Design — written with Brett Eastman — carry the framework to families and to the advisors who serve them.

Four children. Eight grandchildren. Which is to say: the man teaching this framework sits inside a three-generation answer to it. The Collection simply takes the questions he has asked across thirty years of living rooms — and sets them on every table his calendar will never reach.

The Framework

Three Words. Five Areas.

Everything in the Collection is built from the framework the books teach — nothing added, nothing diluted.

1

Clarity

What do we actually believe, value, and hope to hand down? Most families have never said it out loud — so the journeys make them say it out loud, forty mornings in a row.

2

Alignment

Do our plans, our calendars, and our balance sheets agree with our answers? The weekly sessions hold the mirror up, gently and repeatedly.

3

Communication

Has the next generation heard it from us — in our voice, at our table, while we're here to answer questions? The table question exists for exactly this.
