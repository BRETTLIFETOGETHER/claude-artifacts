Family Legacy Intelligence™/Executive platform summary

Every advisor knows the balance sheet. Far fewer know the family.

Family Legacy Intelligence is the system that closes that gap: a repeatable way to discover what a family has received, strengthen what it currently holds, and prepare what it intends to pass forward. It runs the same way for one advisor with ten families and for a firm with four hundred.

What changed

From a library you search to a system that prescribes

The first version of this work was a library. It was large, it was good, and it had the problem every large library has: the advisor still had to know what to reach for, when to reach for it, and how to run it once it was in their hands. The volume was the selling point and the volume was also the obstacle.

What we are building now inverts that. The family is assessed before anything is recommended. The system reads the assessment, the interviews, and the family's own language, and it prescribes the one conversation that matters most right now. The material is written in that family's names and situation rather than handed over generic. And every engagement ends with something the family owns and keeps.

That shift changes the sentence an advisor uses. Not we have a thousand resources, which sounds like homework, but your family only needs the one that matters most right now, and we know which one it is.

Step three — Prescription

The number is not the product. The narrowing is.

A library of 1,090 journeys is an obligation until something decides which one. Three inputs are enough: where the family is in life, what the system needs to know about them next, and how they will actually meet. Choose three and the system names the one journey and the four sessions inside it.

Choose three inputs and run it. The journey is written on the spot for this exact combination — which is the point. Nobody is browsing 1,090 anything.

Positioning

The claim worth making

The temptation in a document like this is to claim to be first. First is unprovable, it invites someone to disprove it, and it is a weaker claim than the true one. The true one is this: the family conversation has been the least systematic part of the most important transfer in history, and this is a working system for it. Not a promise of software. Software that runs today, with an assessment that has been taken, interviews that have been recorded, and a prescription engine that produces something a family can hold.

What cannot be copied quickly is not the AI layer. Any competent firm will have an AI layer within eighteen months. What cannot be copied is a decade of family interview questions that actually open people up, a discernment framework that resists prescribing the lowest score, and a consent architecture that lets a patriarch say something true without wondering whether it becomes a sales note. That is the moat. It should lead every version of this document.
