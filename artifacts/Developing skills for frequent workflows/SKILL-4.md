# Assessment Designer

Brett's instruments follow the Family Legacy Intelligence architecture. Reuse it unless he specifies otherwise.

## Assessment architecture

- Dimensions × statements: N dimensions (commonly 5–6, matching the underlying framework) × 4 statements each
- Each statement rated 1–5; subtotal per dimension (out of 20 with 4 statements); grand total (N × 20)
- Completion target is about ten minutes, so statements stay short, first-person, present-tense, and observable ("We have talked as a family about...", not "Family communication is important")
- Four named score bands covering the full range with no gaps or overlaps. Band names use invitation language, never grades or clinical labels (pattern: Intentional Legacy Formation / Emerging Legacy Alignment / Legacy Conversations Needed / Begin with Trust and Discovery). Check the band math explicitly before delivering.

## The discernment layer

The lowest score is never automatically the first priority. Every assessment ships with three discernment questions that weigh:
1. Impact — where would movement matter most?
2. Urgency — what cannot wait?
3. Invitation — where is God already inviting action?

## Interview instruments

- 10 questions per instrument, plus a closing multi-part legacy statement or reflection
- Question sets are role-specific (couple, patriarch, matriarch, next generation) but written to avoid reinforcing stereotypes about men, women, or family leadership
- Interviews are recorded and transcribed; the transcript becomes primary source material for downstream deliverables, so questions should draw out story, not yes/no answers
- Include the listen-for list in facilitator notes: stories, Scripture, convictions, family language, defining moments, wounds and turning points, lessons, hopes and fears, blessings, unfinished conversations

## Ethics rule (non-negotiable)

Private spiritual and relational disclosures are never treated as sales intelligence. Material stays confidential, permission-based, and securely stored — and that commitment is stated inside the instrument itself where advisors and families will read it.

## Deliverable shape

Each instrument gets: a purpose paragraph, instructions, the items, scoring, bands, discernment questions, and next-step guidance — structured so it can go straight to print layout or a fillable PDF.
