01
Executive Recommendation

The Ruling: one engine, partner-owned doors.

The master prompt's central question — one movement, separate products, stages in one pathway, or a family of brands — is answered by the client structure itself, and it is a strength, not a constraint. LifeTogether cannot build one master consumer brand, because the three strongest properties belong to three different relationships: the obedience flagship belongs to The Signatry, who own the book outright under the proposal already on the table; Generous Living and the financial wisdom line sit in Ron's lane by the August decision to stay there and give Ron more and better; and Family Legacy is a 50/50 relationship with Tom Conway.

What LifeTogether owns outright is the thing underneath all of them: the transformation spine, the assessment architecture, the campaign engine, the Toolbox, and the Collective library. That is exactly the rights position already written into the Signatry proposal — they own the book; LifeTogether retains the curriculum, campaign, and assessment frameworks in perpetuity.

The movement is real. It just doesn't wear one logo. It wears theirs — and the engine underneath every door is ours.

07
Assessment Architecture

One instrument. Two depths. Settled

Twenty-four statements across six stages for everyone — six to eight minutes, so the teenager and the skeptical spouse both finish. The extended instrument for legacy families and advisor cohorts is six stages by seven statements, buying reliability without breaking the routing. Individual results roll up into one family profile that names where the generations disagree, and the advisor questionnaire runs on the visible-to-the-family rule: written as though the family is in the room, correctable, with confidential matters kept out of the system entirely.

Formation language throughout, never diagnostic language: already true of you and still being asked of you. Recommendations run both ways, strengths first. And your own guardrail governs the whole thing: don't let the score become the goal.

08
Stories & Testimony

The Story Engine: resolved by design.

The best design decision in the whole build answers the prompt's Section 10: the daily readings run entirely on Scripture and documented public history — David's offering, Gideon, Monmouth, LeTourneau, the widow's mite — so no permissions and no story bank are required. Contemporary testimony lives in local slots each church fills with its own people, which is where testimony belongs anyway. The One Hundred Questions instrument serves the legacy-family rooms.

The one decaying asset is the forty families on film — and that is already the closing ask to The Signatry: if we get nothing else on this list, get us this.
09
Personalization & Technology

The Platform: the profile is the moat.

The profile-to-recommendation-to-journey-to-toolbox loop is live in the prototype. The defensibility question in the prompt's Section 17 has an answer on record: the moat is not the library — anyone can build a library. The moat is the profile: what the system knows about a person and a family before it produces anything, assembled from the assessment, the written questionnaires, and the reflections a reader leaves inside the devotionals themselves. Answer one question on one day and the next day is chosen a little better.

The platform goes to The Signatry at no fee, co-branded, built and operated at LifeTogether's own cost and risk, on revenue participation. Commercially this is the most significant page in the proposal — and the one that has already vanished once in a restructure. It stays.

10
Economics

The Business Model: prove the funded motion first.

The simplest model to prove first is not hypothetical — it is the funded partner engagement plus the Founding 100. Participation is written as three enforceable instruments — an author royalty on the book, a per-church license on the campaign, a revenue share on the platform — never as points, because a nonprofit has no equity and the IP retention is the equity equivalent in publishing.

Ministry and commerce stay separated the way the price list already separates them: diagnostics free forever as the funnel, the church subscription as the only compounding spine, print as the per-copy line that makes campaigns profitable, custom builds by tier, and big-ticket partner engagements sold by conversation. Philanthropic capital funds creation and initial distribution; revenue sustains what philanthropy launched. For church-facing products, economic design protects ministry trust — the covenant is a business rule, not just a spiritual one.

Portfolio Discipline

What Not to Build.

01
A new master consumer brand
The ownership structure forbids it, and the engine strategy makes it unnecessary.
02
A fifth framework
Four exist — the six stages, the twelve modules, the six H's, the six FLI dimensions. The work is mapping, not inventing.
03
A second generosity book beside Visionary Generosity
The consolidation ruling stands; overlapping titles dilute the argument.
04
Confidence to Give as a standalone
A module, not a brand.
05
The native app
Already ruled: email and SMS first, then their existing church app, then a PWA. Build the app when retention says so, not when a pastor asks.
06
White-label engine licensing
Not until the direct business is proven — a distraction with a large number attached.
07
One fused "ecosystem" artifact
No document that merges Signatry, RBI, and FLBD intellectual property into one piece. The lanes stay distinct because the ownership is distinct.

The Horizon

Three Years Out: the wheel turns.

One engine, five platforms — with the Collective, the register of families who signed, as the only asset in the whole system that appreciates. The Signatry door proves the legacy-family motion. The Founding 100 proves the church motion. One denomination proves the network motion. And the pledge becomes the thing The Signatry's own name has always implied: they have asked the world to receive the last check, and never asked anyone to sign first.

Measured honestly, and separately: spiritual outcomes — surrender, family communication, generations at one table — apart from financial metrics. Scale never proves transformation; a year of evidence is worth more than every opinion in this document.

The One Open Decision

Which operating system wears the umbrella? Awaiting Your Call

Three advisor-facing operating systems now run in parallel — Family Legacy Intelligence, CAN Enterprise, and the publishing platform. The recommendation on record is consolidation to one engine with audience-facing doors, most naturally under Family Legacy Ministry as the umbrella. Nothing in the record settles it. It is the next architecture call worth making — and the only one this entire prompt genuinely opens.
