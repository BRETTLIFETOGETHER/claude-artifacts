Week One — Legacy by Design, Not by Default

Day One

Every family leaves one.

Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks, and look well to thy herds.

Proverbs 27 : 23

Let me begin these forty days the way I have begun with more families than I can count — with five sentences. Nobody has ever argued with a single one of them, and nobody has ever heard them without feeling something shift in the room.

i. I am going to die.

ii. I will take nothing with me.

iii. I will probably die at a time I did not anticipate.

iv. Someone else is going to get my stuff.

v. I can only decide before I die who gets it after I die.

Those are the realities of life, and every one of us lives under them. But over the years I have learned to add a sixth, and it is the one people remember: there will be a family meeting. When you are gone, the people you love will gather, and they will talk about your life and your intentions and, yes, your stuff. That meeting is not optional. The only question — and it is one of the most important questions you will ever answer — is whether you will be there for it.

Now look at the old proverb above. It was written for a man who measured his wealth in sheep and cattle, and it tells him to walk his fences — to know the state of what has been entrusted to him, not approximately, but well. Here is what I have noticed after a lifetime around families of means: most of us could recite the state of our accounts nearly to the decimal. Far fewer of us could describe, with the same confidence, the state of our family — what our children actually believe, what our grandchildren are quietly learning from watching us, whether the people who will one day receive everything we have are ready to receive anything at all.

Every family is leaving a legacy. That is not a goal; it is a fact. The faith, the values, the habits, the silences — something is being handed down at your house this very week, whether anyone planned it or not. So the choice in front of you is never whether to leave a legacy. It is whether the one you leave will be built by design — or left by default.

I once sat with a man who had done very well, most of it in real estate. His plan left a modest gift to each of his four children and nearly everything else to charity — and he had never told them a word of it. His wife wasn't at peace with the plan either, which he also hadn't said out loud. When we finally gathered the whole family — ten adults around one table, every voice counted, Dad holding one vote out of ten — something remarkable happened. He told them everything. And instead of the resentment he had feared for years, there was relief. Because the imaginations we invent in silence are almost always worse than the truth spoken in love.

Here is the good news as we begin: you don't need an advisor, a lot of money, or a perfect plan to start. What you need is the willingness to think deeply, ask honest questions, and take one intentional step at a time. That is all these forty days will ever ask of you — one honest look, one real conversation, one small step, every day.

Today, we simply walk the fences.

Today's Question

If your family continues exactly as it is — the same conversations, the same silences, the same rhythms — what legacy is being left by default?

Today's Step

Finish this sentence in writing: "The legacy I most want to leave my family is …" Keep it somewhere you will see it. You will need it again on Day Forty — and you may be surprised how much it has grown by then.

Today's Prayer

Father, everything I have was entrusted, not owned. Give me the courage to know the true state of my family the way a shepherd knows his flock — and the resolve to build, from this day forward, a legacy by design. Amen.

For the advisor at the table

Day One is your goals conversation, already begun.

If you are walking client families through this journey, today's reading has quietly done your hardest work: it has put the realities on the table and planted the by-design-or-by-default question in every reader's mind. This week, follow it with one question of your own — and then do the most valuable thing a trusted advisor can do, which is listen without steering:

"Have you told your children what you intend — and if not, what are you hoping the silence is accomplishing?"

Write down what surfaces. What you hear this week becomes your agenda for the Week Six family meeting — and very often, the beginning of the goals conversation that reshapes the entire plan. The reading opens the door; you get to walk through it.
