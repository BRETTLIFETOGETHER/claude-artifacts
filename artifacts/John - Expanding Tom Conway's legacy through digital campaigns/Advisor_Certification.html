The diagnostic that started it all

Three questions every wealthy
family answers — eventually.

Long before there was a framework, there were three questions Tom asked the families he served. Their answers revealed the same gap, family after family — and that gap is the seat this work was built to fill.

Question One
Who are your advisors?
The list is never short. Attorneys, accountants, managers, agents — accomplished families are surrounded by expertise. That was never the problem.
Question Two
Does any one of them do all three?
Someone who clearly knows your goals, who knows everything you have — not just the pot they manage — and who builds strategy around all of it, as something entrusted rather than merely owned. The answer, almost always: no.
Question Three
Do your advisors share your biblical worldview?
Technically excellent counsel that quietly flinches at generosity, that treats an inheritance as the children's entitlement rather than the Lord's assignment — families feel that gap even when they cannot name it.

Certification exists so that, for the families you serve, the answers finally become yes, yes, and yes — and you are the reason.

From success to significance

The shift every advisor
eventually faces.

"Every plan you write deserves a family that is ready for it."The conviction beneath the certification

The industry trained us on the what of money — investments, structures, tax strategy — and it trained us well. But a quarter of failed transfers fail for one reason: the heirs were never prepared. We perfected the portfolio and overlooked the people.

The certification is a two-day apprenticeship in the other half of the work: the relational side — purposes, values, trust, and the guided conversations that make a technically excellent plan actually hold when it reaches the second and third generation.
