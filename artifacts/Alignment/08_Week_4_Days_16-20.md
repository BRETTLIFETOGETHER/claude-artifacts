## You Have It the Wrong Place
### We need to put it in the right place

**"Anyone who does not provide for their relatives, and especially for their own household, has denied the faith and is worse than an unbeliever."**
1 Timothy 5:8

A lot of people think, oh, we have life insurance, we are in great shape.

No, you are not in great shape. You have insurance, but you have it the wrong place. We need to put it in the right place, and then it will be great that you have it.

I say it that way on purpose, and I want you to notice the structure of it, because it is the reason the sentence works in a room.

The first half takes something away. The second half gives it back. What is being corrected is not the decision to buy the policy, which was sound, and not the man who bought it, who was doing right by his family. What is being corrected is an administrative fact about where the thing sits.

That distinction is the whole conversation. Get it wrong and you have told a client his judgment was poor. Get it right and you have told him his judgment was fine and his paperwork is in the wrong drawer.

Here is what wrong place means, mechanically.

I was with a man recently who has twenty-five million dollars of life insurance on his life. He is the CEO of the company. It is all owned by the company.

That means if he dies, twenty-five million dollars goes to the company. His estate is going to go up by twenty-five million dollars. The government is going to get forty percent of that. They are going to get ten million dollars more in estate tax.

So we just moved it out of the company.

That is the entire fix. Nothing about the policy changed. The face amount is the same, the insured is the same, the premium is the same. One line moved, and ten million dollars stopped going to the Treasury and started going to his family.

A lot of people buy insurance inside a company and they do not know the difference. That is a real bad decision, because the value of that insurance gets added to the value of the business.

Now the part that surprises clients most, which is the cost of fixing it.

You can move it at a very little cost, because right now the only value in that policy is the cash value, which probably is not that great. You can move it very quickly, and then the money will go outside of the estate and be available to the family to pay estate taxes, or to buy assets out of the estate if they want to.

Small move. Enormous result. That combination is rare enough in this business that when you find one you should act on it immediately rather than saving it for the next quarterly meeting.

Paul is not writing about insurance and I am not going to pretend he is. He is writing about the obligation to provide, and he puts it in unusually severe terms. Failing to provide is not described as an oversight. It is described as a denial of faith.

What that verse does to this session is remove the option of treating placement as a technicality. If providing is a matter of faith and character, then so is whether the provision actually arrives where it was aimed. A policy bought with the right intention and placed where the government takes a substantial share of it has not fully provided. It has partially provided and partially funded something else.

Read down the ownership line on one client's policies this week.

That is where the money is.

# Day 17
## They'll Sell You Insurance Whether You Need It or Not
### You may or may not need it

**"The simple believe anything, but the prudent give thought to their steps."**
Proverbs 14:15

Insurance is a very important product. But you need to make sure. Some people are insurance salesmen. They will sell you insurance whether you need it or not. They will tell you you need it, but you may or may not need it.

I am not making a general accusation about an industry. I hold no insurance license myself and I rely on a man who does, who is honest and knows more about it than I ever will. What I am describing is a structural fact: some people in this business are compensated for placing coverage and not compensated for declining to place it. That shapes advice, whether or not anyone intends it to.

So that is why, in our holistic approach, we look at what the potential estate tax liability is. And if they do have an estate tax liability, how are they going to pay for that estate tax liability?

There are a lot of ways to pay for it. One of them is insurance.

Read that again, because the sequence is the whole protection.

Liability first. Then the question of how it gets paid. Then insurance considered as one of several answers rather than as the answer. An advisor who works in that order cannot accidentally become a salesman, because the recommendation arrives at the end of an analysis rather than at the beginning of one.

An advisor who works in the other order has already decided, and everything after that is justification.

Here is how you can tell which one you are dealing with, and it is worth teaching a client to spot it. Ask what the number is. A man who has sized the estate tax liability can tell you what it is and how he got there. A man who has not will talk about protection and peace of mind, which are real things and are not numbers.

I want to name the reverse failure too, because it is less discussed and I see it regularly.

There are advisors who will not raise insurance at all, because they are afraid of sounding like a salesman, and their clients go uncovered against liabilities that are entirely real. That is not integrity. That is a different kind of self-protection, and the family pays for it the same way.

The cure for both is the same. Do the analysis, produce the number, and then say what the number implies, including when it implies nothing.

I have told a man with sixty million dollars of coverage that he did not need it anymore, and I will tell you that story later this week. I have also told a man carrying two million in liability coverage that he was exposed by several times that, and he raised it. Same method, opposite conclusions, and neither one was a product decision.

Solomon's word for the alternative to simplicity is prudence, and prudence is defined here as giving thought to your steps. Not distrust. Not cynicism about salesmen. Thought, applied in advance, to a specific step.

The simple person in that proverb is not stupid. He is unexamined. He believes the last confident person who spoke to him, and in this business there is always a last confident person.

Be the one who brings a number.

# Day 18
## To Protect the Family Lifestyle
### He doesn't need protection at his net worth

**"Your heavenly Father knows that you need them."**
Matthew 6:32

It is just common sense. It is saying, what do you need?

When you ask what is the purpose of insurance and you dig into that, it is to protect the family lifestyle.

He does not need protection at his net worth.

That is the test, and it is a single sentence, and almost nobody in this business applies it.

Here is why it matters. Coverage decisions are usually made against a number, and the number is usually net worth or income or a multiple somebody in a training class taught. Those produce an amount. They do not produce an answer to what the coverage is for.

Ask instead what would actually be at risk. Not what would be lost on a balance sheet. What would change about how these people live.

For a family in the middle of building, the answer is a great deal. Income stops, the house is at risk, the children's education is at risk, and the surviving spouse faces choices under pressure. That is what coverage is for, and the amount follows from it.

For a family that has already built past that point, the answer can be nothing. Their lifestyle is not at risk under any scenario. The assets produce enough on their own. That family may still need liquidity for an estate tax bill, which is a different problem with a different answer, and it may not need life insurance at all in the sense the policy was originally sold.

The same question produces both conclusions. That is what makes it a test rather than a preference.

Now, coverage that has drifted, which is the more common failure and the one worth your attention this week.

A family might have put coverage in place when their situation looked very different. At that time their income was at one level, their responsibilities were simpler, and the number of people depending on them was smaller. The coverage made sense then.

Over time things change. The company is worth a whole lot more. They have a whole lot more that they could lose. Income has grown, assets have grown, responsibilities have increased. Maybe there is a business now that they did not have before. Maybe there are more people depending on that income.

But the coverage stayed the same.

So on paper they still have insurance. If something actually happened, the protection would not match what needs to be protected today. That is where the gap shows.

Nobody made an error. A decision that was correct in one year simply stayed correct-looking while the facts underneath it moved, which is the same failure pattern you saw in the documents week and will see again.

A healthy approach is to review coverage regularly. Ask what you are protecting, what coverage exists, and whether it still matches your situation today. It is not a one-time decision. It needs to be revisited as life changes and adjusted as situations change.

Jesus says your Father knows what you need, in a passage about anxiety and provision, and it is worth being careful with it here. He is not saying do not plan. The whole of Scripture assumes provision and preparation. He is saying that need is a real category with a real boundary, and that the boundary is known.

Most of the overinsurance I encounter is not a product failure. It is a man buying against a fear rather than against a need, and no amount of coverage settles a fear.

Ask one client what he is actually protecting.

Then check whether the number matches the sentence.

# Day 19
## Hoping That We Don't Screw This Up
### It's not about these 14 people sitting in the room

**"Let Pharaoh appoint commissioners over the land to take a fifth of the harvest during the seven years of abundance. This food should be held in reserve for the country."**
Genesis 41:34-36

I met with one family and there were three generations. We had them in the room, and grandpa was ninety-two years old. The next generation was sixty-five. The next generation was thirty-five. It was a billion-and-a-half dollar company.

And grandpa says, you know, our planning, what we are doing here, is not about these fourteen people sitting in the room. We have eighty-five hundred employees. And if each one of those employees has two people in their family, that is twenty-five thousand people that are hoping that we do not screw this up.

I have been in a lot of rooms. That one changed while he was talking.

Nothing technical changed. The estate structure was what it was before he spoke and what it was after. What changed was the size of the thing everybody understood themselves to be doing.

He is looking at their employees, because if the company is not here there are no more jobs for all his people. What a great perspective for a business owner to have. It was not about the fourteen people in the room who owned all the companies. It was about the twenty-five thousand people who were dependent upon this company doing a good job.

I want to point out something about who said it, because it matters.

The ninety-two-year-old said it. Not the thirty-five-year-old, who you would expect to be idealistic. Not the sixty-five-year-old running the thing. The man closest to the end of his life, at the point where most people's field of vision narrows to their own affairs, was the one counting households in the parking lot.

That is not sentimentality. That is a man who has had enough time to work out what the enterprise is actually for.

Now, what this does to your work practically.

Most planning conversations have a defined perimeter, which is the family. Assets, documents, children, taxes. Everything inside the perimeter counts and everything outside it is somebody else's concern. That perimeter is not wrong exactly, and it is much too small.

An estate tax bill that forces a sale does not stop at the family. A business continuity failure does not stop at the family. A next generation that is not prepared to run something does not fail privately.

When a family owns an operating business, the plan has a blast radius, and nobody has ever drawn it for them.

You can draw it in about four minutes. How many employees. How many of them have someone at home. That is the number, and it is usually a number the owner has never calculated, because payroll is a line item and households are not.

Joseph is standing in front of Pharaoh telling him to take a fifth of the harvest for seven years, and the striking thing is the scale of who it is for. Not the palace. The country. The plan is built so that people Joseph will never meet can eat during years he cannot see.

That is what long planning looks like when it is done properly. It provides for people who are not in the room and do not know your name.

Do the arithmetic with one business owner this week.

Let him sit with the number.

# Day 20
## We Helped Him to Build It by Design
### What does he need $60 million worth of life insurance for?

**"And my God will meet all your needs according to the riches of his glory in Christ Jesus."**
Philippians 4:19

I have a client who has sixty million dollars worth of life insurance, but he is going to give all of his estate to charity.

What does he need sixty million dollars worth of life insurance for?

He does not. He does not need it anymore. He could just cash it all in and he will be in good shape.

He was not always in that position. He was not going to give away his whole estate at one point. But now he is at a place where he says, my kids do not need it, we have given them plenty of money. And he says, why do I want to give it to the government?

We helped him to build it by design.

That is the phrase I want on this day, and I want to be careful with it, because it gets repeated loosely and it means something specific.

By design does not mean carefully executed. His original plan was carefully executed. It means that the man decided, deliberately, what the outcome should be, and then the structure was built to produce that outcome. The alternative is not incompetence. The alternative is accumulation: a series of individually sensible decisions that add up to a result nobody chose.

Sixty million dollars of coverage was a sensible decision when he intended to leave the bulk of his estate to his children. The intention changed. The policy did not, because policies do not notice.

Now, what makes this conversation harder than the analysis.

You are unwinding something rather than building it, and unwinding has no natural momentum. Nobody gets excited. Somebody sold that policy and is being paid to keep it in force, and that pressure is real and runs the opposite direction.

There is also resistance from the client that has nothing to do with money. Sixty million dollars of coverage represented a decision about his children. Letting it go requires him to say out loud that he has changed his mind about what they will receive, and that sentence is harder to say than any number in the file.

Which is why I do not open with the policy. I open with what he has decided, and I let him say it, and then the policy becomes an obvious consequence of a decision he already made rather than a loss.

I also want you to see what he did with the difference, because this is the part that makes the whole thing worth doing.

He has a charitable heart, and he did not want to give that much money to his kids, and he certainly did not want to give it to the government. Those three facts were all true before anyone touched the plan. What we did was arrange the structure so that all three could be true at once.

That is the job. Not generating better intentions. Finding out what a man already wants and removing the structural reasons he cannot have it.

Paul writes that God will meet all your needs, and the load-bearing word is needs. Not everything a man might reasonably arrange for. Not every contingency he could insure against. Needs, which has a boundary, and the promise attaches to the boundary rather than to the arrangement.

A man carrying sixty million dollars of protection he no longer requires is not being prudent. He is paying premiums against a scenario that stopped existing, and the money is doing nothing while it waits.

Ask one client this week what he has already decided.

Then go find out whether his structure knows.
