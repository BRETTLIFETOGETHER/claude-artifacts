# Day 21
## The Most Revealing Document That You Can Find
### All your income goes into your tax return

**"There is nothing concealed that will not be disclosed, or hidden that will not be made known."**
Luke 12:2

An income tax return, a personal tax return, is the most revealing document that you can find out, because all your income goes into your tax return. So even if they did not tell you they own a certain thing, it will show up on their tax return.

I have said a great many things over thirty years that turned out to be roughly true. That one is exactly true, and it is the most underused fact in this business.

Most advisors treat a tax return as a compliance artifact. It gets filed, it gets stored, and nobody reads it unless there is a problem. What it actually is, read properly, is a map of a family's entire financial system drawn by the family itself.

It shows the income. It shows the structure. It shows how different pieces of the financial picture are functioning. It often shows how much they own of a particular asset, because in order to prepare a return you have to get a K-1 that says what percentage of the company they actually own.

Read that last one again. A client tells you he owns a piece of a partnership. The K-1 tells you exactly how much. He may not remember. The form does.

You can look at a tax return and begin to see where all their income is coming from, whether it is earned income, passive income, business-related income, investment income. These are things that are all structured in their estate plan somewhere, and you are just learning how it all relates together.

And once you start to understand that, you are not just looking at numbers. You are starting to see how the whole system works together, because all the assets flow into the income tax return.

But most people do not look at it that way. They file them, and they move on, and they assume someone else is handling it.

Now, the practical instruction, which is smaller than it sounds.

Ask for two years. Not one. One year is a photograph and two years is a direction, and direction is what tells you whether something is growing, ending, or new.

Then read it for what is there rather than for what is wrong. You are not auditing. You are inventorying. Every schedule is a category of asset announcing itself: dividends and interest on one, capital gains on another, rents on a third, distributions from entities on a fourth.

Then read it for what is absent. No gift tax return filed in a decade means the annual exclusion has been sitting unused. Penalties year after year mean nobody projected the liability. A charitable deduction that does not match what you know the family gave means somebody was not told something.

Jesus is not talking about tax returns and I will not pretend otherwise. He is making a statement about the eventual visibility of everything, said to disciples about hypocrisy, and the principle underneath it is that concealment is temporary by nature.

What that has to do with your Tuesday is this. The information you need about a family is not hidden from you. It is sitting in a document they already have, that they have already signed, that they will hand you if you ask.

Ask for two years from one client this week.

Then actually read them.

# Day 22
## Shackled Because of Their Debts
### Not worth what they think they're worth

**"The rich rule over the poor, and the borrower is slave to the lender."**
Proverbs 22:7

Debt is really important, too. A lot of people are shackled because of their debts. They owe a lot of money to someone, to the bank or whatever, and until they get that debt brought down, they really are not worth what they think they are worth.

Shackled is a strong word and I use it deliberately, because the families I am describing do not look shackled. They look successful. That is the problem.

Ron Blue told me one time, if someone looks like they can give you a million dollars, they probably can't. He has a Rolex watch, he is driving a real nice car, he lives in a mansion. He probably cannot afford it. He is probably deeply in debt.

I love that line because it inverts the thing everybody's eye does automatically. Visible consumption reads as capacity. It is at least as likely to be evidence of the opposite, and there is no way to tell from the outside which one you are looking at.

So when we get into liabilities, we want to be real clear on who they owe, how much they owe, what the interest rates are, when it needs to be paid off, and whether it is a balloon payment or something they are going to pay over the next thirty years.

Four things, and most advisors capture two. Balance and rate get recorded. Term and structure do not, and those are the two that determine what actually happens.

A balloon is not a balance. It is a date. And a date that arrives during a soft market or a slow year in the business is a forced decision, which is the worst kind of financial event because every party to it can see that you have no choice.

Now the part that reaches past the arithmetic.

The reason I care about debt in a legacy conversation is not that it reduces net worth, though it does. It is that debt constrains generosity, and it constrains it invisibly. A family with a stated commitment to giving and a heavy debt load has a plan that cannot execute, and nobody has told them the two things are connected.

You can watch this happen over years. The intention stays constant, the capacity shrinks, and the family experiences it as a series of individual decisions to give less this year, each of which feels reasonable.

Solomon's language is not moral. He does not say the borrower is wicked. He says the borrower is a servant, which is a statement about who gets to decide. A man carrying significant debt has handed a portion of his future decision-making to somebody else, and the portion grows quietly.

That is what shackled means. Not poverty. Reduced authorship.

There is one more thing here and I will say it though it makes people uncomfortable. When I counsel people who are in debt, the first thing I tell them is that they need to begin to give. Usually they are not giving if they are in debt. And they say, I cannot afford to give.

I say, you cannot afford not to give.

I am not offering that as a technique and I am not promising anyone a return. I am telling you what I have watched happen in people's lives when they stopped waiting to be solvent before they were generous.

Get the four items on every debt for one client this week.

Look hardest at the two nobody records.

# Day 23
## It's Worth 600,000
### It says it's worth a million dollars

**"Suppose one of you wants to build a tower. Won't you first sit down and estimate the cost to see if you have enough money to complete it?"**
Luke 14:28

A lot of people will have assets on their balance sheet, but they do not realize that if they sell those assets, forty percent of that could go to the government.

So it says it is worth a million dollars. If they sell it, it is not worth a million dollars. It is worth six hundred thousand.

*[Rate figure. Tom uses forty percent on tape. Rates are indexed and vary by asset and circumstance. Use current figures with an as-of date, or present illustratively.]*

That is arithmetic and it takes four seconds, and I have watched it reorganize a family's entire plan.

The balance sheet is not wrong. It is incomplete. It reports what is owned without reporting what is attached to it, and a family that has been reading that page for years has been building expectations on a number that will not survive contact with a transaction.

If you do not look at what is attached to the assets, the debts, the tax exposure, the future obligations, you are only seeing part of the picture. And when decisions are made based on that partial view, it creates problems later.

Here is where the problems show up, and it is predictable.

Taxes become most significant during transitions. Everything can look strong while the assets are growing. When something changes, when assets are sold or transferred or passed on, that is when the full impact shows up. And if that has not been thought through ahead of time, it can change the outcome significantly for a family.

The word to hold is transitions. Growth conceals this problem perfectly, because a growing asset generates no taxable event and therefore no correction to the number in anyone's head. The family can be wrong by forty percent for fifteen years and receive no signal at all.

Then a sale happens, or a death, and the number corrects all at once, usually at the least convenient moment available.

Which is why I want this run in advance and in writing, on the three largest holdings, before anybody needs it. Not because the tax can be avoided in most cases. Because a family that knows the real number makes different decisions, and a family that finds out at closing makes no decisions at all.

Once you understand what is attached to what you have built, you start planning differently. You think ahead, you make adjustments earlier, and you avoid having to react later under pressure.

There is a particular version of this that catches generous families, and it is worth naming. A man decides to make a significant gift, sized against the balance sheet number. He sells the position to fund it. Then the tax arrives and the gift he can actually make is substantially smaller than the one he announced, and now he is renegotiating a commitment with somebody he wanted to bless.

That is entirely preventable and it is prevented by one calculation done in the right week.

Jesus asks about a man building a tower and the question is not whether he wants the tower or whether the tower is a good idea. The question is whether he sat down first and estimated. The failure being described is not ambition. It is arithmetic performed too late, in public, with a half-built structure behind you.

Run the after-tax number on three holdings this week.

Bring all three, so it reads as a category the client has been missing rather than one unlucky position.

# Day 24
## The Worst Asset to Give to Charity Is Cash
### You've got highly appreciated stocks

**"Each of you should give what you have decided in your heart to give, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver."**
2 Corinthians 9:7

So a guy who is charitable is giving away cash to charity.

I say, the worst asset to give to charity is cash.

You have got highly appreciated stocks. You ought to be giving those stocks to charity, then taking your cash and going back and buying the same position. But now you have a step up in what is called basis, and so therefore you have eliminated a lot of capital gains tax you would have had to pay if you were to sell those stocks in the future.

That is one movement and it takes about ninety seconds to explain, and most generous people in this country have never heard it.

Walk through what happens. The charity receives the same value it was going to receive. The client holds the identical position, same share count, purchased back with the cash he was going to donate. What has changed is his basis, which now reflects today's price rather than what he paid years ago, which means the embedded capital gains tax he was carrying into the future is gone.

Nobody gave up anything. A tax that was going to be paid simply is not paid.

There are a lot of critical things that people can do to improve their situation with proper planning.

Now, the reason this matters more than the mechanics suggest.

A lot of families believe cash is the only thing you can give. Their advisors have not raised it, their accountants have not mentioned it, and so the check gets written every year while the appreciated position sits in the account right next to it doing nothing.

Appreciated stock is the entry point rather than the ceiling. Business interests can be given. A portion of a real estate holding can be given. Retirement assets are the best assets for end of life giving because of how they are taxed in the hands of an heir, and naming a charity as a secondary beneficiary on those accounts costs the family nothing today.

Every one of those is available to families who are already giving and giving in the least efficient way available to them.

The timing constraint is the whole thing. This has to happen before the check is written. Once he sells the position and gives cash, the tax has been paid and no advice recovers it. So the question is not whether you know this. It is whether you get in front of the date.

Find out when your generous clients typically give. Most of them give in December, in a hurry, at the end of a year, which means the conversation belongs in October.

Paul writes about giving what a man has decided in his heart, and the emphasis falls on decided. Not extracted, not pressured, not calculated by somebody else. The decision belongs to the giver and it happens before the gift.

Which is why the efficiency question is not a spiritual downgrade. Nobody is deciding for him. He has already decided. All that remains is whether the amount he decided on arrives whole or arrives after a tax that did not have to be paid.

Do not let a generous man be inefficient because nobody told him.

## He Missed the $800,000 Gift to Charity
### He wasn't given that information

**"Give to everyone what you owe them: If you owe taxes, pay taxes; if revenue, then revenue; if respect, then respect; if honor, then honor."**
Romans 13:7

I had a client recently, and his CPA did his tax return, and then they sent me a copy of the tax return.

I said, oh, he missed the eight hundred thousand dollar gift to charity that you made.

That was worth two hundred fifty thousand dollars to the client, from finding that miss.

*[Value figure is Tom's on tape. Confirm the underlying assumption before print, since the benefit depends on rates and circumstances.]*

And it is not because the CPA did not know what he was doing. It is just that he was not given that information. He prepared a return based on the information he had, which did not include the information about the eight hundred thousand dollar gift.

I want to sit on that, because the instinct in the room is to blame somebody and there is nobody to blame.

The CPA did competent work with what he was handed. The client sent what he happened to have, because he did not know what he should have kept and what he should not have. And the return that came out was internally consistent and looked complete, which is exactly why nobody caught it.

That is the failure mode in this entire section of the review. Not error. Omission that produces a plausible result.

Many of the income tax preparers do not understand what the family owns either. All they do is come to you and say, send me all your information. You do not know what to send. You do not know what things you should have kept and did not keep. You send them whatever you have, and they take that like a puzzle and they put it together and it shows a face.

But a lot of times they miss things.

It shows a face. That is the whole diagnosis. A puzzle assembled from incomplete pieces still produces a picture, and the picture does not announce which pieces are missing.

So somebody has to be reading these documents who knows the family rather than only the paperwork. That is the seat, and it is the same seat this module has been describing for five weeks. Not a better accountant. Somebody with the whole picture, reading a document prepared by somebody with a partial one.

Here is the follow-on that shows what happens when a client learns this.

The same man called me close to April fifteenth and asked whether we could be clear about how much money he had given away that year, so we could do his tax projections based on what his real income would be.

That is the shift. He stopped treating the return as paperwork and started treating it as information, and now he is generating the data rather than waiting to see what shows up.

Paul's instruction is to give everyone what you owe, and taxes are named first, without complaint or qualification. The obligation is real and the language is plain about it.

What the verse does not say is that anyone owes more than is owed. Paying what is due is obedience. Paying what is not due because nobody read carefully is not obedience. It is an accident with a moral costume on it.

Read one client's return alongside what you know about the year.

Look for the thing that is not there.
