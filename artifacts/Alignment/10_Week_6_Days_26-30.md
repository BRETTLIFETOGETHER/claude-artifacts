## There's No Such Thing as an Independent Financial Decision
### There's only five things you can do with your money

**"The ground of a certain rich man yielded an abundant harvest. He thought to himself, what shall I do? I will tear down my barns and build bigger ones. But God said to him, you fool. This is how it will be with whoever stores up things for themselves but is not rich toward God."**
Luke 12:16-21

One of the things we say when we talk to people about cash flow is that there is no such thing as an independent financial decision.

When you decide to go and buy a Starbucks for five bucks, you could have done something else. You could have given that five bucks to a poor person.

So it is not a matter of, well, can I afford it. It is a matter of what could you have done with it had you not spent it doing that.

I use five dollars because the number is too small to argue about. Nobody is going to defend a coffee. Once the principle is granted at five dollars it is granted at five hundred thousand, and five hundred thousand is where the conversation actually needs to go.

In the cash flow area we talk about there being only five things you can do with your money. You can use it for your lifestyle. You can give it away. You can pay taxes with it. You can pay off debts, which would include a mortgage. And you can save it for the future.

There are only five things you can do with your money.

That is a closed set, and the closure is what makes the principle bite. Every dollar goes into exactly one of those five. There is no sixth category and there is no dollar sitting outside the system. So a dollar added to one is a dollar removed from another, always, whether or not anyone notices.

Which means a family that says giving matters to them and directs almost everything to lifestyle has not failed to give. They have given to lifestyle. The dollars went somewhere and the somewhere is visible.

Now, the parable, because Jesus tells this one in a way people consistently mishear.

There is one thing it says about that guy. It says that man was going to store it up for himself and was not rich toward God. That is why he got nailed, because he was not rich toward God.

So there is no problem with creating bigger barns if you have got a reason for why you are creating those bigger barns. If you are giving that extra money to the work of God, go make as much money as you can.

Read the parable again and you will find no condemnation of the harvest, the barns, the building, or the wealth. The man is not criticized for having a good year. The indictment is contained entirely in two words, for himself, and in the phrase not rich toward God.

That matters enormously for the people you serve, because most of the successful Christian business owners I know have quietly assumed there is something faintly disreputable about their own accumulation, and they have never been told what the actual line is.

The line is not the size of the barn. It is whether there is a reason for it.

A man building a larger barn in order to give more is doing something entirely different from a man building a larger barn because that is what one does with a good harvest, and from the outside the two barns look identical.

The rich fool's failure is not greed in the way we usually picture greed. It is the absence of a question. He asks himself what shall I do, answers himself, and never asks anyone else, and that is the whole tragedy in about four verses.

Ask a client this week what a decision cost him in the other four categories.

He has almost certainly never been asked.

# Day 27
## Are They Living Within Their Means?
### The first question I usually ask the next generation

**"Dishonest money dwindles away, but whoever gathers money little by little makes it grow."**
Proverbs 13:11

That is the first question I usually ask the next generation. I ask them if they are living within their means.

They are not getting any money necessarily from mom or dad right now. So do they have a budget, or are they living within their means?

And in many cases, if they are living within their means, then another question I ask them is, how is your giving? Are you giving to God's work right now?

Two questions. I have asked them hundreds of times and I have never needed a third.

The reason I ask them before any money has moved is that the answer is real at that stage. A young man running his own household on his own income is producing evidence. The same man ten years into receiving distributions is producing something else, and it is much harder to read.

Because that also will help mom and dad to know that if they are deeply in debt, because they are spending more than they are bringing in every year, that is going to let mom and dad know that they need to be careful in terms of how they are going to be able to help these people.

Careful is doing a lot of work in that sentence, and it does not mean give less. It means the form of the help has to change. Money handed to a household that already runs a deficit does not fix the deficit. It funds it for a while and then the deficit resumes at a higher level.

They may not have built the family's business, but they are receiving the benefit in a form that can be dangerous in its full expression.

Here is a story that shows what this looks like when nobody asked.

One of my attorney friends said to me, my client passed away, his wife was left, and she needed a new car. He was her executor. He said, I went out to buy her a car, and she was used to driving a Lexus, so I went to buy her another Lexus.

And her son came to me and said, mom does not need a Lexus. She can get along with a Ford.

My friend said, your mother is used to driving a Lexus. We are buying her a Lexus.

Because this kid is thinking, hey, whatever is left over when mom is gone is mine. So do not go buy her a Lexus, go buy her a Ford.

Look at what that son was actually doing. He was not being frugal on his mother's behalf. He was doing arithmetic, and every dollar spent on her was a dollar he would not receive, and the first place he chose to economize was his widowed mother's daily life.

Nothing in that estate plan was defective. The documents worked. What the plan never accounted for was a son who would look at his mother and see a diminishing balance, and nobody found that out in time because nobody ever sat down with him and asked him anything.

Solomon contrasts money that dwindles with money gathered little by little, and the second one describes a process rather than an event. Little by little is not a strategy. It is a character, formed over time in ordinary amounts, and it either exists in a person before the large sum arrives or it does not.

That is exactly why I ask a thirty-two-year-old about his own budget. I am not auditing him. I am looking for whether the gathering is happening at the scale he currently operates at, because scale does not change what a person is.

Go meet somebody's children this week.

You cannot prepare a person you have never met.

# Day 28
## You Need to Not Judge People
### The thing you have to be careful about is judgment

**"Don't I have the right to do what I want with my own money? Or are you envious because I am generous?"**
Matthew 20:15

I asked my first client how much he spends a year. He said, oh, we spend about a million dollars a year.

And he did not blink. He did not ask sheepishly. He just said it.

I did not blink either. I said, oh, that is good. I just wanted to have an idea of what that number was.

The thing you have to be careful about when you work with families is judgment. You need to not judge people.

So if a guy flies around in a private plane, you need to not judge him. You could say, well, you could take that money and you could give it to the kingdom of God. And everybody is thinking that. Well, you could. But he does not. That is just not what he has chosen. And he is able to do things that you are not able to do, because he has a private plane.

I like private planes. I fly around sometimes with my clients. It beats Delta.

I put that in because I am not describing a discipline of restraint. I am not gritting my teeth and withholding an opinion. I am telling you that the opinion is not mine to have, and that is a different thing, and clients can tell the difference from across a room.

You need to understand that you are responsible for the stewardship of your assets. God is not going to ask you what did your clients do with their money. He is going to ask you what did you do with what I gave to you.

And so when you get that perspective, then you are not judgmental on anyone.

Now the practical consequence, which is the reason this belongs in a session about cash flow rather than in some chapter on character.

A client who detects assessment stops telling you the truth about numbers. Not immediately and not obviously. He starts rounding down. He mentions the smaller categories and omits the larger ones. Within two years you are advising a family based on figures they curated for you, and you will not know it, because curated figures look exactly like real ones.

You cannot do this work on curated figures.

I remember sitting with a doctor and he said, oh, we spend about a hundred thousand a year. We were meeting in April, and I looked at what he had already spent, and he had already spent sixty.

I said, so you are going to stop eating when it gets to be September, is that true?

He said, no, that is not really true. I guess maybe we are spending more than what we said we were.

People are sheepish when you see how much they are spending. They are not feeling good about it. But you can make a change.

Notice what I did not do in that room. I did not present an analysis and I did not correct him. I asked one question with a month in it and let him do the arithmetic himself, and then I let the moment pass without comment, because the objective was for him to know his number and not for him to feel appropriately bad about it.

I do the same thing when a client misses a target he set. He says, well, I missed that one, did not I. And I say that is okay, I just want you to know.

No shame at all.

The landowner in the parable pays a full wage to men who worked one hour, and when the others complain he does not defend the fairness of it. He asks whether he is not permitted to do what he wants with what is his, and then he asks a much sharper question about why their eye is on his generosity in the first place.

Both questions are for the workers. Neither is for the man who was paid.

Somebody is going to sit across from you this year and tell you a number you have an opinion about.

Do not blink.

# Day 29
## What Did You Do With What I Gave to You?
### God is not going to ask you

**"After a long time the master of those servants returned and settled accounts with them. Well done, good and faithful servant. You have been faithful with a few things; I will put you in charge of many things."**
Matthew 25:19-21

God is not going to ask you, what did your clients do with their money. He is going to ask you, what did you do with what I gave to you.

I said that yesterday about judgment and I am saying it again today for a different reason, because it does two jobs and the second one is larger.

The first job is that it frees you from having opinions about other people's spending. The second is that it puts a bill in your own name.

Ron Blue says that you love your children equally, therefore you treat them uniquely. Which is very biblical, because if you look at the parable of the talents, he gave five to one, two to another, one to the third.

God does not give equally to people. But God is the one who makes that kind of decision, on your behalf.

Because quite honestly, if you really believe that God owns everything, then what you have is what God has allowed you to have. It is either a lot or it is not a lot.

That is the piece that ends comparison, and comparison is the occupational disease of this profession. You will spend your career standing next to balance sheets larger than yours, and there is no arithmetic available that makes that comfortable. The only thing that does is the recognition that the distribution was not an accident and was not yours to arrange.

Now, the accounting itself, because the parable is specific about it.

The master returns and settles accounts. He does not compare the servants to each other. He does not ask the two-talent servant why he was not a five-talent servant. Each man is examined against what he was actually given, and the man with five and the man with two receive word for word the same commendation.

The only servant who fails is the one who did nothing, and his defense is instructive. He was not reckless and he did not steal. He preserved the asset perfectly and returned it intact, and that turns out to be the failure rather than the mitigation.

There is a version of that in this business that I see constantly. An advisor who does competent, careful, unremarkable work for thirty years, harms nobody, and never once asks a family the question that would have changed something. Nothing was lost. Nothing was risked. The talent went back in the ground.

I have another advisor friend who said, my job is to make sure that my clients do not run out of money before they die.

I said, well, I can understand that. That would be success at one level. But that means if you are doing that, their kids are not going to end up with a whole lot.

That is a whole career defined as preservation. It is not dishonorable and it is not enough.

Here is what I want you to do with the settling of accounts, since it is coming for all of us and it is worth thinking about in advance rather than at the time.

You will not be asked about the size of your book. You will not be asked how you compared to the man down the hall. You will be asked what you did with what you had, and the answer will consist mostly of specific conversations you either had or did not have with specific families.

Some of those conversations are available to you this week.

# Day 30
## Where Your Treasure Is
### What am I doing in my life that's taking faith?

**"For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also."**
Matthew 6:21

When it comes to cash flow, there is a principle in Matthew 6:21. It says, where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.

So people sometimes say to me, I have a real heart for God.

I ask them, well, tell me about your giving.

Well, I do not tithe.

I say, well, then you are deceived, because the Bible says that where your treasure is, there will your heart be also. If your money is not in God's kingdom, you are deceived to believe that your heart is in God's kingdom. You may think it is in God's kingdom, but you are deceived.

That is as hard as I say anything, and I have said it to men I like.

I say it because the verse establishes a direction that most people have backwards. The common reading is that a good heart will eventually produce generosity, which lets a man wait indefinitely for a feeling to arrive. Jesus says the opposite. The treasure goes first and the heart follows it. Which means your spending pattern is not a report on your heart. It is a cause of it.

Over time, your spending pattern tells a story about your money, and not what you intended to do, but what you actually did with what you were given.

What you begin to look at over time are the patterns that start to show you where people are in their view of money. And the Bible says the only thing that competes with the love of God is mammon. It is not anything else. It is only that.

Now, where this leaves you at the end of thirty days, because a devotional ought to end with something to do rather than something to feel.

Here is where you coach or encourage or stimulate or challenge your clients to do more than they could possibly do. Because they have the capacity to do that. And to me that is so much fun, to be able to help a guy realize that he can give away a whole lot more money than he is giving right now, and he is never going to miss a meal. He is going to be in great shape.

Never going to miss a meal. That is the actual size of the risk in most of the conversations you are avoiding.

I sat down with someone once who had decided to give a generous amount, and I said I thought he could do better, that I thought he could give more. A pastor who was in the room used the same line later with a different donor, and told him God is bigger than that, so keep that number or come back with a bigger one. The man came back with twice as much.

That is not a technique to get people to give. What I asked was what a sacrificial amount would be for him, and whether, if he was going to give to this ministry, it might be a sacrificial amount.

People need to be encouraged. We all want to be challenged in our faith.

Think of Hebrews 11:6. It says, without faith it is impossible to please God. That is a very condemning verse, because you have to ask yourself, what am I doing in my life that is taking faith?

And if I cannot think of anything, that is not a good thing.

I want to please God, so I have got to be doing something that is taking some faith.

Thirty days ago you started with a page listing what a family owns and owes.

Answer the question about faith before you close this book.
