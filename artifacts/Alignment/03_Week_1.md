# Day 1
## Families Don't Know What They Have
### They don't know who owns it

*Watch the Day 1 video before you read.*

**"Be sure you know the condition of your flocks, give careful attention to your herds."**
Proverbs 27:23

A lot of times, families don't know what they have. They don't know what its value is. They don't know who owns it.

I have been saying that sentence for close to thirty years and it still surprises people, because the families I am describing are not disorganized. Most of them have access to a financial statement. They may have created one. They receive reports, they have accounts, they have advisors. What they do not have is one page that says what they own, what they owe, and whose name it sits in.

The third one is the one nobody expects. It really makes a big difference if it is owned by the husband, if it is owned by the wife, if it is owned jointly. All those things are really important. Is the business owned jointly? Is it owned by the husband? Is it owned by the wife? What about the other assets that they have?

Who owns what.

That matters because the way those assets are going to move really depends upon who owns it. A family can be completely certain about what they intend and completely wrong about what will happen, and the reason is that intention and titling are two different things. Only one of them is legally operative, and it is not the one they have been thinking about.

So what I am after first is not complicated. What do they have, and how much is it worth. That is the whole first pass.

Now here is the distinction that separates this work from account review, and it is the reason a family with excellent advisors can still be lost.

The goal is to move from having a bunch of information to actually understanding all that information. Information alone doesn't create clarity. You have to have a lot of information, and you need to understand your situation.

Those are two separate jobs. Almost everyone around a wealthy family's table has done the first one and almost nobody has done the second. The statements arrive, and they are accurate, and nobody has done anything wrong. Then you ask the wife to explain the family's position and she cannot do it. You ask the husband what happens to the business if he dies on Thursday and he cannot do that either.

They are not uninformed. They are uncomprehending, which is a different condition with a different cure.

And if you don't understand what you've been entrusted with, it's pretty difficult to steward it the way that you want to steward it.

That sentence is the reason I do this rather than something easier. Stewardship is not a posture you adopt. It is a set of decisions made about specific assets, and a decision cannot be made well about something you cannot see. A man who does not know what he owns is not stewarding badly. He is not stewarding at all, because the thing he would be stewarding has never been assembled in front of him.

Solomon is writing to men whose wealth walked around on four legs and could be counted in an afternoon, and even then he does not say be sure you own flocks. He says know their condition. Give careful attention. The instruction is not about accumulation, it is about attention, and it assumes that a man can own a great deal and pay attention to none of it.

Your clients own things that do not walk into a pen at night. They own interests inside entities they have not looked at in nine years. Knowing the condition of that takes deliberate work, and deliberate work is exactly what nobody in their life has been paid to do.

You are going to be the one who does it.

Not because it is sophisticated. It is the least sophisticated thing in this entire module. You are going to ask a family for a list of what they own and what they owe, and you are going to put a column next to it that says who owns each line, and a large share of the value you deliver in the first ninety days will come out of that one column.

Start with what they have. You cannot align anything to a picture nobody has seen.

# Day 2
## Here Are the Huge Gaps
### Here's where you said you wanted to be

*Watch the Day 2 video before you read.*

**"Plans fail for lack of counsel, but with many advisers they succeed."**
Proverbs 15:22

Once you understand where they are going, you have looked at their situation, and you see their gaps. What they said they wanted is not going to happen with this existing plan.

That is when we get into their goals and gaps analysis. We give them a report that explains to them, here is where you said you wanted to be, here is where you currently are, here are the huge gaps. And then we help them to fix those gaps.

That report is the product. Everything in the first four letters of the review exists to make it possible, and if you never produce it you have done an inventory rather than a piece of coaching.

Notice the structure of the three lines, because the order is doing the work. You start with what the family said, in their own words, from the goals conversation. Then you put current reality next to it. The gap is not your opinion about their plan. It is the distance between two things they gave you, and that is why it does not land as criticism.

I have never had a family argue with a gap presented that way. They argue with recommendations. They do not argue with the space between what they told me they wanted and what their documents actually do.

Now, the harder question, which is the one I get from advisors more than any other.

What if you are working with a family and you do not come from a deep financial background? How do you handle this?

One of the important things if you are doing legacy coaching is a collaboration mindset. You are going to collaborate with other people that are better than you in areas that you don't have the expertise that you would like to have.

So that's where you just create a team, quite honestly, to help the family to get where they want to go. Some of those people are already on their team, because they are advisors that they're already using. But sometimes you have to go out and find more expertise than what they currently have.

I want to be concrete about that, because collaboration is a word people nod at without changing anything.

I am a CPA. I do not hold an insurance license. Not one. I am not the expert on business succession and governance. I have somebody for each of those, and when a client needs one of them I call, and the family gets a better outcome than they would have gotten from me pretending.

What I need to know is where my knowledge ends and who I call when I reach the edge. That is a much smaller thing to know than everything, and it is achievable this quarter, which everything is not.

The two halves of this reading are the same idea seen from opposite ends. The gaps report requires you to see across a family's whole situation. Seeing across a whole situation requires more expertise than any one person has. So the report is not the output of a brilliant individual. It is the output of somebody willing to assemble people.

Solomon does not say plans fail for lack of intelligence. He says lack of counsel, which is a different deficiency with a different cure. Intelligence you either have or you do not. Counsel you go and get.

The man who will not go and get it is usually not lazy. He is protecting something, and what he is protecting is the impression that he already knew.

Here is what that costs him. He can only produce a gaps report about the part of the picture he personally understands, which means the family receives a partial analysis presented as a complete one. That is worse than no analysis, because now they believe they have been looked at.

Find out this week what you have been covering for.

# Day 3
## That Would Be Horrible
### Because that's what your plan says right now

*Watch the Day 3 video before you read.*

**"An inheritance claimed too soon will not be blessed at the end."**
Proverbs 20:21

You might have a situation where everything is structured well. Accounts are set up, plans are in place, documents are done. But there is a next generation issue that hasn't been addressed.

What are you going to do to prepare your kid to be able to handle the ten million dollars that you are going to give him? Because that's what your plan says right now.

That last clause is the one that does the work, and it is worth noticing why. Not the amount. The fact that the plan already says it.

The parent has been carrying a vague intention to sort this out someday. The document has been carrying a specific instruction the whole time. And the document does not know that someday has not arrived.

I said this to a man once. I said, do you realize your kids, if you died today, your son is going to get a check for two and a half million dollars?

He said no.

I said yes.

He said, that would be horrible.

He was not objecting to the amount. He was picturing his son. He knew exactly what would happen, which was three or four cars, and he knew it in about a second and a half, because he had known it for years without ever having been asked the question in a form that required an answer.

But the point is, people don't understand. They don't understand. They haven't thought through this.

There is nothing wrong with that man's documents. I want to be clear about that, because the instinct in the room is to reach for the estate plan, and the estate plan is working. It will deliver a check on schedule to a named person, which is what it was built to do.

The gap is not in the instrument. The gap is in the person receiving it.

I separate these into two kinds and they need to be held apart, because they get treated as one thing and they require completely different work. A structural gap is something missing or wrongly placed, a policy inside the estate that should be outside it, a beneficiary designation pointing somewhere nobody intended. That gets fixed with documents. Then there is the other kind, where the structure is fine and the preparation is missing, and no amount of legal drafting touches it.

And so we want to help you to prepare him or her so that they will be able to receive what your plan is planning to give them.

Notice that preparation is not a distribution schedule. Age thirty, age thirty-five, age forty is not preparation. It is a delay mechanism, and a delay mechanism assumes that a person who is not ready at thirty will have become ready at forty through the passage of time. Time does not do that. People do that, or nobody does.

So when a parent tells me the trust handles it, I ask what the trust is handling. It is handling the timing. It is not handling the child.

Solomon is describing a transfer that happens correctly and turns out badly. Nothing is stolen and nobody is defrauded. The inheritance simply arrives before the person is ready to hold it, and the arriving is what does the damage.

That is not an argument for withholding. I have clients who have given their children everything they intend to give them, on purpose, early, because the children were prepared and the money could do more at thirty than at sixty. The variable is never the amount. It is whether anybody did the work.

What we're trying to do is help them to get that clarity so that they can make adjustments now, before it's too late.

Before it is too late is the whole opportunity. The man in my story said no, then he said that would be horrible, and both of those happened while he was alive and healthy in a conference room. He has time.

Most families do this arithmetic in a lawyer's office after a funeral, and by then the check is already written.

# Day 4
## Money Changes People
### They don't become better, they become worse

*Watch the Day 4 video before you read.*

**"No one can serve two masters. Either you will hate the one and love the other, or you will be devoted to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve both God and money."**
Luke 16:13

A lot of times you can deal with the relational issues in a family and forget about the fact that there's money associated with these.

And money changes people, and it doesn't change them for the good in most cases. When there's a bunch of money involved, people become different. And they don't become better, they become worse.

I say that flatly because I have watched it happen enough times to stop hedging it. I am not describing criminals. I am describing decent families with grown children who loved each other perfectly well until there was a number on the table, and then something moved that nobody in that family would have predicted about themselves.

So what you want to do is you don't just want to know where is the family relationally, how are they doing over on that side. You also want to know what kind of stuff do they have, and how's that stuff going to affect the relationships going forward.

Hold that last phrase. How the stuff is going to affect the relationships going forward. Not how the relationships will affect the plan. The other direction. The assets are going to act on these people, the acting has not started yet, and you can see the shape of it before they can.

This is why I want the whole picture and not the part I am paid on. It is not thoroughness for its own sake. A number I have not seen is a number I cannot warn anybody about.

Now here is the practical use of it, and it is the reason your financial background matters more than you think in what looks like a relational job.

You become somebody for them to partner with. You have a financial background, so you can show them, this is where you are today. So if you don't make some changes, this is what's going to happen with your situation. And you're probably not going to like it.

That sentence is available to you and it is not available to almost anybody else in their life. Their attorney will not say it, because he was asked a legal question. Their accountant will not say it, because he was handed a stack of documents and asked to produce a return. You are the only one positioned to describe a trajectory rather than a position, and a trajectory is the only thing a family can still change.

What we're trying to do is, now that we have some clarity, we're going to take your situation and we're going to work around with it so that it aligns with what you said you wanted to do. That way you'll be happier, you'll have much more peace of mind, because you'll feel like I really am where I want to be. And then you'll be able to communicate that to the next generation.

Jesus does not say you should not serve both. He says you cannot. That is a statement about capacity rather than permission, and it is the reason the change happens so quietly. Nobody sits down and elects to serve money. The devotion moves first and the person catches up to it later, if ever.

Which means the families you serve are not choosing between two masters in any conscious way. They are drifting toward one, the drift accelerates when the numbers get larger, and there is generally nobody in the room whose job includes noticing.

That job is available. Almost nobody has taken it.

I want to be careful here, because this cuts toward you as much as toward them. An advisor is not immune to the thing he is watching for in a client. You are handling other people's money all day and forming opinions about it, and the same quiet reordering that happens to a family with a large number in front of them can happen to a man who spends thirty years standing next to large numbers.

Watch for it in them. Watch harder for it in you.

# Day 5
## A Lot of Times It's the Wife
### She has no idea what it's worth

*Watch the Day 5 video before you read.*

**"How good and pleasant it is when God's people live together in unity."**
Psalm 133:1

What is their response when you actually pull this out and help them walk through it?

Oftentimes it's surprise. Because they don't know what the gaps are. And then they also don't know what they can do to fix those gaps. So usually we give them, here's two or three things you could do right away that would make your situation better.

Two or three. Not a plan, not an engagement, not a proposal. Two or three specific things, handed over in the same meeting where the problem became visible.

I do that deliberately and it is worth understanding why. A family that has just seen the full shape of their situation for the first time is carrying something heavy, and if you leave them holding it while you go write a proposal, the weight of it does the opposite of what you want. Give them something to do that afternoon and the discovery converts into momentum instead of dread.

Peace of mind increases. People feel more grounded because they understand their situation more clearly, and conversations become easier because now there's something shared that people can really talk about.

Now the part of this that has nothing to do with money.

A lot of times it's the wife, she doesn't have a clue. She knows he goes to work every day, and he has a business. She has no idea what it's worth. And she looks at it and she says, my goodness, I can't believe we have all this stuff.

Then she asks where she can go shopping.

I tell that lightly and I do not mean it lightly. That woman has been living inside a financial life she could not see the shape of. That is not a spreadsheet problem. That is a marriage carrying an imbalance nobody named, sometimes for thirty years, usually without either person intending it. He did not hide anything. She did not ask. The arrangement simply formed and then hardened.

So just that alone is like marital counseling. That is not a figure of speech. When the picture goes on the table, one person stops holding all the cards.

So instead of one person holding all the cards and having all the knowledge, the family can actually begin to engage together in everything from their investments, their financial future, their generosity and other kinds of opportunities they'd like to do for their children.

That's where alignment really starts to take shape.

Read that carefully, because it locates the beginning of this work somewhere unexpected. Alignment does not start when the documents get fixed. It starts when two people can see the same thing at the same time and talk about it. Everything technical that follows depends on that, and none of it substitutes for it.

The Psalm is short enough that people treat it as a sentiment, and it is not. It is an observation about a condition rare enough to be worth remarking on. Unity is called good and pleasant, which are modest words, and the modesty is the point. Nobody claims it is dramatic. They claim it is better than the alternative, and anyone who has sat with a family after a badly handled transfer knows exactly how much better.

People feel greater confidence and release in knowing that they're working at a level of freedom, financial freedom.

And this is where it becomes very real, because it's one thing to talk about clarity, it's another thing to see that happen.

You will do a great deal of technical work in this business and some of it will save families millions of dollars. You will also, a few times a year, put a piece of paper on a table and watch two people who have been married for decades look at their own life together for the first time.

Do not undervalue the second one because it does not appear on an invoice.
