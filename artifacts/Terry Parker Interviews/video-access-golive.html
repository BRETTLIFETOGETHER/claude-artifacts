Capture Standard · Applies To Every Interview

  • Ten questions plus one closing reflection. Written to draw out story, never yes or no.
  • Recorded and transcribed. The transcript is the primary source for every downstream deliverable.
  • Listen-for list in the facilitator notes. Stories, Scripture, convictions, family language, defining moments, wounds and turning points, lessons, hopes and fears, blessings, unfinished conversations.
  • Release signed before the camera runs. Scope, term, and edition rights, including derivative-of-derivative.
  • Never trim on time. Cut on thought. Three of the ten existing clips prove why.
  • One capture, two books. Same session serves the practitioner book and Russ's history. Neither of you asks anyone twice.

Pilot Design

Three families, not thirty. One in each of shelf one, shelf three, and shelf four, so the sequencing rule gets tested rather than assumed. Facilitated by us, not by an advisor, and observed.

Every session recorded with consent, and a structured debrief within forty-eight hours. The objective is not a testimonial. It is to find out which half of this is wrong, because half of it is.

Instrument Validation

Thirty to fifty completions before the assessment goes near an institution. Check that dimensions do not collapse into each other, that the bands distribute rather than clustering, and that the spread reading holds up against what a facilitator observes in the room.

The cousin consortium pattern is the known failure mode. A uniformly low, uniformly aligned grid reads as calm and is disengagement, and no software can tell the difference.

Platform Access

Five tiers. The family owns the record.

Access is not a feature question. It is the trust architecture, and if it is wrong the instrument dies in the first family that notices.

Tier One · Individual

Any Family Member

Own assessment and score. Own devotional and reading. Own private notes. The forty-day track. Can revoke any consent at any time without explanation.

Tier Two · Family

The Family Record

The spread grid, never individual answers. Decision log with owners and dates. Founding story, failure ledger, teach-one-thing library, interview archive, people who serve the family. Governed by the family, not the advisor.

Tier Three · Facilitator

Certified, Time-Boxed

Spread grid and session guides for the engagement window only. Access expires. Sealed envelope contents never touch the system in any tier.

Tier Four · Advisor

Invited by the Family

Sees what the family grants and nothing else. Default is the decision log and the calendar. No assessment answers, no session notes, no capture archive unless explicitly granted item by item.

Tier Five · Institution

Anonymized Only

Aggregate benchmarks across their population. No family is ever identifiable. This is the tier that makes the instrument valuable to a partner and the tier most likely to be abused, so it is built last and audited.

Admin

Us

Break-glass only, logged, and disclosed to the family. No routine access to any family record by anyone at LifeTogether.

Roles that do not exist yet and must

  • Facilitator bench. Shelf three requires certified facilitators and there are none. This is the binding constraint on going live, not the content.
  • Clinical referral partner. Addiction, active mental illness, and bereavement in the first year are not shelves. A named referral relationship is required before any family engagement.
  • Publishing rights counsel. One template agreement, credit line and split as the only variables.
  • Platform build. Nobody is building the access architecture described above.
  • Pilot family recruiter. Three families, and no one has been asked.

Relationship discipline

  • Historical relationship is not a current working relationship.
  • A verbal yes is a verbal yes, recorded as such, until it is on one page with a signature.
  • Board membership is not institutional authorization. Terry can convene and bless. Kendra VanderMeulen decides.
  • Enthusiasm is not a partnership, a pitch is not an agreement, and an outline is not a book.
  • Do something first. David's rule governs every approach on this page.

Gap Register

What is still missing.

Commercial

  • No revenue model. Dozens of products described and not one sentence about what anyone pays for what. Certification, institutional license, assessment platform, facilitation, or kits. Pick, because the answer changes the build order.
  • No pricing. Individual, family, advisor seat, firm, institution.
  • No cost of delivery. A facilitated overnight has a real cost and nobody has calculated it.
  • Capacity. Fifteen products, one Hannah, one Frank, one Tom's calendar. Nothing here fails because it is a bad idea.

Product

  • Chapter two is unwritten. Five of six drafted.
  • The assessment is unvalidated. It assigns bands and drives recommendations and nobody has taken it.
  • No triage layer for families in crisis. Shelf three in a household with active addiction is dangerous, not merely difficult.
  • No edition for families without wealth, which is most of the church.
  • No longitudinal measurement. No way to prove the handover actually happened at twelve or thirty-six months.
  • No async delivery. Every tool assumes a room, and scattered families are the ones who need this most.

Legal & Risk

  • Consent and data infrastructure does not exist. The permission matrix above is a design, not a system.
  • Professional liability. Facilitating a family conversation that goes badly has exposure nobody has priced.
  • Scope-of-practice line. Where facilitation ends and therapy begins, in writing, in the facilitator guide.
  • Trademark opinion on the forty-day construction.
  • Series mark and format ownership on Faith & Finances and Kingdom Economy, unresolved.
