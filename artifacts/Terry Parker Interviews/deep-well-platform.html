The Founding Claim

Terry Parker is not the founder of Christian donor-advised funds. He is the founder of the modern donor-advised fund.

Fidelity's counsel called him in 1992 and asked him to come to New York, because they had read his ruling and needed to understand how it worked. Schwab and Vanguard followed. Essentially nobody inside that industry knows the instrument was created by a tax attorney working out of a file drawer in Atlanta, because a community foundation told his client that Christians should take care of themselves.

Whoever writes the origin of a field owns a permanent, unpurchasable position in it. Not market share. Standing. Why this book exists

Movement One · How It Was Built

The origin nobody has recorded. The client who was refused, the research, the naming, the ruling, the desk drawer, and thirteen years of nobody calling back.

Movement Two · How It Works

The principles and the process. Pre-liquidity timing, non-cash assets, supporting organizations, and the discipline of asking permission before you need forgiveness.

Movement Three · What He Learned

Forty years of sitting with families. The message to advisors and to the next generation. The part only he can supply.

The Instrument

How a Tax Attorney Built the Donor-Advised Fund, and What Forty Years of Watching People Let Go Taught Him About Money and Families

The reason this serves everyone at once is that the instrument is neutral and the wisdom is not. A sponsoring organization can hand out the first two movements. NCF can hand out all three. Nobody has to be persuaded to care about the founding of the thing they use every day.

Edition Three · Family

The Deep Well

A Family Story About Everything They Tried to Keep

Allegory. Water, not wealth, so no reader can check the number against their own and decide the book is about somebody else.

Why the family edition is allegory and the advisor edition is not

An advisor recognizes his own office. A family cannot. The moment a family story names a number, every reader compares it to their own and finds a reason the book is not about them. A well is inherited, contested, and finite, and the only real question about one is who gets to draw from it. That is the family question with the money taken out.

The safety device

A daughter cannot say to her father, you didn't tell us because while we didn't know you were the only one who could decide. She can say, at a table, with the book between them, that Silas should have told them. And her father can agree with her, out loud, about Silas. The allegory is not a stylistic preference. It is the only mechanism that lets a family have this conversation without someone leaving the room.

He Knew Where the Water Was Outlined

A father teaching a stranger what he will not teach his son. Most families transfer assets and withhold the wisdom that produced them, then are astonished at the result. The reason is rarely selfishness. The knowing is the last thing the father has that the children still need.

Amos Carried the Water Drafted

Twenty-two years, and the narrator was seventeen before it occurred to him that Amos also needed water. The father digs him a well on the ridge and deeds it away. Amos does not say thank you. He says twenty-two years, and nobody asked. Magnificent and unilateral, out of the same root.

04

What He Never Said Drafted

The well is failing and he knows, and says nothing for two years, and those are the best two years of his life. The mother ends it: you decided I couldn't carry it, and you have been deciding that for thirty years and calling it love. No apology. He walks his son to the well before dawn and teaches him to read the rope.

Release · Timing  ·  Cost · The comfort of the present

Before the Dry Year Drafted

The margin was already spent, because the ridge belonged to Amos in writing. The daughter-in-law, eleven years in and never once asked a direct question, says the turn: you are not waiting for information, you are waiting for the decision to become easy, and it does not become easy, it only becomes late.

Dig Your Own Drafted

He will advise. He will not decide, will not fund it, and will not say where. Then you will have chosen wrong. And I will still be here. The narrator does nothing for six years. When he finally brings his father a cup at ninety, the old man drinks it and says: I would not have looked there.

The Family Reading

  • Reading One · The Spread. Highest subtotal minus lowest, per dimension. Nine or more is Divided, and it outranks any low score anywhere on the grid.
  • Reading Two · The Quiet One. Usually the person who married in, or the child who did not go into the business. Ask the family what they have seen that the rest have not.
  • Reading Three · The One Dimension. Choose one. Discernment questions answered aloud, youngest first. Then a date, on a calendar, before anyone leaves the room.
Name one decision that is currently mine, that could become someone else's within twelve months, that I could not reverse afterward. Then name the date. The Founder's Item · answered alone, read aloud to everyone

The Discernment Layer

Impact

If one of these six moved two points this year, which would change the most for the people I love? Name the dimension and name the person.

Urgency

Which cannot wait? Health, age, a pending transaction, a strained relationship, someone who will not be here in five years.

Invitation

Where has God already been at work on this, before I picked up the instrument? Where has the same thing kept surfacing?

Where the three converge, begin. Where they diverge, begin with urgency, because urgency is the only one with a deadline attached.

Twenty-Five Experiential Tools

The story is the delivery. The exercises are the product.

01 · The Rope

Knotted, passed around the table. Whoever holds it speaks, and cannot hand it back to whoever gave it. Watch what happens the first time the founder has to physically give it away.

02 · The Three Names

Three people without whom this family would not have what it has. Youngest reads first, founder reads last. The gaps between lists do the arguing.

03 · The Missing Witness

The oldest living person who knows something nobody has recorded. Leaves the room with a date and two names on it, or it did not happen.

04 · The Failure Ledger

Three failures with operational detail, not confession. The next generation knows things can work and has no idea what it looks like when they don't.

05 · Teach One Thing

One real judgment, one named person, one hour, recorded, with a five-minute teach-back. The highest-value hour in the box and the one that cannot be reconstructed later.

06 · The Invisible List

The people who serve this household and are not in it. If nobody can say what happened in their life this year, that is the finding.

07 · The Sealed Envelope

One thing you know that the others do not, and the real reason for the silence. Nobody opens anything. Whether to open them is the exercise. Consent, not exposure.

08 · What Are We Waiting For

Written as an event, not a feeling. If nobody can name an event, the family is waiting for the decision to become easy.

09 · The Handover

One decision, one name, one date, twelve months, not reversible. Everything else in a family meeting is conversation.

10 · Dig Your Own

The next generation names what they will build that is not the family enterprise. The founder responds with what he knows and not with what to do.

The Capture Protocol

A family meeting that changes something and records nothing has to start over next year.

Open Record

Lists, names, decisions, dates, ventures, the failure ledger, the teach-one-thing recording, the witness interview. Shared, permanent, indexed.

Restricted

Individual assessment responses, the founder's item, anything from a one-to-one. Visible to the person and, with written permission, the facilitator.

Never Recorded

Sealed envelope contents. Anything disclosed in distress. Anything a participant asks to be off the record, at any time, without explanation.

Private spiritual and relational disclosure is never treated as sales intelligence. An advisor who facilitates one of these meetings knows things no advisor should act on commercially, and the moment a family suspects otherwise the instrument is dead in that family and in every family they talk to. Next Generation & Multigenerational

Never run a rising generation program without a parallel founder track. The problem is almost never their competence. It is that they have no authority and no information, and no curriculum supplies either. Educate them without moving the founder and you produce capable adults with nothing to decide, which is worse than doing nothing, because now they know. The central design rule
  • Paired cohorts. Separate weekends, together the last morning. Founder deliverable: one transferred decision. Rising gen deliverable: one venture that is not the family enterprise.
  • The cousin consortium. Third generation onward, founder dead, cousins barely acquainted. Different problem: not release, but whether a shared thing is worth keeping. The tool is a vote nobody is punished for.
  • Cross-family peer cohorts. Six unrelated families. Heirs say things to peers they will never say to a parent or a paid advisor.
  • Rites of passage by age. Thirteen, eighteen, twenty-five, thirty. Most families disclose everything at once at the reading of a will, which is the worst possible design.
  • Grandparent direct. Skipping a generation removes the authority problem entirely, and both of them know why.

The Platform Thesis

Every organization in this field creates a moment. None of them own the follow-through.

Generous Giving creates the moment of conviction and hands the person back to their life. NCF creates the moment of structure and hands the family back to their life. Kingdom Advisors creates the moment of professional formation and hands the advisor back to his practice. The moment is the easy part and everybody has one.

You are not competing with any of them. You are the thing that happens next.

The Terms That Compound

Credit builds your name. These build the business.

Ask for credit as an assumption and ownership as a separate conversation. Raise both in one breath and if they trip on ownership you lose the credit too.

Copyright in the Derivative

Registered to LifeTogether, with source content licensed in. The single most important line and the one most often left unsaid.

Format & Series Mark

Stays yours across all titles. Otherwise every title starts from zero and you have manufactured for someone else again.

The Church Relationship

Whoever holds the list of two hundred churches that ran the campaign holds the business.

Term & Reversion

Five to seven years with renewal. Rights revert if a title goes inactive, or it sits on someone else's shelf forever.

Derivative-of-Derivative

Translations, editions, audience versions. What lets one master become family, marketplace, and Spanish without reopening the deal three times.

Approval Scope, Narrowed

Author approves theology and quotation accuracy. Not design, pricing, tiers, or marketing. Unbounded approval is how a campaign misses a fall launch.

One template agreement, drafted once by a publishing-rights attorney, with credit line and royalty split as the only variable fields. Not legal advice; get it papered by someone who does this. Do not use Terry — forty years of tax and nonprofit law is elite and the wrong specialty.
