# The Deep Well Instruments

### Personal Assessment, Family Assessment, and Ten Experiential Tools

---

## A note on what makes this different

Most family assessments average the household and report a score. This one does not, and the reason is the book.

Chapter three's exercise has each person write a list independently, and the finding is not any list. It is the gap between them. Chapter four turns on one person knowing something the others do not. The entire diagnosis in this material is **divergence**, not level.

So the Family Assessment scores the spread. Every member takes the same twenty-four items privately. A family where the founder scores a dimension 18 and his adult children score it 7 does not have a strong dimension with some noise in it. It has the single most important finding the instrument can produce, and an averaging instrument would hide it.

# Part One · The Deep Well Personal Assessment

## Purpose

This is a private inventory of what you are currently holding. It is not a test of generosity, financial competence, or spiritual maturity, and there is no result that means you have failed. It asks six questions about six kinds of grip, and its only job is to show you where your hand is closed without your having noticed.

Ten minutes. Answer as you actually are, not as you intend to be.

**Confidentiality.** Your responses belong to you. If this instrument is administered by an advisor, coach, or facilitator, your individual answers are never shared with anyone, including family members, without your written permission, and they are never used as sales intelligence of any kind. That commitment is part of the instrument, not a courtesy attached to it.

## Instructions

Rate each statement 1 to 5.

**1** Not true of me · **2** Rarely true · **3** Sometimes true · **4** Mostly true · **5** Consistently true

Do not discuss answers with anyone until everyone taking it has finished.

---

### Dimension One · Ownership

*What I hold and did not build*

1. When I tell the story of how we got here, I name people other than myself without being prompted. ___
2. I can identify at least three specific advantages I received that I did not earn. ___
3. I have said out loud, to my family, that this is not entirely mine. ___
4. When someone corrects my account of our history, I get curious rather than defensive. ___

**Subtotal ___ / 20**

### Dimension Two · Knowledge

*What I know and have not taught*

5. My children or successors know how I actually make decisions, not just what I decided. ___
6. I have told the people close to me about a significant failure of mine, in enough detail to be useful. ___
7. Someone other than me could explain our situation accurately to a stranger tomorrow. ___
8. When I am asked how I do something, I answer fully rather than saying they will understand later. ___

**Subtotal ___ / 20**

### Dimension Three · People

*Who serves me and goes unseen*

9. I can name the people who work for or serve this household, and something true about each of their lives this year. ___
10. In the last year I have met a specific need for someone who works for me, beyond paying them well. ___
11. When I help someone, I ask them what they need before I decide what to give. ___
12. The people who serve this family have somewhere to bring a grievance where it will actually be heard. ___

**Subtotal ___ / 20**

### Dimension Four · Truth

*What I know and have not said*

13. There is nothing significant about our situation that I know and the adults closest to me do not. ___
14. When I have withheld hard news, I have told them within days rather than months. ___
15. I have not decided, on someone else's behalf, that they are not strong enough to hear something. ___
16. I could say the hardest true thing in my life out loud this week without losing my standing in this family. ___

**Subtotal ___ / 20**

### Dimension Five · Timing

*What I could decide now and am postponing*

17. There is no major decision I am waiting on that I could reasonably make in the next twelve months. ___
18. When I am waiting, I can name the specific event I am waiting for, not just a feeling of readiness. ___
19. I have acted on something important before it became urgent, and paid for it, in the last five years. ___
20. I would rather be early and partly wrong than late and certain. ___

**Subtotal ___ / 20**

### Dimension Six · Release

*Who decides when I am gone*

21. There is at least one real decision, with real consequences, that used to be mine and is now someone else's. ___
22. I have let someone close to me choose differently than I would have, and stayed close afterward. ___
23. If I stopped being the source, I believe the people around me would still come. ___
24. I have told a specific person what I know, and then declined to tell them what to do with it. ___

**Subtotal ___ / 20**

## Scoring

**Grand total ___ / 120**

### Bands

**102 to 120 · Intentional Legacy Formation**
You are already living with an open hand in most areas. The work ahead is maintenance and transfer: making sure what you practice becomes what the next generation practices, and that it survives you as a habit rather than a memory.

**78 to 101 · Emerging Legacy Alignment**
Real movement is underway and it is uneven. One or two dimensions are carrying the others. The opportunity is to name the weakest one honestly and give it a season, rather than taking comfort in a good average.

**54 to 77 · Legacy Conversations Needed**
The grip is present and mostly unexamined, which is the ordinary condition of successful people and not a failing. What is missing is not intention. It is a conversation that has not been scheduled, with people who have been waiting for it longer than you think.

**24 to 53 · Begin with Trust and Discovery**
Start here without discouragement. A low score usually means a life spent building rather than releasing, and building was the right work at the time. Begin with one relationship and one true sentence, not with a plan.

### Dimension bands

Apply to any subtotal out of 20.

**17 to 20** Open · **13 to 16** Opening · **9 to 12** Held · **4 to 8** Closed

## The Discernment Layer

Your lowest dimension is not automatically your first priority. Answer these three before deciding anything.

**Impact.** If one of these six moved two points in the next year, which one would change the most for the people I love? Name the dimension and name the person.

**Urgency.** Which of these cannot wait? Consider health, age, a pending transaction, a strained relationship, or a person who will not be here in five years.

**Invitation.** Where do I sense God has already been at work on this, before I picked up this instrument? Where has the same thing kept surfacing?

Where the three answers converge, begin. Where they diverge, begin with urgency, because urgency is the only one of the three with a deadline attached.

# Part Two · The Deep Well Family Assessment

## Purpose

Everyone in the family takes the Personal Assessment separately and privately. Nobody discusses answers until all are complete. Then the family looks at three things together, and only three.

This instrument does not produce a family score. Averaging a household conceals precisely what needs to be seen.

## Instructions for the facilitator

Collect all completed assessments. Build one grid: dimensions down the side, family members across the top, subtotals in the cells. Do not show individual item responses, only dimension subtotals, unless the family has agreed in advance to full disclosure.

Then work the three readings in order. Do not skip to the third.

---

### Reading One · The Spread

For each dimension, subtract the lowest subtotal from the highest.

**0 to 4 · Aligned.** This family sees this dimension the same way. Whatever is true here, it is true for everyone.

**5 to 8 · Drifting.** Different vantage points on the same facts. Usually generational and usually fixable with one conversation.

**9 or more · Divided.** The family is not living in the same reality on this dimension. This is the finding. It outranks any low score anywhere on the grid.

**The critical read:** where the founder or senior generation scores high and the next generation scores low on the same dimension, the gap is almost never a disagreement about facts. It is the shape of the grip, seen from both ends. The person holding does not experience the holding. The person waiting experiences nothing else.

### Reading Two · The Quiet One

Identify the family member with the lowest total, and separately the one whose scores diverge most from everyone else's.

They are frequently the same person, and frequently the person who has said the least in family conversations for years. In this material that person is usually the one who married in, or the child who did not go into the business.

Do not put them on the spot. Ask the family a different question: what has this person seen that the rest of us have not?

### Reading Three · The One Dimension

Choose one. Not three, not all six.

Run the family through the same discernment questions from the Personal Assessment, answered out loud, in order of age from youngest to oldest. Youngest first is not a courtesy. It is the only sequence in which the youngest will say anything true.

Then set a date. Not for the change. For the meeting at which the decision gets made, on a calendar, before anyone leaves the room.

---

## The Founder's Item

One question, answered by the senior generation alone, in writing, and read aloud to everyone at the end.

*Name one decision that is currently mine, that could become someone else's within twelve months, that I could not reverse afterward. Then name the date.*

If the honest answer is that there is no such decision, that is not a failed exercise. It is the most valuable sentence the instrument will produce, and it should be said out loud rather than written around.

# Part Three · Ten Experiential Tools

Designed for a family meeting or a six-week campaign. Each maps to a chapter. None of them require a facilitator, though five are better with one.

**1 · The Rope.** An actual length of rope, knotted at intervals, passed around the table. Each person holds it while they speak and hands it on when finished. Crude, and it works, because the book has trained everyone to know what it means, and because a founder who has to physically hand something over in order to stop talking learns something in his hands that he will not learn in his head. *Any chapter. Best in four.*

**2 · The Three Names.** Everyone writes, separately: three people without whom this family would not have what it has. Read aloud youngest first. The founder writes too, and reads last. The gaps between the lists are the exercise. *Chapter one.*

**3 · The Missing Witness.** Identify the oldest living person who knows something about your family's history that nobody has recorded. Schedule the interview inside the meeting, with a date and a person assigned. This is the exercise that produces the most regret when skipped and the most value when done. *Chapter one.*

**4 · The Failure Ledger.** The founder writes down three significant failures with enough operational detail to be useful, not confessional. Reads one aloud. The rest go into the family record. Most founders have transmitted every success and no failures, which is why the next generation has no calibration for risk. *Chapter two.*

**5 · Teach One Thing.** The founder teaches one real skill or judgment to one person, in an hour, in the room, on the spot. Not a lecture on values. How he actually reads a deal, a person, a market, a field. Someone records it. *Chapter two.*

**6 · The Invisible List.** Everyone independently names the people who serve this household and are not in it. Compare. Then choose one name that appears on every list and answer: what happened in their life this year, and what do they need that we have never asked about. *Chapter three.*

**7 · The Sealed Envelope.** Each person writes one thing they know that the others do not, and the real reason for the silence. Envelopes are sealed and stacked in the center of the table. Nobody opens anything. The family then decides together whether they want them opened, at this meeting or a later one, and that decision is the exercise. Consent, not exposure. *Chapter four, and the one that requires the most care.*

**8 · What Are We Waiting For.** Everyone names the thing the family is currently waiting on, separately, before anyone speaks. Then together, write the answer as an event rather than a feeling. If no one can name an event, the family is waiting for the decision to become easy. Set a date. *Chapter five.*

**9 · The Handover.** The founder names one decision that transfers within twelve months, with a date, in front of everyone. It has to be real, consequential, and irreversible. Everything else in a family meeting is conversation. This is the only item that changes anything. *Chapter six.*

**10 · Dig Your Own.** Each member of the next generation names one thing they will build that is not the family enterprise, and states what they need from the founder, which may only be advice. The founder responds to each one with what he knows and does not respond with what to do. *Chapter six.*

---

## Facilitator notes

**Listen for:** stories rather than positions, family language and inside phrases, defining moments, wounds and turning points, unfinished conversations, Scripture that surfaces unprompted, and the sentence someone starts and does not finish.

**The order is not optional.** Tools two through six are safe and build affection. Seven is the hinge and should never be attempted at a family's first meeting. Nine and ten give the founder somewhere to go, and a meeting that reaches seven and stops there has opened a family and left it open.

**Confidentiality holds throughout.** Nothing disclosed in these exercises leaves the room, appears in a proposal, or informs a recommendation, without explicit written permission.
