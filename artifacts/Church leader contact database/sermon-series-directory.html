What the field says · four findings

The week after Sunday is almost empty. Of eight churches publishing sermons, only Florence ties a group guide to the series, and only Bell Shoals publishes a weekly guide. Nobody publishes transcripts; nobody publishes discussion questions as a standing product. The closest substitutes are 12Stone's application blocks inside episode descriptions and Grace's overflow podcast. Eleven churches, and the sermon-to-strategy gap is visible in every archive.

Series structure splits the set in two. Grace, 12Stone, Bell Shoals, and Blue Springs run named, dated, described series — the raw material of a campaign. Crossroads Lafayette, Palestine, and Florence's media library title by message or by date, so their own preaching history is effectively unsearchable. That second group is who the Pastor's Library aggregation move serves first.

The relationships aged more than the churches did. Four of the eleven changed hands since the file — two of them (Acevedo, Myers) in the same year, 2023, both handing multisite churches to successors. Two of the four names still standing, Ables and Abney, have been in or around the same pulpits for twenty years, and Acevedo in retirement fits the over-fifty archive-owner profile exactly.

On "most popular" video: YouTube renders its Popular sort in the browser, not to a crawler, so per-video view counts couldn't be captured server-side. Every channel link above lands on the channel itself, where the Popular tab is one tap. The podcast feeds (12Stone, Crossroads, Bell Shoals) are the better sampling surface anyway — dated, described, and downloadable.
