## C · Long bio (≈650 words)
*Book jackets, keynote introductions, board packets, major partnership documents.*

Brett Eastman came to faith in college because someone took a chance and invited him
to a small group Bible study. He heard the message of the gospel in a room safe enough
to argue with it, and was encouraged to gather friends of his own and start another
group. Seven of the young men in that group came to Christ, and three of them went
into full-time ministry. Nearly everything he has built since has been an attempt to
reproduce that room at scale.

His first career was in technology. After a marketing degree from San Diego State
University he worked in Silicon Valley during the early personal-computing era, in
training and marketing roles alongside Apple, Xerox, and Novell — an education in how a
product becomes a category and how a category becomes a movement. When he sensed a call
into ministry he earned an M.Div. from Talbot School of Theology at Biola University and
later completed a management program at Northwestern's Kellogg School of Management.

He joined the senior staff at Willow Creek Community Church under Bill Hybels, spending
seven years in Chicago before Rick Warren and the Saddleback team invited him to
California to champion small group ministry, leadership development, and the membership
process. Saddleback had more than ten thousand people and seventy-three off-campus small
groups when he arrived. The math was the whole problem. With only two or three pastoral
staff assigned to groups, the ministry had to shift from a staff-driven model to a
volunteer-driven one, which connected roughly half the congregation, and then to a
resource-driven model built on video curriculum. The first series they produced, on the
book of James, mobilized a hundred people to open their homes. The production was crude;
the lesson was not.

That lesson became the 40 Days of Purpose campaign, which Brett helped design and which
he took to market one church at a time — coaching the first twenty-nine congregations to
run it before the model spread to tens of thousands of churches worldwide. He wrote and
produced the first Purpose Driven small group series for Rick Warren, *Doing Life
Together*, published by Zondervan, which has sold more than four million copies and
shaped the design of dozens of small group series and church campaigns that followed.
The "smart curriculum" model he engineered for that series — content built so a church
could run it, adapt it, and teach from it without a consultant in the room — is still the
architecture underneath most of what he builds.

In 2006 he founded LifeTogether Ministries. Over the two decades since, he and his team
have produced hundreds of custom video and print campaigns for many of the hundred
largest and fastest-growing churches in America, along with denominations, faith-based
nonprofits, and most of the major Christian publishing houses, working with authors
including Max Lucado, John Maxwell, John Ortberg, and Christine Caine.

His recent work has moved the same campaign engine toward families and the professionals
who serve them. With Tom Conway he co-founded Family Legacy by Design, which coaches
families of wealth through the five areas of legacy and certifies advisors to lead those
conversations rather than outsource them. He serves as chief marketing officer at Equity
Partners and is writing *Built to Grow*, a book for Christian financial advisors on
scaling with purpose and exiting with peace. He continues to collaborate with Ron Blue
and the Ron Blue Institute on biblical financial wisdom resources for local churches, and
he has spent the past two years building AI-assisted publishing systems that let a single
message be produced as a sermon, a small group series, a family journey, and a churchwide
campaign at once.

Brett married his high school sweetheart, Dee, right out of college. Dee is the founding
director of The Daniel Plan and a contributing author to the number one *New York Times*
bestseller of the same name. They have five children — Joshua, Breanna, and identical
triplets Melody, Meagan, and Michelle — and live in Southern California.

> *"In the dream-it-up phase of a project, where you imagine the possibilities and produce
> a scenario for communicating your message in a compelling way, I never met anyone who can
> match Brett's genius."*
> **— Rick Warren, Saddleback Church**

## D · Family Legacy edition (≈280 words)

Wealth transfers. Values don't, unless somebody decides they will.

Brett Eastman has spent his career building the conversations that make that decision
possible — first for churches, now for families. With Tom Conway he co-founded Family
Legacy by Design, which helps families gain clarity across the five areas of legacy,

generation will receive, but who they are becoming before they receive it.

He brings an unusual background to the question. For more than a decade he served as

learned something most legacy work still overlooks: a family, like a congregation, does
not change because it receives good information. It changes because it has the right
conversation, in the right order, with the right people in the room.

Brett also serves as chief marketing officer at Equity Partners and collaborates with
Ron Blue and the Ron Blue Institute on biblical financial wisdom resources. He holds an
M.Div. from Talbot School of Theology and completed a management program at
Northwestern's Kellogg School of Management. He and his wife, Dee, have five children

## Claims I softened, and why

- **"Primary designer of 40 Days of Purpose."** Two files say *the* primary designer, two
  say *one of* the primary designers. I used "one of." It is the version that survives
  someone from that era reading it, and it costs you nothing.
- **"Best-selling book of all time next to the Bible."** Cut. It's contested, easy to
  fact-check against, and it's a claim about Rick's book rather than about you.
- **"Original designer behind RightNow Media, a platform grossing over $15 million a year."**
  Cut pending your confirmation. This is the single highest-risk line in the old files —
  it's a specific attribution to a company you don't control, with a revenue figure
  attached. If it's accurate and you can source it, it's a strong line and I'll write it
  back in. If it's "we did early design work that influenced it," that needs different
  wording.
- **"Produced over 5,000 church leaders."** Almost certainly a typo for *worked with* 5,000
  church leaders or churches. Confirm which and I'll restore it.
- **Kellogg.** Your files say a management certificate / one-year business management
  program, not a degree. I wrote it that way. My prior note had it as "graduate of
  Kellogg" — the files are more precise, so the files win.
- **Willow Creek before Saddleback.** Two files list Saddleback first, but the narrative
  passages clearly go Willow → Saddleback. I sequenced it that way throughout. Confirm.
