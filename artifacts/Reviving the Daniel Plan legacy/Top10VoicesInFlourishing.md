## TIER 1: HARVARD FLOURISHING RESEARCH TEAM (The Scientific Foundation)

### 1. **Tyler J. VanderWeele, PhD**
**Role:** Director, Human Flourishing Program at Harvard  
**Title:** John L. Loeb and Frances Lehman Loeb Professor of Epidemiology, Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health

**Core Keywords & Big Ideas:**
- **"Flourishing as a multidimensional construct"** — not happiness alone, but integration of physical health, mental health, meaning, purpose, character, virtue, close relationships, financial stability
- **"The promotion of human flourishing"** (foundational 2017 PNAS paper)
- **Causal inference methodology** — rigorous distinction between association and causation (his specialty before flourishing)
- **Religion and health integration** — co-director of Initiative on Health, Spirituality, and Religion
- **The five domains framework** — universal human desires that constitute ends in themselves

**Books:**
- *A Theology of Health: Wholeness and Human Flourishing* (2024) — his most recent, bridges theology and epidemiology
- *Measuring Well-Being* (2021)
- *Handbook of Religion and Health* (2023)
- *Explanation in Causal Inference* (2015)

**Problems He's Solving:**
- Fragmented wellbeing research (people study income OR happiness OR health in isolation)
- Narrow metrics that miss what actually makes humans thrive
- The disconnect between modern epidemiology (disease prevention) and human flourishing (what makes life good)
- How to measure flourishing across 22 countries with cultural validity

**Public Platform:**
- Monthly newsletter for Human Flourishing Program (Psychology Today)
- Over 600 published papers
- 2017 COPSS Presidents' Award (highest honor in statistics)

**Why He Matters for Your Work:**
VanderWeele is the *methodological foundation*. He built the flourishing framework itself. His work validates what you're trying to do: physical health is not separate from meaning, relationships, and spirituality. He provides the research credibility.

## TIER 3: FOUNDATIONAL POSITIVE PSYCHOLOGY PIONEERS (The Scientists)

### 5. **Martin E. Seligman, PhD**
**Role:** Founder of Positive Psychology Movement  
**Title:** Director, Positive Psychology Center at University of Pennsylvania

**Core Keywords & Big Ideas:**
- **"Positive Psychology"** — pivoted psychology from "what's broken" to "what works"
- **"PERMA model"** — Positive emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, Accomplishment
- **"Authentic Happiness" vs. "Well-being"** — important distinction (engagement/flow matters more than pleasure)
- **"Learned optimism"** — people can train themselves toward resilience
- **"Strength-spotting" vs. only problem-solving**

**Books:**
- *Flourish: A Visionary New Understanding of Happiness and Well-Being* (2011)
- *Authentic Happiness* (2002)
- *Learned Optimism* (1991)

**Problems He's Solving:**
- The "disease model" dominating psychology (fixing problems vs. building strengths)
- Measuring positive states (not just absence of illness)
- Making wellbeing science rigorous and testable
- Military resilience (Pentagon contracted him post-9/11)

**Public Platform:**
- Founded PERMA model that shapes institutional wellbeing initiatives globally
- University of Pennsylvania's Positive Psychology Center (training ground for the field)

**Why He Matters for Your Work:**
Seligman is the *grandfather* of this entire field. Everything in the Harvard flourishing framework traces back to his 1998 pivot to positive psychology. His PERMA model is foundational vocabulary for understanding the cascade you're building.

## THEIR SHARED PROBLEMS THEY'RE SOLVING

**Across all 10:**

1. **The fragmentation problem** — Researchers study one domain (mental health) ignoring others (relationships, meaning, physical). Solution: integrated frameworks
2. **The materialist blind spot** — Wealthy nations aren't happier. Solution: meaning, virtue, connection matter more than stuff
3. **The youth crisis** — Young people report lowest flourishing despite digital connection. Solution: in-person community, purpose, spiritual anchoring
4. **The reduction problem** — Can't reduce flourishing to just neuroscience or just virtue or just happiness. Solution: multidimensional integration
5. **The faith gap** — Secular institutions ignore religion; religious organizations ignored by science. Solution: integration (VanderWeele, Johnson, Pawelski leading this)
6. **The translation problem** — Brilliant research stays in journals; doesn't reach people. Solution: Arthur Brooks, Ben-Shahar model (academic rigor + accessible teaching)
7. **The implementation gap** — People know what flourishing looks like but can't sustain it alone. Solution: community, small groups, scaffolding (your curriculum angle)
