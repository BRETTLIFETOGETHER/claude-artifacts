## 0. THE ONE THING TO DECIDE FIRST

**The source is a script, not a transcript.**

Every prior title in this series was built from six recorded VTT transcripts of Brett and Tom in conversation. This title's source is a pre-production script with camera and blocking directions still in it: `BRETT AND TOM CENTER`, `TOM TURN`, `BRETT CENTER`, `BOTH CENTER`. Session 1 carries them; Sessions 2 through 6 have them stripped.

That changes the risk profile. If these videos have not been shot, or if Tom departs from the script when he shoots them, the guide will not match the video the advisor is watching. The Learn section in particular says *"Watch the Session N teaching video"* and then states in bold what Tom and Brett covered. If Tom ad-libs a story or drops a framework, those capture prompts go wrong.

**Decision 1.** Confirm one of the following:

- **(a) Shot to script, verbatim.** Build now against the script. Lowest risk.
- **(b) Not yet shot.** Build now, then reconcile against the recorded transcripts before layout. Adds a reconciliation pass.
- **(c) Already shot, loosely.** Do not build yet. Send the recorded transcripts first.

**Recommended default: (a) or (b).** Do not proceed under (c).

## 3. CONTENT DISPLACEMENT: THE STRUCTURAL ISSUE

This is the most consequential finding in the report.

Four of six sessions in this title cover the same ground as four completed titles:

| Foundation session | Existing six-session title |
|---|---|
| 3. What Matters Most | **Clarity** |
| 4. Getting on the Same Page | **Alignment** |
| 5. The Conversations That Matter | **Communication** |
| 6. Building a Legacy That Lasts | **Next Generation** |

Only **Session 1 (The Heart of Legacy)** and **Session 2 (Advice to Guidance)** are genuinely new territory. Session 2 in particular is the strongest original content in the script and has no home anywhere else in the series.

This is not a defect. It reads as deliberate: this is the **on-ramp title**, the one an advisor takes first, and each of Sessions 3 through 6 previews a full title. But it has to be built that way on purpose, or the Scenarios, Tools, and Frameworks will collide with the deeper titles and the advisor will feel he has already done this.

**Decision 3.** Position this title explicitly as the series foundation, with Sessions 3 through 6 written as previews that end by naming where the advisor goes next.

**Recommended default: yes.** Concretely, that means:
- Session 3 Framework = **The Five Areas of Legacy**, and the session names *Clarity* as the deep dive
- Session 4 Framework = **Understanding Not Agreement**, naming *Alignment*
- Session 5 Framework = **The Three Keys to Communication**, naming *Communication*
- Session 6 Framework = **Preparing the Next Generation**, naming *Next Generation* and *Meetings*
- The certification pathway gets stated once, in Using This Guide, not repeated per session

This also resolves an open item carried from the prior builds: the ordering of *Meetings* and *Next Generation* in the certification pathway. If this title is the on-ramp, the pathway order can be stated here once and inherited everywhere else.

**Decision 4.** Certification pathway order. **Recommended default: Foundation, Clarity, Alignment, Communication, Meetings, Next Generation.** Meetings before Next Generation, because the family meeting is the mechanism through which the next generation is engaged, and the Coaching Book orders it that way in Chapters 11 and 12 only by a narrow margin. Flagged as genuinely open.

**Six story beats total. All are already de-identified in the script. None can be named. All are thin.**

| Session | Story | Named? | Assessment |

| 1 | A family with strong resources whose next generation had no shared sense of values or purpose | No, generic | Usable, but no mechanism detail |

| 4 | Parents who believed they had been clear, children who held very different interpretations | No, generic | Usable |
| 5 | A family that avoided a conversation about expectations, had it badly, then had it again guided | No, generic | Strongest emotional arc of the six |
| 6 | Two families contrasted: one assuming the next generation would figure it out, one involving them gradually | No, generic composite | Composite. Must be labeled illustrative |

**The problem.** The master prompt asks each Opening Story to be sourced, given a short title, and told with enough specificity to land. These six have no dollar figures, no number of children, no business, no decision, no scene. Every one of them is already at the level of abstraction that de-identification usually produces *after* editing. Written up as Opening Stories they will read as generic and the advisor will not feel Session 1 personally, which the master prompt names as the failure mode that sinks the series.

**The bench.** The Coaching Book carries a much deeper story inventory, all already de-identified, all with the mechanism detail these lack. Available candidates include: the family with four children and a $50 million estate; the family with 75 trusts whose younger generation could not follow them; the father who had years earlier given something to one child; the parents who required their children to tithe; the 85 year old business owner from a Generous Giving conference; the family reviewed annually since 2016 with revised documents, a funded donor advised fund, and paid off mortgages; the client asked how much he spent on his lifestyle. The Coaching Book also carries Tom's own origin story, working as head of development for an international missions agency.

**Decision 5.** Story sourcing.

**Recommended default: use the script's story as the spine of each Opening Story and import the fuller matching version from the Coaching Book where one exists, with relay framing where Tom's proximity is secondhand.** This keeps the video and the guide aligned while giving the story enough weight to carry the page. Where no match exists, the script version runs as is and gets flagged.

**Alternative if you want strict source fidelity:** run the six script stories only, and accept that they are short. I do not recommend this for Sessions 1 and 3.

## 10. NOTE ON DENSITY

The script runs about 4,900 words across six sessions, roughly 820 words per session, each labeled twenty minutes. A twenty minute conversational segment normally transcribes to two to three times that. Either the sessions run shorter than labeled, or the recorded conversation will expand well beyond the script.

Practical consequence: the four Learn capture prompts per session are the section most exposed to this. Each one states in bold what Tom and Brett covered and then asks the advisor to apply it to his own book of business. Built from the script alone, they will be accurate but sparse. If recorded transcripts arrive later, the Learn sections are the first thing to reconcile.
