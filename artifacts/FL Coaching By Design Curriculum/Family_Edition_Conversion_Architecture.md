# FAMILY EDITION
## Conversion Architecture for the Curriculum and the Devotional
Working document. Decisions flagged, nothing built.
## THE FOUR DECISIONS THAT DETERMINE EVERYTHING ELSE
Every question below the line depends on these. They cannot be deferred into the build.

### Decision 1. Which family?
The advisor edition has one audience: a practicing advisor. There is only one of them and he is reading alone. The family edition has at least three audiences and they are not compatible.

- **The first generation couple.** Wealth creators, sixties to eighties, deciding. They read well, they have time, and they are the ones who buy.
- **The whole family together.** Ten adults, ages twenty-five to eighty, in a room, with unequal standing and unequal information.
- **The next generation individually.** Twenty-five to forty-five, receiving, frequently uninformed, and often reading because a parent handed them a book.

A single guide written for all three will fail all three. The G1 book can be direct about mortality and money. The whole-family book cannot say anything that puts one person on the spot in front of the others. The next-gen book has to explain things the other two already know without being condescending about it.

**Recommended default: build the whole-family edition first**, because it is the one an advisor uses in an engagement and it is the one that drives multiple copies per household. The G1 edition and the next-gen edition follow as variants rather than as separate titles.

### Decision 2. Facilitator or no facilitator?
The advisor series deliberately has no facilitator layer. That was correct there, because an advisor works alone.

A family does not. Somebody runs the session, and in almost every case it is either the certified coach or the founder, and those two produce very different books.

**Recommended default: build for a coach-led family, with a self-guided fallback.** That matches the business, since certification is the point of the advisor edition and the family edition is what a certified coach puts in a family's hands. The self-guided path exists for families who buy the book without an advisor, and it is a clearly marked alternate route rather than the primary one.

This is the single biggest structural change. The family edition needs a facilitator layer the advisor edition explicitly refuses.

### Decision 3. Video.
The six existing scripts are addressed to advisors. Not incidentally. Structurally.

Most advisors are trained to give advice. You're drawing things out, not just putting things in. Take one client family. Every second sentence has an advisor as its subject, and no amount of editing converts that into family-facing teaching.

Three options:

- **Reshoot Tom for families.** Same six themes, new scripts, Tom speaking to a family rather than about one. This is the right answer and it is the expensive one.
- **No video.** The family edition becomes reading-driven, anchored to the Family Book. Cheaper, and it removes the Learn section entirely, which is one of the seventeen.
- **Reuse with a bridge.** A short new intro from Tom before each existing segment. This will not work. A family watching an advisor training video will feel like they are watching someone discuss them.

**Recommended default: reshoot.** Six segments, twenty minutes each, Tom to camera or Tom with a family rather than Tom with Brett. If reshooting is not available this year, build the no-video version rather than the bridge version.

### Decision 4. What replaces Session 2?
Session 2, Moving from Advice to Guidance, is entirely about the advisor's role. There is no family equivalent. It cannot be converted, only replaced.

**Recommended default: replace it with a session on what the family received.** The Three Kinds of Legacies gives three sessions worth of material and the advisor edition only uses one of them as a framework. What you received is the richest untapped session in the whole system, it is backward-looking where everything else is forward-looking, and it is the safest possible second session because it is about grandparents rather than about anyone in the room.

## THE SEVENTEEN SECTIONS, CONVERTED
What survives intact, what changes, and what has to be rebuilt.

### 1. Session Divider
No change.

### 2. Introduction and Key Verse
Structure unchanged. Two substantive changes.

**The commercial case comes out entirely.** In the advisor edition it runs once in Session 1: retention through a generational transition, the next generation in the room, assets under management preserved. None of that belongs in a family book and including any of it would be corrosive.

**What replaces it** is the family's own stake, and the argument already exists in the sources. Something is being passed on whether or not anyone is managing it. There will be a family meeting, and the only question is whether you are in it. That is the Reality Check argument, it is Tom's, and it is the family-facing equivalent of the commercial case: stated once, in Session 1, and then left alone.

### 3. Opening Story
Structure unchanged, sensitivity changes considerably.

An advisor reading about a family that came apart is reading a case study. A family reading the same story is being shown a mirror, and if the story is too close they will read it as an accusation and if it is too far they will dismiss it.

Two rules for the family edition. Stories should show the family that did the work at least as often as the family that did not, which is a different balance than the advisor edition uses. And the failing family should fail in a way that is clearly not this family, because the point of a story here is safe distance rather than diagnosis.

### 4. Get Started
This section changes more than any other in the front half.

In the advisor edition the privacy reminder protects absent third parties, which is easy. In the family edition the people being discussed are sitting in the room, which is a completely different problem and the current one-line reminder is nowhere near sufficient.

The family edition needs a real safety architecture, established in the front matter and reinforced every session:

- Nobody is required to answer anything out loud
- Passing is a normal thing to do and requires no explanation
- Nothing said here is used later as evidence in a decision
- No dollar figures are required from anyone at any point
- What is discussed here is not discussed with people who were not present

That last one is the hardest and the most necessary, because the side conversation after the meeting is where families do the most damage to each other.

### 5. Building a Healthy Culture
In the advisor edition this is a half page in Session 1 about the advisor's own practice and a one-line reminder thereafter.

In the family edition it is about the family's own culture, and it should be larger rather than smaller. Recommend a full page in Session 1 and a half page in every session, because family culture is the actual subject of this book rather than a side note to it.

### 6. Learn
Survives only if there is video. The four capture prompts convert cleanly: apply it to your own book of business becomes apply it to your own family. If Decision 3 goes the no-video route, this section is removed and the seventeen becomes sixteen.

### 7. Reflect
This is the section that requires the most redesign, and the reason is structural.

The advisor edition runs seven questions where six are about clients and the seventh is personal. That design gave the advisor six questions of professional distance before asking anything of him.

A family has no client layer. Every question is the seventh question. Written without adjustment, a family opens the book to seven consecutive personal questions in a room with their children.

**The replacement is graduated exposure.** Questions one and two about families in general or about the previous generation. Three and four about this family's past. Five and six about this family now. Seven personal and explicitly optional. The gradient does the work that professional distance did in the advisor edition.

### 8. The Framework
Tom's frameworks split cleanly.

**Convert directly, already family-facing:** The Three Kinds of Legacies. The Twin W's of Legacy. The Five Areas of Legacy. Understanding, Not Agreement. The Three Keys to Communication. The Six Areas of Life.

**Advisor-only, cannot convert:** The Two Sides of Legacy Planning. Legacy Planning Distinctives. The Four Essential Skills. The Five Key Characteristics of a Legacy Planner.

**Convert with reframing:** Preparing the Next Generation. The seven steps are written from the coach's side, including securing permission from Generation One and advocating to them. A family edition holds the same seven from the family's side, where step one is deciding to include the next generation rather than getting permission to approach them.

### 9. Scenarios
Counterintuitively, this section becomes more valuable rather than less.

For an advisor, a scenario is a professional judgment exercise. For a family, a scenario is the only place in the book where they can discuss their own situation without appearing to. A family that cannot yet say what it thinks about its own business succession can say a great deal about a fictional family facing the same thing, and everybody in the room knows what is actually being discussed.

That makes scenarios the safest on-ramp in the family edition and they should probably run earlier in the session rather than after the Framework.

The eighteen Scenario Responses change character. In the advisor edition they are Tom's professional counsel and the only answer key in the book. In the family edition they should be shorter, less conclusive, and framed as what families in this situation have found rather than what you should do. A family reading a confident answer will feel corrected.

### 10. The Tool
The tools split like the frameworks.

**Family-usable:** The Reality Check. The Goals Conversation. Legacy Letters and Family Blessings. The Five Uses of Money. The Family Legacy Picture.

**Advisor-only:** The FAMILY Review. The Family Legacy Goals and Gaps Analysis. The Next Generation Interview, which is by definition conducted by an outsider.

Note that four of the five family-usable tools already exist as appendices in the Family Book. The family edition should reference them rather than reproduce them, or the two products compete.

### 11. Exercise
Three parts in the advisor edition: apply to a client, go deeper, turn it on yourself. The family edition has no client layer so the structure collapses.

**Recommended replacement, also three parts, and it maps to how families actually change:** alone, then with one other person, then brought back to the group. Part One private. Part Two a single one-to-one conversation between sessions. Part Three what you are willing to say to everyone.

That structure is better than the advisor version because it names the pair as the unit where family change actually happens.

### 12. Circles of Influence
Converts into the most useful question in the family edition. Not who else should read this, but **who is not in the room.**

Almost every family working through this has excluded somebody: a spouse of a child, an estranged sibling, a next generation considered too young. Naming them on paper, with no commitment attached, is the beginning of most of the good outcomes in this material.

### 13. Next Steps
No change beyond referent.

### 14. Practice Conversation
Converts directly and becomes arguably the most important section in the book.

The advisor took four questions to a colleague. A family member takes them to one specific relative, one to one, between sessions. Families do not transform in the group meeting. They transform in the car afterward and in the pair conversations during the week, and this section is the only part of the architecture that instruments that.

Recommend it gets more space and a named partner assignment each session rather than an open invitation.

### 15. Going Deeper
No structural change. Three passages, two questions each.

### 16. This Week's Readings
Source swaps from *Family Legacy Coaching by Design* to *Family Legacy by Design*. The family-facing companion already exists and is structured in parts that align to these themes, with Part One titled The Heart of Legacy.

Five readings per session, thirty total, same as the advisor edition. Requires a page-level mapping pass against the Family Book.

### 17. Final Thoughts
No change beyond referent.
## THE SOLO FORK INVERTS
This is the detail most likely to be missed in a conversion.

The advisor edition carries one tag, verbatim, everywhere:

> Team or pair: discuss. Solo: write it down.

Solo is the default there and the group is the accommodation. In a family edition the group is the default and the individual is the accommodation, so the tag inverts:

> **Together: discuss. On your own: write it down.**

There is also a third condition the advisor edition never had to handle. Many families are not currently able to be in a room together. Estrangement, a recent death, an unresolved business dispute. Those families still buy the book and they need a route through it that does not assume a functioning group.

**Recommended default: a third mode, stated in Using This Guide.** If your family is not ready to do this together, work through it alone and use the Practice Conversation as your only shared element. That is an honest path and it will describe a meaningful share of buyers.
## THE DEVOTIONAL, CONVERTED
Simpler than the curriculum, and probably the higher-volume product.

### What stays
Thirty days, five a week, six weeks, weekends open. Same shape. Same six-minute read. Tom's voice, first person, plain and direct.

### What changes

**Audience and copies.** The advisor devotional is one copy per advisor. The family devotional is one copy per family member, which is where the volume is. A family of ten working through this buys ten. That has a design consequence: it must work for a twenty-eight-year-old and a seventy-eight-year-old reading the same page on the same day.

**The Bucket.** In the advisor edition it turns the day on the advisor personally, and it works because everything else that day was professional. In a family edition the whole day is already personal, so The Bucket needs a different function.

**Recommended replacement:** it becomes the place for what you are not ready to say out loud yet. Same privacy promise, different job. That gives a family member somewhere to put the thing they will not raise at the table, and over thirty days it accumulates into something they may eventually be willing to say.

**One Question to Carry.** Converts directly and gets stronger. The advisor took it to a client. A family member takes it to one specific relative. Thirty days produces thirty conversations, one to one, distributed across a family. That is a more powerful mechanism in the family edition than in the advisor edition.

**Titles and subtitles.** Every one of the sixty phrases in the advisor devotional is verbatim Tom from advisor-facing scripts, and a significant share of them have an advisor as the subject. Not just what you leave behind survives. Most advisors are trained to give advice does not. New video for the curriculum solves this. Without it, the family devotional titles have to be sourced from the Family Book instead, which is a different sweep and a different tone.

**Practice sections.** Same tool split as the curriculum. Roughly a third of the advisor devotional's Practice exercises are advisor-only and need family equivalents.

**Length.** Recommend holding at 700 to 900 words rather than shortening. A shorter family devotional reads as though the family is assumed to be less capable, and the G1 couple who buys it will notice.

## THREE THINGS I WOULD FLAG TO TOM
**The Family Book already exists and this competes with it unless positioned.** *Family Legacy by Design* is the family-facing volume and it carries several of the tools this curriculum would use. The clean separation is that the book is what a family reads and the guide is what a family works, but that has to be decided rather than assumed, or Hannah will design two products that overlap.

**The next generation edition is the real opportunity and nobody has scoped it.** Everything in this system is built for the person who has the wealth. The recurring finding across all six sessions is that the failure originates with the people who receive it, and there is currently no product addressed to them. A next-generation guide is the least crowded position in the whole ecosystem.

**The three-audience problem cannot be split later.** If the family edition is built for an unspecified family and the G1 and next-gen variants are planned as later derivatives, the base book will be written at a level of generality that serves nobody. Pick one, build it properly, derive the others from a finished thing.
