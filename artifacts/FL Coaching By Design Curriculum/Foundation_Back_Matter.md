## FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
**I am the only person in my firm doing this. Does it still work?**

Yes, and this guide was built that way. Every exercise is designed for one advisor working alone. The prompts that can be discussed carry a fork so you can write instead. Nothing here requires a partner, a team, or a facilitator.

**How long does this actually take?**

Ninety minutes per session and about fifteen minutes a day for the readings, across six weeks. The Practice Conversation and the Exercise happen between sessions. If you are moving faster than that, you are skipping the writing, and the writing is where the work happens.

**Do I need to be a CPA or an attorney to do this?**

No. Technical background helps and Tom is a CPA, but his own summary of the job puts the relational skills ahead of the technical ones. What the work does require is a willingness to bring in genuine experts where you are not strong.

**My clients are not people of faith. Does this apply?**

Most of it, directly. Tom adapts his own language for this. In the fifth area of legacy he uses charitable or philanthropic rather than Kingdom, and the substance of the conversation does not change. The scripture in this guide is for you rather than for the meeting.

**What do I charge for this?**

Tom charges a flat fee for the Family Legacy Review as its own engagement, and his reasoning is diagnostic rather than commercial. Nobody can scope a plan before knowing where the gaps are, and doing the review without charge quietly converts it into a sales process, which changes what the client tells you. His range appears in the companion book. *[Fee guidance for certified coaches pending.]*

**Will this take time away from my existing practice?**

It redirects hours rather than adding them. Advisors who try to run legacy coaching as additional service volume on top of a full book tend to burn out within a year. Advisors who treat it as a different posture inside hours they were already spending tend to sustain it.

**What if a family refuses to have the conversation?**

Some will. Your job is to make the first attempt more likely to go well, not to compel it. A family that declines this year has often moved by the third time it is raised, and raising it a third time is only possible if the first two times were handled without pressure.

**What if I open something I cannot handle?**

Know your limit in advance and name it in the room when you reach it. Serious family conflict, addiction, mental illness, and estrangement are outside the scope of legacy coaching. Referring well is part of the work, not an admission of failure.

**Can I use this with families I do not manage assets for?**

Yes. The process is independent of the asset relationship, and Tom's own entry into this work came through a role that had no asset management in it at all.

**Do I have to do the personal parts?**

Part Three of every Exercise turns the session on you, and it is the section most likely to be skipped. It is also the reason the material lands with clients. A conversation about legacy that you have never had yourself is a conversation you will run at arm's length, and families can tell.

**Where do I go after this guide?**

The five deeper titles are *Clarity*, *Alignment*, *Communication*, *Meetings*, and *Next Generation*. Each takes one session of this guide and gives it six of its own. See Next Steps.

**How does certification work?**

Completing this guide counts as one module. Module completion is self-attested. The Certified Family Legacy Coach credential is a separate instrument issued after all modules and the Training Manual are complete. *[Requirements, timeline, and fees pending.]*

## TOP 10 COACHING EXERCISES
The ten instruments Tom uses most, with where each one is documented. Six of the ten appear in this guide. The remaining four belong to the deeper titles and are listed here so you know the full set.

**1. The Reality Check**
The five realities of life, read aloud, ending on the only decision available. Removes the option of treating legacy as hypothetical. *Session 1 of this guide. Workbook Legend, Session 1.*

**2. The Three Kinds of Legacies**
What you received, what you will pass on, what you are living out today. Run it on a client, then on yourself. *Session 1 of this guide. Workbook Legend, Session 1.*

**3. The Goals Conversation**
The five areas of legacy, worked in order, personal first. Step one of Stage 1. *Session 3 of this guide. Coaching Book, Chapter 7 and Appendix A. Family Book, Appendix B.*

**4. The FAMILY Review**
Structured inventory of what a family actually has: financial statement, assets under management, mandatory documents, insurance, liabilities and taxes, yearly cash flow. Step two of Stage 1. *Coaching Book, Chapter 8 and Appendices C and D. Family Book, Appendix A.*

**5. The Family Legacy Goals and Gaps Analysis**
Sets what the family says it wants against what the current plan does. The deliverable of Stage 1. *Session 4 of this guide. Coaching Book, Chapter 9 and Appendix E.*

**6. The Next Generation Interview**
Nine areas, one to one, with permission secured and confidentiality established first. *Session 6 of this guide. Workbook Legend, Session 9. Coaching Book, Chapter 11. Family Book, Appendix F.*

**7. The Family Meeting**
Four types, four categories of issue, rules set before the agenda. *Coaching Book, Chapter 12. Workbook Legend, Session 10. Full treatment in* Meetings.

**8. Developing Family Vision, Mission, and Values**
Every generation in the room, every voice counted. The process matters more than the statement. *Coaching Book, Chapter 13.*

**9. Legacy Letters and Family Blessings**
Written by the client, in their own hand, saying what the documents cannot. *Coaching Book, Appendix B. Family Book, Appendix D.*

**10. The Five Uses of Money**
Living expenses, giving, debt repayment, taxes, savings and investment, with the five money management principles underneath. *Workbook Legend, Session 6. Coaching Book, Appendix G. Family Book, Appendix E.*

## ABOUT FAMILY LEGACY BY DESIGN
Three words sit at the heart of this work: **clarity, alignment, and communication.**

We help families gain clarity around their goals in five areas of legacy, personal, family, financial, business, and charitable. We review the existing plan to see how well it matches that clarity. Then we help them communicate and discuss the plan with the next generation, which is done in family meetings.

All of it runs through the Multi-Generational Legacy Coaching Process, which helps families gain confidence about what they believe God wants them to do with their resources. It helps them quantify what they need for the future, what they wish to leave to their family, what they will do with their business, and how the remainder can be released to organizations that reflect their values.
