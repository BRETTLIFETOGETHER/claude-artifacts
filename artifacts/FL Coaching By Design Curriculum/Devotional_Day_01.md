# DAY 1
# Not Just What You Leave Behind
## It's What You Pass On Every Day
**"A good person leaves an inheritance for their children's children."**
PROVERBS 13:22

Most people think of legacy as something that happens later. I understood it that way for a long time, and I was trained in a profession that reinforces it at every turn. The documents get drafted for a moment none of us will be in the room for. The plan gets built around a date nobody can name. Everything about the way we work says this is a future event.

Legacy is not just what you leave behind. It's what you pass on every day.

Those are two different verbs and they operate on completely different terms. Leaving is a transaction. It happens once, it happens after you are gone, and it is administered by people you hired and paid. Passing on is not a transaction at all. It is happening right now, in how you live, what you prioritize, and how you relate to others. Nobody has been hired to handle it. There is no closing date and no signature page.

Which changes the question. For most of my career the question in the room was some version of, what will I leave. The better question, and the one I put to families early now, is, what am I building into the lives around me.

Here is the part that took me the longest to accept. Often, what gets passed on most clearly is not what was intended. It's what was lived.

The intended legacy is the one in the documents. It is careful, it has been reviewed, and it usually reflects a person at his best. The lived legacy is what the children actually observed over thirty years, and it was never reviewed by anyone. No child in the history of this work has ever read the trust and concluded something about his father. He concluded it from watching his father.

I want to be precise about the mechanism, because this is where advisors get it wrong. It is not that the lived legacy competes with the intended one and sometimes wins. It is that the lived legacy is the only one currently transmitting. The other one has not started yet. A family can spend a decade perfecting a document that has not moved a single thing, while the thing that is actually moving goes entirely unexamined.

And you cannot pause it while you get organized. That is the uncomfortable part. There is no version of this where a family suspends the transfer of values for two years, gets its planning in order, and then resumes. Whatever is true this year is being passed on this year.

Now put that against your book of business. You have families right now where the intended legacy and the lived legacy are a long way apart, and nobody around those families is assigned to notice. Everyone around them is working on the intended one. The attorney is drafting it, the accountant is optimizing it, you may be funding it. Not one professional in that family's life has been asked to look at the other one, and the other one is the one running.

That is either a gap in the market or a gap in your service. I have come to think it is both.

I will say the harder version too, because I do not think this lands for anyone who keeps it professional. Your children are not reading your estate plan either. Whatever you have arranged for them is not what they are currently learning from you. They are learning from what you did last Tuesday, and from what you said about the client who called at dinner, and from whether the thing you tell them matters is the thing your calendar says matters.

I have never met an advisor who found that comfortable. I have met a number who found it useful.

There is a principle in Proverbs 13:22. A good person leaves an inheritance for their children's children. That includes financial inheritance, but it also includes values, perspective, and direction.

Notice the reach in that verse. Not children. Children's children. It is describing something that arrives two generations out, to people the person may never meet, which means it has to survive a handoff by someone else.

Money survives that handoff without any help. It has custodians, statements, and a legal system built to move it. Everything else requires a carrier, and a carrier is a person, and a person has to be prepared. That preparation takes years and it cannot be started at the end. It is the reason this work exists and the reason it cannot wait for a better quarter.

The documents are for later. Everything else is already underway.
## REFLECT
**1.** Where in your own work have you treated legacy as a future event, and what did that assumption cost a family you serve?

**2.** Take one client. What is the distance between his intended legacy and his lived one, and how would you know if you were wrong about it?

**3.** Nobody around your families is assigned to the lived legacy. What would it take for that to become part of your role rather than an interest of yours?

**4.** What are your own children, or the people closest to you, currently learning from what you do rather than from what you have arranged?
