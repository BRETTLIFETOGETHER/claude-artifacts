> **MATTHEW 6:21**

Every family you serve already has values. They are operating from them right now, in how they spend, who they help, what they tolerate, and which grandchild gets called first when something goes wrong. None of that required a document.

What most of them do not have is any articulated version of it. The values are running the family without ever having been said out loud, which works reasonably well until the family has to make a decision that the values would have settled and instead has to be argued.

That is the actual mechanism. It is not that families lack values. It is that undeclared values cannot be used as a reference point, so every decision gets litigated on its own terms, and the results drift apart from each other over a decade until somebody notices the family has been going in two directions.

Your role here is smaller and more useful than it sounds. You are not helping a family invent what matters to them. You are helping them find language for something that has been there the whole time.

You are not trying to force a list. You are helping them discover what is already there.
## OPENING STORY
### Two Families, One Difference
Tom puts these two side by side because the contrast is cleaner than either one alone.

The first family was busy and capable and made a great many important decisions. Financial ones, relational ones, decisions about the shape of the next twenty years. What they had never done was step back and say what mattered most to them, so each decision arrived on its own and got handled on its own.

Sometimes those decisions happened to line up. Sometimes they did not. Nobody was being unreasonable and nobody was being careless. But over a period of years the accumulated inconsistency produced something that felt like confusion and eventually produced tension, and the tension was hard to trace to any single decision because it had not come from any single decision.

The second family had done the work. Not elaborately. Nobody had produced a bound document or hired a consultant. They had simply taken the time to get clear about what mattered most, clearly enough that everyone in the family could say it.

What that gave them was a reference point.

When something came up, they had a question available to them that the first family did not have. Does this line up with what we have said matters most.

Tom's summary of the difference is worth holding onto. One family was reacting. The other had a framework.

## THE FRAMEWORK
### The Five Areas of Legacy
This is the spine of everything Tom does with a family, and the reason he can hold a values conversation without it becoming abstract. Each area is a place to look, and each one produces different material.

- **Personal Legacy.** Who this person is and is becoming. Faith, character, health, the things being modeled without commentary.

- **Family Legacy.** The relationships themselves. How the family communicates, what it celebrates, what it avoids, and what the next generation is being prepared for.

- **Financial Legacy.** The resources, the plan, and what the resources are actually for. This is the area every other advisor is already working.

- **Business Legacy.** For owners, the continuity of the enterprise and its effect on the people in it. Tom's two entry questions here are whether there is a business continuity plan, and how the business is affecting its employees.

- **Kingdom or Charitable Legacy.** What goes beyond the family. Tom adapts the naming to the client. Where a family does not share a faith commitment, he uses charitable or philanthropic rather than Kingdom, and the conversation is the same conversation.

The reason five areas works better than a values exercise is that it gives a client somewhere concrete to stand. Asked what he values, a man will produce abstractions. Asked what he wants to be true of his business in ten years, he will tell you something real, and the value is sitting inside the answer.

Most families arrive with one of the five heavily developed and the other four unexamined. The unexamined ones are where the work is.

**Take one client family and mark which of the five you have actually discussed with them. Which area have you never once raised?**

## THE TOOL
### The Goals Conversation
This is the first step of Stage 1 of the Multi-Generational Legacy Coaching Process, and it is the instrument that makes everything downstream possible.

**What it is.** A conversation with the client across the five areas of legacy, asking what they would hope to accomplish in each one. Not a questionnaire handed across the table. A conversation, run by you, with the five areas as the structure underneath it.

**What you are listening for.** Tom keeps three things in view throughout: their goals and objectives, their needs, and their challenges. The third is the one advisors miss, because clients volunteer goals and conceal challenges.

**Where it sits.** Stage 1 is the Family Legacy Review, and it has three steps. The Goals Conversation across the five areas comes first. The review of the existing plan comes second. The Family Legacy Goals and Gaps Analysis is the deliverable that comes third, and it is what the client is actually paying for.

**How Tom handles the fifth area.** Where a family does not share his faith commitment, he does not use the word Kingdom. He uses charitable or philanthropic. The substance of the conversation does not change. This is worth copying, because the alternative is losing the fifth area entirely with a large part of your book.

**On charging for it.** Tom charges a flat fee for the Family Legacy Review, in a range he names in the companion book, and his reasoning is straightforward. He does not know where the gaps are until the preliminary work is done, so the review has to be its own engagement rather than a free precursor to one. *[Fee guidance for certified coaches pending from client. Tom's own range is stated in the Coaching Book, Chapter 5.]*

**Running it for the first time.** Take one existing client, book ninety minutes, and walk the five areas in order without bringing any material into the room. Personal first, not financial. The order is doing work. A client who starts in the financial area will stay there.

### Day Three: The Five Areas of Legacy
*Family Legacy Coaching by Design*, Chapter 6, opening sections

The five areas laid out in full, with the questions Tom uses to open each one.

**Coaching insight.** Read this with a specific client in mind and mark the questions you would not currently be comfortable asking that person. That list is more useful than the chapter. ### Day Four: Personal, Family, and Financial Legacy
*Family Legacy Coaching by Design*, Chapter 6, middle sections

The first three areas in depth.

**Coaching insight.** Notice how much of the personal area a client will answer if simply given room, and how rarely anyone gives them the room. The financial area is the one you are expert in and it is also the one where a client is most guarded, because it is the area where he has been sold to most often. Going there last is a trust decision, not a sequencing preference.

### Day Five: Business and Charitable Legacy
*Family Legacy Coaching by Design*, Chapter 6, closing sections

The last two areas, including the fifth child idea and the reasoning behind naming a donor advised fund rather than specific charities in the documents.

**Coaching insight.** The fifth child reframe is the single most transferable move in this chapter. A direct question about a large charitable gift gets a quick no. The same gift, framed as a structural share of the family estate, has been accepted by families who would have declined the direct version, and Tom notes it took eighteen months in the case he describes. Patience is part of the technique rather than a substitute for it.

Clarity is not a deliverable a family receives from you. It is something they arrive at, usually slowly, and often while saying things they have never said in front of each other. Your contribution is the structure and the patience, and the five areas give you both.

This is also the point where this series opens into something larger. The full treatment of this work is a six-session title of its own, called *Clarity*, and everything in this session is the doorway into it.

Next session takes up the problem that clarity alone does not solve, which is that five people can each be perfectly clear and still not be aligned.
