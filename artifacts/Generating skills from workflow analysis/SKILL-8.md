# Pricing Packager

## Governing decisions (established Aug 2026 — don't re-derive, extend)

- **Church products price by attendance, never by seat.** Unlimited seats at every tier; attendance is the only variable; one invoice, one renewal date. Per-seat pricing makes churches ration access and turns every volunteer into a purchase order.
- Attendance bands: **Under 250 / 250–1,000 / 1,000–2,500 / Over 2,500.**
- **The free tier is permanent, not a trial**, and diagnostics/assessments are never gated — they're the funnel. Gate the builder, the generation engine, full text, and print files.
- **Ladder integrity rule:** à la carte must never undercut the subscription. Test every price list: two à la carte purchases must exceed all-access at every size. Print the comparison side by side — a buyer about to spend $590 on one thing will spend $400 more for everything.
- **Digital is the sample; print is the product.** Digital free at every tier where strategic; per-copy revenue in print and fulfillment.
- **The church is the unit, not the small group.** Never quote custom work for one group of 12. Configured-from-profile is the product (90% of perceived value at ~5% of cost); named tiers (Configured / Adapted / Fully Custom), never hourly.
- Big-ticket engagements ($8K–$250K+) are **sold by conversation, kept off the self-serve price list.** Advisor/practice licensing is a separate sales motion, off the church price list entirely.
- Founding-cohort offers: real discount, price lock, named permanently, direct access — with asks framed as participation (use it, honest quarterly call, one case study), and intros requested "only if it's working, never as a condition."

## Delivery-cost realities

Email is a rounding error — include it everywhere, absorb it, never mention it. SMS is not — meter it as a priced add-on by subscriber count. Native apps: default answer is no; the sequence is email+SMS → integrate into the church's existing app → PWA → native only if retention data demands. App stores take 15–30% of in-app purchases.

## Ministry-trust constraint

For anything church-facing, economic design must visibly protect pastoral trust. Churches are a transformation environment, not a sales channel. Prefer hybrid models where philanthropic capital launches what revenue sustains.

## Honesty rules

- Every number is labeled **reasoned or validated**. Nothing in Brett's pricing has been market-tested unless he says so; carry the standing caveat: the structure will survive contact with the market, the dollars probably won't.
- Recommend the validation move: take the offer to ~10 real buyers across bands and ask "what would you have to stop paying for to say yes."
- **Flag collisions.** If a new price conflicts with an existing price architecture for the same product (multiple ladders, split naming), surface the conflict and propose one reconciliation — never silently create a third price.

## Output format

Deliver a complete straw-man price architecture: tiers with numbers, the à la carte set with the ladder-integrity check shown, what's free and why, the metered items, the big-ticket line, and the caveats — then the two or three decisions Brett actually has to make. Rank models by **defensibility, not size** (strong / promising / speculative).
