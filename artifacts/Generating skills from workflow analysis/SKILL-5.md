# Donor Brief

## The posture (governs everything)

Wealthy families are **invited into mission, never treated as funding sources.** The objective is discipleship and deployment, not extraction. They can contribute time, talent, treasure, testimony, networks, experience, leadership, business wisdom, influence, and intergenerational legacy — a donor document should make room for more than the check.

Framing vocabulary: legacy, stewardship, calling, significance, family mission, generosity, multiplication, Kingdom impact.

## What donors need to see

**Leverage, vision, credibility, measurable outcomes, spiritual meaning.** Every ask must answer: what does this dollar multiply? Donor communication is visionary, relational, spiritually grounded, strategic, and specific about leverage — never manipulative, never pressured, never vague.

## Credibility rules

- Track record stated precisely (25 years, 500+ church relationships, 50M+ campaign resources distributed — verify current figures with Brett before print).
- Field evidence verified only; no invented testimonials, no implied endorsements.
- Status labels on everything funded: what exists, what's pilot, what the gift builds. Never present a concept as an operating program.
- Where economics are reasoned but not market-validated, the document says so — donors respect honest math ("this doesn't fund the business, it funds the answer").

## Ask architecture

- Name specific giving levels with what each level accomplishes (typical ranges in Brett's world: $25K / $100K / $250K / strategic multimillion — confirm per project).
- Prefer the hybrid model: philanthropic capital builds or launches what becomes sustainable through revenue — show the donor the handoff point.
- Show the multiplication chain explicitly: gift → asset built → churches/families reached → testimonies → further multiplication.
- **Never end on the invoice.** Structure: vision → credibility → the plan → outcomes → giving levels (second-to-last) → the ask wrapped back into the vision (last).

## Prose

Full paragraphs, authored voice, grounded story. One real story of transformation outworks three pages of strategy. No line-stacking, no generic inspirational filler, no unearned superlatives. Scripture (NIV) only where it does real work.

## Formats

Default deliverables: a one-page executive version AND the full document, in the same response when feasible. For designed versions, hand off to the brochure-builder skill (navy/gold — this is partner/buyer-facing material).
