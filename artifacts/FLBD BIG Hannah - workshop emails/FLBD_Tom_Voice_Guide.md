## 1. Voice & point of view
- **First person.** Tom says "I," "my heart," "here's what I've come to believe," "I've learned the hard way." Biggest shift from the spring emails (which avoided "I"). It's what makes him trustworthy.
- **Flowing, connected sentences** that build an idea — not stacked one-line fragments. Tighter than a live transcript, but warm and unhurried.
- **Teaches through conviction and story:** "After years of sitting with families of wealth, here's what I've come to believe…"
- **Generous and humble** — credits others by name (Randy, Jim, Jerry, John Warnick, Jay Hughes), admits he's "a slow learner," jokes "that's why I'm so smart." Never self-important.

## 2. Faith — in the posture, not the pulpit (for cold-ish sends)
- Tom is **deeply faith-rooted**: discipleship, stewardship, "God gave you everything you have," quotes Paul ("impart not only the gospel but our lives").
- **For August's re-engagement (faith advisors, but lukewarm): lead with the faith *posture* — stewardship, "heart for the next generation," serve-before-you-ask — and let explicit scripture/God language deepen later in the sequence.** Don't open a cold-ish inbox on overt God language unless Tom wants to.
- His real anchor line: **"Figure out what you can do for a family before you ask anything from them."** Use it.

## 3. Tom's actual teaching (real content for value-add blocks)
**Four characteristics of a legacy planner** (his words):
1. **Heart preparation** — genuinely care about the soft-side work, even though it takes time.
2. **A multi-generational mindset** — think beyond your client and spouse to their kids and grandkids.
3. **Skills** — the relational/conversation skills you can actually learn.
4. **A collaboration mindset** — "you don't live in a silo"; bring in others' perspective.

**The three questions** that started it all (he'd ask wealthy clients):
1. "Tell me who your advisors are."
2. "Do you have anyone who: (a) clearly knows your goals, (b) knows *everything* you have — not just the pot they manage, and (c) built you a strategy to accomplish those goals with what you've been entrusted?"
3. "Do your advisors share your biblical worldview?"
→ The answer was almost always "no, no, no." **That gap is why Family Legacy by Design exists.**

**"The right side of the table"** (Scott Fithian's image Tom uses): the trusted advisor sits *next to* the client — not scattered among the CPA, attorney, and insurance reps. The goal is to be that person.

## 8. Do / Don't
| Do (real Tom) | Don't (spring agency style) |
|---|---|
| First person — "I've come to believe…" | Third-person brand voice — "Tom walks through…" |
| Flowing, connected sentences | Choppy stacked one-line fragments |
| Warm, story-driven, names real people | Polished, abstract, name-free |
| Faith in the posture (stewardship, next gen) | Faith stripped out entirely |
| Humble, generous, a little funny | Slick / urgent / hype |
| "I'd love to show you" | "Reserve Your Seat" on every block |
