# Family Legacy by Design — August Workshop
## Revised Campaign Strategy (for Tom & Brett)

Built on what the spring (May 6–7 Atlanta) campaign actually told us.

---

## 1. What actually happened in the spring

- ~42,000 faith-based financial advisors, ~20 sends over ~3.5 weeks → **9 registrations.**
- Opens looked healthy (~25%) and early click rates looked spectacular (32%, 9.8%) — but those early numbers were almost certainly **inflated by security bots** scanning links on a brand-new sending domain. The honest human signal was the *later* sends: ~25% opens, **~1.7% clicks.**
- A **deliverability crash** mid-campaign (Apr 15–16: 2.5% and 1.8% opens, a 23% bounce) followed a ~39k single-day blast — classic new-domain reputation damage.
- **Over-mailing:** 7 sends in the final 8 days, including three "LAST CALL" emails in a row. Opens drifted down (26% → 19.9%); unsubscribes rose to ~0.29%.

**The only metric bots can't fake is registrations. That number was 9.**

---

## 2. What 9 registrations actually means

It rules things in and out:

- **Not an email-craft problem.** You cannot subject-line your way from 9 to a full room. The copy was never the bottleneck.
- **Not (mainly) a broken-page problem.** If most early clicks were bots, real human traffic to the page was small — so "lots of traffic, no conversion" isn't the story.
- **It IS a re-engagement + offer-friction + targeting problem.**

The audience is *right* — faith-based financial advisors are exactly Tom's people. But two things worked against us:

1. **They didn't know Tom.** Credibility was never established before the ask.
2. **The ask was huge and cold:** $2,500 + two days + travel, pitched directly to people who'd never heard of him.

Cold-ish audience + unknown authority + high-friction offer = the hardest sell in marketing. 9 isn't a broken campaign; it's the predictable result of skipping the trust step.

---

## 3. The audience truth (important nuance)

These advisors are **not cold — they're familiar-but-unconverted.** They saw ~20 emails in the spring. That means:

- **Upside:** Tom's name now has some recognition. "Oh, the legacy workshop people" beats "who is this?"
- **Catch:** they already met the offer and **overwhelmingly passed.** Repeating the May playbook to the same people confirms their decision to tune out.

So August is **not** a cold-introduction campaign, and **not** a re-run of May. It's a **re-engagement campaign with a fresh angle and an easier first yes.** They didn't reject Tom — most rejected (or ignored) "$2,500 + travel" before they trusted it was worth it. They're stuck at "is this worth my time and money," which is far more winnable than pure cold.

---

## 4. Five strategic shifts for August

1. **Sell Tom before you sell the workshop.** Lead with credibility a faith advisor respects — NCF (Eisenberg), Blue Trust (Crosson), CAP, the stewardship community. The spring proved this: the faith-targeted sends were the only ones that genuinely landed.
2. **Make the first ask free and low-friction.** Do not open a familiar-but-skeptical advisor with "$2,500 — reserve your seat." Offer a small yes first (free webinar / intro session / guide / brochure / clip). Sell the workshop as the **second** ask, to people who raised their hand.
3. **Segment by spring behavior — this is new and high-leverage.** Mailchimp now knows who engaged in May. Split the list:
   - **Warm (opened/clicked in spring):** more direct workshop push; they're past "who are you."
   - **Cold (never opened):** soft re-introduction + the free step; do not hit them with deadlines they haven't earned.
   Treating these the same flattened the spring results.
4. **Fresh angle, not a May reprint.** New hooks, new framing, "a new chapter from the legacy workshop" — not the same "Common Questions / LAST CALL" beats they already ignored.
5. **Protect deliverability: fewer, spaced, warmed sends.** The Saturday-anchored 7-week calendar already fixes the spring's over-mailing and blast pattern. Start smaller, ramp volume, never blast a cold 39k in a day, never stack three last-calls.

---

## 5. The funnel, redesigned

**Spring (what failed):** Cold list → "$2,500, reserve your seat" → 9.

**August (proposed):**
1. **Re-introduce + give value** (free articles, clips, faith credibility) — build trust, ask nothing big.
2. **Low-friction first step** (free webinar / intro session / guide / brochure) — the easy yes.
3. **Sell the workshop** to the warmed subset who engaged — escalate to pricing, then deadline.

The workshop is the *upsell to the warm*, not the opening line to the lukewarm.

---

## 6. What this means for the 16 emails we built

The mockups are solid, but several are pitched at a **warm** audience ("Reserve your seat," pricing, deadlines) that this list isn't ready for yet. Changes:

- **Early emails (awareness/education):** strip the hard "Reserve your seat" CTA; replace with the **free first step** (webinar/guide/clip) and Tom-credibility proof. Pure trust-building.
- **Middle emails:** keep the value/education, add the faith-credibility proof up front, soft CTA.
- **Late emails (ROI/urgency/final):** keep mostly as-is — but send the **pricing/deadline/urgency emails primarily to the warm segment**, not the whole cold list.
- **Across all:** more value/"beef" (your instinct was right — for this list, value-add *is* the strategy), faith-credibility forward, and behavioral-segment routing.

---

## 7. Decisions needed before the rebuild

1. **Is there a free, low-friction first step?** (free webinar / intro session / the site's "Free Introductory Course" / a downloadable guide). *This is the single most important decision — it defines the funnel.* If yes, it becomes the early CTA. If genuinely nothing, brochure + clips + faith proof must carry trust-building.
2. **Can we segment by spring behavior** in Mailchimp (openers/clickers vs never-opened)? (Almost certainly yes — confirm access.)
3. **Articles:** draft the 4–5 trust-building articles now, or placeholder blocks?
4. **More endorser proof** beyond the seven we've used, for the credibility-forward early emails?

---

## 8. Metrics that actually matter going forward

Stop optimizing against bot-inflated opens/clicks. Track:
- **Registrations** (the only honest number).
- **Free-step signups** (the new mid-funnel conversion).
- **Click-to-registration rate** on the *warm* segment.
- Mailchimp's **bot-filtered** data, and clicks-per-human-open over a longer window.
- Deliverability: delivery %, bounce %, and the spam-folder warning signs (sudden open-rate collapse).

---

### Bottom line
The spring didn't fail because the emails were bad. It asked a familiar-but-unconvinced, faith-advisor list to make a $2,500 cold commitment to someone they didn't yet trust — and skipped the trust step. August wins by **re-engaging with a fresh angle, leading with Tom's faith credibility, offering a free first step, and segmenting by who actually engaged** — then selling the workshop to the warm.
