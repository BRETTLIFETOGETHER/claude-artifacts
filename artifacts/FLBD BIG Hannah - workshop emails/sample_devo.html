What Do You Want to Be Remembered For?

Helping families focus on what matters most

“Teach us to number our days, that we may gain a heart of wisdom.”

Psalm 90:12, NIV

When people think about legacy, the word tends to stay big and broad. It floats. Everyone agrees it matters, and almost no one can say what it is. Ask a successful couple what they want their legacy to be, and you will often get a long pause, because the question is too large to hold all at once.

That is why Tom does not start with one question. He starts with five areas, and the first is the most personal of all. Your personal legacy. What do you want to be remembered for? And inside that question sits another one, quieter and deeper. What is your spiritual legacy going to be?

From there the areas unfold. Your family legacy, the story your children and grandchildren are living in right now. Your financial legacy, the wealth transfer, and whether the inheritance you leave will be a blessing or a curse, because money left to unprepared hands does not benefit a life, it burdens one. Your business legacy, if you own one, and the honest questions of succession and continuity. And your charitable legacy, what you want to do for the Kingdom of God, both now and at the end of life.

Five areas instead of one squishy word. That is the whole move. Clarity does not come from thinking harder about a vague idea. It comes from breaking the idea into places you can actually stand and look around.

The Principle

Legacy becomes clear when it becomes specific. A family cannot aim at “leaving a legacy,” but they can answer what they want to be remembered for, what they hope for their children, what their wealth should do, what happens to the business, and what they will give. Specific questions produce real answers. Real answers produce direction.

Today’s Practice

Take one sheet of paper and write the five areas down the side: personal, family, financial, business, charitable. Beside each one, write a single honest sentence. Do not polish anything. The goal today is not a plan. It is a first look at where clarity already exists in your life, and where it does not yet.

Reflection Questions

1.If the people closest to you described what your life is about right now, what would they say, and is that what you want to be remembered for?

2.Which of the five areas felt easiest to write a sentence for, and which one did you want to skip?

3.What is one conversation this week that would bring more clarity to the area you wanted to skip?
