# A Welcome From Tom

*[Drafted from Tom's own language across the six teaching sessions. Needs Tom's review and signature before print.]*

Most of the clients I serve are older. That one fact has shaped everything I do, because it means the future of their legacy is not with them. It is with their children.

I have spent a long time in rooms with families who had done everything right on paper. The documents were current. The entities were correct. The tax work was excellent. And the thing still came apart, because the generations had stopped talking to each other, or because nobody had prepared the people who were about to receive it.

That is what this series is about. Not the transfer. The people.

I want to say two things before you start.

The first is that this is simpler than it looks. People assume legacy work is complicated because the estates are complicated. It is not. Love Jesus. Teach your children to love Jesus. Hope they teach theirs. If that happens, most of the rest takes care of itself, because a person who knows God's principles will generally try to live by them. Everything in these six sessions is downstream of that.

The second is that I am not the expert here, or at least not the only one. God has given me some wisdom, and I have also got a lot of friends who have done a better job raising their families than I did. I ask them questions. I learn from them. You should be doing the same thing. Look around at the families near you who have done this well, go to them, and ask them how. Do not take one person's word for it, including mine.

You will notice that almost everything I do with a family comes down to asking a question and then being quiet. That is not a technique I developed. Look at how Jesus handled people. When somebody asked him a question, he usually asked one back, and he already knew the answer. If that is how he did it, it is good enough for me.

One last thing. I have four decades of this behind me and I still tell people that God has done a good job on my kids in spite of me. We are all flawed. We are trying our best and we are not always doing our best. If you are waiting until you have your own house in order before you take this to a client, you will wait forever. Start where you are.

Thank you for doing this work. The families you serve are not short of advice. They are short of somebody who is interested in who their children are becoming.

Tom Conway

# Using This Guide

## This works alone, in a pair, or on a team

Read that first, because it changes how you use everything that follows.

You may be working through this by yourself. You may be doing it with one partner. You may be running it across an entire team. All three work, and no arrangement is the preferred one.

Wherever a prompt would benefit from conversation, you will see this line:

> **Team or pair: discuss. Solo: write it down.**

That is your fork. If there are other people in the room, talk it through. If there are not, write your answer in the space provided. Nothing in this guide requires another person to be present in order to work.

There is no facilitator layer in this series. There is no leader's guide, no separate script, and nobody is designated to run the meeting. Everyone holds the same book.

## Some things stay private on purpose

The Exercise in each session is done alone. It is not a discussion item, and it is not meant to be read aloud even when you are working with others. It asks you to apply the session to a specific client family and then to yourself, and the second half only works if you are honest.

The same is true of every self-check.

## Your privacy, and your clients'

You are never required to disclose sensitive personal or financial specifics, your own or anyone else's.

Write at whatever level of detail you would be comfortable having in a book you carry into a client's office. The exercises are built to work without names and without numbers. Where a prompt asks about a client family, "a family I have served for twelve years" is a complete answer.

You already hold professional confidentiality obligations. This guide does not ask you to set any of them aside.

## The videos are the teaching

Each session has a teaching video, and the Learn section assumes you have watched it. The videos are conversations between Brett Eastman and Tom Conway rather than lectures, which is why the Learn section does not use fill-in-the-blanks. Instead, each prompt names what Tom and Brett covered and asks you to apply it to your own book of business.

Watch the session video before you work through the Learn section. Everything else in the session will hold up either way.

## The Scenario Responses are in the back

Each session includes three scenarios drawn from situations Tom has actually worked. The responses to all eighteen are collected at the back of this guide, behind a divider, in session order.

They are in the back deliberately. Write your own answer first. The value is in committing to a position before you see how Tom would handle it, and that value disappears entirely if you read ahead.

## If you are doing this with a group

Rosters, calendars, and a team agreement live in an optional appendix section called *If You're Doing This With a Group*. Skip it if it does not apply to you. Nothing in the six sessions depends on it.

## Pacing

Six sessions. Most teams run one every two weeks, which leaves room for the readings and for at least one real client conversation between sessions.

The readings at the end of each session come from *Family Legacy Coaching by Design*, five per session, one per day. They assume the daily read actually happens. If you only do one thing between sessions, do that.

# Outline of Each Session

Every session follows the same order.

**Introduction.** The key verse, and the frame for the session.

**Opening Story.** One story from Tom's work or his own life.

**Get Started.** A privacy reminder and two questions to open with.

**Building a Healthy Culture.** Your own team and practice, not your client's family. Treated at length in Session 1 and carried as a one-line reminder after that.

**Learn.** Watch the session video, then four capture prompts that move the teaching onto your own book of business.

**Reflect.** Seven questions. The seventh is always personal rather than about a client.

**The Framework.** The session's named framework, with one application question.

**Scenarios.** Three situations, with space to answer. Responses are in the back.

**The Tool.** One of Tom's real instruments, described the way he actually runs it.

**Exercise.** Private, three parts, done alone. A client, then deeper, then you. Closes with a five-item self-check.

**Circles of Influence.** Two names. Invitation only, no commitment.

**Next Steps.** Three or four actions with checkboxes.

**Practice Conversation.** Four questions to take to a colleague, a partner, a mentor, or a family you serve.

**Going Deeper.** Three passages, two questions each.

**This Week's Readings.** Five readings from the companion book, one per day, each with a coaching insight telling you how to use it in practice.

**Final Thoughts.** Two prompts and a closing word.
