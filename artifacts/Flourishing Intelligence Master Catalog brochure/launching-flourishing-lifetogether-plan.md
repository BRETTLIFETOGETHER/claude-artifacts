# Launching Flourishing LifeTogether
### What Transfers from Purpose Driven, What's Actually Missing, and the Plan to Close the Gap

---

## Part One: Transferable Principles (Not Just Tactics)

The nine mechanics from Part Two are tactics. Underneath them are five principles that transfer regardless of brand — these are what Flourishing LifeTogether should actually borrow, not the specific tools:

1. **Content follows commitment, not the reverse.** The book didn't summarize the church campaign — the campaign followed the book's momentum. Individual pull has to exist before institutional push works.
2. **Completeness beats brilliance.** A finished, mediocre kit gets used. A brilliant, half-finished framework gets admired and shelved. Nothing launches until the whole kit exists — sermon outlines, session content, family and kids editions, all of it.
3. **Doctrinal minimalism is a growth strategy, not a compromise.** The fewer specific theological commitments required, the wider the door. This is already true of the five-purposes foundation; the discipline is refusing to add anything more specific as it scales.
4. **Proximity-based coordination beats isolated asks.** A pastor says yes faster when twenty peers in the same city are already saying yes. Coalition-building is a recruitment mechanism, not a downstream outcome of success.
5. **Trust compounds from a small, credible base.** National reach is never the starting move — it's what happens after a handful of pilots produce results real enough to talk about without exaggeration.

None of these require copying Purpose Driven's specific brand, book, or campaign structure. They're the actual mechanics underneath the brand — which is what makes it possible to build a genuinely different model that still learns from what worked.

## Part Two: What Makes This a Different Model, Not a Copy

Three things are already true of Flourishing LifeTogether that weren't true of the original Purpose Driven system, and they're worth building the difference around on purpose rather than downplaying them:

- **It's whole-life from day one, not purpose-first.** Purpose Driven Life answered one question (why am I here) and let the church model follow. Flourishing LifeTogether starts with ten (or seventeen) domains of an actually whole life — joy, health, relationships, work, money, leadership, legacy. That's a broader promise, which is both the opportunity and the discipline risk (see Gap 3 below).
- **It has a secular-to-faith deployment path built in.** Purpose Driven never had to answer "can a non-Christian company run this." The Universal/Values-Ready/Formation-Ready tier structure already built for Employee Intelligence means Flourishing LifeTogether can enter workplaces and secular institutions Purpose Driven never touched — a genuinely new distribution channel, not just a new market.
- **The founder is the same person, twenty-five years later.** Brett isn't building a parallel system hoping to learn from Purpose Driven's success — he built the original infrastructure. That's not a marketing claim to make carefully; it's the single strongest asset in this whole plan, and it should be leveraged directly and early, not held back for later credibility-building.

## Part Three: The Honest Gap Analysis

Here's what actually exists today versus what Purpose Driven had in place before it scaled.

| Component | Status | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Whole-life content architecture | **Built** — 425-study and 17-dimension versions both exist | Two versions share one name; nothing can launch until this resolves (Priority 1 below) |
| Church health model (circles, diamond, snapshot) | **Designed** — The Flourishing Church model is built out | The Snapshot is a concept, not yet a usable one-page tool a staff can actually fill out monthly |
| Founder credibility | **Real and available** | Not yet built into any launch material — the founder story isn't in front of anyone yet |
| Flagship consumer book | **Does not exist** | This is the single largest gap. Purpose Driven's entire pull strategy depended on it |
| Campaign kit (sermons, videos, guides) | **Titles and session names exist; scripts do not** | 85–100 campaigns have six-session structures, but none have been written as deliverable sermon outlines or video content |
| Results tracking | **Not built** | No mechanism exists yet to produce the "22% attendance, 20% giving" kind of reportable number |
| Training/certification conference | **Named tiers exist (Guide, Coach, Host, Ministry Leader)** | No curriculum, no format, no date |
| Pilot church list | **Does not exist** | No specific churches or pastors have been identified or approached |
| Sequel campaign | **Does not exist** | Only one flagship six-week campaign is drafted; nothing built for what comes after week six |
| City coalition target | **Does not exist** | No city has been chosen for a first regional launch |

This is not a criticism of the work so far — it's an accurate map of a system that has built its foundation (content architecture, church model, founder asset) but not yet its launch mechanics (book, kit, tracking, pilots). That's actually the right order to have built things in. It's just not launch-ready yet.

## Part Four: Priorities, in Sequence

Not everything above matters equally right now. In order:

1. **Resolve the naming collision.** Pick which Flourishing LifeTogether — the 425-study or the 17-dimension version — is the flagship. The book can't be written from an undecided foundation. (Recommendation: the 17-dimension version is more comprehensive but far less tractable as a single reader's journey; the 425-study version, already organized as ten clear domains, is the more natural spine for a single consumer book. The 17-dimension version can become the deeper, second-tier curriculum for churches that finish the flagship and want more — which conveniently also solves Gap 9, the missing sequel.)
2. **Write the flagship book.** This is the pull mechanism everything else depends on. It needs to exist before the campaign kit does, not alongside it.
3. **Build the actual campaign kit from the resolved catalog** — real sermon outlines, real small-group video scripts, not just session titles.
4. **Build the Flourishing Snapshot as a working tool**, even a simple one, so pilot churches can produce real numbers from week one.
5. **Identify and personally approach five to ten pilot pastors** — this is where the founder relationship asset gets spent, not saved.
6. **Draft the sequel campaign** (using the 17-dimension catalog, per the recommendation above) before any pilot church finishes week six.
7. **Design a first, modest training conference** — regional, not national; livestream is acceptable for a first cohort.
8. **Choose one city for the first coalition launch**, timed after pilot results exist, not before.

## Part Five: The Launch Plan

**Phase 0 — Foundation (now, before anything is public)**
Resolve the naming collision. Write the flagship book. Build the campaign kit for the flagship six-week series. Build the Snapshot as a real tool.

**Phase 1 — Quiet Pilot (a small number of trusted churches only)**
Personally recruit five to ten pastors Brett already has real relationship with — not a public invitation. Run the six-week flagship campaign. Track results with the Snapshot from week one. Say no to any request to expand until this phase produces genuinely reportable numbers.

**Phase 2 — Proof and Sequel**
Publish the pilot results honestly, including where it didn't work as well as where it did. Release the sequel campaign (drawn from the deeper 17-dimension catalog) to pilot churches finishing week six. Hold the first small training conference for the pilot pastors' staff and key volunteers, building the first cohort of certified Flourishing Guides.

**Phase 3 — First City Coalition**
Choose one city with an existing base of relationship — likely wherever Brett's own network is strongest. Recruit twenty-plus pastors for a synchronized regional launch, using the pilot results from Phase 2 as the actual pitch. Launch the Flourishing Church Award for this cohort.

**Phase 4 — Regional Expansion and National Visibility**
Repeat the city-coalition model in two or three more markets. Only at this point does broader visibility — media, denominational partnerships, national conferences — become the goal rather than a side effect of results already proven.

---

## What This Plan Deliberately Does Not Do

It does not launch publicly before the book exists. It does not build a training conference before there's anyone trained enough to teach it well. It does not choose a city before there are real numbers to bring into that city. Every phase above exists specifically to avoid the failure mode most movements hit: momentum before infrastructure, visibility before proof. Purpose Driven's real advantage was never the five purposes — it was the discipline of building the whole machine before turning it on. That discipline is the one thing worth copying without any adaptation at all.
