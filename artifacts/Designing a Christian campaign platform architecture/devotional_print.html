Day 01God Owns It All · Week One

Whose Is It, Anyway?

"The earth is the LORD's, and everything in it."
Psalm 24:1 (NIV)
Reflect
Most of us live as owners. We say my money, my time, my house, my career — and that small word "my" quietly runs our lives. But Scripture opens with a bigger story: before anything was ours, it was already His. God owns it all — not because He needs it, but because He made it.

That one truth changes everything. If God is the Owner, then you and I were never owners at all. We're stewards — trusted managers of what belongs to Someone else. A steward doesn't ask, "How much of my money should I give to God?" A steward asks, "How much of God's money should I keep for myself?" That shift takes the pressure off. You don't have to clutch and worry over what was never ultimately yours to hold. Over the next forty days, we'll learn to open our hands — with our money, our time, and our very lives — and discover the freedom of trusting the Owner.
Pray
Father, everything I have is Yours. Loosen my grip on what I've called "mine," and teach me to hold it all with open hands. Trade the weight of ownership for the freedom of trust. In Jesus' name, amen.
Reflect & Respond
Where in your life have you been living as an owner instead of a steward?
One step today: Write down three things you tend to call "mine." Beside each, write: "God's — entrusted to me."

Day 02God Owns It All · Week One

The Freedom of Open Hands

"Godliness with contentment is great gain."
1 Timothy 6:6 (NIV)
Reflect
Closed hands can't receive anything. When we grip what we have out of fear, we not only hold on tighter — we shut ourselves off from everything God wants to hand us next. Paul knew a secret that set him free in both plenty and want: contentment. Not resignation, not apathy — contentment. The settled confidence that God is enough, and that what He provides is enough for today.

Discontentment always whispers the same lie: you'll be happy when you have a little more. But "a little more" is a horizon that keeps moving. Open hands break the spell. When you loosen your grip, you stop measuring your life by what you're missing and start seeing what you've already been given. Generosity, it turns out, isn't first about money — it's about freedom. The most generous people you know aren't the richest; they're the ones who've stopped being owned by what they own.
Pray
Lord, I confess how easily "a little more" pulls at my heart. Teach me the secret of contentment. Make my hands open — ready to receive from You and to give to others. Amen.
Reflect & Respond
What is the "little more" you've been believing will finally make you content?
One step today: Name one thing you already have that you often overlook — and thank God for it out loud.

Day 03God Owns It All · Week One

First, Not Leftover

"Honor the LORD with your wealth, with the firstfruits of all your crops."
Proverbs 3:9 (NIV)
Reflect
There's a world of difference between giving God our first and giving Him our leftovers. Leftovers are what remains after everything else has had its claim — the bills, the wants, the upgrades. First is different. First is a decision made before the month begins, before the pressure builds, before fear has a chance to argue.

When we honor God first, we're preaching a sermon to our own hearts: God comes before my comfort, and I trust Him with what's left. It's not that God needs the first of our income — He owns it all already. It's that we need to give it. The first check we write, the first hour we set aside, the first "yes" we offer — each one quietly re-orders our lives around the right center. Try it, and watch what happens: putting God first has a way of putting everything else in its proper place.
Pray
Father, I want to give You my first and not my leftovers — my best hours, my best energy, my best resources. Reorder my life around You. I trust You with what remains. Amen.
Reflect & Respond
In your budget, your calendar, or your energy — where has God been getting the leftovers?
One step today: Choose one area — giving, time, or attention — and plan how God gets the first of it this week.
